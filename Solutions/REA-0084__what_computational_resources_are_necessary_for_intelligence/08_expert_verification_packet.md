# Expert verification packet: the Tether on computational resources and intelligence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in computational limits can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of capability against a ground is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "what computational resources are necessary for intelligence?" re-censes into a question about capability. Capability is a property of agent and habitat, never of the agent alone, so an answer that counts only resources inside the agent is aimed at the wrong denominator. Cost has two currencies that do not add: one prices the description, one prices finding out whether it holds against the ground; summing them into one efficiency figure is the named error. There are two categorically different limits: a budget ceiling, where more search finds a term already in the closure, and a barrier, where no search inside the same primitives adds a primitive and an import is required. No finite budget arrives, because there is no last room. Benchmark and hardware metrics are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. The framework returns this shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not return a resource number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - capability is agent-and-habitat, not an agent budget (re-census)

- **Statement:** the word "intelligence" does no work in the unmaintained case; the answerable question is what makes a system capable, and capability belongs to the agent-and-habitat pair.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :34-36, *"What is intelligence? The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* and *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* Fig 11 :39-40: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **What to check:** does the cited figure actually say this (it does, in the local verbatim extract); and is the re-census legitimate for a question about computational resources, or does the resource domain stay independent?
- **Refuter:** a controlled case in which a purely agent-internal increase in compute, with habitat and contact held fixed, moves the ground. Fig 11 :72 names this directly: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

### C2 - two currencies, and a single efficiency figure is the named error

- **Statement:** description cost and settlement cost are different quantities in different units. A single "efficiency" number that sums them is the error, not the fix.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125, **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"** Fig 12 :129: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."** Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**
- **What to check:** is the action price reducible to the bit price without loss, in which case the figure's distinction collapses? The physical literature prices erasure in energy (Landauer) and bounds total ops (Lloyd), but does not price settlement against a ground; check whether that is a gap or a real distinction.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single accounting that prices both in the same units and does not suffer the shortfall named in Fig 12 :127: *"and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."*

### C3 - two limits, with different instructions

- **Statement:** a budget-limited gap is closed by more search; an underivability barrier is crossed only by an import. Treating them alike either wastes compute or imports an atom that was not needed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32, **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Fig 8 :40, **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."** Fig 6 :23, **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 7 :30-31, *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives."*
- **What to check:** whether the no-free-lunch results (Wolpert and Macready) and the Levinthal case constitute genuinely independent corroboration of the barrier side, or merely a shared evidence pool restated. The ledger records them as low overlap; a checker may disagree.
- **Refuter:** a case in which the two limits issue the same instruction; or a search inside a fixed closure that adds a primitive to that closure; or a finite last room after which nothing further can be asked.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a resource number, an efficiency constant, or a scaling exponent. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that extra compute never helps. For a budget-limited gap, more search is exactly the remedy (Fig 8 :41).
- Not that physical ceilings are unreal. Landauer's bound, its experimental confirmation, and Lloyd's bounds are real and are secondary corroboration only.
- Not that the two readings are isomers as normally written. They share no atoms; the isomer form is the stronger, unmeasured claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not that benchmark scores are worthless. They are filters for the budget and are explicitly excluded from the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The five pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 are repeated here in compressed form:

1. Agent-internal compute with contact fixed moves the ground.
2. Description and settlement cost priced in one accounting without loss.
3. Search inside a fixed closure adds a primitive.
4. The two limits issue the same instruction.
5. A finite last room exists.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :34-40, Fig 12 :125-129, Fig 8 :32 and :40-43, Fig 7 :30-31. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own field: is the capability denominator agent-and-habitat, or is an agent-only budget sufficient in the cases you know?
3. Check C2 against your own accounting: do you already price description and settlement in one currency, and if so do you avoid the shortfall?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a budget-limited gap from a barrier in your domain, or say why none is possible. Fig 6 :34 is the relevant rule: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
