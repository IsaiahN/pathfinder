## The question restated as an effect

The question asks us to decide between two answers and says what each leads to.

- **Answer A (the resource-budget reading):** intelligence is a quantity that a system has, and the way to get more of it is to supply more compute, data, parameters, memory, time, or energy. Efficiency is a single frontier over those inputs. This leads to a research programme that measures the inputs and ranks systems on a curve.
- **Answer B (the contact/closure reading):** capability is a property of an agent together with its habitat, and the binding constraint may lie outside the agent's budget. Efficiency has two incommensurable prices, and there are two different kinds of limit. This leads to a research programme that first describes the residual and the habitat, then asks which limit is in play.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run: *a shape giving what any sufficient answer must contain, namely (i) the unit in which capability is measured, (ii) the currencies in which the cost is paid, (iii) a distinction between budget limits and underivability, and (iv) the ground that would settle the claim.* The framework's own rule requires this because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question; making the sought-for shape an ordinary term lets it accrue a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the conclusion.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies agreement.
2. **The verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Sending the full framework before asking for a verdict spends the only thing the outside frame contributes.
3. **Budgets are filters, not witnesses.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Compute, venue prestige, and publication count are budget quantities.
