## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran it on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-40 (capability as agent-and-habitat), Fig 12 :125-129 (two currencies), Fig 8 :32 and :40-43 (closure and two limits), Fig 7 :30-31 (no last room).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: "capability is contact; two currencies; two limits; no last room; metrics are filters."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the question as posed: "what computational resources are necessary for intelligence and what is the fundamental efficiency limit?"

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **habitat denominator** from Fig 11 :39-40, which the resource question drops;
- the **action price** from Fig 12 :126, which an efficiency figure in FLOPs or watts cannot carry;
- the **two-limit distinction** from Fig 8 :40-43, which a single "fundamental limit" phrase collapses.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: physical ceilings are real and some are tight (Landauer, Lloyd), so a budget framing is not empty, it is merely incomplete. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error in Fig 4 :30. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
