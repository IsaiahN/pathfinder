# The Tether's answer: how the five disturbances produce ecological change

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `gap` on the numerical direction and magnitude of a named compound's effect. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Five disturbance types are usually studied one at a time. The observation that fails to resolve is that they overlap and chain: drought changes the fuel before fire, fire changes the canopy before rain or storm, heat and low water act at once, and the same total forcing in a different order yields a different recovery trajectory or a different state. The question is therefore not "what is the impact of each event" but "what is the shape of the compound that produces ecological change". Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** The compound has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape, with the four verdicts kept separate

**D1, the disturbance regime. Verdict: union compound, not a list.** Heat, drought, fire, storm and flood are conditions and events in the habitat, indexed by intensity, duration, timing, sequence and bond type. Anchor: the instrument record of the event. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** The intersections are categorically different from the single-condition regions. Verdict status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and two independent world fields (disturbance ecology and climate science).

**D2, the habitat and contact. Verdict: enumerate, never compose.** Actors are survivors, legacies, species and relations; currency is material and information legacy; anchor is field observation of who survives, colonises and interacts. Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** Effects cascade past direct contact: Fig 11 :52 **"And contact cascades."** Verdict status: `general`, single-source within the figures (Fig 11), world-corroborated at low overlap.

**D3, the ecological response. Verdict: per compound, per slot, sign unbound.** Track each compound against each ecological slot, not against an aggregate impact score. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Valence is relative to the residual, so the same legacy can buffer one outcome and amplify another (Fig 11 :49). There is no universal synergy verdict: the empirical record includes less-than-sum mortality (Lucash et al. 2018), sign reversal across time (Seidl et al. 2014) and an underrepresentation of buffering studies (Kleinman et al. 2019). Verdict status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 11 and independent ecology sources.

**D4, verification and management. Verdict: the ground decides; management adds but cannot subtract.** Anchor is the measured recovery trajectory or state variable, never model agreement. Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Management is asymmetric: Fig 11 :61 **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Verdict status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10, with one ecology case.

The four verdicts are not averaged. Their anchors differ: an instrument record, a field census, a recovery trajectory, and the ground itself. Summing them would be Fig 1 :22's error (*"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

## 3. The five claims that survive

1. **Compound before impact.** The ordered compound with its typed linkage is the object to track; the event list is only its ingredient list. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
2. **Route through habitat.** The effect runs through enumerated legacies and contact and is steps out before the residual that matters appears (Fig 11 :43, :52).
3. **No fixed sign.** A legacy's valence is relative to the outcome being tracked (Fig 11 :49-51), so the direction of a named compound is a ground question.
4. **Per-slot tracking.** Report the compound-by-slot response, not one ecosystem impact number (Fig 1 :19, :22).
5. **Direction and magnitude are a gap.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named compound raises or lowers a particular ecological variable by a particular amount.
- Not that compound effects are always synergistic, amplifying or promoting. That reading was retracted in audit section 6.
- Not that the five disturbances are members of the six-term substrate. That mapping was retracted.
- Not a replacement for event-by-event field measurement; the shape is a constraint on what such measurement must track.
- Not a theory of everything, and not grounded. No measurement settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A path-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. The corresponding questions here:

- **Order test.** Do the same two events in reversed order, at comparable sites with legacies mapped, produce distinguishable recovery trajectories? If not, F3 fires.
- **Reach test.** Does the measured effect appear beyond direct contact, at two or three steps out in the contact web? If not, F4 fires.
- **Sign test.** Does the same legacy change sign when the tracked residual changes (recovery from the first event versus resistance to the next)? If not, F5 fires.
- **Discrimination test.** Can a landscape recovery index distinguish drought-then-fire from fire-then-drought and from heat-plus-drought? If it cannot, it is a metamerism instrument and cannot name the compound.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the eight conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ecological ground exists, recovery trajectories are measurable, and the compound events are already being recorded. What is missing at this budget is the paired event-by-response dataset with legacies mapped across enough sites to discriminate the arrangements. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not predict how much a given heat wave, drought, fire, storm or flood will change an ecosystem, and it does not claim that their effects simply add. It says that the five form an overlapping compound in which order and the kind of link are part of the object, that the ecological effect runs through legacies and contact and can be three steps out before the signal that matters appears, that the same legacy can buffer one outcome and amplify another, and that the response must be tracked per compound and per ecological slot rather than folded into one impact score. The direction and magnitude of any named compound are a declared gap, owed to a path-discriminating field measurement, not to a further argument. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap. The gap stays open until the ground settles it.
