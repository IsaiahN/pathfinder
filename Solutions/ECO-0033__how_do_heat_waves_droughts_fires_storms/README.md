# Solution ECO-0033: how heat waves, droughts, fires, storms and floods interact to produce ecological change

**Question:** How do heat waves, droughts, fires, storms, and floods interact to produce ecological change?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 42

**Section:** 4. Climate change, disturbance, and ecological thresholds

**Field for public contact points:** compound disturbances research.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the numerical direction and magnitude of any named compound's effect. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Question restated as an effect.** The prediction currently available is a set of single-disturbance response curves: one for heat, one for drought, one for fire, one for storm, one for flood, each indexed by intensity and duration, with ecological change read as recovery toward a reference state. The observation that fails to resolve is that these events overlap and chain (drought preconditions fire, fire preconditions debris flows after rain, heat and drought act together), so the same total forcing in a different arrangement yields a different recovery trajectory and sometimes a different ecosystem state. The decision is between (A) treating the five as a list of separable effects whose impacts add, and (B) treating them as a union compound whose intersections have their own loop and their own ground. Each option leads somewhere different: A licenses a single aggregate impact score and a single lever per disturbance; B requires the compound, the order and the contact path to be tracked separately, and the direction of any named compound to be left open until a measurement settles it.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run:** a map from an ordered disturbance compound, through an enumerated habitat of legacies and contact relations, to a per-slot ecological response, with direction and magnitude left unbound until the ground pays. The sought-for shape is a decomposition into compound, habitat and response, not a list of named outcomes.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: a room does not supply an anchor (Fig 2 :35); selecting for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40); and asking a reader to absorb the framework before giving a verdict spends the only independence they bring (Fig 10 :42). A Track B direct-outreach list is out of scope for this folder.
