# Expert verification packet: the Tether on compound disturbances

**What this is:** a claim in a form a compound-disturbance researcher or disturbance ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `gap` on numerical direction and magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Heat waves, droughts, fires, storms and floods are not five separable effects that add. They form a union compound in which order and the typed link between events are part of the object. The ecological effect runs through an enumerated habitat of legacies, survivors and contact relations, and can be three steps out before the residual that matters appears. A legacy's sign is relative to the outcome being tracked, so no universal synergistic direction follows. The response must be indexed per compound and per ecological slot, never folded into one aggregate impact score. The direction and magnitude of any named compound are a declared gap owed to a path-discriminating field instrument. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus ecology-facing; checkable by reading Fig 9 and the event record

- **Statement:** the five disturbances form a union compound, not a list; the intersections have their own loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** and Fig 9 :57, **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- **What to check:** in the system you know, do the co-occurring disturbances produce a result that is not the sum of single-event results? Buma (2015) separates linked interactions (next event's likelihood or severity altered) from compound interactions (recovery altered); Zscheischler et al. (2020) and NCA5 (Singh et al. 2023) classify preconditioned, multivariate, temporally and spatially compounding events. Do those distinctions survive in your data?
- **Refuter:** show the five effects are additive and separable in all systems and all orders, so the union reduces to a list and no intersection carries residual.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against event sequences and recovery trajectories

- **Statement:** order and bond type are constitutive, and the route runs through legacies and contact past direct effects. Same event set under a different bond meets Fig 12 :117, **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 11 :52, **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- **What to check:** do drought-then-fire and fire-then-drought, with legacies mapped, produce distinguishable trajectories? Is the measurable effect confined to directly affected individuals, or does it appear in the contact web two or three steps out? Seidl et al. (2014) found the sign of a remnant legacy reversing over 500 years; Niggli et al. (2022) found cascading impacts propagating through sectors; NCA5 gives the fire-then-heavy-rain mudslide case.
- **Refuter:** reversed order yields the same trajectory in every measured system, or the compound's effect is strictly zero beyond direct contact in every system studied.

### C3 — framework-internal with an ecology-facing consequence

- **Statement:** no universal sign follows; report per compound and per slot. Fig 11 :49, **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 1 :19, **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **What to check:** does the same legacy (retained seed trees, dead wood, a surviving canopy) change sign when the tracked outcome changes? Does one aggregate recovery index hide a compositional shift that a per-slot reading sees? Kleinman et al. (2019) found the recovery-metric choice sometimes determined whether a compound interaction was detected; Lucash et al. (2018) found less-than-sum mortality under concurrent disturbances.
- **Refuter:** a legacy has a fixed sign for every ecological residual, or one aggregate metric fully determines every per-slot response with no slot-level information.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a direction or magnitude for any named compound, and no threshold number.
- Not that compound effects are always synergistic or amplifying. That reading was retracted; Lucash et al. 2018 is the recorded counterexample at the level of a reported result.
- Not that the five disturbances are members of the six-term substrate. That mapping was retracted.
- Not a substitution for field measurement, and not a management prescription beyond the asymmetry of addition versus subtraction (Fig 11 :61).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an ecosystem measurement.
- Not that the world sources corroborate independently. The ecology reviews share one evidence pool, NCA5 and Zscheischler share a typology, and the four essays share the framework author's pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A path-discriminating field instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case by discriminating sources, not by re-describing the reading.

1. **Order test:** reversed event order with legacies mapped yields distinguishable trajectories, or does not.
2. **Reach test:** the effect appears at two or three steps out in the contact web, or does not.
3. **Sign test:** the same legacy changes sign as the tracked residual changes, or does not.
4. **Discrimination test:** a recovery index separates drought-then-fire from fire-then-drought and from heat-plus-drought. If it cannot, it cannot name the compound.
5. **Standing falsifiers:** the eight conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3, plus the per-finding refuters in section 2.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55/:57, Fig 11 :43/:49/:52 and Fig 12 :117, five short passages.
2. Check C2 against one system you know: does event order plus a legacy map change the recovery trajectory, and does the effect travel past direct contact?
3. Check C3 against one recovery metric you use: does it hide a compositional or interaction-level change?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate two arrangements of the same events, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
