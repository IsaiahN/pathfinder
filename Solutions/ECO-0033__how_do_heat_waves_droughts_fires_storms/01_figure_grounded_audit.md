## 0. The residual in effect terms

> Question: How do heat waves, droughts, fires, storms, and floods interact to produce ecological change?

State it as a prediction gap, per Formula step 1 (`R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot):

- **The prediction currently available.** Each disturbance is tracked on its own: an intensity-duration curve, a return interval, a single-event recovery trajectory. Ecological change is predicted as the sum, or the worst case, of the five single-event responses. This is the library Γ as it stands in much of the literature (Buma 2015; Burton, Jentsch and Walker 2020).
- **The observation that fails to resolve.** Drought changes the fuel and the tree's water status before the fire arrives; fire removes the canopy before the storm or the heavy rain arrives; heat and low water act at once; the same total forcing in a different order produces a different trajectory, and sometimes a shift to a different state rather than a slower return to the old one. Seidl, Rammer and Spies (2014) found that the legacy patch which buffered the first reburn later *increased* fire severity. Niggli et al. (2022) found impacts propagating through sectors three steps from the heat and drought event. The relation, not the event's magnitude, is what moved.
- **The residual is indexed by the ordered disturbance compound and by the ecological slot** (a population, an interaction, a function, a state variable), never by a single event and never by one aggregate "ecosystem impact" number. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step."**

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a map from an ordered compound, through an enumerated habitat of legacies and contact relations, to a per-slot response, with the direction of each response left open until the ground settles it. The sought-for shape is *a decomposition into compound, habitat and response*, not a list of named ecological outcomes. I write that shape down here so it can accrue a residual like anything else rather than remaining an invisible preference. Fig 5 :65: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"**.

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** A disturbance compound is at least binary in events, but the ecological effect is third or higher order through the habitat: contact cascades past the directly affected patch. Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** The effective arity of the residual is therefore at least three, not one event and not one pair.
- **Symmetry.** The bond between two events is ordered and typed. "Linked" (event A changes the likelihood, extent or severity of event B) is not the same relation as "compound" (event A changes the recovery from event B). The order is constitutive. Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** A legacy that buffers one outcome amplifies another; the same survivor patches reduced early reburn severity and raised later severity in Seidl et al. (2014).
- **Scale.** First order is the event and the directly disturbed patch. Second order is the legacy and the contact path. Third and higher order is the community or landscape state shift. Cross-scale compounding is where the remainder re-enters and amplifies: Fig 13 :31: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"**. The question asks about the second and third order, while most instruments sit at the first (Fig 3 :27).

## 2. Findings

### F1 — Compound disturbances are a union compound, not a sum (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Fig 9 :58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Five disturbance types are not five independent questions. Heat and drought co-occur; drought and fire are linked; fire and heavy rain are temporally compounding. The world literature converges on the same structural point. Buma (2015) separates *linked* interactions (one event alters the likelihood, extent or severity of another) from *compound* interactions (one event alters the recovery from another). Zscheischler et al. (2020) and the Fifth National Climate Assessment (Singh et al. 2023) classify compound events as preconditioned, multivariate, temporally compounding and spatially compounding. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** An aggregate "total disturbance load" is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the ecological outcome.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Triangulation:** Fig 9 (framework) and Buma/Zscheischler (low-overlap world fields, ecology and climate science) agree on the union structure; the world sources are corroboration only and one pool each. **Refuter:** show that the five effects are additive and separable in all systems and all orders, so the union reduces to a list and the intersections carry no residual.

### F2 — Disturbances act through the enumerated habitat, not from forcing values (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**

Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Heat, water and season are entries in the condition set. Fire, storm and flood are events that alter that set and leave legacies in it. The figure forbids composing an ecological outcome from forcing values alone. The effect must be read off the world through the survivors, the dead material, the seed source and the contact paths that remain. This is why single-event response functions, which compose a damage score from an intensity variable, cannot answer the compound question by construction. Buma (2015) reaches the same practical conclusion from inside disturbance ecology: interactions require *"disaggregating disturbances into their constituent legacies"* and finding where prior legacies alter the mechanism.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** Fig 11 for enumeration, Fig 12 :5 (**"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**) for the structural point that the ingredient list is not the arrangement, and Buma 2015 from a different field. **Refuter:** derive a compound ecological outcome from the five forcing magnitudes with no habitat observation anywhere in the chain.

### F3 — Order is constitutive: the same events under a different bond are a different compound (triangulated)

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :95: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Drought then fire is not fire then drought. Fire then heavy rain is not heavy rain then fire. The event list can be identical while the compound is different, because the typed relation between the events is not recoverable from the event list. The operational distinction in the literature matches: a *linked* interaction changes the next event's severity, a *compound* interaction changes the recovery from it (Buma 2015; Walden et al. 2023). Seidl et al. (2014) show the sign of a legacy reversing with time since the first fire: remnant patches lowered severity in the first 100 years and raised it later. NCA5 gives the concrete case of wildfires in 2017 followed by heavy rainfall on burned landscapes producing mudslides and debris flows (Singh et al. 2023).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 12 (composition) and Fig 3 :20 (dependency order), with low-overlap world corroboration from disturbance ecology and climate assessment. Fig 12 is used as an analogue for order and typed linkage, not as a literal taxonomy of ecological disturbances (see section 6, strain S1). **Refuter:** exhibit a pair of disturbances for which every ordering gives the same recovery trajectory and the same state outcome in every measured system.

### F4 — Contact cascades: the residual that matters is steps out (single-source, world-corroborated)

Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

A disturbance that hits one patch does not stop there. The dead wood changes the next fire, the lost canopy changes the soil temperature, the fish kill changes the predator, the flood carries the ash into the stream. Scoping to direct contact is exactly the first-order enumeration the figure warns against. Niggli et al. (2022) found cascading impacts propagating through numerous sectors with far-reaching consequences, and specifically that heat and drought stress weaken organisms, which are then more vulnerable to storms and wildfires. NCA5 documents heat, drought, low streamflow and low tides combining into toxic algal blooms and mass die-offs of shellfish and low survival of salmon (Singh et al. 2023).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 11 and its DDT case), corroborated but not proved by two low-overlap world fields. **Refuter:** exhibit a compound whose measurable ecological effect is strictly zero beyond the directly disturbed individuals in every system studied.

### F5 — Valence is relative to the residual: no legacy has a fixed sign (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

This is the figure-grounded reason there is no universal sign for a compound interaction. A remnant seed source is a benefactor for the recovery-of-carbon residual and a malfactor for the resistance-to-next-fire residual when it becomes ladder fuel. Seidl et al. (2014) report exactly this reversal. Lucash et al. (2018) found that tree mortality under four concurrent disturbances was *less* than the sum of the single-regime mortalities, evidence of negative feedbacks among disturbances, which is a dampening, not an amplifying, compound interaction. Kleinman et al. (2019) found that studies of buffering interactions that increase resilience were underrepresented, which is a literature bias, not evidence that buffering is absent. Burton, Jentsch and Walker (2020) note that disturbance-promoting interactions *apparently prevail* over inhibiting ones, a frequency claim, not a law.

**Mode:** `general` (valence rule) / `specified` (sign of a named compound). **Triangulation:** Fig 11 and three independent ecology sources, low overlap with the framework. **Refuter:** show a legacy whose sign for every ecological residual is fixed and independent of the compound that follows it.

### F6 — Track per compound and per slot, never as one aggregate impact score

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step."**

The "object" here is the compound acting on one ecological slot. A landscape-level index can stay nearly constant while one interaction is lost and another replaces it, or while composition shifts for centuries and carbon recovers in decades. Seidl et al. (2014) found that legacy effects persisted for 500 years on species composition, converged after 236 years on ecosystem carbon, and persisted to year 446 on structure. Kleinman et al. (2019) found that the choice of recovery metric sometimes determined whether and what kind of compound interaction was detected. Reporting only one aggregate is how the live signal disappears into the average.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** Fig 1 (agent) and Fig 11 (habitat), low overlap; corroborated by two independent ecology studies. **Refuter:** show that one aggregate metric fully determines the per-slot response of every compound with no slot-level information.

### F7 — The response link is the unmeasured middle link

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Events are well instrumented: meteorological stations, remote sensing, fire severity indices. Recovery trajectories, interaction changes and state shifts are much less so. Kleinman et al. (2019) found that only a limited set of disturbance types, regions and response metrics had been studied, and called for more holistic recovery quantification. NCA5's traceable account records that compound events are rare, the observational record is short, and literature on their societal and ecosystem impacts is limited and difficult to quantify (Singh et al. 2023). By Fig 3, effort spent above the break cannot rescue the link below it. The honest report is which link the chain stops at.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `single-source` (Fig 3), corroborated by two world sources. **Refuter:** name an instrument that measures compound ecological response at the same resolution and coverage as event forcing, and show the middle link is measured rather than assumed.

### F8 — The ground settles it; agreement among models does not

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

The anchor here is the measured recovery trajectory or state variable: who survived, what regenerated, which interaction changed, which function crossed a threshold. Two models that draw on the same occurrence and event data are one frame wearing two names; their agreement on which compound will flip a system is not a witness. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The cost here is an ecosystem state shift that is hard to reverse, so the independent bearing is worth buying.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulation:** Fig 2 and Fig 10 :42 (**"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**), low overlap. **Refuter:** show a case where agreement between two models settled the direction of a compound ecological change without any field measurement of that change.

### F9 — The direction and magnitude of a named compound's effect is a genuine gap

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

The six-term set plus five disturbance names does not contain the direction of a particular compound's effect on a particular ecosystem, nor the threshold at which recovery flips to a state shift. To get "drought then fire then a wet winter moves this forest to shrubland at this soil-moisture threshold" you need an enumerated habitat and a field measurement, not a derivation. This is a `gap` after a genuine census, stated plainly. It is not an excuse for silence: the shape (union compound, enumerated habitat, per-slot tracking, serial order, open direction) is a checkable result, and the instruments that would close the gap are nameable (paired event-response monitoring, legacy mapping, before-and-after control data at the interaction level).

**Mode:** `general` (limit) / `specified` (the shape). **Status:** `gap` on direction, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and the canonical README's *"a shape, not a name"*. **Refuter:** derive the sign and magnitude of a named compound's effect from the six terms and the event list alone, with no field input.

### F10 — Amplification across scales is a remainder effect, stated as an analogue

Fig 13 :31: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"**

Fig 4 :20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Disturbance cascades cross scales: a patch-level fire becomes a landscape fuel pattern, a regional drought becomes a synchronous fire danger across states. The figure's statement about a remainder compounding through an amplifying chain is a general structural claim, and the ecological reading is an analogue rather than a quote about disturbance. Marked `specified`, `single-source` within the figures, corroborated at low overlap by the NCA5 account of synchronous events across connected locations and by Burton et al. (2020) on cascades in chains or networks. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A patch-scale result crosses up only as a method, never as a recording of one occasion.

**Mode:** `specified` analogue. **Refuter:** show a disturbance cascade whose effect at the landscape scale is fully recovered from a single patch event with no remainder and no change of actor or currency.

### F11 — Management is asymmetric: you can add legacies, not subtract contact

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

You cannot remove a disturbance from a habitat; you can add surviving trees, add roughness, add a seed source, add monitoring. Seidl et al. (2014) frame retention forestry as exactly this addition of legacies, while noting that the same retention can raise later fire severity. Moving a population away from a disturbance-prone site substitutes a habitat and inherits that habitat's actors, which were not chosen. The management verdict is therefore permanent work under an asymmetric power, not a solved state.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 11), corroborated by Seidl et al. 2014. **Refuter:** show a management action that subtracts an established habitat contact without substituting another one.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the formula's "Direction" discipline and the seat map's own rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* (Seat Map :287). The sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term earlier in section 0, as Fig 5 :65 directs.

The shape claim (five disturbances form a union compound; order and typed linkage are constitutive; effects run through an enumerated habitat and cascade past direct contact; valence is residual-relative; response is tracked per slot; the numerical direction is a gap) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The five effects are additive and separable in all systems and all orders, so Fig 9 :55/:57 does not govern them (breaks F1).
2. A compound ecological outcome is derivable from the five forcing magnitudes with no habitat observation anywhere in the chain (breaks F2).
3. Every ordering of at least one disturbance pair gives the same trajectory and state in every measured system (breaks F3).
4. A compound's measurable ecological effect is strictly zero beyond directly affected individuals in every system studied (breaks F4).
5. A legacy's sign for every ecological residual is fixed and independent of the following compound (breaks F5).
6. One aggregate metric fully determines the per-slot response of every compound, with no slot-level information (breaks F6).
7. The sign and magnitude of a named compound's effect are derivable from the six terms and the event list alone (breaks F9 and closes the gap; this would strengthen the framework but refute the claim that direction is owed to the ground).
8. A management action subtracts an established habitat contact without substituting another one (breaks F11).

If any fires, the decomposition is withdrawn or revised. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not an honesty device.
