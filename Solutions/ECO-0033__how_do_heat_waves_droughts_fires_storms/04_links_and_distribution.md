## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Buma 2015** gives the terminology this question needs: *linked* interactions alter resistance (likelihood, extent, severity of the next event), *compound* interactions alter resilience (recovery time or trajectory). It is the reason F1 and F3 keep order and bond type separate.
- **Kleinman et al. 2019** is a systematic review of compound disturbances in forests and shows that the choice of recovery metric changes whether an interaction is detected. It is the empirical backing for F6.
- **Burton, Jentsch and Walker 2020** classify random co-occurrence, individual links and chains or networks of multiple links, and explicitly include biological legacies, thresholds and lag effects. It is a classification of events, not a derivation of ecological direction, so it cannot close F9.
- **Seidl et al. 2014** is the strongest single case for F5: the same remnant legacy reduced early reburn severity and raised later severity, and doubling the fire frequency reduced total ecosystem carbon by 10.5 percent and late-seral presence by 18.1 percent.
- **Lucash et al. 2018** (record found via the Ecosphere listing and the search index) found tree mortality under four concurrent disturbances was less than the sum of single-regime mortalities, a negative feedback result. It is recorded as a conflict with a universal-synergy reading and returned to the census in section 6 of the audit, where the universal sign was retracted.
- **Cascading failure** in power grids and finance is the low-overlap analogue: the failure of one part increases the load on the next, and interconnection, not the individual component, carries the risk. It corroborates the shape of F4 by analogy only. Its agreement is not evidence about an ecosystem.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them any bibliography.
- **Fig 2 :40**, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A set of recipients selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the local links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Overlap declaration

The four ecology reviews (Buma, Kleinman, Burton et al., Johnstone) draw on one broad disturbance-ecology pool and count as **one witness**, not four. NCA5 and Zscheischler et al. share the compound-events typology and count as one witness. Niggli et al. and the engineering cascading-failure source are genuinely low-overlap fields and corroborate only the shape of the cascade verdict. The four framework essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent weight.
