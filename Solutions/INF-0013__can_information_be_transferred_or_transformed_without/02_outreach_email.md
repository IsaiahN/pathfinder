**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the gap in F8 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is preserved in the text. The email must not claim a thermodynamic verdict the figures do not give. If it does, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about information and physical change

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A change in the informational description of a carrier never comes for free. In the framework this rests on two separate observations. First, the bond is the rest of the information, and a bond that costs nothing is the flat case which carries nothing. Second, where two arrangements share all their ingredients but differ in the bond, the difference is decisive in behaviour, so the physical carrier did change even if the ingredient list did not. So my claim is narrow: any information change that is settled against the ground has a physical companion change in the carrier or its environment. I am **not** claiming that the framework tells us which thermodynamic quantity pays for it. That sub-question is open and I mark it as a gap.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. A settled information transfer with literally unchanged physical state of carrier and environment at every scale.
> 2. A bond carrying information that is settled at zero description cost.
> 3. A transfer in which no energy and no spendable difference is involved anywhere, including the receiver.
> 4. A demonstration that a change in contact can alter a reading with no physical contact change.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that information is a substance, that "information is physical" settles the cost of erasure, or that this derives Landauer's bound. Those are claims from physics, not lines from the figures. The figures price descriptions in bits; they do not price joules. I keep those two accounts separate.
>
> **Question for you:** what would you count as a failure of the shape claim above? I will record your answer before showing you any theory.
