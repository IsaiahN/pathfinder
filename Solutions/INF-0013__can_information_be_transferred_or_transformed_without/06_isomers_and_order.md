# Structure, identity and order in the information-change question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit separates three conditions that the question bundles: an information delta, a physical state delta, and a dissipation delta. Two supporting questions follow. (1) Is a "change of information with no physical change" an identity relabeling or an isomer change? (2) What is the order of the union, and what does the round trip lose?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms, plus the physical states named in the world sources (spin, charge, colloidal position, field). Bonds held: the seven, of which identity (≡) and conjunction (+) are symmetric, while sequence (→), production (⇒), subtraction (−) and comparison (⋛) are ordered. Negation is an operator but not a bond. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Identity relabeling versus isomer change

- Operators table :24: **"two names, one referent"**; :26: **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."** This is the identity bond, symmetric. Two names for one referent change the description at zero cost to the referent. This is the only honest reading of "information changed, physical carrier did not."
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Here the atoms are unchanged but the arrangement is not, and the behaviour differs. That is a physical change even under the narrowest reading of "carrier."
- Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."** The ingredient list cannot decide the case. The bond must be recorded.
- **Consequence:** the question's phrase "transferred or transformed" covers two different structures. An identity relabeling is neither transfer nor transformation; it is a convention. An isomer change is a transformation with a physical companion. Keeping them distinct is what stops the question from looking paradoxical.
- **Falsifier for Q1:** a relabeling under ≡ that settles against the ground (an instrument returns a different reading after only a name change). Then identity is not free and the distinction fails.

### Q2 - The union and its order

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The lever "information is physical" clears the region where an information delta coincides with a physical delta. It leaves the intersection with the dissipation delta standing, because reversibility decouples logical from thermodynamic cost (Bennett 2003; Sagawa 2014; both in `10_source_ledger.csv`). This is why F8 in `01` is a gap rather than a hidden answer.
- **Order by dependency:** the physical delta must be named before the information measure is scored (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*), and the dissipation condition must be asked only after the first two are unbundled.

### Q3 - What the ordered bonds add

- Fig 12 :92: **"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"**. Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms (Operators table :59-62).
- A transfer has direction (source to receiver), so it is not an identity bond and not a symmetric conjunction. In the figure's notation it is closer to sequence (→) or production (⇒): the receiver's state is produced by the transfer, or at least follows it. Transformation of information inside a carrier is an arrangement change (isomer), which may be ordered or symmetric depending on the bond.
- **Why this matters:** a "no physical change" claim smuggled in under a symmetric bond (identity) cannot be carried over to the ordered case (transfer) without an argument. The question asks about both words at once; the figures force them apart.

### Q4 - The round-trip gap R_T

- Fig 4 :31: **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"**.
- Sent up (x): the figure text (bond price, arrangement is substance, flow = energy plus gradient).
- Abstracted (T_A): "information change requires physical change, but cost is open."
- Brought back down (T_E): "erasing a bit dissipates kT ln 2."
- **R_T is nonzero.** The abstraction dropped two constraints: the distinction between a bit price and a joule price, and the requirement that the paying quantity be settled by an instrument in the frame that uses it. The concrete claim is a world import, not a figure derivation. `11_audit.md` enumerates the gap in full.

## ANALOGUE, secondary only

The no-deleting theorem (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-23) states that information is conserved under unitary evolution and that copying requires import from another part of the universe. This corroborates F3's import/export distinction from quantum information, a field with low overlap with the framework author. It is a witness for the shape, not a source of it.

Bennett 2003 (arXiv, fetched 2026-11-23) states that a logically reversible transformation can be implemented in a thermodynamically reversible fashion, *"by an appropriate physical mechanism."* The physical mechanism survives even when the dissipation goes to zero. This is the cleanest world corroboration of the split the figures force.

## FALSIFIER

Written before the conclusion: if a purely conventional relabeling under identity changes a ground reading, or if a single lever clears all three conditions of the union including the dissipation intersection, the supporting derivation fails and the main verdict must be re-censused.

## CONCLUSION

The apparent paradox dissolves under the figures' own distinctions. An identity relabeling changes a description at no cost to the referent and is not a transfer. An isomer change keeps the atoms and changes the arrangement, and the behaviour differs, so the physical carrier did change. The question bundles these with a third condition about dissipation, which the figures leave open. What survives is a checkable shape with a named gap, not a one-line answer.
