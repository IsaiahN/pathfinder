## Residual, stated as an effect

A frame reports a change in the informational description of a carrier (a message, a channel, a receiver, a library). The question asks whether that change can occur with zero corresponding change in any physical state of the carrier or its environment.

- **Option A.** Yes. Information is abstract, so semantic content can change with no physical difference. This leads to an information measure with no nameable anchor, so no instrument can test the claim (Fig 1 :27).
- **Option B.** No for any change settled against the ground. What can change with no physical change is a frame-internal re-description, and that is not a transfer. This leads to a checkable claim with an anchor (Fig 2 :29).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"):** for every settled change in the informational description of a carrier there is a physical change in the carrier or its environment, and the kind of physical change (state reconfiguration or thermodynamic cost) is stated separately with its own mode. Binding this before the run makes the sought-for shape refusable by the same test as everything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own terms:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. **Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*
3. **Showing the interior before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
