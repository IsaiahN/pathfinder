## 0. The residual, in effect terms

**Question as written:** Can information be transferred or transformed without a corresponding physical change in the carrier?

**Effect statement.** A frame reports a delta in the informational description of a carrier. The question asks whether that delta can be nonzero while every physical state of the carrier and its environment is unchanged.

| option | what it asserts | what it leads to |
|---|---|---|
| A: yes, information is abstract | semantic content can change with no physical difference | the information measure has no nameable anchor, so no instrument can test it (Fig 1 :27) |
| B: no, for anything settled | a settled information delta requires a physical delta somewhere; frame-internal re-descriptions are the only zero-physical-change case and are not transfers | a checkable claim, a stated refuter, and an anchor (Fig 2 :29) |

**Bound answer shape** (Formula "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ordinary term before the run): *for every settled information delta there is a physical delta in the carrier or its environment, and the kind of physical delta is stated separately with its own mode.* This bound shape is refusable below.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A free information change carries nothing (mode `specified`)

Fig 5 :45: **"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."**

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :99: **"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."**

Fig 12 :101: **"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."**

The framework's own information measure is a **price in bits**: stating the term plus what remains unexplained must cost less than the confusion removed. If an alleged information delta leaves every arrangement and every bond unchanged, it is the flat case in the figure's own vocabulary, and the figure says a free bond carries nothing. So a change of information with no change in the carrier is not a small transfer; it is no transfer.

**Refuter:** show a bond that carries information at zero description cost and is settled by an instrument, so the figure's price is wrong at the ground.

### F2 - The arrangement is the carrier, not just the ingredients (mode `specified`)

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The usual objection says: same physical carrier, different information, so no physical change. The figure separates two readings of "carrier." If the carrier is only the atom set, the two isomers have the same carrier; but the figure states the difference is *"decisive in the behaviour,"* which is a physical difference registered by an instrument. If the carrier includes the bond arrangement, then the carrier did change. Either way the claim of a settled information change with no physical change fails. This is the chemistry instance, not an analogy imported from elsewhere.

**Refuter:** exhibit two settled arrangements with identical atoms *and* identical bonds that nevertheless differ in measured behaviour.

### F3 - Import moves the frame, not the world (mode `specified`)

Fig 6 :25: **"An import adds no atom to the world."**

Fig 6 :26: **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."**

For a transfer into a library Γ, the figures say exactly which thing changes: the frame gains access, the world gains no primitive. So the question "transfer without physical change in the carrier" is answered by naming the carrier. If the carrier is the frame's library, it changed (an atom was added to Γ). If the carrier is the world's physical inventory, nothing was added to it. These are different verdicts in different domains and must not be averaged. The figure line does **not** say that no physical state changed; that wider claim was tried and withdrawn in section 7.

**Refuter:** exhibit an import that adds a primitive to the world rather than access to one.

### F4 - Information measures cannot be the anchor (mode `general`)

Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

An information measure is produced by the frame that uses it. By Fig 1 it cannot settle whether a physical change occurred; that needs the D2 anchor, a measurement that neither frame can move. This is why Option A is not a verdict at all: it replaces the ground with a frame-internal score.

**Refuter:** produce an information measure that varies with physical state, is independent of the frame computing it, and settles the physical question without any measurement.

### F5 - The verdict is scale-relative and the round trip must be measured (mode `specified`)

Fig 4 :27: **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**

Fig 4 :28: **"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable."**

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

At one scale (atoms) the answer can be "no physical change"; at the next (arrangement, or a settled molecule reused as an atom) it can be "physical change." Both are correct at their own level, and collapsing them into one average is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names. The round-trip gap R_T is the honest report of what the coarser "information changed" description dropped. See `11_audit.md` for the enumerated gap.

**Refuter:** show a composition whose abstraction and concretisation round trip is lossless at every scale, so R_T is measured to be zero.

### F6 - Transfer has a physical substrate (mode `specified`)

Fig 13 :76: **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."**

A transfer through a channel is a flow in the substrate's own terms: a state (energy) bonded to a structure (gradient, *"a difference that can be spent"*). A channel with no spendable difference carries no signal. This is a structural claim, not a measurement of any particular channel.

**Refuter:** exhibit a settled transfer in which no energy and no gradient is spent anywhere, including the receiver and the environment.

### F7 - A change with no contact change changes nothing (mode `specified`)

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The figure's object is capability, so applying it to information transfer is a structural analogue, marked `specified` and single-source. Read in effect terms: an information delta that leaves all contact unchanged produces no change in any reading the ground can return. It is a re-description, not a transfer. The same rule is stated for instruments in `THE_FORMULA.md` step 8: *"Nothing here maintains the ground. The ground does not decay; the channel does."*

**Refuter:** exhibit an information delta that changes the ground's reading while leaving every physical contact unchanged.

### F8 - The thermodynamic-cost sub-question is a genuine gap

After re-census, no figure line prices thermodynamic dissipation. Fig 5 :45 gives description length (bits). It does not give joules or entropy. The operator and substrate tables do not say whether a logically reversible transformation dissipates heat. Statements that do are world sources, not figure lines (Bennett 2003, Sagawa 2014, Maroney 2009, Bérut et al. 2015; all in `10_source_ledger.csv`). They are secondary corroboration and cannot be promoted into the TETHER verdict.

This is a gap, not a silence. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** and Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The figures can return the shape (a physical companion change is necessary) but not the name of the thermodynamic quantity (state flip, heat, or another conserved quantity).

**Refuter for the gap:** produce a figure line that directly prices thermodynamic dissipation, or show that Fig 5 :45's bit cost is identical to a physical entropy cost under the figures' own definitions.

1. **R1.** A settled information transfer is demonstrated with literally unchanged physical state of carrier and environment at every scale, registered at the ground. (Breaks F1, F2, F6, F7.)

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there a physical delta at all?** Yes for any settled reading; the anchor is a measurement (Fig 2 :29). D2.
2. **Which information measure?** Description length, a frame-internal price (Fig 5 :45, Fig 1 :27). Not a ground.
3. **Does a free information change carry anything?** No. Fig 12 :101. F1.
4. **Is the atom set the carrier?** Only at one scale. The arrangement is the substance (Fig 12 :94, :117). F2, F5.
5. **What moves in a transfer?** The frame's Γ, not the world's inventory (Fig 6 :25-26). F3.
6. **What is the physical substrate of transfer?** Energy bonded to a gradient, a flow (Fig 13 :76). F6.
7. **What is still owed?** A thermodynamic cost principle inside the figure set. Absent, so `gap` (Fig 13 :134). F8.

### Updated verdict

The structural shape survives: a settled information delta always has a physical companion change, and the kind of physical change must be stated separately by domain. The specific strong reading ("information is physical, therefore erasure always costs kT ln 2") is **not** figure-derived; it is a world claim about the dissipation sub-question, which the figures leave as a gap. Strains after re-census: one, named (the two senses of physical change), none hidden.
