# Expert verification packet: the Tether on numerical measures and accountability

**What this is:** a conditional claim in a form an evaluator, statistician or accountability researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the condition, `general` on its structural core. Not `grounded`.** No controlled comparison is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A numerical measure M increases accountability when it remains an anchor: external to the measured frame, unmoved when pushed, read per slot, used as a filter or witness rather than the verdict, and either constitutively about the goal or paired with a second low-overlap reading. It inverts into a target when the measured frame authors it, can move it, hides slot residuals in an average, or promotes it to the deciding outcome. The failure is a union of overlapping conditions, and an accountable measure and a gamed target are isomers over the same atoms, differing in whether the number is bonded to the ground as a witness or to the reward as the verdict. The framework returns that shape and four falsifiers written in advance. It does not name a metric and does not argue against measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 1

- **Statement:** a measure that moves when the measured party pushes on it is not an anchor; it becomes another frame and the drift is coherent, so it is invisible from inside. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it produces.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33-34 "The anchor must not update. If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **What to check:** does the anchor test actually classify a self-authored, targeted number as a frame rather than an anchor, and does the coherent-drift claim match what evaluators observe when a metric is high-stakes?
- **Refuter:** a controlled high-stakes comparison in which a self-authored, pushable number keeps tracking an independent ground with no decoupling.

### C2 - framework-internal plus practice-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** accountability requires per-slot residuals and a filter or witness role for the number; a single average hides the live signal, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** is the filter and verdict distinction a real operational distinction in evaluation practice, and does a published average genuinely mask worst-case slots in the domains you know?
- **Refuter:** a case where one aggregate preserves per-slot correction as well as a per-slot reading, or where promoting a filter to a verdict does not change the actor's behaviour toward the goal.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 9, 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the four conditions form a union compound, not a partition; an accountable measure and a gamed target are isomers over a shared atom set, differing in the bond they carry; a number is an ordering that needs a comparison and a subtraction bond to become a goal and a progress signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 13 :29-30 "A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- **What to check:** is the union reading preferable to a single-lever explanation, and does the isomer reading survive when you write the same atoms with the number bonded to the ground versus bonded to the reward?
- **Refuter:** two arrangements sharing the same atoms and the same bond produce the same goal outcomes under the same stakes; or a system with no comparison and no subtraction bond still drives the activity toward an independently specified goal without any external reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that measurement is harmful, and not that all metrics become targets. Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
- Not that any specific metric, threshold or reform is the answer. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that self-reporting is inherently corrupt; the poison is promotion to a deciding outcome (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that a settled measure stays an anchor forever. Fig 5 :69 "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient" (formula text, not a figure citation).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement (Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."). The four refuters in §2 are the operational tests.
