# The Tether's answer: when do numerical measures increase accountability?

**Mode: `specified` on the condition, `general` on its structural core. Not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A numerical measure M is introduced to make an actor's contact with a goal G visible and correctable. The residual is R_s = |M_s − G_s|, per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The two outcomes to separate are: accountability increases, meaning R_s trends down because drift becomes visible and correctable; and measure optimization, meaning M_s rises while R_s does not trend down because the actor acts on the number instead of the goal. This is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: four overlapping conditions, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :58).

## 2. The shape

1. **A measure is an anchor only while it does not move when pushed.** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33-34 "The anchor must not update. If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Once the measured party can move M, M becomes another frame and the drift is invisible from inside because the readings agree.
2. **The number must not be authored by the frame it scores.** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Accountability needs a reading the measured party cannot author, recorded before the stakes attach.
3. **The residual is per slot, not an average.** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A healthy mean is compatible with a catastrophic single slot. Publish the distribution and the worst cases.
4. **A measure is a filter or witness, not a verdict.** Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." The moment M decides the outcome, it stops being a neutral convention and becomes part of the game.
5. **Legitimacy is constitutive or requires a second reading.** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Where the goal is richer than the number, name the axes the instrument cannot measure and provide a second, low-overlap reading.
6. **The objective link breaks first, and it is the least instrumented.** Fig 3 :25-26 "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Effort spent raising M cannot rescue a link that stopped tracking G: "a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing." (Fig 3 :23).
7. **Contact is the thing that matters.** Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Raising M without changing contact with G changes nothing, however good the number looks.
8. **A number alone needs bonds to be a goal.** Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said." Fig 13 :29-30 "A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes." A goal is a comparison, progress is a subtraction (Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."). A bare KPI is an ordering over nothing.

The four-part condition in `(1)-(5)` is **necessary together**, not independently sufficient. Under Fig 12's definition an accountable measure and a gamed target are **isomers**: "same atoms, different bond, different substance" (Fig 12 :117). Same dashboard notation, opposite behaviour.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that measurement is bad, and not "ban the metrics." Campbell's own "Achievement tests may well be valuable indicators of general school achievement under conditions of normal teaching aimed at general competence" (world source) and the OECD/HLEG position support measurement; the claim is about what makes a measure an anchor.
- Not that any specific metric, threshold, regulator or jurisdiction is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that self-report is always corrupt. It is corrupt when it decides an outcome; as a non-deciding witness with an external check it is a different term (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that a settled measure is safe forever. "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient" (Fig 5 :69). A settled measure can still be moved at the next level.
- Not `grounded`. No controlled comparison was run here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A ground reading, not more argument. The four pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are repeated here and were written before the conclusion:

1. A controlled high-stakes case where a self-authored, pushable number keeps tracking an independent ground with no decoupling.
2. A case where one aggregate preserves per-slot correction as well as a per-slot reading.
3. A case where a number on an axis it does not measure keeps goal contact unchanged under stakes.
4. A case where a group chosen for prior agreement catches a ground failure that an independent checker missed.

Operational instruments already exist in the world literature: independent external observers (Campbell's Texarkana and Seattle cases, via Fordham), measures of effectiveness rather than measures of performance, data not generated by the measured organization, randomized measures and red-teaming (CNA 2022), and source-discriminating instruments in the metamerism sense (*The Hard Problem is About Source*). Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is stated with its falsifiers, and the audit stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish. Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures decide the shape; the world literature corroborates it from fields with different evidence pools, and no world source was allowed to overrule it. The second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this run's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. Indiscernibility is not identity; the record of the route is what keeps the result checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A numerical measure increases accountability only while it stays an anchor: sourced outside the people it scores, unmoved when they push on it, read per case rather than as a flattering average, used as a filter for where to look rather than as the final verdict, and either constitutively about the goal or backed by a second, independent reading for the axes it cannot see. It becomes a target when any of those fails, and the drift is hard to notice because the number and the report rise together while contact with the goal quietly disappears. The framework does not name a metric or ban measurement; it returns the shape any accountable measure must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close the question. The center stays open until a ground reading settles it.
