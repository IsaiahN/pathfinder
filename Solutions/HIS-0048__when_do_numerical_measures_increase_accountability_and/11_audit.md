## 1. Independent domain census (written from the residual line alone)

I redid the census before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is R_s = |M_s − G_s| per measured unit. Arity: one residual per slot, so an aggregate is already a data transformation, not the residual. Symmetry: exchanging the number producer with the ground changes the evidence even when the values agree (Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence."). Scale: first order acts on the goal, second order acts on the number; the failure is a crossing of these levels. Question type: union compound (Fig 9 :55), because anchor mobility, self-production, aggregation and promotion to verdict can overlap.
- **Domain 1, the measurement frame.** Actors: metric designers and the institution. Currency: numbers and the activity-to-score map. Anchor: should be the ground. If M is self-referential there is no nameable anchor, so the domain is not yet a domain. Question type: anchor legitimacy.
- **Domain 2, the measured frame.** Actors: the people or organizations being scored. Currency: actions that move M, and M itself now self-produced. Anchor: a local goal that updates to M once M is the target, so coherent drift is live. Question type: frame-internal scoring.
- **Domain 3, the ground.** Actors: patients, students, citizens, the world. Currency: actual outcomes. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Question type: contact.
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: external checkers and this seat. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is approximately zero.

Order by dependency: test the anchor before trusting the number; settle what counts as progress toward G before acting on M (Fig 3 link 3); refuse the promotion of a filter to a verdict before composing governance over it (Fig 10 :47); describe the failure before choosing a literature (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran it on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29-30, :33-34, :36-38, Fig 1 :19, :22, :27, Fig 9 :55, :60 and Fig 11 :40.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: "a measure increases accountability when it is an unmoved, externally sourced, per-slot reading used as a filter or witness to a constitutive goal; it becomes a target when any of those fails."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete governance instruction: " Publish the distribution, source the number independently, keep it advisory, add a second instrument, and red-team it."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **coherent-drift mechanism** of Fig 2 :33-34 (why nobody notices) is compressed into "it becomes a target" and is lost unless the second seat restates it;
- the **constitutive condition** of Fig 2 :36-38 is compressed into "constitutive goal" and can be read as a slogan rather than the anchor test;
- the **isomer claim** of Fig 12 :117 (same atoms, different bond) is dropped entirely by the shorthand.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: an unmoved, independent, per-slot reading used as a filter and not a verdict tracks the goal better than a movable self-authored average. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
