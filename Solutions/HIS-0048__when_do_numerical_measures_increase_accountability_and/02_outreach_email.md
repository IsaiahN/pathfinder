# Outreach draft: check one conditional claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0 is written down and the four refuters in §4 are in the message. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** A testable condition for when a metric helps accountability and when it is gamed

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a numerical measure M is proposed as an accountability device for a goal G. The prediction is conditional, not "metrics corrupt." M increases accountability when four things hold together:
>
> 1. M is an anchor the measured party cannot move, so it does not change when they push on it;
> 2. the number is read per unit, not collapsed into one average;
> 3. M is used as a filter for where to look, or a witness to the ground, and never as the final verdict of whether the goal was met;
> 4. the question is constitutively about the number, or, where it is only an instrument, a second low-overlap reading covers the axes M cannot measure.
>
> M inverts into a target, so that it rises while G does not, when any of those fails, and especially when the measured party authors the number and the number updates under their push. The drift is coherent, meaning the dashboard looks good while contact with the goal is lost, which is why it is hard to see from inside.
>
> This is a shape of the condition, not a universal rule and not a measurement. I am explicitly **not** claiming that all measurement is harmful. The literature I have read says the opposite about abandoning measurement; the claim is about what a measure must be made of to stay an anchor.
>
> **What would refute it (written before I sent this):**
> 1. a controlled high-stakes case where a self-authored, pushable number keeps tracking an independent ground with no decoupling;
> 2. a case where one aggregate preserves per-unit correction as well as a per-unit reading;
> 3. a case where a number on an axis it does not measure keeps goal contact unchanged;
> 4. a case where a group chosen for prior agreement catches a ground failure that an independent, ground-visible checker missed.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text and line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures and formula repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a measurement problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
