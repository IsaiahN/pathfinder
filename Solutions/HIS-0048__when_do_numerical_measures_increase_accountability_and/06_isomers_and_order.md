# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues for the measure condition

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the analogue. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The main audit names four failure conditions (c1 anchor mobility, c2 frame-internal production, c3 aggregation, c4 filter promoted to verdict) and calls their combination a union compound. Three questions are owed: (1) are those conditions a partition or a union, and what does the figure's isomer idea say about them; (2) what is the order of the levels at which the failure happens; (3) what is a numerical measure *as a composition*, and which bonds does a governance system need in order to use one.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union, and are the conditions isomers?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :58-59: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- c1 to c4 are therefore a **union**, not a partition. A case with c1 and c2 is not "twice as gamed"; it is a specific compound (a self-authored number that moves when pushed) whose correction differs from c1 alone (an externally sourced number that is nevertheless targeted) or c2 alone (a self-authored number that stays a witness).
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Under Fig 12's definition, an accountable measure and a gamed target can share **all the same atoms** (a number, a goal, a reward, a reported slot) and differ only in the bond: in one, the number is bonded to the ground as a witness (a comparison ⋛ against the goal); in the other, the number is bonded to the reward as the verdict. Same notation on the dashboard, different substance. That is an **isomer claim** with checkable consequences, not a metaphor.
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." All four conditions share the functional group "a number is used in a decision with consequences"; the qualifier (which bond it carries) decides whether it is an anchor or a target.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two accountability arrangements share the same atoms and the same bond to the goal/several to the reward, yet produce the same goal outcomes under the same stakes, the isomer reading fails. Then the difference is in the atoms (which number, which reward) and not the arrangement.

### Q2 - What is the order of the levels?

- First order: an agent acts on G. Second order: a governance layer acts on M. Fig 12 :103-104: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- When M is settled against the ground (an independent, unmoved reading), it may become an atom for governance: budgets, comparisons and thresholds can compose over it. When M is not settled, composing over it is invalid. Fig 3 :22-23 makes the consequence concrete: "a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."
- So the order is fixed: **settle M against G before using M as an atom of governance**. Raising M at second order cannot repair an unsettled first-order link (Fig 3 :19). This is the structural reason "more measurement" is not the lever for c1 to c4.
- Recursion caveat, kept open: a settled M can still be moved at the next level (c1). Settling is necessary but not sufficient for composing over a term (Fig 5 :69-72: "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient"). A settled measure is not automatically an unmoved anchor. This is a strain on the recursion claim, named, not hidden.

### Q3 - What is a number as a composition, and which bonds are missing?

- Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said" (spectrum). A number by itself is ordering only.
- Fig 13 :29-30: "A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes." A naked KPI is an ordering over nothing.
- Fig 13 :16: "it names what is being measured"; Fig 13 :26: "it decides what the measurement would mean." A number bonded to a state and a decision about what the measurement means is a different term from the same number unbonded.
- Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A governance system that holds only the number can say where things rank, and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere or how far along it is. The missing bonds are ⋛ (a goal is a threshold comparison against G) and − (progress is a subtraction, R_s = |M_s − G_s|).
- Fig 12 :40: "same two atoms, three substances and the bond is charged for what it could have been." A number bonded to a reward as a verdict and the same number bonded to the ground as a witness carry different information, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond. This is the composition-level restatement of the isomer finding in Q1.
- **Falsifier:** if a system with no comparison and no subtraction bond can still drive a measured activity toward an independently specified goal without any external reading, the bond reading fails.

### Q4 - Analogues and their limits

Three low-overlap analogues corroborate but never decide:

- **Cobra effect** (CNA 2022, from the bounty on cobra skins in colonial India): a reward on a proxy increases the proxy's source. Predicted shape: a targeted M decouples from G. This is the same pattern as c1 from a different field (policy analysis). Overlap with the Goodhart/Campbell pool: high within the policy literature, low with the figure text.
- **Uncertainty principle analogy** (McIntyre, Cambridge): "measuring a system usually disturbs it." Predicted shape: M moves when pushed. Field: mathematical physics. It is an analogy, not a derivation; the figure that decides is Fig 2 :33-34.
- **Metamerism** (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, re-fetched): several sources give one reading, and only a better instrument separates them. Predicted shape: per-slot independent readings, not a single aggregate. Field: perception and instrumentation. Corroborates F3 and F8.

Shadow test: each analogue predicts the four-part condition before being adopted. All three do. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :50-52), so only the record of where each came from separates them; the ledger records the fields and overlap.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions are a **union of overlapping compounds**, not a list; under Fig 12's definition an accountable measure and a gamed target are **isomers over a shared atom set**, differing in the bond to the ground versus the reward. The failure is **second order**: a governance layer composes over a measure that was never settled at first order, and settling alone is insufficient to keep it unmoved. A numerical measure is a **spectrum (ordering) that needs a comparison and a subtraction bond** to become a goal and a progress signal; without them it is an ordering over nothing. Three cross-field analogues corroborate the shape and are recorded with their provenance. Two falsifiers are written above before any conclusion.
