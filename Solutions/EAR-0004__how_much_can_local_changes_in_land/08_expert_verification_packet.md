# Expert verification packet - the Tether on local land or water change and climate

**What this is:** a claim in a form a land-atmosphere researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the refusable parts, `gap` on the magnitude.** Not `grounded`. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A local land or water change affects climate only by changing the fluxes (water, energy, momentum, carbon) across the land-atmosphere interface. If the interface does not change, nothing downstream can register the change. Once it does, the effect is a contact cascade that must be enumerated outward from the point of change, because the residual that matters is often several steps out. The result is indexed per region and per currency; a global mean near zero is compatible with a strong regional effect, and the currencies do not add. No land use has a fixed climate sign, because valence is relative to the residual. The land-atmosphere interface is a seam between two closures, so agreement within a coupled modelling system is a convention that makes the mismatch statable, not independent corroboration. The magnitude is a declared `gap` until an instrument separates the land contribution from other forcings.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and by field experience

- **Statement:** contact condition and no standing valence. A land change must alter surface fluxes to register, and the sign is not intrinsic to the land use.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Also Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is there a documented land or water change with a measured regional climate response but no detectable change in any surface flux? Is there a land-use class whose climate sign is invariant across currency, region and season?
- **Refuter:** either finding, replicated on two instruments with low overlap, breaks C1 and the shape is narrowed or withdrawn.

### C2 - field-facing; checkable by a land-atmosphere researcher against the coupling literature

- **Statement:** the effect is a contact cascade reported per region and per currency; the direct footprint is not a sufficient scope, and a pooled global number is the wrong instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52-53 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 12 :129 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **What to check:** in the cases you know, does the measured signal persist beyond the direct surface footprint, and does it change sign or magnitude across water, energy and carbon readings? Does a global mean conceal a live regional slot? Compare with Koster et al. 2004, "Regions of Strong Coupling Between Soil Moisture and Precipitation", *Science*, 2004, which reports "a lack of observational data and ... model dependence" and per-region coupling strength (abstract read via Semantic Scholar; full paper not opened here).
- **Refuter:** a controlled perturbation whose regional and global residual stops at the surface footprint at every instrument resolution after a three-step enumeration; or a demonstrated common measure in which the currencies add without loss.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the land-atmosphere interface is a seam, so coupled-model agreement is not triangulation; the magnitude is a `gap` pending a source-discriminating instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :13 - *"two frames, one field, no shared reading"*. Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **What to check:** do the land and atmosphere components of the models you use share parameterisations, calibration datasets and tuning targets? If so, their agreement is one pool. Is there a published observation design that separates the land contribution from greenhouse forcing, aerosols and internal variability, with two instruments drawing on genuinely independent evidence?
- **Refuter:** two genuinely independent instruments returning the same land signal per region and per currency (that closes the gap under Fig 2 :31, it does not refute C3); or a demonstration that the coupled components draw on independent evidence at every link.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that land use has no climate effect. The effect is real; the single number is what is refused.
- Not that any land use is always good or always bad. Sign depends on the residual and the currency (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that global means are useless. They are one labelled reading.
- Not that water, energy, momentum and carbon effects can be blended into one index.
- Not that coupled models are wrong because they agree. Agreement is a convention that makes the mismatch statable (Fig 10 :16-18).
- Not a new primitive, not an import that moves the wall, and not a derivation of one climate state from another.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in producing this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground is an instrument, not a further argument. From "The Hard Problem is About Source": an instrument that reads a shared climate signal *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here:

1. **Contact Test:** does the land change alter surface fluxes at instrument resolution?
2. **Cascade Test:** enumerate three steps out; does the signal persist and what is the feedback sign?
3. **Per-slot Test:** is the answer reported per region and per currency, with the global mean labelled separately?
4. **Source Test:** can an observation design separate the land contribution from other forcings with two low-overlap instruments?

All four must be settled before the magnitude moves from `gap` to `specified` and eventually `grounded`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :40, :49, :51, :52-53, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :129, Fig 10 :13 and Fig 2 :40. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against land-change cases you know: does the flux-contact condition hold, and does the signal persist beyond the footprint?
3. Name one observation design that would separate the land contribution from other forcings, or say why none exists at current resolution.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
