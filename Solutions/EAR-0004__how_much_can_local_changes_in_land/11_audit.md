## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :40/:43/:49/:52, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 10 :13 and Fig 12 :125/:129.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`, namely land change registers only through changed contact; the effect is a per-region, per-currency cascade; coupled-model agreement is not triangulation; the magnitude is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a requested answer to "how much": a single global climate sensitivity to land or water use, expressed in one unit and usable directly in policy.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing**, which collapses if the recipient asks for one number (Fig 1 :22);
- the **seam condition**, which disappears if coupled-model output is treated as an independent witness (Fig 2 :40);
- the **source-discriminating instrument**, which is owed but not described, so the gap becomes an unspecified deficit rather than a named instrument (Fig 3 :27, Fig 6 :34-35);
- the **operand type of the comparison**, since Fig 12 :129 holds currencies apart while a policy number would have to combine them.

One thing survives cleanly: a local land change with no surface-flux change cannot produce a climate response through this chain. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
