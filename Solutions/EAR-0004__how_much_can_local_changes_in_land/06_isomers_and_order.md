# Isomers, functional groups and order in the land-climate residual

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `gap` on the magnitude.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The record asks how much local land or water use can affect regional and global climate. Before a magnitude can be sought, two structural questions are owed:

1. Are different land changes (deforestation, irrigation, urbanisation, drainage) **isomers** under Fig 12, or members of a **functional group**, or unrelated?
2. What is the **order** of the union, and does the order carry information that a flat list of effects loses?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition? CORRECTION TO MY FIRST READING

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Fig 9 :55-56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

The four conditions (contact change, regional transport, global composition, source discrimination) overlap. The flux belongs to both the land and the atmosphere; the seam is their intersection; the scale-up consumes both. Therefore this is a **union compound** with its own loop.

- **I first read it as a partition** of land effects and climate effects to be ranked. That was wrong: it would treat a case in two conditions as a harder case of one. Retracted here and in `01` §6. The union reading is triangulated across Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 3 :20-21 (dependency order), which have low overlap (one is about unbundling, the other about chain order).

### Q2 - Are deforestation, irrigation and urbanisation isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

As the cases are normally written, they share **no full atom set**: deforestation changes albedo, roughness and carbon storage; irrigation changes soil moisture and latent heat; urbanisation changes imperviousness, roughness and waste heat. They share a **substructure**, not all atoms: each is a surface-flux perturbation that changes contact. By Fig 12's definition they are therefore **functional-group variants**, not isomers: same kind of channel, different members in the slots.

The stronger form: if each is written over the full shared habitat set `{water, energy, momentum, carbon, terrain, season, vegetation, soil}` and differs only in which flux-bond is emphasised, then they **are** isomers (same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour). That is the claim worth testing, because it would explain why two land changes with the same magnitude in one currency behave differently in another.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either case can be written without an atom that another requires (for example, an irrigation case with no water flux, or a deforestation case with no carbon flux), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. As currently written, it fails: the shared substructure is the honest claim. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), unmeasured on the physics.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The order is fixed by dependency, not by importance:

```
1. contact change      (did the surface fluxes change?)
        ↓ consumes 1
2. regional transport  (did the changed flux reach a regional response?)
        ↓ consumes 2
3. global composition  (did the regional response compose into a global reading?)
        ↓ consumes 3
4. source discrimination (can the ground separate the land contribution?)
```

A flat list loses this. In particular, step 4 is the link most likely to have no instrument (Fig 3 :24-26: the middle link has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests), and a link with no instrument is not a link that works. Effort spent building a finer global mean while step 4 is unmeasured is work above the break.

- **Falsifier:** if a result at step 4 can be used to revise step 1 without passing through steps 2 and 3, the dependency order is wrong and the chain must be re-censused. None was found in the sources read.

### Q4 - What order does the cascade itself have?

- Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

The cascade is not a straight chain: land change alters flux, flux alters atmosphere, atmosphere alters land surface state, which re-enters as input. That is Fig 12's cycle, and "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes". A chain cannot close by construction, so any account that models land use as a one-way forcing and omits the feedback back to the surface has already dropped the cycle. Its sign (amplifying or damping) is exactly what the holding question asks about, and it cannot be decided from the bond type alone; it needs measurement.

### Q5 - Does a vocabulary of effects only name a state, not a route?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- Operators table :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

"How much" is a comparison (⋛), not a property of a number. It needs a threshold between a with-land-change reading and a without-land-change reading, and that threshold needs a ground. A vocabulary holding only the magnitudes of water, energy, carbon and momentum changes can name the state and cannot name the route from a local change to a judged global effect. The bond that carries the route is the measured attribution, which is the missing instrument again.

## CONCLUSION

The land-climate residual is a **union compound**, not a partition, and its conditions are ordered by dependency (Q1, Q3). The land-change classes are **functional-group variants**, with a real but unmeasured isomer claim available if they are written over the full shared habitat set (Q2). The cascade is a **cycle**, not a one-way chain, and its sign is a measurement question (Q4). "How much" is a **comparison**, so it needs a threshold and a ground rather than a bare magnitude (Q5). No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no single blended number are required or licensed.

This rescues what is defensible in the question without pretending to answer it: the shape is checkable and its falsifiers are written down. The magnitude stays a `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence (Fig 13 :134).
