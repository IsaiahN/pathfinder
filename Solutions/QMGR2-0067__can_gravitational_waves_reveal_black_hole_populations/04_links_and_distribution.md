## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The direct answer from the instrument frame.** The LIGO summary for invisible intermediate-mass black holes states plainly that gravitational-wave observation would reveal sources which do not emit or reflect light. This is the world-side counterpart to the figure-derived shape: a second closure with a different currency.
2. **Population inference is a separate link.** The GWTC-3 paper and the O3a summary show that a detection is not yet a population claim. They carry false-alarm thresholds, selection functions and explicit uncertainty about mass gaps. This is exactly Fig 5 :69: *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."*
3. **A null is a result, not a silence.** The O4a subsolar search and the earlier intermediate-mass search both return non-detections converted into rate upper limits. That is the practical form of Fig 8 :39 *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
4. **A named population arrives with provenance.** The 2026 Caltech report attributes a ten-solar-mass peak to a possible failed-supernova channel, while explicitly saying direct evidence is still lacking. This is an import from the gravitational-wave frame with a source gate and a shadow test, not a figure derivation.
5. **The two frames are not one pool.** Nature Reviews Physics notes that electromagnetic and gravitational-wave observatories have now both "seen" black holes. Different currencies and different instruments make this convergence at independence rather than agreement inside one frame.
