## RESIDUAL

The question arrives bundled: "populations telescopes cannot see" mixes a light-null condition with a strain-positive condition, and it states the object at population scale while the figure residual is indexed by object slot. Three questions are owed: (1) is this a union or a partition, (2) what is the dependency order of the two conditions, (3) do the analogues used to explain it carry the figure structure or merely the vocabulary.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The two conditions overlap: a slot that is light-null and strain-positive is not a harder light-null, it is a specific compound. A single lever (longer exposure) clears the region where only the light-null condition holds and leaves the intersection standing because the strain condition still binds. So the split is a **union** and the conditions must get separate loops. Seat Map :211 states the same rule for verification: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single instrument can settle both conditions at once without a lossy transform between them, the split is a partition and this union reading is wrong.

### Q2: What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

1. Describe the residual in effect terms (null on a non-luminous slot).
2. Classify the light null as a channel fact, not a ground fact. Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and Seat Map :20 names *"populations pooled across a change"* as a channel failure.
3. Ask SEARCHED: is the population already inside the light closure and unfound? No, because closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32).
4. Range the strain frame against the slot (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
5. Infer the distribution from resolved events (Fig 9 :42 *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*).

Effort spent on step 5 before step 4 is effort above the break: a population model built on an unresolved channel.

**Falsifier:** if a population model can be settled without any per-slot strain reading, step 4 is not load-bearing and the order collapses.

### Q3: Is the light-null population an isomer of the strain-positive population?

Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two black-hole populations inferred from different instruments share the same physical atoms (mass, spin, distance, rate) but differ in the bond to the observing frame: flux in one case, strain in the other. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers of the observation, not of the objects**: same underlying quantities, different arrangement, different observable behaviour. As the question currently words them they share only the object class "black hole", which makes the weaker **functional-group** claim: different members of "compact population" selected by different instruments.

**Falsifier:** if the two selections can be shown to draw on the same evidence pool with no difference of closure, Fig 8 :48 applies and they are *"one frame wearing two names"*, not isomers at all.

### Q4: Does the metamerism analogue carry structure?

*The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the analogue. A colour instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source"*; several distinct sources produce one reading; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolves it. The light-null is that green reading: empty slot, cold black hole, obscured accretion and failed supernova all look the same.

The analogue carries the structure because it has the same arity and symmetry: one reading, many sources, with no internal test of the reading that distinguishes them. It does not carry the astronomy, which is why it is an analogue and not an import. Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*: the residual was described in effect terms before the gravitational-wave frame was chosen.

### Q5: Does the triangulation analogue carry structure?

Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Electromagnetic and strain instruments are two bearings on a fixed object, not two votes. The LIGO and Nature sources both describe the two observatories as having "seen" black holes by different means. Convergence here is at independence (Fig 8 :50 *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence"*), not agreement from a shared pool.

**Falsifier:** if both instruments are calibrated on the same simulated source model and share their selection function, independence falls and the triangulation analogue stops carrying structure.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union**, not a partition. Its dependency order is residual, channel classification, SEARCHED, second frame, distribution. The two selections are isomers of the observation under Fig 12's definition, with a functional-group reading as the weaker form. The metamerism and triangulation analogues carry structure because their arity and symmetry match; neither supplies the astronomy, which remains an import with provenance. The one live strain, population versus object slot, is not a figure conflict. It is the question's wording meeting Fig 1 :19, and it is resolved by treating the population as a downstream distribution over slots.
