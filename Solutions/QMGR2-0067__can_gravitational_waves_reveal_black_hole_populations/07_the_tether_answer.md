# The Tether's answer: can gravitational waves reveal black-hole populations telescopes cannot see?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A set of object slots returns no electromagnetic light. From inside that frame the null looks identical whether the slot is empty or occupied by something that emits or reflects no light. Fig 6 :27 *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* The residual lives per slot, not in the population average (Fig 1 :19). The question is whether the null is a fact about the population or a fact about the channel.

## 2. The shape

1. **A light-null is not settled absence.** Closure is idempotent, so no amount of extra exposure converts a non-luminous object into a light source. Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* The null is a channel reading, and the ground is elsewhere.
2. **The remedy is a second closure with a different currency and anchor.** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Gravitational-wave strain is such a currency, with coincident detector strain as its anchor. It does not create black holes; it changes what can be represented (Fig 6 :26 *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).
3. **The second instrument must already return something.** Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The proto-reading here is the indirect evidence from binary pulsar orbits, then bar detectors, then interferometers. That is why this is a hard question rather than a foreclosed one.
4. **The population is a downstream inference, not a direct reading.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Detectors resolve events; the distribution, selection function and merger rate come after. Fig 5 :69 *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."*
5. **The framework names no population.** Fig 13 :133 *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* A mass peak or a formation channel is a provenanced binding from the gravitational-wave frame, not a figure derivation. It carries a source gate and a shadow test.
6. **The two conditions form a union compound.** Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Light-null and strain-positive must be kept as separate loops and joined after, never averaged into one "observation" score.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the framework discovered or derived any particular black-hole population. That is a world result, with authors, a date and a selection function.
- Not that gravitational waves see everything. They see accelerating mass with a changing quadrupole, in a sensitivity band, with a selection function. A dark slot with no merger in the band remains dark.
- Not that electromagnetic observations fail. They are a different frame with a different anchor, and convergence between the two is at independence (Fig 8 :50), not a shared pool.
- Not that a null is a proof of absence. Fig 8 :39 *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not `grounded`. The astronomical answer is settled by instruments and catalogs, not by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating measurement chain, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Discriminator test:** does a strain measurement return mass, spin and rate for the same slots that are null in light, with a published selection function? A world source that passes this is a witness.
- **Depth-versus-absence test:** can any operation inside the electromagnetic closure turn the null into a resolved population without changing closure? If yes, F1 breaks.
- **Per-slot test:** is the population-level signal lossless relative to the individual events? If yes, the per-slot rule is inapplicable here.
- **Name test:** is a claimed population peak derived from the six-term substrate alone, or imported with provenance? There is no third honest option.
- Standing refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 remain open, including the requirement of a prior coarse gravitational-wave reading.

## 5. The honest stop

The derivation stops at the limit of what the figures can decide: the shape of the answer, not the name of the population. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the canonical figures README says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"*, then *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* What occupies the slots is an empirical question. The world sources in `09` and `10` answer it provisionally and with uncertainty: catalogs report strain-positive black-hole populations invisible in light, including a ten-solar-mass peak attributed to failed supernovae. That answer is `grounded` only to the extent the measurements pay for it, and it is never a verdict handed over by the framework.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that gravitational waves found any particular black-hole population. It says that a light-null cannot distinguish an empty slot from a non-luminous occupant, because more of the same looking is the same operation repeated, so the null is a channel reading rather than a ground verdict. The question therefore has the shape of a second-frame question: an instrument with a different currency and anchor, here strain rather than light, must range the same residual, and the population is a distribution inferred from resolved slots rather than a number read off the sky. The framework refuses to name the population; names such as a ten-solar-mass peak are imports from the astronomy frame, provenanced as such. What it offers is that shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
