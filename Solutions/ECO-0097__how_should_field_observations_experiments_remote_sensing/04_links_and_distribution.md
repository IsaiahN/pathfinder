## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Forecasting is a cycle, not a weight.** Dietze et al. 2018 make out-of-sample prediction the test of a hypothesis and require uncertainty on every data stream. This corroborates Fig 1 :23 and Fig 2 :29 at the ground, and supplies the operational form of Fig 4 :30's round trip. It does not name a winning combination, so it does not close F9.
2. **Data fusion produces a surplus, but has a price.** Worden et al. 2025 say the integration of models and observations yields insights neither achieves alone, while warning that equifinality and data quality propagate into model skill. This is Fig 8's surplus and Fig 5's bargain in ecological language. It also narrows the earlier reading that new data automatically fix equifinality (F11.4).
3. **Multiple patterns, not one fit.** Grimm and Railsback 2012 require several patterns across levels and say equifinal models cannot be easily rejected. This is low-overlap corroboration for Fig 2 :31 and Fig 12 :117, from complex systems rather than from ecology's data-assimilation pool.
4. **Knowledge systems are parallel, not subordinate.** Tengö et al. 2014 and Robinson et al. 2022 corroborate Fig 6 :23 and Fig 2 :29 for D4. They are one evidence pool (knowledge-systems literature), so they count as one witness, not two.
5. **Complementarity is scale-structured.** Moller et al. 2004 give a concrete case: aerial surveys supplied lodge counts over a large area; hunters knew which lodges were occupied. That is a union surplus with distinct closures, not an average of two estimates. Same knowledge-systems pool as items 4 and 5.

Two pools are visible: the model-data pool (Dietze, Worden, Pasetto, LeBauer lineage) and the knowledge-systems pool (Tengö, Robinson, Moller). Grimm and Railsback is a third, complex-systems pool. Agreement among all three on the shape is therefore not one pool talking, but it is still only corroboration: the verdict comes from the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who absorbs the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source links.
- **Fig 2 :42**, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A list selected for people likely to agree manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
- **Fig 9 :60**, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The venue rank in `03` is a contact-cost filter only.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Require each user to write their own refuter and state their own mode. The framework's author warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. If refuter 1 or 2 in `01` section 3 fires, stop and retract before sending.
