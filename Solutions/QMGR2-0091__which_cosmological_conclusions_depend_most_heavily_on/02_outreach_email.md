# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the ranking

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mechanism ranking in `01` F2 and F7 is stated with its mode and its refuter attached. A recipient who agrees with the ranking before checking the separator has given a convention, not a verdict (Fig 2 :35).

**Subject:** Which cosmological result moves when you change the ruler?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one question I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the check does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The check (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Take any cosmological conclusion and ask what it terminates in. If it terminates in something that changes when you push on it, it was not an anchor. The anchor test I am using is: whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. Apply that to the Hubble constant and you find two moving legs. The local leg ends in a stellar calibration (Cepheid, TRGB, SBF, Mira) and the absolute magnitude of a Type Ia supernova. The early-Universe leg ends in the sound horizon, which is itself computed from the assumed pre-recombination energy budget. Swap either leg and H0 moves. By contrast, the primary CMB quantities (the acoustic scale, the baryon and matter densities, the spectral index) are reported as only weakly dependent on the model. So the split is not "everything is model-dependent". It is base versus derived, and it is per conclusion.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a number for how much each conclusion moves, and I am not claiming this analysis has measured anything. It is a simulated derivation checked against a framework, and it returns a separator and a mechanism ranking, not a measured sensitivity table. I am also not claiming that apparent independent probes are independent. Some "non-Planck" CMB results still use Planck-based calibration or priors, and distance-ladder rungs share systematics. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names. The provenance of the calibration decides, not the number of papers.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. a conclusion that stays fixed under every admissible model and calibration while its anchor still fails the anchor test;
> 2. two probes with disjoint instruments, disjoint calibrations and disjoint model assumptions that agree, while the separator still predicts high dependence;
> 3. a figure line that returns a numeric per-conclusion sensitivity ordering, which would make the ranking a result rather than a gap;
> 4. a ground-level sensitivity analysis that reverses the mechanism ranking.
>
> I wrote these down before this message. If any holds, the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text extract and formula (the primary material): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md` in Project Pathfinder.
> - The Hubble tension: a decade review, Cai and Wang 2026: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20434v2
> - Cosmic Expansion: a mini review of the Hubble-Lemaitre tension, Cyr-Racine 2021: https://arxiv.org/html/2105.09409v2
> - The Hubble Hunter's Guide, Knox and Millea: https://arxiv.org/html/1908.03663v2
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the separator on a problem you know and where you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
