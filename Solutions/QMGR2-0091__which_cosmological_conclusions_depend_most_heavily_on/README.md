# Solution QMGR2-0091

**Question:** Which cosmological conclusions depend most heavily on a particular model or calibration?

**Source:** QM & GR Questions.txt line 260

**Section:** J. Methods, evidence, and competing explanations

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separators and the per-conclusion ranking of mechanisms, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal numeric sensitivity ordering, not `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this derivation.** Everything in this folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading. It cannot settle which cosmological conclusions actually depend most heavily, because that requires a published cross-model, cross-calibration sensitivity analysis (an instrument and a record), which this folder does not perform. What it returns is the shape of the answer and a mechanism-ranking, with refuters written in advance.

## Question restated in effect terms

Given a set of cosmological conclusions (H0, the age of the universe, Ωm, σ8, the dark-energy equation of state, spatial curvature, the sound horizon, light-element abundances, the existence of expansion and of dark matter), decide which ones move most when the model or the calibration used to infer them is swapped. Each conclusion is a slot, so the dependence is indexed per conclusion and is never averaged into one "model dependence of cosmology" number (Fig 1 :19). An option A answer asserts a ground-settled ranking. An option B answer returns the separator that makes a conclusion heavily dependent, ranks the mechanisms, and leaves the numeric ordering to a ground. This solution takes option B.

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

A checkable answer names, for each conclusion, (i) the anchor its inference chain terminates in, (ii) whether that anchor passes the anchor test (Fig 2 :29), (iii) the number and kind of calibration rungs between anchor and conclusion, and (iv) the evidence overlap between the probes that appear to corroborate it. That bound term then accrues a residual like anything else and is refusable. The shape claim is in `01` section 3.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, no receptiveness ranking, no bulk-send plan. Contact is through public institutional and opt-in venues.
- No claim that this simulated derivation settles the ranking. Fig 10 :42: `A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.` Sending the framework interior before a reader records a verdict on stage 1 would spend the only thing the reader contributes.
- No claim that model dependence is a defect. It is a structural property of an inference chain that can be bounded, and the bounding is the useful output.
