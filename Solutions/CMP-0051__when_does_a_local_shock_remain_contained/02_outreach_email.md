# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in §4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is written down and dated in the message. The recipient's verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any framework material is shown.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about when a local shock stays contained

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about cascades on networks that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a local shock is a per-slot residual, not a global average. It stays contained when that residual trends down to zero as the disturbance is composed outward through contact. It cascades when the residual persists and does not trend down, so that more slots go live. Under the Tether figures this is a **union of four separable conditions**, not one threshold: (1) topology and contact, (2) the boundary as a directional filter rather than a wall, (3) transmission versus common cause, and (4) the sign of the feedback loop. A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds; the intersections remain.
>
> **What this is not.** It is not a universal critical number. The figures return a shape ("you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"), so the threshold in any particular grid, supply chain, or bank network is ground work: a measurement with a specified instrument. It is not a claim that modularity always helps; the sign depends on the process. It is not a claim that two nodes failing together prove contagion; they may share a source and never have talked.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. every per-slot residual trends down to zero yet a cascade still occurs;
> 2. two nodes with a proven shared source and no path between them are shown by intervention to be transmitting the shock;
> 3. one lever reliably separates contained from cascaded across all four conditions;
> 4. a universal numeric threshold is derived from the six-term set alone and confirmed at the ground.
>
> If any holds, I will withdraw the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (GitHub, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a network you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode (general, specified, or grounded) of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the claim, its four conditions, and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2 (opt-in only):** if curious, offer the three figure links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3 (opt-in only):** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
