**Subject:** Check one claim about "minimum properties of life" before you read the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the question form, `specified` on the contact shape):** the question "what minimum properties must a system have to count as alive?" does not shrink with effort. Each proposed rule works well on a different subset: the energy rule catches fire and crystals, the evolution rule catches viruses and prions, the reproduction rule excludes mules and sterile workers, and "self-sustaining" excludes the laboratory RNA system that otherwise evolves. The figures' rule is: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* So I split it into a convention question (what shall we mean by alive for this purpose) and a contact question (what makes a system capable of self-maintenance and evolution in a habitat).
>
> The substrate question dissolves under the lift move. In the figures' words, *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground"*, and *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The same move applies to "alive". The word is applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground; the contact question does not dissolve.
>
> What survives is a **shape**, not a definition: a spendable gradient, a cycle whose output re-enters as input, a part that survives its own use, a network over which influence travels, and a boundary maintained in space. The figures say *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*, so I am not presenting this as a necessary-and-sufficient list. NASA's working definition and the textbook lists remain useful as conventions; the figures say agreement is *"efficient for a convention"* while being *"disqualifying for a verdict"*. I am not promoting a convention to a verdict.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. One list of necessary and sufficient properties that survives fire, crystal, virus, prion, mule, sterile worker, dormant seed and the laboratory self-sustaining evolving RNA system without a robust counterexample.
> 2. Two systems with identical contact relations that differ in aliveness, where the difference changes a ground-level prediction.
> 3. An agent-only improvement that moves the ground with no change in habitat contact.
> 4. A composing system that lacks one of the six substrate terms, or a self-maintaining evolving lineage that composes with none of the five contact structures above.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
