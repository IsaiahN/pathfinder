## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The definitional disagreement is structured by type of definition, not only by content.** SEP distinguishes theoretical, operational, nominal, demonstrative and stipulative definitions and says *"Operational definitions are quick and admit counterexamples. Philosophical definitions tend to be precise and theoretically laden."* That is the Fig 9 split in the field's own vocabulary: several well-formed questions, not one hard one. It predicts that a single property list will keep failing; corroborates F1 and F6.

2. **The capability claim is corroborated from a separate evidence pool.** Joyce's account of the NASA working definition puts capability in the population or ecology, not the individual entity, and explicitly says a virus plus a host cell can meet the information requirement. That is Fig 11 :39 stated by a NASA exobiology working-group member with no knowledge of the figures. It predicts that property lists applied to isolated entities will misclassify viruses and sterile individuals; corroborates F2 and F3.

3. **An operational threshold is admitted to be a convention.** NASA's own pages call the definition *"less-than-formal, non-binding"* and Joyce says *"there's nothing official about it."* The Lincoln/Joyce RNA system met it technically yet Joyce refused to call it true life. That is exactly the Fig 2 :41 and Fig 10 :47 distinction: efficient for a convention, not a verdict about the world; corroborates F6 and F7.

4. **The water analogy is an instrument-shaped prediction.** Cleland and Chyba argue that the controversy is inescapable until there is a theory of living systems analogous to molecular theory for water. Under the figures that is an `unreached` verdict with a named requirement: a source-discriminating instrument, not another definition. Corroborates F7 and the honest stop in `07`.

5. **The thermodynamics frame names the gradient, not a new property list.** England's dissipation-driven adaptation, reported by Wolchover, and Schrödinger's open-system argument both put a spendable difference at the centre. That is Fig 13 :22, *"a difference that can be spent"*, in an independent field. Franck's remark that the living/nonliving distinction is not sharp corroborates the shape-not-a-name limit. Low overlap with the Tether sources.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule (back to the census) was not triggered. All of them are secondary; the figures decide.
