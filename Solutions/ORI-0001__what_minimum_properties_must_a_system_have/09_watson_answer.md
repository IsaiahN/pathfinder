## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question "what minimum properties must a system have to count as alive?" Option A: produce a necessary-and-sufficient property list, which leads to a list that keeps failing on fire, virus, mule, prion and the laboratory RNA system. Option B: choose one operational threshold, which leads to a non-binding convention that excludes viruses and excludes mules. Option C: treat the question as the wrong shape, split it into a convention question and a contact question, and state the shape of the contact answer with a refuter. What each leads to: A and B keep patching a union as if it were one question; C gives a checkable contact question with an open boundary.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed is the wrong shape and is dissolved, not answered: split the union into a convention question and a contact question. "Alive" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground; the answerable question is what makes a system capable of self-maintenance, heritable change and adaptation in a habitat, and capability belongs to agent and habitat together. The contact answer has a shape, a functional group of spendable gradient, re-entering cycle, catalyst-like persistence, influence-carrying network and boundary, which fixes a kind of thing and not a sufficient condition. The six-term substrate is the minimum for any composing system, not a life definition. NASA's working definition and the textbook lists are useful conventions and must not be promoted to verdicts. The boundary stays open until an instrument settles it. This is `general` on the dissolution and `specified` on the shape, ungrounded and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The dissolution is stated with its refuter and its provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished definition before asking for a verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the split and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. Conventions are revised by decision and contact claims by evidence. The SEP entry notes that physicians resolved the definition of death by a shared decision (whole-brain death) rather than a discovery, and that scientific categories such as planet were redrawn when new cases arrived. Neither the dissolution nor the shape commits a community to a permanent boundary, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A property list surviving fire, crystal, virus, prion, mule, sterile worker, dormant seed and the laboratory evolving RNA system would refute the dissolution; two systems with identical contact relations differing in aliveness with a prediction difference, or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, would refute the contact reading; a composing system lacking one of the six terms, or a derivation of one from the other five, would break the substrate scope claim; a self-maintaining evolving lineage with none of the five contact structures would break the shape; and a repeatable source-discriminating instrument that names the boundary would settle the contact question and move it to `grounded`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
