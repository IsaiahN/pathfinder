## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question asks for the minimum properties a system must have to count as alive. Three candidate answers are in play in the field, and each leads somewhere different:

| option | what it produces | where it leads |
|---|---|---|
| A: necessary and sufficient property list | metabolism, reproduction, heredity, boundary, homeostasis, response, evolution | a stable list with no counterexample on any subset |
| B: one operational threshold | NASA's working definition, a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution | a decision procedure that excludes viruses and excludes mules |
| C: the question is the wrong shape | split into a convention question and a contact question, then give the shape of the contact answer | a checkable contact question with an open boundary until a ground settles it |

Formula step "Direction" requires the sought-for shape to be made an ordinary term. I bind it as: **"a short, stable, counterexample-free property list that settles the boundary."** That term accrues a residual on every subset tried (fire, crystal, virus, prion, mule, sterile worker, dormant seed, laboratory RNA system), so it is treated as REFUTED, not as an unmet target to keep filling. Formula step 2: *"REFUTED: a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*

## 1. Residual, stated in effect terms

**R:** There is no stable set of properties whose presence or absence settles "alive" across the cases the field actually argues about. Each proposed rule keeps working well on a different subset: the energy-flux rule catches fire and crystals, the evolution rule catches viruses and prions, the reproduction rule excludes mules and sterile workers, the self-sustaining rule excludes the laboratory RNA system that otherwise evolves. The disagreement does not shrink with effort. It recurs at a new subset each time a rule is patched.

- **Arity:** several conditions, not one. The cases form a union of overlapping conditions (evolves, self-maintains, bounded, heritable), not a list of separate boxes.
- **Symmetry:** the conditions are not interchangeable. A comparison between "can evolve" and "has a boundary" is not a symmetric join; swapping them changes what is being asked.
- **Scale:** the candidate property is stated of one entity at one moment, but the property that actually does the work (capability to evolve) is stated of a population or ecology over generations. The question and its answer are at different scales.

This is not a shortage of data. It is the shape of a union compound, and Fig 9 governs that shape directly.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The question is a union compound; split it rather than search it (decisive for the question form)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :4 names the test first: *"First: is there a fact here?"* The property-list disagreement fails that test in exactly the way the figure describes. The energy rule works on fire and crystals and fails on viruses; the evolution rule works on viruses and prions and fails on mules; the reproduction rule works on organisms and fails on sterile workers; the self-sustaining rule works on cells and fails on the laboratory RNA system. Each rule works well on a different subset. That is several well-formed questions (what maintains, what evolves, what is bounded, what is heritable), not one hard question about a single list.

Fig 9 :54-56 adds the union case: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions, and a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds while leaving the intersections standing.

Mode: `general`, structural. Triangulated: Fig 9 (union/split) and Fig 11 (the label is applied afterwards, F2) have low overlap, one about question shape and one about what a label does. World corroboration is strong but from separate fields: the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's *Life* entry lists "Specify: divide the concept into distinct types and label them" among the available moves, and Cleland and Chyba (2002) argue that defining life without a theory of living systems is analogous to defining water before molecular theory. These corroborate; they do not decide.

**Refuter:** exhibit one list of properties with no robust counterexample on any of the subsets above, and show that the disagreement shrinks when that list is applied. If such a list survives fire, crystal, virus, prion, mule, sterile worker, dormant seed and laboratory RNA system, F1 is refuted and the property-list program stands.

### F2 — "Alive" is a label applied afterwards; the substrate question dissolves into the contact question (decisive for what is being asked)

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :41: *"The lift move is Cayley's, 1799: separating the forces from the flapping is what made powered flight a problem anyone could work on."* Fig 11 :41 continues: *"The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."*

"What minimum properties must a system have to count as alive?" has exactly the grammar of "what is intelligence?". It asks for a property of the thing considered in isolation, a substrate answer. The figures' replacement is the lift move: separate the contact relations that do the work from the label applied afterwards. Under selection, "alive" is a label applied to a lineage that met its ground; the word does no work as a cause. What can be answered is the contact question: what makes a system capable of self-maintenance, heritable change and open-ended adaptation in a habitat.

This is not a refusal to answer. It is a refusal to answer the *wrong* question. The surviving answer is a shape for the contact question (F5), and the convention question is answered separately as a convention (F6).

Mode: `general`. Triangulated with F1 (Fig 9) as noted. World corroboration from an independent field is unusually direct: Gerald Joyce, in the NASA Astrobiology interview (2013), says of the working definition, *"It's the population, or even the ecology, that makes the living system. Capable doesn't refer to one individual entity; that doesn't constitute a living system."* He also says of a virus plus a host cell, *"the virus plus the host cell can"* supply the information needed for evolution. Low overlap with the figures (a NASA panel discussion, not the Tether), so this is genuine corroboration of the agent-and-habitat reading.

**Refuter:** show a case where two systems have identical contact relations with their habitat, the ground settles both identically, yet one is alive and the other is not, and the difference changes a prediction. Such a case would give the label causal work and refute F2.

### F3 — Capability lives in the habitat; enumerate it, do not compose it

Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

The practical consequence for this question is that there is no intrinsic minimum set of properties to read off a single entity in isolation. The minimum lives in the contact: a source of energy that can be spent, something that relates and carries influence, a loop that re-enters, a part that survives its own use, and a boundary maintained against the surroundings. These are read off the world (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*), not composed from a definition.

Fig 11 :72 states the standing falsifier for this finding: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

Mode: `general`. Single-source inside the figures (Fig 11), with strong low-overlap corroboration from Joyce's interview and from the NASA astrobiology pages' emphasis on gradients, water and an energy source (Barge and Kopparapu in *Life on Other Planets*, 2023). Corroboration only.

**Refuter:** an agent-only change that moves the ground, for example a single internally rewritten molecule that starts a new self-sustaining evolving lineage with no change in habitat contact, refutes F3.

### F4 — The only figure-licensed minimum set is the six-term substrate, and it is not a definition of life

Fig 13 :3:

> **"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."**

Fig 13 :41: **"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."** Fig 13 :113: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

The six terms are space, time and energy (states) and spectrum, gradient and network (structures). This is the framework's only literal "minimum set", and it is a minimum for **any system that composes anything**, not a minimum for life. Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* A rock and a river compose under the same six. Identifying the six with "the minimum properties of life" is a strain, and the strain test sends it back to census: Fig 13 governs D3 (the substrate of any composing system), while the life-specific content lives in D2 (contact). Different objects, so no contradiction after re-census, but the identification must be refused.

The figures README makes the same point in its own register: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops where instruments cannot distinguish. The six are a visible guess at compression axes, not nature's own list.

Mode: `general` for the six-term structure, `specified` and ungrounded for any claim that a particular living system instantiates it in a particular way. Single-source (Fig 13). The metalaws note (`60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`) reads the six as *"the minimum set any composed reality needs"*, confirming the scope.

**Refuter:** derive one of the six from the other five (breaks indivisibility), or exhibit a system that composes anything while lacking one of the six (breaks the "no system functions without any of them" claim). Either fires against Fig 13 itself, not just against this reading.

### F5 — The familiar property list survives as a shape, not as a definition

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 13 :22: **"a difference that can be spent"** (gradient). Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"** (network).

The textbook properties map onto these structural terms: energy use maps to a spendable gradient; metabolism and self-production map to a cycle; heredity maps to a catalyst-like part that survives its own use; compartmentalisation maps to a boundary in space; responsiveness maps to a network over which influence travels. This mapping is the *shape* of the contact answer. It is not a definition, because the figures return *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32), and because Fig 12 :123 says the group fixes the kind while the qualifier picks the member. The list names a functional group, not a sufficient condition.

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two systems with the same property list can therefore be different substances; the difference is carried by the arrangement, which a flat list does not record. That is a second reason a property list cannot settle the boundary.

Mode: `specified` for the mapping, `general` for the shape claim. Triangulated: Fig 12 (structures) and Fig 13 (substrate states and structures) have low overlap, one about what a composition carries and one about what a system minimally has. World corroboration: NASA's *Life on Other Planets* (2023) reports Barge's emphasis on gradients and Kopparapu's on atmospheric disequilibrium, and Quanta's *A New Physics Theory of Life* (Wolchover, 2014) reports Schrödinger's point that an open system maintains order by increasing the entropy of its surroundings. Both are separate evidence pools.

**Refuter:** a self-maintaining, evolving, bounded lineage that composes with none of spendable gradient, cycle, catalyst-like persistence, network or boundary. If one is observed, the shape claim fails.

### F6 — Correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention; the definition question is partly a convention

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

These three lines look like a strain until the census is redone. They govern different objects. The contact question (does this system self-maintain and evolve in this habitat?) is about the world, so agreement among people sharing an evidence pool is worthless for it. The definition question ("what shall we mean by alive for this purpose?") is constitutively about shared meaning, so agreement there is the product. Fig 2 :37-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

Consequence: an operational definition such as NASA's is legitimate *as a convention*, precisely because it is non-binding and used to focus debate; the NASA pages say it is *"not used to design missions or research projects"* and that *"there's nothing official about it. Nobody voted on it."* It becomes a defect only when a convention is promoted to a verdict about the world. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

Mode: `general`. Triangulated with F1 and F2. The SEP entry's distinction between operational and theoretical definitions, and its observation that "some disagreements dissolve after clarifying and understanding the type of definition used", corroborate from philosophy.

**Refuter:** show that "alive" is constitutively about the ground in the same way that "is this a good answer for a person" is, so that human agreement is the anchor rather than the product. Then F6's convention/verdict split collapses.

### F7 — Minting definitions is not the same as settling the boundary

Fig 5 :29-30: **"nothing new here / the machinery worked; the answer was already known."**
Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Cleland and Chyba (2002) report that suggested definitions face robust counterexamples and that interminable controversy is inescapable without an analogous theory of living systems. The SEP entry says plainly: *"there is no consensus forthcoming."* Under the figures, that is not a scandal; it is the expected output of a frame searching inside a closure whose arrangements are not exhausted. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The honest report is `unreached`, not `unreachable`, and not silence.

Mode: `general`. Single-source for the figure reading (Fig 5 + Fig 8 + Fig 9 together, but all framework-internal). World corroboration from Cleland and Chyba and from the SEP, both opened.

**Refuter:** a definition that predicts a new discriminator before it is built, where the discriminator is then built and returns the predicted result. That would be a settlement, not another mint.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map :287, carried by the exemplar's `01`).

The result is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One list of necessary and sufficient properties survives all of these without a robust counterexample: fire, crystal, virus, prion, mule, sterile worker, dormant seed, laboratory self-sustaining evolving RNA system. (Breaks F1.)
2. Two systems with identical contact relations differ in aliveness, and the difference changes a ground-level prediction. (Breaks F2 and F3.)
3. An agent-only improvement moves the ground with no change in habitat contact. (Breaks F3, the figure's own standing falsifier.)
4. A system composes while lacking one of the six substrate terms, or one of the six is derived from the other five. (Breaks F4 and Fig 13 itself.)
5. A self-maintaining, evolving, bounded lineage composes with none of spendable gradient, cycle, catalyst-like persistence, network or boundary. (Breaks F5.)

A sixth event is not a refutation but a settlement: a repeatable, source-discriminating instrument that names the boundary and returns the same result in an independent lab. That would move the contact verdict from `specified` to `grounded` and would retire the convention question for that purpose. Until one of these fires, the findings stand as `candidate`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape of the question, not a definition: the property-list question does not shrink with effort, so split it (F1).
2. State the two questions separately: a convention question about shared meaning, and a contact question about capability in a habitat (F2, F6).
3. Keep NASA's working definition and the textbook lists as **tools with provenance**, not as verdicts. They are efficient conventions; they are not anchors (F6, F7).
4. Offer the contact shape (gradient, cycle, catalyst, network, boundary) with its refuter attached, and ask what would refute it (F5, `02`).
5. Do not pool agreement from the same evidence pool as corroboration. Use the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and ask for a failure, not belief (Fig 2 :40; Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*).
