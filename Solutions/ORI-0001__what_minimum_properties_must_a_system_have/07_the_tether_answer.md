# The Tether's answer: What minimum properties must a system have to count as alive?

**Mode: `general` on the dissolution of the question as posed, `specified` on the contact shape that survives it.** Nothing here is `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The field wants a list of properties whose presence or absence settles "alive". Candidate rules each work on a different subset: energy flux catches fire, evolution catches viruses and prions, reproduction excludes mules and sterile workers, self-sustaining excludes the laboratory RNA system that otherwise evolves. The disagreement does not shrink with effort. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

So the answer does not choose among the candidate lists. It changes the question.

## 2. The shape

1. **The property-list question is a union compound, not one hard problem.** It must be split before it is searched (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-56). One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
2. **"Alive" is a label applied afterwards.** Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* The same lift move applies here. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not.
3. **The answerable question is about capability in a habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* This is corroborated from an independent field by Joyce (2013): it is the population or ecology that makes the living system, not the individual entity.
4. **The contact shape is a functional group, not a sufficient condition.** A spendable gradient (Fig 13 :22, *"a difference that can be spent"*), a cycle (*"cycle. The output re-enters as input"*, Fig 12 :121), a part that survives its own use (*"catalyst. A part that survives its own use"*, Fig 12 :119), a network (*"that entities relate, and influence travels"*, Fig 13 :24), and a boundary in space. Fig 12 :123: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
5. **The six-term substrate is not a life definition.** Fig 13 :3: *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."* That is a minimum for any composing system, including rocks and rivers. Using it as a definition of life is a strain that re-census sends back to D2 (contact).
6. **An operational definition is a convention.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* NASA's working definition is legitimate as the former and must not be promoted to the latter. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* So the answer leaves the boundary of "alive" open until a ground settles it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a new definition of life, not a seventh property, and not a promotion of NASA's working definition to a verdict.
- Not that fire, viruses, mules and prions have been definitively classified. The point is structural: the property list fails on them for different reasons, so it is a union, not one question.
- Not that life is undefinable in principle. It says the current disagreement is `unreached` with a named requirement (a source-discriminating instrument), and *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that the six-term substrate is nature's own list. It is a visible guess at compression axes (figures README; *They're Scanning Books* as catalogued in `05`).
- Not `grounded`. No instrument or experiment was run in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument or a repeatable construction, not a further definition. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (catalogued in `05`): a reading cannot comment on its own source, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved metamerism. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **List test:** a property list that survives fire, crystal, virus, prion, mule, sterile worker, dormant seed and the laboratory self-sustaining evolving RNA system without a robust counterexample. This would refute the dissolution.
- **Contact test:** two systems with identical contact relations that differ in aliveness, where the difference changes a ground-level prediction. This would give the label causal work.
- **Capability test:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground with no change in habitat contact. This is the figure's own standing falsifier (Fig 11 :72).
- **Substrate test:** a composing system that lacks one of the six terms, or a derivation of one of the six from the other five.
- **Shape test:** a self-maintaining, evolving, bounded lineage that composes with none of spendable gradient, cycle, catalyst-like persistence, network or boundary.
- **Settlement:** a repeatable instrument or construction that names the boundary and returns the same result in an independent lab would move the contact verdict from `specified` to `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README). What would reverse it is listed in §4 and recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` under "reversed by".

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a new list of properties for life. It says the property-list question is a union of several well-formed questions, so it must be split rather than searched; that "alive" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground, so the substrate question dissolves while the contact question remains; and that the answerable question is what makes a system capable of self-maintenance and evolution in a habitat, since capability belongs to agent and habitat together, never to the agent alone. What survives is a shape: a spendable gradient, a re-entering cycle, a part that survives its own use, a network that carries influence, and a boundary. That shape fixes a kind of thing, not a member, and it is offered with falsifiers written in advance. The boundary stays open until an instrument, not another definition, settles it.
