## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a property list a molecule?

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

A list such as "metabolism, reproduction, heredity, boundary, response, evolution" names states and capacities. It does not record the bond between them. Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two systems with the same six properties can therefore be different substances, with the difference carried entirely by the arrangement. A flat list cannot tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost (Fig 12 :14-16: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost"*).

World illustration, reported but not read directly: Cleland and Chyba's reference list includes Koshland's *"The Seven Pillars of Life"* (Science, 2002), a list of properties. It is named here only as an instance of the flat-form move, not as a source I opened.

**Conclusion:** a property list is a flat formula, not a molecule. It cannot settle a boundary whose differences are differences of arrangement. Triangulated (Fig 12 :5 and :111, low overlap with the pricing argument); no ground reading.

### Q2 — Functional group, not sufficient condition

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The five contact structures (spendable gradient, cycle, catalyst-like persistence, network, boundary) behave as a functional group. They fix the *kind* of thing that can enter the contact question; they do not pick the member. The qualifier for a given case (which gradient, which loop, which boundary) is a further, measured matter. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** This is the structural reading of heredity: a part that survives its own use, so that a lineage can carry a record. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** This is the structural reading of self-production and reproduction. Fig 13 :22, *"a difference that can be spent"*, is the reading of energy use; Fig 13 :24, *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*, is the reading of responsiveness and ecological embeddedness.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a self-maintaining, evolving, bounded lineage can be written without any one of these structures (no spendable difference, no re-entering loop, no part surviving its own use, no network carrying influence, no boundary in space), the functional-group claim fails and the flat-list reading stands instead.

### Q3 — Is the question an isomer question at all?

Fig 13 :121-125: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance... Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*

The two famous programs in origins of life, RNA-first and metabolism-first, can be read as two routes toward overlapping substances rather than rival lists of properties. Under Fig 12 they may be isomers over a shared atom set if both compose from the same six with different arrangements; or they may share only a functional group if each uses different atoms in the slots. The figure does not pick. Deciding it needs a measurement of which atoms each program actually requires, which is exactly the sort of thing the contact question can ask and a list cannot.

**Falsifier:** if one program can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands. Recorded as a live open question, not as a result.

### Q4 — What is the order of operations?

Fig 9 :4-5: **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The correct order is:

1. Perceive the residual as a union (Fig 9 :4-5). Describe it in effect terms, before choosing a discipline (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Refuse the bound term "a short property list that settles the boundary" (formula step 2, REFUTED). Refusal precedes minting; negation is not a bond (Operators table :53-58).
3. Split into a convention question and a contact question (Fig 9 :54-56: unbundle a union before searching again).
4. Compose the contact shape from the six and the bond set (Fig 12, Fig 13). This is a legal composition, not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).
5. Attach the refuter, then check against the ground (formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*).

Working in any other order is the apophenia the exemplar names: pick a frame (a favorite definition), then map the residual onto it so that any frame seems to fit. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

**Falsifier:** a case where a definition is successfully settled by building it first and only then describing the residual, with the description surviving independent re-derivation, would reverse this order and refute Q4.

## CONCLUSION

A property list is a flat formula, not a molecule; the difference between two systems with the same properties is carried by the bond, which the list does not record. What survives is a functional group of five contact structures, which fixes a kind of thing rather than picking a member. The isomer question between rival origins programs is live and unmeasured. The order of operations is split before define, refuse before mint, ground after compose, and the falsifiers for each step are written above.

This framing keeps what is defensible in the property-list tradition (the five structures are real and checkable) without promoting it to a definition the figures forbid. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
