## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "what minimum properties must a system have to count as alive?" is a union compound, not one hard question, because each candidate property rule works well on a different subset and the disagreement does not shrink with effort. The right operation is to split it into a convention question (what we shall mean by alive for a stated purpose) and a contact question (what makes a system capable of self-maintenance, heritable change and adaptation in a habitat). The substrate question dissolves under the lift move: "alive" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground, and capability is a property of agent and habitat together, never of the agent alone. The contact answer has a shape, a functional group of five structures: a spendable gradient, a re-entering cycle, a part that survives its own use, a network carrying influence, and a boundary. That group fixes a kind of thing, not a sufficient condition; the boundary of "alive" stays open until an instrument settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — question form; checkable by reading Figure 9 and the cases

- **Statement:** the disagreement is a union compound; split rather than search.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 — *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54-56 adds the union case: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **What to check:** do the standard counterexamples actually fall on different subsets? Fire and crystals for energy flux; viruses and prions for evolution; mules and sterile workers for reproduction; the Lincoln and Joyce RNA system for self-sustaining. The SEP *Life* entry and Cleland and Chyba (2002) report the same pattern in the field's own terms.
- **Refuter:** one list of necessary and sufficient properties that survives all eight cases without a robust counterexample, with evidence that the disagreement then shrinks. If it exists, C1 fails.

### C2 — question dissolved into contact; checkable by reading Figure 11 and by field knowledge

- **Statement:** "alive" is a label applied afterwards; the answerable question is about capability in a habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :36 — *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* Fig 11 :39 — *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 — *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** Joyce's 2013 NASA interview says of the working definition, *"It's the population, or even the ecology, that makes the living system. Capable doesn't refer to one individual entity"*, and that a virus plus host cell can supply the information needed for evolution. Does that match how your field actually uses "capable of Darwinian evolution"?
- **Refuter:** two systems with identical contact relations that differ in aliveness, where the difference changes a ground-level prediction; or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground. Either gives the label causal work and refutes C2.

### C3 — contact shape; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the surviving shape is a functional group of five structures, not a necessary-and-sufficient list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123 — *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :119 — *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121 — *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 13 :22 — *"a difference that can be spent"*; Fig 13 :24 — *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*.
- **What to check:** can you name a self-maintaining, evolving, bounded lineage that composes without a spendable difference, a re-entering loop, a part surviving its own use, a network carrying influence, or a boundary? If yes, C3 fails.
- **Scope caution:** the six-term substrate of Fig 13 :3 (*"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."*) is a minimum for any composing system, not a definition of life. Do not read C3 as claiming otherwise.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a new definition of life, and not a promotion of NASA's working definition to a verdict. Fig 2 :41 makes agreement *"efficient for a convention"*; Fig 10 :47 says *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- Not that fire, viruses, mules or prions are now definitively classified. The claim is about the shape of the question.
- Not that life is undefinable in principle. The status is `unreached`: Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not that the six-term substrate is nature's own list of life's properties.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was used.
- Not a claim that the "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming atoms already held in the six. It governs a missing primitive; naming held atoms is a binding, not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).

## 5. What would settle it

1. The eight-case property list test (refutes the dissolution if passed).
2. An identical-contact-different-aliveness case with a prediction difference, or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (refutes the contact reading).
3. A composing system missing one of the six terms, or a derivation of one from the other five (refutes the substrate scope claim).
4. A self-maintaining evolving lineage with none of the five contact structures (refutes the shape).
5. A repeatable, source-discriminating instrument that names the boundary and returns the same result in an independent lab (settles the contact question and moves it to `grounded`).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 11 :36 and :39, Fig 12 :119, :121, :123, Fig 13 :22 and :24 — seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against the eight counterexamples. Do any two of them fail for the same reason? If so, the union reading weakens.
3. Check C2 against how your own field uses "capable of Darwinian evolution". Is it a population-level, habitat-involving term?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate a spendable internal gradient from an externally maintained one, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
