## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The observed pattern class in the CMB and large-scale structure, near-isotropy, flatness, a nearly scale-invariant adiabatic scalar spectrum with a slight red tilt, acoustic peaks and a small tensor upper limit, does not identify the early-universe process that produced it. Different routes can arrive at the same substance, and the order is not recoverable from the artefact but from the lineage. The pattern fixes the shape any candidate process must have and refuses the name. Inflation and the non-inflationary alternatives are different closures whose two-point predictions can coincide; over the predicted quantity set they stand as isomers, with the difference in tensors, tilt, non-Gaussianity and isocurvature. Whether any named alternative actually works is a ground question, left here as a `gap`.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Tensor test:** a primordial tensor background at a level that excludes the low-tensor alternatives, or a tighter upper bound pushing toward zero.
2. **Tilt test:** a red scalar tilt excluding blue-tilt routes, plus a measured tensor tilt.
3. **Bispectrum test:** non-Gaussianity amplitude and shape matching slow-roll or the order-unity bounce prediction.
4. **Adiabaticity test:** isocurvature constraints excluding routes that require entropy modes.
5. **Standing falsifiers:** a one-to-one pattern-to-process map; a higher-order fingerprint that encodes the route in the artefact; a discriminator excluding all non-inflationary routes while agreeing with inflation.
