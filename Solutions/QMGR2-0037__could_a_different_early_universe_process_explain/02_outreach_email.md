# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about any theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 are stable. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what the CMB patterns can and cannot tell us

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the observed pattern class in the CMB and large-scale structure, namely near-isotropy, flatness to about 0.4 percent, a nearly scale-invariant adiabatic scalar spectrum with a slight red tilt near 0.965, acoustic peaks, and a small tensor upper limit, does not by itself identify the process that produced it. The figures put it plainly: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance"* (Fig 13 :121) and *"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage"* (Fig 13 :128-129). The pattern is the artefact. It fixes the shape any candidate process must have, and it does not name the process.
>
> This is a claim about underdetermination, not a claim that inflation is wrong and not a claim that a named alternative works. Whether any specific alternative reproduces the full pattern class is a measurement question, and I mark it as a gap rather than filling it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that inflation has been refuted, that a bounce or an ekpyrotic or string-gas model has been confirmed, or that a common diagram of the six structural terms classifies early-universe models. Those mappings are bindings from physics onto a vocabulary, not derivations. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a one-to-one map from the observed pattern class to a single early-universe process, with every other closure excluded by that same pattern;
> 2. a demonstration that the artefact does encode its route, for example a higher-order fingerprint that uniquely identifies a mechanism;
> 3. a source-discriminating measurement that excludes every non-inflationary route while agreeing with inflation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

## Two-stage rule

Stage 1: send the shape claim, the six observables, and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure and what discriminator they would reach for. **Record that verdict before any theory is shown.**

Stage 2, opt-in only: if they are curious, offer the three local source paths. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the framework interior their agreement is one evidence pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

Stage 3: offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust. No stage asks for agreement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
