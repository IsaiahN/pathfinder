# The pattern as a many-to-one projection: isomers, order and discriminators

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the isomer shape, `gap` on which route is actual. NOT `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The observed pattern class P does not say which process produced it. Three questions are owed:

1. Is the map from process to P one-to-one or many-to-one?
2. Under Fig 12's definition, are two routes that produce the same P **isomers**, or something weaker?
3. What is the dependency order between "what pattern was produced" and "which process produced it", and what closes the second question?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the map one-to-one or many-to-one?

- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Fig 13 :123-124: **"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms"**
- Fig 13 :128-129: **"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."**
- Brandenberger and Peter (2016, opened this session): *"The successful predictions which inflation has made for the spectrum of cosmological perturbations are not specific to inflation."* Their review shows matter bounce, pre-big-bang, ekpyrotic and string-gas constructions each converting an initial spectrum into a nearly scale-invariant one.
- Therefore the map is **many-to-one** at the level of the statistical pattern class. P fixes a shape; it does not identify a route.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a demonstrated one-to-one map from the full pattern class to one process, with all other closures excluded by that same pattern. If it holds, this answer is withdrawn. Triangulated: Fig 13 route law plus Fig 12 isomer law plus the world review. Two figures with low overlap, one world source with high overlap to the cosmology pool.

### Q2 - Are the routes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :124: **"the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."**
- **Strict reading:** inflation and a matter bounce do **not** share a fixed atom set in D3. The inflaton potential and the contracting matter phase are bindings from D2, not members of the six. Under Fig 12's strict definition they are not isomers of one another inside D3. They are **different closures** whose projections happen to coincide (Fig 6 :23).
- **Weaker but useful reading:** hold the observational ingredients fixed, the quantities that P actually reports: amplitude, tilt, adiabaticity, Gaussianity, tensor bound, isocurvature bound. Two histories that give the same values over that fixed set are **isomers in the space of predictions**, with the "bond" being the dynamical relation between the early state and the observed spectrum. Expansion plus slow roll, or contraction plus bounce, are different bonds over the same predicted quantities. The difference is invisible in the two-point function and decisive in the higher-order and tensor observables.
- This is the claim worth testing. **Falsifier:** if two routes cannot be written over the same predicted quantity set, or if their difference is already visible in the two-point function, the isomer reading fails and the different-closures reading stands instead. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), triangulated with Fig 13 :124, unmeasured at the ground.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- First order: a process produces P. The pattern is settled against the ground (D1).
- Second order: the settled pattern becomes an atom, and the source question composes over it. "Which process" needs a second operand, a quantity on which routes differ.
- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Describe P before naming a route, or inflation will seem to fit simply because it was named first.
- Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
- **Falsifier:** show that a settled first-order pattern cannot serve as an atom for a second-order source question, or that no second-order composition exists here.

### Q4 - What closes the second-order question?

- Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
- Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
- The discriminators named in the opened sources:
  1. **Tensor amplitude r.** Physics Today (2008) states that ekpyrotic models predict an extremely low amplitude for primordial gravitational waves, so a large detected background would falsify that scenario.
  2. **Tensor tilt and spectral tilt.** Brandenberger and Peter note that measuring a slight red tilt rules out some alternatives such as string gas, which predicts a slightly blue spectrum, and that tensor tilt is a distinct observational signature.
  3. **Non-Gaussianity amplitude and shape.** The matter-bounce review and the bouncing-cosmologies review report that bounce models often produce non-Gaussianity of order unity with a bispectrum shape different from simple slow-roll inflation.
  4. **Isocurvature and adiabaticity.** Planck's polarization data test the adiabaticity of the initial conditions and constrain isocurvature.
- These are filters that narrow model space (Fig 9 :60), but each becomes a **witness** only when it is read as a source-discriminating measurement between two stated routes, not as a fit inside one closure. That is the distinction Fig 9 :60 enforces.
- Cross-field analogue: the spectrometer in metamerism (opened this session, UEA Colour Group and RP Photonics). A trichromatic reading merges physically different spectra; adding spectral channels separates them. Here the two-point spectrum merges physically different histories; a discriminator with a different response separates them.
- **Falsifier:** a discriminator that all candidate routes predict identically, so it cannot separate them even in principle.

### Q5 - Does cosmic variance block the closure?

- Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
- Kamionkowski and Loeb (1997, opened) state that a limiting precision arises because the surface of last scatter probes only a finite two-dimensional slice, and that probing other surfaces from different locations would reduce it.
- So the ground has an irremovable floor on some quantities. That is not underivability in principle: it is a limit of the available instrument at this budget. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**.
- **Falsifier:** a second independent realization, or an instrument reading a genuinely different quantity rather than a finer reading of the same projection.

## CONCLUSION

The pattern class is a **many-to-one projection**. P does not identify its process (Q1). Two routes over the same predicted quantity set are isomers in prediction space, while in the framework's strict atom set they are different closures whose projections coincide (Q2). The pattern question is first order and the source question is second order over the settled pattern (Q3). The source question is closed by a source-discriminating instrument on tensors, tilt, non-Gaussianity or isocurvature, not by more precision on the same projection (Q4), subject to a cosmic-variance floor that is a budget limit rather than an underivability (Q5).

This framing rescues what is defensible without claiming more: the underdetermination is figure-grounded; the viability of any named alternative is a ground question left as a `gap`.
