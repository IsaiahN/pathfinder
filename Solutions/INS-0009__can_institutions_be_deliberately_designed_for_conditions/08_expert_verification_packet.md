# Expert verification packet - the Tether on designing institutions for conditions that do not exist

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of institutional design can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and each per-domain verdict; `general` on the structural two-regime claim. Not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can institutions be deliberately designed for conditions that do not yet exist, or do they mainly adapt through trial and error?" is a false either/or. Deliberate design and adaptation are two regimes of one habitat relation, distinguished by whether a maintainer seat exists. The maintained regime can introduce actors and instruments but cannot subtract the habitat, and its work is permanent. The unmaintained regime is selection by the ground, not intelligence. A design for a not-yet-existing condition is possible only where something at some resolution is already returning a signal that fails to resolve; theory may precede the instrument, but no first sensor can be specified for a condition that has never registered anywhere. Where no signal registers, what can be carried is a seed (a method and a head start), an enumerated habitat, and a reachable channel to the ground. The empirical "mainly" remains a `gap` until a natural experiment measures contact.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** the two named mechanisms are two modes with different seats, and the seat's power is asymmetric. Design can add, not subtract; adaptation has no seat and is selection.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :55 - *"Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."* Fig 11 :59 - *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :61 - *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Fig 11 :36 - *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit an institution whose maintainer seat subtracted an actor from the habitat rather than substituting a habitat; or a design that permanently solved contact without maintenance; or a case where what survived was set by agreement among frames with the anchor shown to have moved.

### C2 - the hard half; checkable by reading Figure 6 and by an empirical signal test

- **Statement:** designing for a not-yet-existing condition is licensed only when a signal at some resolution is already failing to resolve. Theory may precede the instrument, but a sensor for a never-registered condition cannot be specified.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :34 - *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* Fig 6 :35 - *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 6 :40 - *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."* Qualification, from the scope note after :38: *"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point"*
- **What to check:** for a named future condition, was anything at any resolution already returning a failing signal before the design was made? It can be a wandering point, a residual in an existing dataset, or a predicted pointing. If nothing was, the claim predicts that specification fails and only a seed plus maintained contact works.
- **Refuter:** an institution specified from scratch for a condition where no signal at any resolution was returning anything, documented as absent, which then changed contact with the ground and survived. That breaks Fig 6 :40.

### C3 - framework-internal plus an empirical check; union, enumeration and the middle link

- **Statement:** the union must be unbundled before search; enumeration runs outward past first order; the usual break is at affordance and progress terms, not at planning.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades."* Fig 3 :26 - *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- **What to check:** was the habitat enumerated to second and third order, or only direct contact? The figure's standing case is DDT reaching eagles three steps out. Then ask whether the observed failure was at affordance and progress terms or at planning.
- **Refuter:** a designed institution whose first-order enumeration is shown by instrument to have left no material third-order residual; or a case where the failure is demonstrably a planning defect with the middle link instrumented and correct.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, and no final verdict about which mechanism dominates. "Mainly" is reported as a union with separate verdicts.
- Not that deliberation is unnecessary, or that trial and error is blindly random. Each is a regime with a seat condition.
- Not that any particular constitution, court, market or policy is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that a first sensor can be specified for a condition that has never registered anywhere at any resolution.
- Not that the held set of primitives is nature's own list. It is a visible guess, and the essays say so.
- Not `grounded`. No institution was measured in this run. A reported success by a reader is not corroboration if that reader shares this evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

1. **Signal test:** document what was already failing to resolve for a named future condition before the design was made.
2. **Contact test:** measure whether the institution's contact with its conditions changed, not its rules or its reputation (Fig 11 :40).
3. **Cascade test:** enumerate to second and third order and measure the residual that matters (Fig 11 :52).
4. **Middle-link test:** instrument what the future condition affords and what counts as progress (Fig 3 :27).
5. **Source discriminator:** several origins give the same persistence reading, so build an instrument that distinguishes designed from selected persistence, or state that none exists yet (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).

2. Read Fig 6 :34, :35, :40 and the Neptune scope note. Is the signal precondition stated correctly, with the qualification intact?
3. Check C2 against one case you know: was there a signal before the design, and did contact change? A one-line "this fails because..." is enough.
4. Read Fig 3 :26 and Fig 11 :52. In your field, where does the chain actually break, and how far out does the residual sit?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
