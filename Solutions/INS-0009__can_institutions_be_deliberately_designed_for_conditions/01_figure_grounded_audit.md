## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question offers two mechanisms for an institution facing conditions that do not yet exist:

- **A. Deliberate design.** A seat acts now, chooses rules and instruments, and tries to make the institution fit a future state of the world.
- **B. Trial and error.** No one targets the future state; variants are tried, the world keeps what works, and the institution is the surviving lineage.

Stated as a residual: **what gap between an institution's present contact and the contact that not-yet-existing conditions would require is closed by A, by B, and by something that is neither?** R is not "which mechanism is better in the abstract." It is the gap at the contact surface, and the figures require that the gap be indexed to an anchor rather than averaged into a verdict (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): *a rule that says which of the two mechanisms is active, at what scale, under what condition on the availability of a signal about the future, with a named anchor and a stated refuter.* That shape is written down here before any world search, so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two named options, but the census below shows they are not two values of one quantity. They are two regimes of one relation (habitat maintenance). The question is therefore a **union compound**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever aimed at "design" leaves the intersection (designed institutions that still undergo selection) standing.
- **Symmetry:** the two options are **not symmetric**. Fig 11 :59: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Design has a seat and can introduce; trial-and-error has no seat and cannot subtract the habitat either. The asymmetry is the first thing that breaks the question's even-handed "or."
- **Scale:** trial-and-error runs on a **lineage across trials** (selection over many instances); design runs **within one maintained habitat at a chosen moment** (Fig 11 :55: *"Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."*). They are different strata of the same loop, which is why averaging them is a category error.

### 1.2 One line per domain, with actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the maintained design frame (the garden) | the maintainer seat, the designer, rules, instruments, introduced actors | introduction of actors and conditions; "arrival worked on" | the ground, plus the enumerated habitat. **Yes.** Fig 11 :58: *"That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."* | union member |
| D2, the unmaintained selection frame (trial and error) | institutional lineages, conditions, the ground | differential persistence: what the ground kept | the ground, which does not update. **Yes.** Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."* | union member |
| D3, the anticipation frame (conditions that do not yet exist) | the designer, the residual, proto-instruments, possible import frames | description, prediction, round trip `R_T` | whether anything at any resolution is already returning something that fails to resolve. **Yes.** Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* | union member, and the binding constraint on D1 |
| D4, the verification and outreach frame | expert readers of institutional design research | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement. **Yes.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* | seat, not a mechanism |

No domain is admitted on the strength of an analogy alone. D1 to D4 each have a nameable anchor. Overlaps (D1 and D2 both range against the ground; D3 and D1 share the designer) are treated as compounds with their own loop, not smoothed into one verdict.

### 1.3 Partition or union, and dependency order

**Verdict: union with its own loop.** The two options are not rival answers to one question. They coexist in every real institution: a designed constitution is still selected against the conditions it meets, and an "emergent" norm usually has someone who maintained it. Unbundle (Fig 9 :54), then run one loop per member.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3) is fixed before findings:**

1. **Habitat before design.** You cannot introduce an actor until you have enumerated what is in contact with the residual (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*).
2. **Vocabulary and objective before planning.** Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."* Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Signal before instrument.** Ask whether anything is already returning a failing signal before asking what institution to build (Fig 6 :34).
4. **Refuse before mint.** If a held belief about the future condition is false, the honest move is a refusal (Formula step 2), not a new design.
5. **Ground before agreement.** Frames range against the ground; they do not negotiate with it.

Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19). Most institutional design effort lands above the break, where measurements exist, while the broken link (terms for what an unseen condition affords, and what would count as progress) has no instrument (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question's "or" is a false partition (decisive for the framing)

Fig 11 :55: *"Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Design and adaptation are not two answers to one slot. They are the maintained and unmaintained modes of one habitat relation. Every designed institution that survives is still being selected; every "emergent" institution that persists usually had someone maintaining the channel. Asking "which one mainly" averages across a union and throws away the live signal (Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

**Refuter:** show a real institution in which one mechanism is wholly absent across the whole cascade, with the anchor named and the absence measured. Barring that, the union reading stands. **Triangulated:** Fig 11 and Fig 9, low overlap (habitat modes versus union or partition logic).

### F2 - Deliberate design is real, but bounded by asymmetry, enumeration and permanent contact

Fig 11 :58: *"That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."*

Fig 11 :59: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*

Fig 11 :61: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

So the design option is not refused. A seat can choose instruments, distil impactful actors, and introduce what was not there. But three hard limits follow: the seat can add and not subtract; the work never ends; and a change that leaves contact with the ground unchanged is inert however elegant the design. A constitution rewritten on paper without changing what the institution registers from its environment is exactly the inert case.

**Refuter:** exhibit an institution where a seat subtracted an actor from the habitat (rather than substituting a habitat, Fig 11 :66), or where a design permanently solved a Living Institution's contact without further maintenance. Either breaks the asymmetry or permanence claim. **Triangulated:** Fig 11 :58, :59, :61, :40, plus Fig 11 :73 (*"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*).

### F3 - Trial and error is the unmaintained mode, and it is selection, not intelligence

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*

Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The adaptation option is also not refused. Where no maintainer seat exists, the ground keeps what survives, and that is a real mechanism. Calling the surviving institution "well designed" is a label applied afterwards. The ground votes through selection between variants, not through agreement inside a room. Trial and error is therefore not the absence of a mechanism; it is the loop running without a seat.

**Refuter:** show a lineage in which what survived was determined by agreement among frames rather than by what the ground kept, with the agreement shown to have moved the anchor. That would turn panel 3 into panel 1 of Fig 2. **Triangulated:** Fig 11 and Fig 7, low overlap (habitat survival versus room-chain selection).

### F4 - Designing for conditions that do not yet exist has a sharp, stated limit

Fig 6 :33: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."*

Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*

Fig 6 :35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."*

But theory is allowed to precede the instrument:

Fig 6 :37 (the scope note following the rule): *"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point"*

So the precise figure-grounded answer to the hard half of the question is: **you can design an institution that will meet a not-yet-existing condition only to the extent that something, at some resolution, is already returning a signal that fails to resolve.** You can build the seed and maintain the contact (Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*). You can point an instrument with a prediction, as with Neptune. What you cannot do is specify a first sensor for a condition that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. For that regime, the only available moves are to maintain a habitat that will be selected against whatever arrives, and to keep the channel to the ground reachable.

Fig 6 :27 adds the reason you cannot tell these apart from inside: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*

**Refuter:** exhibit an institution specified from scratch for a condition about which nothing at any resolution was returning a signal, where the institution then changed contact with the ground and survived, with the absence of any prior signal documented. That breaks Fig 6 :40. **Triangulated:** Fig 6 :33 to :40 plus the scope note, and Fig 4 :34 (seed versus recording). **Mode `specified`; the ground half is a `gap` until a natural experiment supplies it.**

### F5 - Design must enumerate the habitat, not compose a wish list

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*

Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades."* The figure's example runs: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

A deliberate design that lists only first-order actors inherits the DDT failure. The residual that matters is often three steps out. Because exclusion is unavailable (F2), a badly enumerated introduction is not cleanly reversible: cane toads and kudzu are named in Fig 11 :65 as *"the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

**Refuter:** show a designed institution whose cascade was enumerated only to first order and whose third-order effect was then shown to be negligible by an instrument, with the enumeration and the measurement both on record. That would narrow the cascade rule. **Triangulated:** Fig 11 :43, :48, :52, :65, and the ecology citation (Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994; Carson 1962) verified in the framework essay's citation list.

### F6 - The typical design failure is at the middle link of Fig 3, not at planning

Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."*

Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

For a not-yet-existing condition, the missing terms are precisely: what would this condition afford the institution, and what would count as progress toward meeting it? Institutional design effort commonly lands in planning (a constitution, a roadmap, a task force), which is above the break. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*).

**Refuter:** show a case where the design failed because of a planning defect while the affordance and progress terms were instrumented and correct. That moves the break above the middle link for that case. **Triangulated:** Fig 3 :19, :24 to :27, plus Fig 6 :34 (availability of a signal) as the upstream dependency.

### F7 - What crosses between scales is a method or a head start, never a recording

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Fig 4 :23: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

An institution copied wholesale from another time or place is a recording replayed downward. It produces the past success and cannot produce a new one, because the conditions it lands in decide what the seed becomes (Fig 4 :34). What can travel is the method that generated the design and the head start that makes the next search cheaper. Any condensation of the figures into a shorter rule carries a round trip gap `R_T` that must be measured, not assumed zero (`11_audit.md` §Round trip gap).

**Refuter:** show a copied institution whose success was reproduced at full fidelity in a different habitat with no method and no head start carried, and with the round trip gap measured at zero. **Triangulated:** Fig 4 :22, :23, :30, :34.

### F8 - The ground settles the question; prestige and agreement do not

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

So the verdict about design versus adaptation is not to be taken from a census of scholars who prefer one answer, nor from the prestige of the venue in which the claim appears. The anchor here is a natural experiment, a reform that changed contact and was measured, a counter, or a patient outcome in the institutional sense. Selecting expert readers for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. The first contact therefore carries only the residual and its refuter (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).

**Refuter:** show a ground reading of institutional adaptation where the measured outcome changed because the frames agreed, with the anchor shown to have moved when pushed. **Triangulated:** Fig 2 and Fig 10, plus the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap's prestige rule.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the world search and before the conclusion, per Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A real institution is exhibited in which one of the two modes is wholly absent across the whole cascade, with the anchor named and the absence measured (breaks F1).
2. A maintainer seat is shown to subtract an actor from the habitat rather than substitute a habitat, or a design is shown to permanently solve contact without maintenance (breaks F2).
3. An institution is specified from scratch for a condition about which nothing at any resolution was returning a signal, and it then changed contact with the ground and survived, with the absence of prior signal documented (breaks F4).
4. A designed institution's first-order enumeration is shown by instrument to have left no material third-order residual (breaks F5).
5. A ground reading shows that agreement among frames moved the anchor in a selection process (breaks F8).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Refuse the either/or before answering it.** Lead with F1: the two named mechanisms are two regimes, not competing values.
2. **Give the future condition a signal test before a design test.** Ask what, at any resolution, is already failing to resolve (F4). If nothing registers, the honest answer is a maintained habitat and a seed, not a specification.
3. **Enumerate before introducing.** List actors, conditions and relations outward until the cascade stops mattering; price the introduction against a third-order effect (F5).
4. **Instrument the middle link.** Write down what the not-yet-existing condition would afford and what would count as progress before drawing the plan (F6).
5. **Send methods, not recordings.** The reusable artifact is the derivation and its refuter (F7).
6. **Use public opt-in venues and ask for a refutation, not belief** (F8). Record the stage-1 verdict before any theory is shown.

Fig 7 :42: *"the truth is where the schedule is heading"* is the right posture here: the next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced, not booked to resemble the last one.

### Strains, named not hidden

- **Strain 1, live.** Designing for "conditions that do not yet exist" is simultaneously permitted (Neptune, theory before instrument) and forbidden (no sensor for never-registered conditions). This is resolved, not erased: the two statements govern different cases, distinguished by whether anything at any resolution is returning a failing signal. The discriminator is an instrument, not an argument. It remains a strain at the ground because no measurement in this run decides which case a given future institution is in.
- **Strain 2, live.** "Mainly" in the question invites an average. The figures forbid averaging across slots (Fig 1 :22) and across a union (Fig 9 :54). The word cannot be answered as posed; it is reported as a union with two separate verdicts.
- **After re-census: two strains, both named, none hidden.**

### Updated verdict of the audit

The shape survives: a union of two regimes with different seats, a hard signal condition on designing for the not-yet-existing, enumeration before introduction, a middle-link failure mode, and a ground requirement for any verdict. What does not survive is the question's own framing as a single either/or with a "mainly" score. **Mode `specified`; verdict `candidate`; no ground reading taken.**
