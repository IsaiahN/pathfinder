The canonical figures README adds the governing sentence: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32 adds: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the particular answer. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is not "the right institution." It is:

> two regimes of one habitat relation, with the maintained one able to introduce but not subtract, and with a design for a not-yet-registered condition carrying a signal precondition that must be met before any specification is licensed.

That is a constraint on any acceptable answer. It does not select a constitution, a legislature, a court, or a policy. Those would be names.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse on overlapping terms. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* For an unseen condition this is the only portable message: describe the effect you would expect, not the institution you would build.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- Fig 9 :31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* The stop is a narrowed shape, not a final artefact.

For this question the effect terms are: a seat or no seat; a signal that registers or does not; contact changed or unchanged; a cascade enumerated or unenumerated. Each can be checked against the world without using the framework's private vocabulary.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself.

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The canonical README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For institutions that means a reform whose effect on contact was measured, a natural experiment, a court, a market, a counter.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). A design principle imported from ecology or mechanism design is licensed only if its source and the gap it explains are recorded.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the first. An instrument that reads a projection *"cannot comment on the source."* The example is metamerism: one green reading, many possible compositions. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied here: observing that an institution persists does not tell you whether it was designed, emerged, or was imposed and then selected. Several paths give the same reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not a story about origins. This is a live limitation of the whole exercise, not a solved point.

## 4. The design-specific reading: seed, not recording

Four of the essays carry most of the weight for this particular question.

- *Arrival of the Fittest*: *"Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground."* And: *"The same code runs when there is a Ground Maintainer. It runs in cultivation, when humans select which plants to breed."* This is the figure's two modes, stated in plain prose: not two mechanisms but one loop with and without a seat.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"A seed grows in the ground and its lineage learns how to survive in a new environment without already having the answer. The seed is not the recording of a tree, it is instead the encoding of something that will grow one, wherever it lands in conditions that are hospitable."* This is the correct form of "designing for conditions that do not yet exist." You carry a generator, not a snapshot.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Neither alone gets you there. A ground with no seed gives you a scorched earth. A seed with no ground gives you unbounded generation with no resilience to harsh conditions and constraints. Together they're the whole package and everything else is the machinery that connects them together."* This is why neither option in the question can be refused outright.
- *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"The group shares methods, never recordings."* The transferable object is the method that produced the design and its refuter.
- *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: *"You cannot accumulate your way from the category of outputs to the category of generators; they are different kinds of thing, and no quantity of the first sums to the second."* A pile of successfully copied institutions is not a design method, and a pile of successful adaptations is not a guarantee for the next condition.

The habitat warning in *How Intelligence Actually Works* is the cost side: *"when altering a habitat by unintentionally introducing a predatory threat into the ecosystem, or disturbing the balance by adding or removing vital resource points, you can inadvertently create major unpredictable consequences downstream."* And, on the cases where the damage was caught: *"we were lucky to find traces of the impact of our changes that we could observe in the first place."* Luck is not an instrument. Fig 11 :43 (*"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*) is the rule that replaces it.

## 5. Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming an institutional design? Correction to my own §1

**Correction.** I initially overstated the rule. The canonical phrase is about a *missing* thing: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* For this question the relevant primitives, a seat, a habitat, introduction, enumeration, a seed, a ground, are already held. Naming one of them, or naming a known institutional form as an instance, is a **binding from the world frame onto held atoms**, not the invention of a new primitive.

What remains forbidden:

- inventing a seventh mechanism to make the either/or look resolved;
- claiming that a binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure"*; a binding adds none);
- asserting that the framework *selects* a particular constitution when Fig 11 :58 only says a seat *can* work on arrival.

*They're Scanning Books* records the standing caveat about the held set itself: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* So the shape is a visible choice, not a discovery, and it is reported as such.

Both readings are kept. The narrowed one governs; the earlier one is preserved so the correction is not silently erased.

## 6. The ground requirement in the essays

- *Arrival of the Fittest*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"intelligence is the emergent result of extensive mapping of a complex system against unknown and unforgiving environments. The test that environment applies is what we call fitness."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: institutions that only check against themselves *"drift together and don't notice"*; the safeguards that work, independent replication, apprenticeship, courts, are all ways of keeping an anchor reachable.
- *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A verdict about design versus adaptation therefore cannot be delivered by counting which camp has more scholars, nor by naming a prestigious reviewer. It needs a reading that an institution's contact with its conditions changed in a measured way.

## 7. My own status, stated explicitly

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."* The caveat attached to the architecture demo is explicit: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

This audit is therefore a **simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures**. It is not a measurement of any institution, not a test of the framework, and not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on. The essays also warn that a search which finds nothing proves nothing: something to keep in mind when §4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` reports a `gap` at the ground.
