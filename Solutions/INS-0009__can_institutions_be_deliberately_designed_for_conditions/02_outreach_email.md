**Subject:** Can you refute one claim about designing institutions for conditions that do not exist yet?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about institutional design I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can institutions be deliberately designed for conditions that do not yet exist, or do they mainly adapt through trial and error?" is posed as an either/or, but the two options are not rival answers to one question. They are two regimes of one relation: a maintained habitat, where a seat can introduce actors and instruments, and an unmaintained habitat, where selection keeps what the ground kept. A designed institution is still selected; an "emergent" one usually had someone maintaining the channel. So I am not asking which one is bigger. I am claiming the either/or is a union that has to be unbundled, and that each half has a different seat and a different limit.
>
> The sharp part is the not-yet-existing condition. A design for one is possible only where something, at some resolution, is already returning a signal that fails to resolve. Theory can precede the instrument, as a prediction preceded the observation of Neptune. But a first sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution, cannot be specified from a description. Where no signal registers, the available moves are to carry a seed (a method and a head start, not a recording of someone else's success), to enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering, and to keep the channel to the ground reachable.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that deliberation is unnecessary, that trial and error is blind in some absolute sense, that any particular institution is the answer, or that a natural experiment in this folder settled anything. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The design half is asymmetric: a seat can add actors and cannot subtract the habitat, and a managed habitat is permanent work. A design that leaves contact with the ground unchanged is inert, however good it looks on paper.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an institution in which one of the two modes is wholly absent across the whole cascade, with the anchor named and the absence measured;
> 2. an institution specified for a condition where nothing at any resolution was returning a signal, which then changed contact with the ground and survived, with the absence documented;
> 3. a maintainer seat shown to subtract an actor from the habitat rather than substitute a habitat, or a design shown to permanently solve contact without maintenance;
> 4. a ground reading in which agreement among frames moved the anchor in a selection process.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the figure text and the derivation. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** I can send the eight-step formula. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
