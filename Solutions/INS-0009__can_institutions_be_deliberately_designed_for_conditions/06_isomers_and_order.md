# Institutions as bonded arrangements: isomers, cycles and order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit concluded that deliberate design and trial-and-error adaptation are two regimes of one habitat relation, not two values of one quantity. Three questions remain:

1. What kind of composition is an institution, as opposed to a list of its rules?
2. Are a designed institution and an adapted institution **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or only members of the same **functional group**?
3. What **order** does each regime express, and in what order must the two be composed?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (this derivation only)

Atoms held: a formal rule, an enforcement actor, an information channel, a sanction, a boundary, a maintainer seat. Bonds held: the seven (three symmetric, four ordered) plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The anchor is the figure text and, for any real case, the ground. Strains are named at the end.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is an institution a molecule or an ingredient list?

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

Fig 12 :7 (collapse 6, the flat formula): *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*, with the consequence *"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."*

A constitution that lists rules, agencies and penalties without recording which relation holds between them has recorded the atoms and not the institution. Two polities with the same written rules can differ because in one the court reviews the legislature and in the other the legislature reviews the court. That difference is a bond, not an ingredient. In Fig 12's terms they are different substances whose notation cannot tell them apart.

**Falsifier:** show two institutions with identical formal ingredients and identical bonds in every test below that nevertheless behave differently on a measured outcome with the ground held fixed. That breaks the claim that the bond carries the rest of the information.

### Q2 - Isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- **Weak reading.** "Designed institution" and "adapted institution" name two regimes, not two atom sets. A designed court and an evolved norm share at most the pattern `state → structure → state` (a condition, an institutional form, a changed condition). They are members of the same **functional group**: the group fixes the kind (a composition that registers a condition and changes contact), the qualifier says which member (seat or no seat). They are **not isomers** on this reading, because they do not share the full atom set.
- **Strong reading.** If both are written over the full shared atom set, formal rule, enforcement actor, information channel, sanction, boundary and seat, differing only in which bond mediates which relation, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The designed case binds rule to enforcement by production (⇒) and sequence (→); the adapted case binds them by a cycle in which the outcome re-enters as input.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either regime can be written without an atom the other requires (a designed institution with no information channel, or an adapted one with no sanction), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Both readings are kept; the strong one is the claim worth testing.

### Q3 - Which bonds dominate each regime?

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

The Operators table defines production as *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* and its test as *"is there a B before A fires? No."*

- **Designed regime (maintained seat).** Its characteristic bonds are production (⇒, a new agency or rule is brought into being), sequence (→, staged implementation), comparison (⋛, the threshold that counts as meeting the future condition), and subtraction (−, progress measured as remaining gap). Negation (¬) is available and essential: a design must be able to refuse a held belief about the future condition. Fig 5 :56: *"Same mint operator, same three guards, same bargain. Only the direction of the residual differs."* The seat's power is asymmetric, so production is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :59).
- **Adapted regime (no seat).** Its characteristic structure is the **cycle**. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Trial and error is exactly a cycle with variation inside and selection between (Fig 7 :34: *"→ variation inside, selection between"*). It also exhibits the **catalyst**: Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A norm that survives each use and is regenerated by being used is a catalyst. Nothing in the notation records which a term is, which is why two institutions that look identical can have different persistence.

This is not an analogy imported from chemistry. Fig 12 :97: *"This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"*, with isomerism named by Berzelius in 1830. The bond is a typed relation, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands.

**Falsifier:** show an adapted institution whose loop does not re-enter its output as input (a one-shot selection with no retained variant), or show that the catalyst reading fails because the supposed norm is spent on first use. Either breaks the cycle or catalyst claim.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*

A regime is not "before" or "after" the other by importance. The dependency order is:

1. **A condition must register** before either regime can act (Fig 6 :34).
2. **An institution must settle against the ground** before it can become an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104).
3. **Only then** can design compose over the settled adapted norm, or adaptation select over the designed rule. Each becomes the habitat of the other.
4. **Refusal precedes mint** when a held belief about the condition is false (Formula step 2).

So the union has a loop, not a linear sequence. Fig 12 :121 is the structural reason: a chain cannot close, a cycle can, and the institution/regime relation is a cycle. This is why a one-off answer to "which one mainly" loses the thing being asked about.

### Q5 - Price check, held apart

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. Fig 12 :127: *"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."*

A designed constitution is cheap to state and expensive to settle against a future condition; an adapted norm is expensive to describe after the fact and cheap in actions already spent by selection. Summing these into one "efficiency" score is the error the figure names. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Any "mainly" ranking in this question is a filter masquerading as a witness.

## CONCLUSION

An institution is a bonded arrangement, not an ingredient list. Designed and adapted regimes are members of one functional group; over the full shared atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The designed regime is characterised by production, sequence, comparison, subtraction and negation; the adapted regime by cycle and catalyst. Their union is ordered by settlement, not by importance, and it closes as a cycle. The two currencies, description bits and settlement actions, are held apart. Nothing here is `grounded`: no institution was measured.
