**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive a read by someone who has not seen the framework. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not stand on its own.

**Subject:** A checkable claim about why rejected scientific ideas come back

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about a historical case. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the history):**
>
> A refused scientific idea cannot simply recur. In the framework I am using, a refusal is an addition to the record, not a deletion, and the reachable set falls when something is added. The rule is stated plainly: a term "cannot cycle in and out, because the refusal never leaves the record: it becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth." So when an idea reappears, what changed is not the idea. It is the frame: a new term was built, an atom was imported from another frame, or an instrument was improved from a worse one already returning something.
>
> A second rule matters for historians. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." So "there was no known mechanism" was never a ground settlement. It was a reading at a link that had no instrument.
>
> **What would refute the output (written down before I looked at any cases):**
> 1. A refused term re-enters a frame's reach with no new mint, no import, no instrument change and no growth in the record.
> 2. A third operator, besides mint, import and instrument extension, is shown to expand what a frame can reach.
> 3. A sealed frame is exhibited, so that a failed search inside it does prove absence.
> 4. A community's agreement settles a question about the world with no anchor and no new ground contact.
>
> If any one holds, the output is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that the ideas were "right all along," that their rejection was always irrational, or that the world changed to make them true. The ground does not decay; the channel does. Social acceptance is a convention, and I am not counting it as a verdict about the world.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as Fig N :line.
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the figures link. Run it on a historical case you know well and can check against a record. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
