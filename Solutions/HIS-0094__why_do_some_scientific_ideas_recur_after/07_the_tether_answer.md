# The Tether's answer: why rejected ideas recur

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the reading of historical cases, nowhere `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

R1: an idea is refused or unreachable in a frame at time t, and present at t+n. R2: the ground later pays for it, and the community reverses its verdict. These are a union compound (Fig 9 :5: "Split it rather than search."), with two loops and two verdicts that are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **A refusal is an addition, not a deletion.** Fig 6 :42: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." The reachable set falls because something was added. So recurrence cannot be a stored idea simply reasserting itself.
2. **A term cannot cycle.** Fig 6 :45: "a term cannot cycle in and out, because the refusal never leaves the record: it becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth." Recurrence is therefore a dependent variable: it is explained by new growth, not by the idea's merit or by time.
3. **Only three things move reach.** A mint inside the closure, an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"), or an inward instrument extension from something already returning a signal (Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."). Fig 6 :34 forbids specifying a sensor from a description of the target: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."
4. **The classic rejection was never a proof of absence.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." "No known mechanism" is a failure to find inside a finite reach, and no frame is sealed (Fig 9 :44).
5. **What returns is a shape by another route, not the same object.** Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." So the historian's question "is this the same idea back again?" is answered only by provenance, never by the content of the idea.
6. **Later settlement is a change in the channel.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." The view that the world changed is named as an open undecidable (Fig 10 :47), not chosen as the default.
7. **Social acceptance is a convention, not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." A community reversing itself is real, but it belongs to the convention layer and cannot settle R2.
8. **Once settled, the term becomes an operand.** Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." This is why rediscovery is genuine work across a boundary that drops the record, even though it needs no new primitive inside a run.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "the idea was right all along." That is a `general` verdict with no ground reading.
- Not "all rejection is prejudice." Conventions are legitimate for shared meanings (Fig 2 :41); the error is counting them as verdicts about the world.
- Not "the world changed to make it true." Undecidable from inside, recorded as such.
- Not a single theory of recurrence. Two domains, two verdicts, no average (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
- Not grounded. No historical case was measured here; the cases in `10_source_ledger.csv` are secondary corroboration only.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A provenance-discriminating historical instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," and the question was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

- **Provenance test:** for a named recurrence, does the record show which atom crossed, from which closure, on what date, with what source gate? Convergence and adoption must be separated by the record (Fig 8 :51).
- **Mechanism test:** was the restored reach a mint, an import or an instrument extension? If none, F1 is broken.
- **Settlement test:** did the ground pay, or did only the convention change? Agreement alone is not the ground (Fig 2 :35).
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a refused term returning with no new growth; a third operator that moves the wall; a sealed frame; agreement settling a world-question with no anchor; a demonstrable change in the ground with no channel change.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures can say what a recurrence must involve and what would have to have happened for it to occur. They cannot name the missing atom in a given case without a provenance record and a ground reading. "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands" (Fig 13 :134 style claim: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"). Indiscernibility is not identity: two appearances with the same content are not thereby the same term. The lineage is what makes the answer checkable, and where the record is missing, the answer stops.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that rejected ideas were right all along. It says that a refusal is an addition to the record, so a term cannot simply cycle back into reach; it returns only if something new is minted, imported from another frame, or made visible by a better instrument. That is why "there was no known mechanism" was never a proof of absence. What looks like the same idea coming back is usually the same substance reached by a different route, and only the provenance record separates a convergent derivation from an adopted import. The ground does not decay; the channel does. Later acceptance has two layers that must be kept apart: a change in what the frame can reach and settle, and a change in the community's convention. The second is not a verdict about the world. What this offers is the shape any explanation must have, four refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The case stays open until a record with provenance settles it.
