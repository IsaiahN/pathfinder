# Expert verification packet - why rejected scientific ideas recur

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the reading of cases, not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A refused scientific idea cannot recur as the same term. A refusal is an addition to the record, so the reachable set falls when something is added, and the term returns only if new growth restores reach: a mint inside the frame, an import from a frame whose closure differs, or an instrument extended from something already returning a signal. "There was no known mechanism" was never a proof of absence. What looks like the same idea coming back is the same substance reached by a different route, separable from an adopted import only by provenance. Later acceptance has two layers that must be kept apart: a change in what the frame can reach and settle, and a change in the community's convention. The second is not a verdict about the world. The ground does not decay; the channel does.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 6

- **Statement:** a refused term cannot cycle into reach again on its own; new growth is necessary.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :42 - "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." Fig 6 :43 - "So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not." Fig 6 :45 - "a term cannot cycle in and out, because the refusal never leaves the record: it becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth"
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where a refused term re-enters reach with no new mint, no import, no instrument change and no growth in the record. That breaks the necessity claim.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 9

- **Statement:** the three movers of reach are mint, import and inward instrument extension; a failed search inside a frame proves nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14 - "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :29 - "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44 - "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."
- **What to check:** whether "no known mechanism" rejections in the historical record were treated as proofs of absence, and whether the later acceptance coincided with a new atom or a better instrument (for example germ theory arriving from Pasteur's frame, or magnetometers already returning oceanic stripes).
- **Refuter:** show a third operator that expands closure(Γ), or a sealed frame whose failed search proves absence.

### C3 - framework-internal plus a historical split; checkable by reading Figures 8, 10, 2 and 13

- **Statement:** recurrence is convergence or adoption, distinguishable only by provenance; social acceptance is a convention, not the settlement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :121 - "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 8 :50 - "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Fig 8 :51 - "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" Fig 10 :24 - "the ground does not decay, the channel does" Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** in a case the reader knows, can the later acceptance be identified as a ground settlement rather than a change in the community's shared meaning? Is there a provenance record that separates convergence from adoption?
- **Refuter:** a case where community agreement, with no anchor and no new ground contact, settles a world-question; or a case where two occurrences of the idea have identical contents and no record distinguishes their routes, yet the surplus is claimed anyway.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the ideas were true all along. No retrospective verdict is asserted.
- Not that rejection is always irrational. Conventions are legitimate for shared meanings (Fig 2 :41); the error is only counting them as verdicts.
- Not that the world changed. Fig 10 :47 names that as undecidable from inside; it is not the default explanation.
- Not that this is a theory of everything, and not a claim that this framework is the only one that could describe recurrence.
- Not `grounded`. No historical case was measured in producing this packet.
- Not a claim to have named the mechanism in any specific case. Naming requires provenance and a ground reading, which this packet does not supply.

## 5. What would settle it

A provenance-discriminating historical instrument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched this session): an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source," and the case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

1. **Provenance test:** does the record show which atom crossed, from which closure, on what date, under what source gate?
2. **Mechanism test:** mint, import or instrument extension. If none, C1 fires.
3. **Settlement test:** ground payment versus convention change. Agreement alone is not the ground (Fig 2 :35).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :42 to :45 and Fig 8 :39, :50 to :51 (five short passages).
2. Check C2 against one case you know: was the earlier refusal a proof of absence, and what new atom or instrument actually crossed?
3. Name one record or instrument that would distinguish convergence from adoption in that case, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
