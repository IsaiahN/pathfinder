# Isomers, unions and order in a recurrence case

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

"Why did the idea recur" and "what explains its later acceptance" look like one question. The figures require a check before searching: is this a partition or a union? And when two episodes carry the same label, are they the same term, an isomer, or merely members of one functional group?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: refusals (¬), mints (φ), imports, instruments, the ground, conventions. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The two occurrences of "the idea" are not assumed identical; that is the thing under test.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the question one question or two? UNION.

Fig 9 :4: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset"
Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

R1 (recurrence) is a claim about reach: what made the term derivable again. R2 (later acceptance) is a claim about settlement: what made the ground pay. A single lever, such as "the evidence finally accumulated," can clear the region where only one condition holds and leave the intersection standing, because the other condition still binds (Fig 9 :56). So the question is a **union compound** with its own loop. Two verdicts, never averaged (Fig 1 :19).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single observation settles both recurrence and acceptance in every case, so that no case recurs without acceptance and none is accepted without recurrence, the union reading fails and the two loops should be merged.

### Q2 - Same idea, or isomer, or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Take the two most-cited cases. Continental drift and germ theory share almost no atoms: one is built from mantle convection, seafloor magnetism and plate motion; the other from microorganisms, culture and antisepsis. As recorded, they are not isomers. They are two members of one **functional group** whose substructure is `refusal → new growth → reach restored → settlement`: the group says what kind of event happened, the qualifier says which case.

The stronger isomer claim is available when both episodes are written over a full shared atom set, for example two episodes in the same science where the same refused atom is re-entered under a different bond (one by import, one by instrument extension). Then Fig 12 :117 applies: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour. **This is the checkable version worth offering to a historian**, because it makes a prediction: the two episodes should have the same atom inventory and differ only in which bond carried the atom across.

**Falsifier:** if either episode requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Recorded in advance.

### Q3 - What is the order?

Fig 3 dependency order, applied to the compound:

1. **Perception:** was anything at the rejected link already returning a signal? A "no known mechanism" rejection fails here (Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what the rejected item affords? If the frame lacks them, the refusal aimed at a shape, not a term (Fig 5 :62).
3. **Objective:** what would count as the idea being back? Without a bound answer-shape, any later mention counts and the search is meaningless (Formula step "Direction").
4. **Planning:** which closure holds the missing atom, and what is the source gate (Fig 8 :12)?
5. **Learn and carry:** once the ground pays, the term is an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world.").

The order is by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."). Effort spent on step 5 before step 1 arrives at a step that cannot use it.

### Q4 - What are the analogues, and where do they stop?

- **Recurrence as convergence, not as cycle.** Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Two appearances of a label are not one object moving in a circle. They can be two derivations arriving separately at the same substance, which is corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50). The analogue stops at the record: without provenance, convergence and adoption look identical (Fig 8 :51).
- **Recurrence as round trip.** Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." A community's compressed memory of an old idea, carried forward and brought back, will not match what was sent. The gap `R_T` is the measurement of what the compression dropped, and the honest report of recurrence must include it (measured qualitatively in `11_audit.md`).
- **Recurrence as habitat substitution, not removal.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A later frame that "discarded the old paradigm" has not removed the conditions that produced the rejection; it has moved the residual into a different habitat and inherited actors it did not choose.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union of two conditions**, not one hard question. The two occurrences of "the same idea" are, at minimum, members of a functional group defined by `refusal → new growth → reach restored → settlement`; the stronger isomer claim is checkable only when both share a full atom set and differ by the bond that carried the atom across. Dependency order runs perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry. Both falsifiers are written above and neither has fired.
