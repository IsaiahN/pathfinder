## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms before any figure is chosen

A person acts. Call the act, as observed by the person, `o'`. The person also holds a self-theory: a belief `b` about what sort of agent they are (character) and a worth metric that ranks acts and persons. Before acting they predict `Γ(b,a)`. After acting they face `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`: the gap between the person they thought they were and what they just did. The question is how the character and moral-worth ideas alter the reading of that gap.

Three things must be held apart, and each is a separate domain below:

1. **Slot indexing.** Does the person read the act as a local event or as evidence about the kind-of-person slot? The figures forbid averaging: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
2. **Belief formation.** Where do the character ideas come from, and how are private history and inherited priors mixed? Symbols table: `b` is *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w."* `w` is *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."*
3. **Routing and refusal.** Does the act get sorted as held, novel, mis-attached, mechanism-broken, or refuted, and does the self-theory get affirmed or refused? Fig 5 :53 names the fifth kind: *"a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

I write down the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run (Fig 5 :65: *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*): **a mechanism with four parts, slot indexing, belief weighting, bond supply and routing, and valence relative to the current residual, each with a nameable ground.** Anything that cannot name its ground is not yet a domain.

## 1. Domain census (R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain)

**R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity is two or more: an act, a self-theory, and in most cases a social audience. Symmetry is broken: the person infers internal states from their own behavior *"in a manner similar to the inference processes they use when making judgments about other people"* (APA Dictionary, self-perception theory), so observation and prediction are not interchangeable. Scale is two loops: one inside the person (per-slot prediction and routing), one across person and habitat (import of character vocabulary, drift, correction). This is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

## 3. Findings, each with verbatim figure text and a refuter

### F1: Character ideas act through slot indexing, not a global moral score (triangulated, `general`)

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :20-21: a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state, and support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass.

The character concept "the kind of person I am" is a slot, and moral-worth ideas decide which act carries live mass in that slot. An act is not read against an "overall moral self-worth" number; it is read as a local deviation on a specific slot (honest, fair, loyal, brave). A scale that averages across slots will show near-zero residual while one slot is on fire, which is exactly how the signal disappears. This is why a person can report high overall moral self-regard while one specific act troubles them: Fig 1 makes that a legal state, not a contradiction.

**Refuter:** a within-person design in which the same act is judged under different character primes shows identical per-slot residual distributions, and a global score predicts the interpretation as well as or better than the slot profile. If so, F1 is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 (slot arithmetic) and the world measurement literature (Aquino and Reed's two-factor internalization/symbolization measure, Section 4), which distinguish private and public moral self-conception rather than one score. Low overlap: Fig 1 is an agent-loop claim, the scale is a psychometric instrument.

### F2: The belief is a mixture of private history and inherited prior, and the weighting is learnable (triangulated, `general`)

Symbols table: `b` is *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w."* `w` is *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* The same entry adds: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*

Ideas about character and moral worth are the inherited prior half of `b`; the person's own behavioral history is the private half. They enter the prediction before the act, not after. The weighting `w` is learnable from outcomes, so a person can come to lean more on their own record than on the inherited character story, or the reverse. This is the cleanest figure-grounded statement of the mechanism the question asks for: the ideas influence interpretation because they are an input to `Γ(b,a)`, and the residual is computed against that prediction.

**Refuter:** self-interpretation of an act is uncorrelated with both the stated character priors and the person's own behavioral history, and no learnable `w` maps the two onto the interpretation. If so, F2 is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across the Symbols table (belief/weighting) and Fig 5 :65 (bind the sought-for shape so it can accrue residual). The world source Xu et al. (2024) corroborates the inherited/context half: situational cues *"may influence social information processing by activating or deactivating the moral self-concept"*. Low overlap: symbol definition versus meta-analytic review.

### F3: The vocabulary supplies bonds, so an act becomes a route rather than a state (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

An act by itself is a state. Character ideas supply the typed relations that bond it into a route: disposition to act, act to self, act to worth. With those bonds the person can say what would count as getting somewhere ("I am improving", "I fell short"), which a vocabulary of ingredients alone cannot say. Fig 12 :117 gives the comparison: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The same act bonded as evidence of a stable trait and bonded as an exception to one are different substances, not one substance described twice. As written in the psychology literature, this is the difference between framing an act concretely as evidence of virtue and framing it abstractly as bearing on moral identity (Effron and Conway, 2015).

**Refuter:** show that the interpretation of an act is fully determined by its behavioral description and situation once character vocabulary is removed, with no residual carried by the bond type. Or show that two different bonds over the same act and self-theory produce the same interpretation in a controlled test. If so, F3 is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 (bond, route, isomer) and APA's self-perception definition (the person infers internal states from overt behavior). Low overlap: composition theory versus a dictionary definition of an inference process.

### F4: The fifth routing bin is a refusal, and moral-worth ideas license it (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 5 :53 names the fifth kind: *"a term is owed that says so"*, in full *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 12 :112: *"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Operators table :73-74: *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*

"I am not that kind of person", "that was not really me", "everyone would have done it" are operations on the self-theory, not new descriptions of the act. They refuse the standing of the self-attribution so that the person can keep the theory and live with the act. Under Fig 5 the refusal pays the same bargain as a mint: stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than the unexplained residual. The framework's own comment (Fig 5 :55) is exact here: *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* The character vocabulary does not delete the act; it removes the act's standing as something the self-theory must compose over.

Bandura's moral-disengagement mechanisms are a world-side instance of this: *"people in all walks of life behave harmfully and still maintain positive self-regard and live in peace with themselves. They do so by disengaging moral self-sanctions from their harmful practices."* This corroborates the shape; it does not supply it.

**Refuter:** a person's self-interpretation of a norm-violating act is invariant under removal of the refusal vocabulary (no change in self-regard, attribution, or subsequent behavior), or the refusal is shown to be a re-description with no negation operator. If so, F4 is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 5 (fifth bin), Fig 12 (negation not a bond), and the Operators table (accumulation without `¬`), with Bandura (1999) as a low-overlap world witness. Single-source for the exact claim that the refusal is a negation; that mapping rests on the Operators table and Fig 12 together.

### F5: The valence of a character idea is relative to the current residual, not intrinsic (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Applied to D2: there is no standing list of good character ideas. "I am honest" is a benefactor to a residual of threatened self-regard (it restores consistency) and a malfactor to a residual of unfinished self-knowledge (it closes enquiry). The same trait belief can license and constrain depending on what the person is trying to preserve. This dissolves the single empirical question "does moral self-concept increase or decrease moral behavior?" into at least two conditions. It predicts that a study which fixes one residual will find licensing, and a study which fixes another will find consistency, and neither refutes the other.

**Refuter:** the same character idea has the same effect on interpretation across all measured residuals, with no interaction between idea and goal. If so, F5 is withdrawn and the valence claim is wrong.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 (valence relativity) and Fig 2 :40 (correlation valence: *"disqualifying for a verdict"* in one use, *"efficient for a convention"* in another). The world literature splits exactly along this line (Sachdeva et al. 2009 versus the three replication attempts, Section 4), which is corroboration of the split, not of either side.

### F6: Character ideas are imports from a social habitat, and agreement without an anchor is coherent drift (triangulated for drift, single-source for anchor legitimacy, `general`)

Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 2 :13-15 describes collapse 1: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*, *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The character vocabulary a person uses to interpret themselves is supplied by a habitat (family, peers, institutions, culture), not invented at the moment of interpretation. That makes D3 a real closure with a different boundary from D2, and makes the import subject to a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*). If the person checks their self-interpretation only against the people who supplied the vocabulary, the system is panel 1 of Fig 2: coherent drift that nobody notices.

For the anchor: Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* This question is constitutively about people interpreting their own behavior, so human judgment is a legitimate ground here, not a convenient proxy. That is a claim from one figure; I mark it **single-source** rather than manufacture a second one. It is also a limit: human judgment is an instrument, not the ground, for questions that are not about people (Fig 2 :37-38).

**Refuter:** show a person's self-interpretation converging on the majority position with no behavioral record or independent experience changing, and that the convergence tracks influence rather than evidence (supports the drift claim), or show that for a constitutively about-people question a non-human instrument settles "good answer for a person" without any human judgment in the loop (refutes the anchor claim).

**Status:** drift triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7; anchor legitimacy single-source (Fig 2 :36-38) by honest declaration.

### F7: The licensing-versus-consistency conflict is a union compound, not a failed effect (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 9 :4-5 *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

World sources disagree: Sachdeva, Iliev and Medin (2009) report that affirming a moral identity licenses less donating; three replication attempts (2014) found no support; a meta-analysis (Blanken et al., 2015) estimates a modest pooled effect (`d = 0.31`) with larger effects in published than unpublished studies; Effron and Conway (2015) report that concreteness versus abstraction and framing determine whether licensing or self-consistency occurs; Li, Zhou and Ding (2013) find a prescriptive/proscriptive asymmetry. Under Fig 9 this is not one effect that failed to replicate. It is a bundle of at least two conditions that a single lever ('prime moral traits') cannot clear, because the second condition still binds. Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

This is a genuine figure-governed prediction, and it is falsifiable: the split should be visible in the moderator structure, not only in a narrative review.

**Refuter:** a single clean condition (one operationalization, one frame, one morality type) accounts for both the licensing and consistency results with no interaction term and no residual subset. If so, the union reading is wrong and the conflict is an ordinary replication failure.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 (union/partition, split-before-search) and the pattern of the world results themselves. The figure gives the instruction; the world gives the phenomenon. Low overlap between the two.

### F8: The specific magnitude is a gap, and "unreachable" must be said plainly (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and :138-139 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."*

No figure line yields the magnitude of the influence, which traits matter in which culture, or the size of the licensing effect. World sources give estimates (`d = 0.31` pooled, with publication bias), but a pooled effect is a filter for the budget, not a witness for this question's verdict. The honest status of the specific empirical answer is `gap` at this budget, not a number and not silence.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line from which the magnitude follows, or a ground reading (a settled behavioral instrument) that fixes it for the population of interest. If so, F8 is withdrawn and the status upgrades.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 8 and Fig 13.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Slot refuter.** Per-slot residual profiles fail to predict self-interpretation while a global self-worth score predicts it as well or better (F1).
2. **Belief refuter.** No learnable `w` maps inherited character priors plus private behavioral history onto the person's interpretation; the two make no distinguishable contribution (F2).
3. **Bond/refusal refuter.** Interpretation is invariant under changes of bond type and under removal of the refusal vocabulary; the act is a state and never becomes a route or a refused attribution (F3, F4).
4. **Valence refuter.** A character idea has the same effect across all measured residuals and goals, with no interaction (F5).
5. **Union refuter.** One clean condition accounts for both licensing and consistency findings, with no subset in which each rule keeps working (F7).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until a ground reading exists, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1, F2, F3 and F4 as a four-part shape, each with its refuter, and with F8's gap stated in the same breath.
2. Describe the residual first (Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*), then let a reader pick a frame. Do not open with "moral identity theory explains...".
3. Split the licensing question into conditions before searching (F7). A single pooled number is a budget filter, not a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
4. Bring a characterised residual to the venues in `03`, never the framework interior (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
5. Record the reader's verdict on stage 1 before showing the figures. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
6. Declare the stake. An interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*), and this derivation is authored inside the framework it tests.
