# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used anywhere in this package.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four parts of the shape and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly and the gap is declared. A two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 cannot survive.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about moral character and self-interpretation

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (stated in effect terms).** A person acts, then interprets their own act. They hold two things before the act: an idea about what kind of person they are (character), and a metric for what counts as better or worse (moral worth). The residual is the gap between what that self-theory predicted and what the person actually did. The question is how the character and worth ideas change the reading of that gap.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures give a four-part shape:
>
> 1. The gap is indexed by slot, not by a global score. An act is read as a local deviation on a specific self-slot, and a global "how moral am I" average can hide one live slot entirely.
> 2. The character ideas are an inherited prior mixed with the person's own behavioral history by a learnable weighting. They enter before the act, so they are part of the prediction, not a caption added afterwards.
> 3. The vocabulary supplies the bonds that turn an isolated act into a route (disposition to act to self to worth). Without those bonds a state can be named but a route cannot. The same act under a different bond is a different interpretation, not a different wording of one.
> 4. The worth ideas can refuse a self-attribution rather than re-describe the act ("that was not the kind of thing I do"). That is a negation, which the figure set treats as a separate operator, not a bond. It removes the act's standing as evidence about the self while leaving the act in the record.
>
> Each part has a nameable ground: a per-slot residual profile, a learnable weighting, a bond-sensitive interpretation, and a refusal-sensitive attribution. This is a shape, not a measurement, and I have not taken a ground reading.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** No specific character trait is named by the framework. No moral-identity scale is the mechanism. I am not claiming a pooled effect size settles anything, and I am not claiming that moral self-concept always licenses bad behavior or always produces consistent good behavior. The literature conflicts on exactly that point, and the figures predict a split into conditions rather than one effect. The specific magnitudes are a gap at this budget.
>
> **What would refute it.** These were written before this message:
>
> 1. A global self-worth score predicts self-interpretation as well as the per-slot profile.
> 2. No learnable combination of inherited character priors and personal history predicts the interpretation.
> 3. Interpretation is invariant under changes of bond type and under removal of the refusal vocabulary.
> 4. A character idea has the same effect across all measured residuals and goals, with no interaction.
> 5. One condition accounts for both the licensing and the consistency findings.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the affected part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1, before any theory:** what would you expect to see if this shape were true, and what single result would make you discard it?
>
> Please record that answer before reading anything further. Once you have the framework's interior, your agreement stops being an independent check.
>
> **If it survives, and only then,** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority:
>
> - Figures text with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you,
> [name]
