## The residual in effect terms

A person acts (the observation `o'`, their own behavior). They also hold a self-theory: a belief `b` about what kind of person they are (character) and a metric against which acts and persons are valued (moral worth). The residual `R` is the gap between what that self-theory predicted (`Γ(b,a)`) and what the person actually did and then has to explain. The question asks how the character and moral-worth ideas change that explanation. The options lead somewhere different:

- If those ideas are only post-hoc labels, then changing them changes vocabulary and nothing else, and self-interpretation is driven by the act plus situation.
- If they are part of the controller's library and weighting, then changing them changes which slot carries residual mass, which of the figure's routing bins the act falls into, and whether the self-theory is affirmed or refused. Self-interpretation is then a composition, not a caption.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a mechanism with four named parts, (1) slot indexing, (2) belief/weighting formation, (3) bond supply and routing, (4) valence relative to the current residual, with a ground that could settle each part. That is the shape this package derives and tests.

## Key verdict (one line)

The figures govern the **shape** of the mechanism (per-slot residual, inherited-prior mixing, bond-supplied routes, fifth-bin refusal, valence relative to the residual), triangulated across domains 1 and 2; the **specific empirical answer** is a `gap` at this budget because the world sources conflict in a way that a union re-census explains but does not settle.
