## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bonds compose an interpretation?

- Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* The bond carries information the operands do not.
- Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- The seven named bonds give at least three distinct interpretations of the same act and self-theory:
  - `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Operators table :29-31). The act is a step in a story about becoming: "I did X, therefore I am becoming Y."
  - `⋛` comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :47-50). The act is measured against the worth spectrum: "that was below the standard I hold."
  - `−` subtraction: *"A with B removed or absent"* (Operators table :41-45). The act is a shortfall: "I failed to do what I should have."
- And the eighth operator, `¬` negation, is not a bond (Operators table :70-74): *"Negation joins nothing, so its symmetry entry is empty rather than no"*. This is the refusal of the attribution, not a re-description of the act.
- **Result:** the same atoms under different bonds are different terms. Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* This is the structural reason two people can share an act, a character vocabulary and a worth spectrum and still arrive at incompatible interpretations.
- **Falsifier for Q1:** show that the bond type between act and self-theory carries no information beyond the two operands in a controlled test. If so, Q1 fails and Fig 12 :111 does not govern this domain.

### Q2: In what order do the operations run?

- Fig 3 :4-5 perception first: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before."* Here: the act must be perceived as a change relative to the self-theory before it can be routed.
- Fig 3 :6-7 vocabulary second: *"it has terms for what things afford"*. Here: the character vocabulary must name what the act affords for the self before a worth comparison can be made.
- Fig 3 :9-10 objective third: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms."* This is the un-instrumented middle link the main audit flagged. A person who cannot say what a good interpretation would be cannot be said to have a bad one.
- Formula step 2: refuse before mint. A held term whose residual *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* is the candidate for refusal. Applied here: if "I am the kind of person who does X" keeps producing residuals, the move owed first is to refuse that term, not to mint a new character explanation over it.
- **Result:** the order is perceive, name affordances, state the objective, refuse the persistently wrong self-term, then re-describe. An empirical test that presents the refusal vocabulary before the objective will measure compliance, not interpretation.
- **Falsifier for Q2:** show a reliable case in which re-description precedes and replaces refusal without a residual decrease, with no later re-opening. That would put the order in doubt.

### Q3: Is the licensing-versus-consistency conflict a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :4-5 *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54-56 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- The world results occupy different subsets: priming a moral self-image with concrete trait words gives licensing (Sachdeva et al., 2009); three replication attempts with the same manipulation give no effect (2014); focusing concretely on the virtuous act gives licensing while focusing abstractly on values gives consistency (Effron and Conway, 2015); prescriptive and proscriptive morality behave differently (Li et al., 2013). Each rule works on a subset. That is the Fig 9 signature of a **union**, not one noisy effect.
- **Result:** the compound has at least two conditions, roughly (a) concreteness of framing and (b) prescriptive versus proscriptive domain, with an intersection where both bind. A single "prime moral traits" lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
- **Falsifier for Q3:** exhibit a single operationalization that yields both licensing and consistency with no interaction and no surviving subset. Then the conflict is a failed replication, not a union, and this derivation is wrong.

### Q4: What analogue does Fig 12 supply for the two outcomes?

- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The `state → structure → state` self-interpretation core is a functional group. Licensing and consistency are two members of that group, differing in which qualifier (which bond and which worth framing) is attached.
- Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If licensing and consistency are written over the same atom set (act, self-term, worth spectrum, prior) with different bonds, they are **isomers** in the figure's exact sense, not two strengths of one variable. This is a stronger claim than the functional-group claim and is the one worth testing.
- The weaker form, if the two outcomes turn out to use different atoms (one requiring a public audience, say, and the other not), is the functional-group reading. Both are checkable; the isomer claim is falsified if either outcome requires an atom the other does not.
- **Falsifier for Q4:** show that licensing requires an atom (an audience, a public credential, a social comparison) that consistency does not, or the reverse. The isomer claim then fails and the functional-group claim stands.

## CONCLUSION

An interpretation is a bonded molecule over an act, a self-term and a worth spectrum. Sequence, comparison and subtraction give different routes; negation refuses the attribution rather than re-describing it. The operations run perceive, affordance, objective, refuse, re-describe, with refusal before mint. The licensing-consistency conflict is a union of at least two conditions, and the two outcomes are best read as isomers over a shared atom set, with the functional-group reading as the fallback if the atom sets differ.

This rescues what is defensible in the question without naming a mechanism: the structure and order are figure-derived; the specific construct is not.
