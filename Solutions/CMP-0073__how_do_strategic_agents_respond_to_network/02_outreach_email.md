**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the residual, the shape claim and the three refuters below are stated in your own words and checked against a ground you can name. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).

**Subject:** One prediction about interventions on networks, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have a specific, checkable output about how strategic agents respond to network interventions. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive a check against what you already know, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a network intervention works by making something consequential, a score, a threshold, a reward, a ranking or a norm. Agents optimise the quantity that is made consequential. They do not optimise the intervener's intended effect, because that effect is a preference held by the intervener, not a term held by the agents. So the response can be locally rational and still leave the agent's target untouched. The failure condition I am claiming is this: the response defeats the intended effect when the success measure is a quantity the agents produce or can move, that measure is used to decide an outcome, and the feedback loop created by the response has a positive sign. A network convention that only coordinates meaning is not the failure. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every intervention fails; that agents are irrational or malicious; that convergence or agreement is itself the failure; that any named platform, network, or field is the example. I am not claiming this is settled by my analysis. I am not asking you to trust the framework.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent the message:**
> 1. A case where a consequential, agent-producible measure moves under adversarial response but an independent ground measurement shows the intended outcome unchanged or improved.
> 2. A case where agents optimising their own payoff reduce the gap to the intended outcome on every tracked slot, at first and second order, measured against an independent ground.
> 3. A case where the intervention genuinely removes contact (excludes an actor from the habitat), the effect is stable without further work, and the response stays confined to direct neighbours.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture and citation list (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Canonical usage rule, figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the formula on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the architecture article warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage order, kept distinct

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
