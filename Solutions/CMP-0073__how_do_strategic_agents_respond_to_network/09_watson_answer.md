## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about an intervener who changes a network intending outcome E while strategic agents optimise their own payoffs. Option A: treat the clear metric as the verdict and conclude the intervention worked. Option B: treat the metric as a filter, require an unpushable ground, and state the condition under which the response defeats E. What each leads to: A can report success while the target slot stays live, because the metric is a quantity the agents also produce and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. B returns a shape, seven written falsifiers, and the instrument needed to close it. This is a union compound, not one hard question.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** |E - E'| after the strategic response. Arity: at least two conditions, intervention and response, plus an evaluator. Symmetry: the response is ordered after and because of the intervention; it is not a symmetric join. Scale: first order (direct re-optimisation), second order (agents responding to the response), third order (the uncensused cascade). Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Domain 1, the network habitat.** Actors: nodes, edges, seeds, added or removed links. Currency: contact and influence paths. Anchor: an outcome E measured by something the agents cannot move; nameable only if that measure is specified. Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **Domain 2, the consequential measure.** Actors: score, threshold, ranking, reward, norm. Currency: agent payoff. Anchor: the real outcome, distinct from the proxy; nameable if an unpushable measure is given. Boundary question: proxy versus ground.
- **Domain 3, the agents' own frames.** Actors: strategic agents, beliefs, utilities, strategies. Currency: payoff. Anchor: each agent's own utility, which settles its choice and does not change because the intervener thinks. Ordered response, not symmetric.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: evaluator, evidence, data sources. Currency: agreement versus ground contact. Anchor: an instrument or outcome not produced by the measured agents. Triangulation, never negotiation.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Domains 1 and 2 overlap in the same nodes and the same score, which are both target and author. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Name the ground first; then the consequential measure; then the agent response; then the cascade. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :40 (an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing) governs agent capability, not the intervention that changes contact; Fig 2 :41 (agreement efficient for a convention) governs meaning coordination, while Fig 2 :43 governs a convention made consequential. Both resolved by re-census, not by softening. See `01` section 6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape closes; no domain is named and nothing is grounded. Agents respond by re-optimising the consequential quantity inside their own closure, with valence relative to the intervention's residual; the response is a habitat answer, re-enters as input, and cascades beyond direct contact. The response defeats the intended effect when the success measure is agent-producible and consequential (the anchor updates, the convention decides an outcome, the frame scores itself), when the agents' own residual goes near zero while the target slot stays live, and when the feedback sign is positive. Convergence alone does not defeat an intervention: Fig 2 :41 protects conventions that only coordinate meaning, and Fig 2 :43 removes that protection when the convention decides an outcome. The compound is a union, so domain verdicts are not averaged. Seven falsifiers were registered before the run in `01` section 4. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** acting on the wrong proxy can make the system worse while the score improves, which is the Goodhart and specification-gaming signature. In figure terms the further cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a recording instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). Both costs are avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and by using a measure not generated by the measured agents.
- **undoable:** yes. The remedy does not require withdrawing any primitive. Bind the intended effect as an ordinary per-slot term before the run (Fig 5 :65-66), re-anchor the measure on data the agents do not generate, enumerate the habitat two steps out, check the loop sign, and keep cuts ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41 *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible."*). A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independent outcome measurement showing E reached while the proxy moved adversarially would fire F1; monotonic per-slot improvement would fire F2; a genuinely subtractive, stable intervention would fire F3; a response confined to direct contact would fire F4; a negative-sign loop with damped deviations would fire F5; an independent measurement showing E reached despite convergence would fire F6; one lever closing all four domains would fire F8. Until one of these fires the verdict stands as `candidate`.
