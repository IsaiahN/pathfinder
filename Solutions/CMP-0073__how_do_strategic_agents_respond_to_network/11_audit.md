## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is |E - E'| after the response. Arity: at least two conditions, intervention and response, plus an evaluator, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*), not one hard question. Symmetry: the response follows and depends on the intervention, so it is ordered; it is not a symmetric join. Scale: first order (re-optimisation of the consequential quantity), second order (agents responding to the response), third order (cascade out to where the residual actually matters).
- **Domain 1, the network habitat.** Actors: nodes, edges, seeds, added or removed links. Currency: contact and influence. Anchor: an outcome E measured by something agents cannot move. Question type: enumerate, never compose (Fig 11 :43).
- **Domain 2, the consequential measure.** Actors: score, threshold, ranking, reward, norm. Currency: agent payoff. Anchor: the real outcome, distinct from the proxy. Question type: a boundary between proxy and ground.
- **Domain 3, the agents' frames.** Actors: strategic agents, beliefs, utilities. Currency: payoff. Anchor: each agent's own utility, which is not the intervener's ground. Question type: ordered response inside a closure.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: evaluator, evidence, data sources. Currency: agreement versus ground contact. Anchor: an instrument not produced by the measured agents. Question type: triangulation.
- **Union, not partition.** Domains 1 and 2 overlap: the same node is target and author, the same score is measure and incentive. A single lever cannot clear the intersection. Verdicts stay separate.
- **Order by dependency.** Ground, then measure, then response, then cascade. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 2, Fig 1 and Fig 11, I derived from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 3's dependency order. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries, low overlap between the routes. That is weak but not circular agreement within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.
