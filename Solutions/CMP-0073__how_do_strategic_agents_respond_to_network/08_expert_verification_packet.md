# Expert verification packet: the Tether on strategic agents and network interventions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on strategic behavior in networks can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the conditional claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at any network is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Agents respond to a network intervention by re-optimising the quantity the intervention made consequential, inside their own closure, with valence relative to the intervention's residual. The response is a habitat answer, not an agent defect, and it re-enters as input to the next round. The response defeats the intended effect when three conditions hold together: the success measure is a quantity the agents produce or can move and is used to decide an outcome; the agents' own residual falls near zero while the target slot stays live; and the feedback sign is positive so deviations amplify and cascade beyond direct contact. Convergence alone does not defeat an intervention. A convention that only coordinates meaning is the product; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not a name or a verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 1
- **Statement:** a consequential measure that the measured agents can move stops being an anchor; the intervention then has no ground and its own score cannot witness success.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :43 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 1 :27 *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **What to check:** is the distinction between a coordinating convention and a consequential one (Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*) actually preserved in the framework, or does the same line govern both? If both, the "when" answer collapses.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an agent-producible, consequential proxy whose movement under adversarial response leaves the gap to an independently measured outcome unchanged or reduced. Then the anchor did not update in the claimed way.

### C2 - framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by a researcher in networks or policy
- **Statement:** a locally rational response can leave the intervener's target slot live while the aggregate looks clean, and the response cascades beyond direct contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :52-54 *"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** do the three world cases (Goodhart/Campbell measurement corruption, specification gaming in RL, free-riding under voluntary vaccination) actually show the per-slot gap surviving a clean aggregate, or only a different aggregate? Are they low-overlap enough to count as more than one witness?
- **Refuter:** measured agent optimisation that reduces the gap to E on every tracked slot at first and second order against an independent ground, or a response confined to direct neighbours with no third-order effect.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation
- **Statement:** the response is a cycle whose sign decides amplification or damping; a gaming strategy is a catalyst that survives its own use; convergence inside a room is variation, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 13 :32 *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*
- **What to check:** does the framework anywhere make the sign of a social feedback loop measurable from inside the loop, or is this a label applied afterwards? If it is only a label, C3 is `general`, not `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a network feedback loop with demonstrably negative sign under the response, with ground data showing deviations from E damped rather than amplified.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all network interventions fail, and not a named network, platform, or field as the culprit.
- Not that agents are irrational or malicious; they optimise their own payoff under a changed habitat.
- Not that convergence or agreement is itself the failure. Fig 2 :41 protects coordinating conventions.
- Not that the metric is the verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not a derivation of any specific field's result from the figures. World sources corroborate; they do not set the verdict.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement taken in this run.
