# Solution CMP-0073

**Question:** How do strategic agents respond to network interventions, and when does that response defeat the intervention's intended effect?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 90

**Section:** 8. Adaptation, learning, and evolution

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No measurement settles the defeat condition, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms:** an intervener changes a network intending outcome E; strategic agents optimise their own payoffs; the realised outcome E' differs from E. The residual is |E - E'| after the response. The work asks for the form of the response and the conditions under which it enlarges, rather than closes, the gap. What an answer would look like is bound before the run: it names the actors changed, the quantity optimised, the anchor that would settle E, the order of response after intervention, and a refuter that fires if the claimed defeat condition is absent.

**Key verdict (shape, not a name):** agents re-optimise the quantity the intervention made consequential, inside their own closure, with valence relative to the intervention's residual; the response re-enters as input and cascades beyond direct contact. The response defeats the intended effect when the success measure is agent-producible and consequential (the anchor updates, the convention decides an outcome, the frame scores itself), when the agents' own residual goes near zero while the target slot stays live, and when the feedback sign is positive. Convergence alone does not defeat an intervention: a convention that only coordinates meaning is not corrupted; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 2 :41 and :43). The compound is a union and must be unbundled. Per-domain verdicts are never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A list ranked by likely agreement is a list selected to agree.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The public opt-in routes in `03` avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
