## 0. The question in effect terms

> How do strategic agents respond to network interventions, and when does that response defeat the intervention's intended effect? (Source: `complexity science.txt` line 90, section 8. Adaptation, learning, and evolution.)

**Residual R, stated as an effect.** An intervener changes a network (adds or removes nodes or edges, seeds a cascade, sets a score, a reward, a ranking, or a norm) intending outcome E. Strategic agents observe or anticipate the change and optimise their own payoffs. The realised outcome is E'. The residual is the gap between E and E' after the response. The question asks for (a) the form of the agents' response and (b) the conditions under which that response enlarges rather than closes the gap.

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run.** An answer must state: the actors changed, the quantity they optimise, the anchor that would settle whether E was reached, the order in which response follows intervention, and a refuter that fires if the claimed defeat condition is absent. This is the Formula's "Direction" discipline, applied to the question itself: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

**Arity / symmetry / scale of R.** Arity: at least two conditions, the intervention and the response, plus the evaluator who judges E; Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Symmetry: the response is ordered after and because of the intervention, so it is an ordered relation, not a symmetric join; the intervention's goal is a preference held by the intervener, not a term held by the agents (Fig 5 :62). Scale: first order, a direct response to the changed measure; second order, the response of other agents to that response; and third order, the cascade that was not enumerated (Fig 11 :52-54).

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - The defeat condition is an anchor failure, not a quantity failure (decisive)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 2 :43:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

Fig 1 :27:

> **"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A network intervention typically works by making something consequential: a ranking, a threshold, a reward, a norm. Once agents can move that quantity, it stops being a measure of the intended effect and becomes another frame. The convention that was supposed to coordinate meaning now decides an outcome, so by Fig 2 :43 it has stopped being a convention. The intervention still reports success because the metric improved, which is exactly why the drift is coherent and unnoticed (Fig 2 :34). The CNA report on Goodhart's law states the same structure in world terms: when we reward a measure, agents manipulate it, with results that *"actually reduce the effectiveness of the measured system while paradoxically improving the measurement of system performance."*

**Mode: `specified`. Refuter:** show a consequential, agent-producible proxy whose movement under adversarial response leaves the gap to the intended outcome unchanged or reduced, with an independent ground measurement to prove it. Absent that measurement, the claim remains a candidate. A second refuter: show an intervention defeated even though its success measure is not agent-producible and is not used to decide any outcome; then F2 to F5 must carry the explanation and F1 is not sufficient.

### F2 - Agents optimise their own frame, not the intervener's direction

Fig 5 :62-64:

> **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."**
> **"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"**

Fig 1 :13, :19, :22:

> **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**
> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The intended effect E is a preference held by the intervener. Agents do not hold it as a term, so their own checks run on their own mechanism. A population can therefore show a near-zero aggregate residual, such as high average engagement, high average uptake, high average compliance, while the single slot the intervention cares about stays live. The aggregation is how the live signal disappears. This is why a strategic response can be locally rational and globally defeat the intervention without any agent doing anything irrational.

**Mode: `general` for the structural claim, `specified` when applied to a named network. Refuter:** demonstrate agent payoff optimisation that reduces the intervention residual monotonically on every slot, first and second order, measured against an independent ground. Then the direction limit is not doing the work here.

### F3 - The response is a habitat change, and introduction is asymmetric

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :61-64:

> **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
> **"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

A network intervention is an introduction: a seed, a new edge, a new reward, a new norm. It cannot subtract the agents' capacity to respond, because contact is a fact about the world. The same node is a benefactor to the metric and a malfactor to the intended outcome; there is no standing list of good nodes that survives a change of the residual. So the response is not a defect in the agents; it is the habitat answering the introduction. Treating the intervention as a solved state is the named error.

**Mode: `specified`. Refuter:** exhibit a network intervention that genuinely removes contact (excludes an actor from the habitat) and whose effect is stable without further work, or a response with no second-order contact change. Then the asymmetry and the permanent-work claim do not apply.

### F4 - Cascades outrun first-order enumeration

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :52-54:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 13 :29:

> **"a network with nothing at the nodes"**

A network is a structure whose nodes are states; a network with nothing at the nodes is empty. Scoping an intervention to direct neighbours enumerates the first step and misses the residual three steps out. Centola and Macy's result is the world-side instance: adding long ties, the obvious intervention for speeding diffusion, can *"preclude diffusion entirely"* for a complex contagion because a signal arriving across a long tie arrives without social reinforcement. The intervention aimed at reach defeated its own diffusion goal.

**Mode: `specified`. Refuter:** a measured case where the response stays confined to direct contact, with no second or third-order effect on E. Then first-order enumeration was adequate and this finding does not bite.

### F5 - The loop sign decides amplification or defeat

Fig 12 :121:

> **"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 13 :32:

> **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"**

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

The response re-enters as input: agents adapt to the adapted state of other agents, and a strategy that survives its own use (a catalyst) propagates. If the loop sign is positive, a small adaptation compounds; if negative, it self-corrects. A chain of agents all converging on the same behaviour is variation inside a room, not a verdict about E. Information cascades make the point in world terms: an agent acts *"without regard to his own information"* (Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch, 1992, abstract), so the public pool stops accumulating and the cascade can be wrong.

**Mode: `specified`. Refuter:** identify a network feedback loop whose sign is demonstrably negative under the response, so that deviations from E are damped rather than amplified, with ground data to show it. Then this loop does not defeat the intervention, though F1 still might.

### F6 - Agreement among agents cannot certify the intervention

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :42:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 7 :36:

> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Widespread adoption, high engagement, consent, or unanimous compliance are not verdicts. Agents who all read the same metric share an evidence pool, so their agreement is the pool talking. The verdict has to come from a measure neither the agents nor the intervener's score can move. Seat Map :163 puts it plainly: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

**Mode: `general` structural, `specified` for any named venue. Refuter:** produce an independent ground measurement, not produced by the measured agents, showing E was reached even though all agents converged on the same visible behaviour. Then convergence was harmless in that case.

### F7 - Order the work: ground, measure, response, cascade

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 8 :53:

> **"Residual first, frame second."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 5 :65-66:

> **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

Choosing the intervention before describing R in effect terms guarantees a fit: any frame picked will seem to fit. The intended effect must be bound as an ordinary term with a per-slot residual before the network is changed, so it can be refused by the same test as everything else. Formula step 7 keeps three questions distinct: SEARCHED (is the answer in here and unfound?), INWARD (sharpen the instrument), OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs). They are not interchangeable.

**Mode: `general` (method). Refuter:** an intervention derived from a well-described residual that still fails for the F1 to F5 reasons. Then ordering is necessary but not sufficient, and that must be recorded rather than hidden.

### F8 - This is a union compound, so verdicts stay separate

Fig 9 :54-55:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57-58:

> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

"How do agents respond" and "when does the response defeat the effect" are not one question. The first is a question about D3 and D1; the second is a question about D2 and D4. A single lever, such as changing the reward, clears the region where only D2 holds and leaves the intersection, where D2 and D3 both hold, standing. The residue is categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

**Mode: `general`. Refuter:** show that a single change closes R on all four domains with a common mechanism and a common ground. Then the union reading was wrong and a partition was correct.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is demonstrated against an independent ground:

1. A consequential, agent-producible proxy whose movement under adversarial response leaves the gap to E unchanged or reduced.
2. Agent payoff optimisation that reduces the intervention residual monotonically on every slot at first and second order.
3. An intervention that genuinely excludes an actor from the habitat, with a stable effect that needs no further work.
4. A measured response confined to direct contact, with no second or third-order effect on E.
5. A network feedback loop whose sign is demonstrably negative under the response, with ground data showing deviations damped.
6. A ground measurement showing E reached while all agents converged on the same visible behaviour.
7. A single change that closes R on all four domains with one common mechanism and one common ground.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the verdict is recalculated. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. This refuter was written before the recommendation and verdict below.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Bind the intended effect as an ordinary term with a per-slot residual before any network change is made (F7, Fig 5 :65-66). Do not let E remain a preference.
2. Pick the success measure from data the measured agents do not generate. The CNA report's first recommendation is *"Use measurement data not generated by the organization being measured."* This is Fig 2 :29-30 applied to a metric.
3. Enumerate the habitat two steps out, not just direct neighbours (F4). Fig 11 :43-44 is the instruction.
4. Treat the intervention as permanent work, not a solved state (F3). Budget for re-seeding and re-measuring.
5. Check the loop sign before seeding. If it is positive, a small adaptation will compound (F5).
6. Ask what would refute the claim before showing any framework or score (F6, F7). Record the answer before stage 2.
7. Keep every cut ranked and reversible: Fig 9 :41 *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible."*
8. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

## 7. Verdict of the audit

Mode: `specified` (conditional), with a `general` structural core. Not `grounded`.

The residual closes to a shape, not to a single measured case. Agents respond to a network intervention by re-optimising the quantity the intervention made consequential, inside their own closure, with valence relative to the intervention's residual; the response re-enters as input to the next round and cascades two or three steps out. The response defeats the intended effect when the success measure is agent-producible and consequential (the anchor updates, the convention decides an outcome, the frame scores itself), when the agents' own residual goes near zero while the target slot stays live, and when the feedback sign is positive. It is not defeated merely by convergence or agreement. The compound is a union and must be unbundled. Every claim is triangulated or single-source as marked in section 2; the two apparent strains are resolved by re-census; no line needed forcing. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* This search found lines, but it did not measure anything, and it says so.
