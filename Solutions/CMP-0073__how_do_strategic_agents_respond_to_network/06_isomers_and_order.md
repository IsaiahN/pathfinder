## RESIDUAL

The question supplies two things that need ordering before they can be answered: the response has an order (direct reaction to the intervention, then reaction to the reaction, then the uncensused cascade), and an intervention is a structure made of atoms (nodes, edges, seeds, scores) whose bond determines its effect. Two questions are owed: (1) what order is the response, and (2) can two interventions sharing all their components be inverses of one another under Fig 12's definition of isomer? A third follows: the network is a structure, so what composes two networks?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What order is the response?

- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* An intervention that only names a state (higher engagement, more edges) cannot name the route to it.
- Operators table: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* The intended effect must be stated as a comparison before the run, or it stays an invisible preference (Fig 5 :62).
- Fig 12 :121: *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A chain of interventions does not close; a cycle does. The first-order response is a chain step; the second-order response re-enters as input and becomes a cycle.
- Fig 13 :88: *"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."* This is why the second pass differs from the first and cannot be inferred from it.
- ⇒ The response is **at least second order**. First order: agents re-optimise the consequential quantity. Second order: other agents respond to that, and the loop sign decides amplification or damping. Fig 11 :52-54 puts the residual three steps out. **Mode `specified`, triangulated for the loop, single-source for the three-step claim.**

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a measured response is fully described by a one-pass map with no re-entry, and the outcome is unchanged after the second round, the second-order claim fails.

### Q2 - Are two interventions over the same components isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."*
- Consider two interventions over the same atom set `{nodes, edges, seed, score}`: (A) add a seed and a reward for adopting it, bonded `⇒` (production: the seed produces adoption that did not exist); (B) add the same seed and a reward for *appearing* to adopt it, bonded `⋛` (comparison of a threshold, where the threshold is the score). Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- Under A, the loop sign can be negative (adoption becomes real, the signal closes). Under B, the loop sign is positive (appearance is cheaper than adoption, the signal amplifies without the outcome). So they are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same ingredients, opposite behaviour, and the formula that lists only the ingredients cannot tell them apart.
- This is the structural reason why "we added the same incentive" is not an explanation of an outcome. The bond is the missing information. **Mode `specified`, triangulated for the isomer definition (Fig 12 plus Fig 8 :50-52 provenance), single-source for the bonding of the two example interventions.**

**Falsifier:** if a controlled comparison of the two bondings produces the same outcome distribution on an independent ground, the isomer claim fails and the two are the same substance under a different name.

### Q3 - What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55-58: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it becomes a specific compound.
- The four domains in `01` are a union: intervention and response overlap in the nodes and the score. The compound has its own loop and its own ground, so it is **second order relative to either condition alone**. This is not a ranking of importance; it is a dependency order.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Once a first-order response is settled against the ground, it becomes an atom for the second-order analysis. Until it is settled, composing over it is premature.

**Falsifier:** if a single lever closes the residual on all four domains with one common mechanism and one common ground, the union reading fails.

### Q4 - The catalyst and the cycle

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A strategy that propagates without being spent is a catalyst in this sense. A gaming strategy is exactly such a part: once found, it survives its own use and spreads.
- Fig 12 :121 again makes the sign decisive. A positive cycle with a catalyst closes the loop and amplifies; a negative cycle damps it.
- Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The selection between strategies is where the ground votes; the spread of a strategy inside a room is variation.

**Falsifier:** show a strategy that does not survive its own use (it is spent on adoption) and still produces the same amplification. Then the catalyst reading is not needed.

### Q5 - What composes two networks?

- Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* defines network as a structure.
- Fig 13 :75 (as carried in the exemplar's reading): two structures join only through a state. Read structurally here: two networks join through the nodes that belong to both, not network-to-network directly. A bridge between platforms is a composition through agents, and the agents' response is part of the bridge. This is a `specified`, single-source extrapolation from the substrate rule, not a measured claim about any platform.
- Fig 13 :29: *"a network with nothing at the nodes"* is empty. A structural intervention that ignores the nodes composes nothing.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a network-to-network composition that does not pass through shared states and that still changes the outcome on an independent ground. Then the substrate rule does not govern this domain and the extrapolation is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The response is at least second order: a direct re-optimisation, then a re-entry whose loop sign decides amplification or damping, with the residual potentially three steps out. Two interventions sharing all components can be isomers, same atoms different bond, opposite behaviour, which is why listing the components cannot explain an outcome. The compound is a union, second order relative to either condition alone, and must be unbundled before searching. A gaming strategy is a catalyst: it survives its own use. Two networks compose through their nodes, not directly. All claims are `specified` and ungrounded. The falsifiers above were written before the run and are recorded in `01` section 4 as well.
