# The Tether's answer: strategic agents and network interventions

**Mode: `specified` on the conditional claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An intervener changes a network (nodes, edges, seeds, scores, rewards, norms) intending outcome E. Strategic agents optimise their own payoffs. The realised outcome E' differs from E. The residual is |E - E'| after the response. The question asks for the form of the response and the conditions under which it enlarges rather than closes the gap. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It has its own loop and its own ground, and its domain verdicts are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Agents respond to what the intervention made consequential, not to the intervener's goal.** The goal is a preference held by the intervener. Fig 5 :62: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."* Their checks run on their own mechanism, so a response can be locally rational and globally defeat E. The Lucas critique states the same structure in economics: a change in policy systematically alters the decision rules the evaluation assumed.

2. **The response is a habitat answer, not an agent defect.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* An intervention introduces; it cannot subtract the agents' capacity to respond. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same node is a benefactor to the score and a malfactor to the goal. There is no standing list of good nodes that survives a change of residual.

3. **The defeat condition is an anchor failure.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* When the success measure is a quantity the agents produce or can move, and that measure decides an outcome, the anchor updates and becomes another frame. Fig 2 :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* This is the Goodhart, Campbell and specification-gaming collapse: the metric improves while the system it was supposed to measure degrades.

4. **A clean aggregate is compatible with a live target slot.** Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The population can look compliant while the one outcome the intervention cared about remains unachieved.

5. **The loop sign decides amplification or damping, and the cascade runs beyond first contact.** Fig 12 :121: *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 11 :52-54: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A gaming strategy is a catalyst (Fig 12 :119): it survives its own use and propagates. Information cascades show agents following predecessors against private information, so public information stops accumulating and the mass behaviour can be wrong.

6. **Convergence is not defeat, and agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* A norm that only coordinates meaning is fine. The failure begins when the convention decides an outcome (Fig 2 :43). Even then, agent agreement is variation, not a verdict: Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

7. **The remedy is to bind the effect, re-anchor, enumerate outward, and keep the work permanent.** Bind the intended effect as an ordinary term with a per-slot residual before the run (Fig 5 :65-66). Use a measure not generated by the agents being measured. Enumerate the habitat two steps out (Fig 11 :43-44). Check the loop sign before seeding. Keep cuts ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41). Budget for re-seeding: *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"* (Fig 11 :63-64).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that every intervention fails, or that any named network, platform or field is the example.
- Not that agents are irrational or malicious. They are optimising their own payoff under a changed habitat.
- Not that convergence or agreement is itself the failure. A convention that only fixes shared meaning is the product, not the corruption.
- Not that the metric is irrelevant. It is a filter for the budget; it must not be the witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not grounded. No measurement at any network was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does the clean metric rise while an independently measured outcome stays flat or falls? That would fire F1.
- Does agent payoff optimisation reduce the intervention residual on every slot, first and second order, against an independent ground? That would fire F2.
- Does the intervention genuinely remove contact and hold its effect without further work? That would fire F3.
- Does the response stay confined to direct neighbours, with no second or third-order effect? That would fire F4.
- Is the feedback sign demonstrably negative, with ground data showing deviations damped? That would fire F5.
- Does an independent measurement show E reached while agents converged on the same visible behaviour? That would fire F6.
- Does one lever close all four domains with one common mechanism and ground? That would fire F8.

The standing falsifiers are written before the run in `01` section 4. Each must be evaluated against a measure the measured agents cannot move.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is a condition, not a name. Naming a particular intervention as the defeater would require a provenanced import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), or a ground measurement. Neither exists here. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops at the limit of the instruments available to this simulated derivation and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that network interventions always fail, and it does not name a culprit. It says that agents respond to the quantity an intervention makes consequential, not to the intervener's goal, and that this response is a property of the agent-and-habitat rather than a defect in the agent. The response defeats the intended effect when the success measure is one the agents produce or can move and is used to decide an outcome, so the anchor updates and a convention stops being a convention; when the agents' own residual falls to near zero while the target slot stays live; and when the feedback loop they create has a positive sign and cascades beyond direct contact. Convergence alone is not the failure. What the framework offers is the shape of that condition, falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close it. The center stays open until an independent ground settles it.
