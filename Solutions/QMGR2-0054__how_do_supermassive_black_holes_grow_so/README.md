# Solution QMGR2-0054: figure-grounded package

**Question:** How do supermassive black holes grow so quickly in the early universe?

**Source:** `Questions/QM & GR Questions.txt` line 209 (astronomy and cosmology block, question 54).

**Section:** F. Galaxies and cosmic structure.

**Field for public contact points:** supermassive black hole growth research (early black hole seeds, accretion physics, high-redshift quasar observations).

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain structural claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on which channel dominated in nature. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file states that it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry pipeline, not by this folder).

## Restating the question as an effect

Not "what is the correct growth mechanism" (that asks for a name the framework is not licensed to give), but: what is the gap between the measured mass of a high-redshift quasar and the mass a candidate channel predicts in the time available, per object, and what would have to be imported or re-bonded to close it? What an answer would look like is bound as an ordinary term: a growth channel is a cycle with a sign, a ceiling, an anchor and a carrier, and each of those is checkable. That binding is written down here before derivation so the preference is refusable (formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :65).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, a room does not upgrade a label: adding agreeable readers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor (Fig 2 :29). Second, selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40). Third, showing a recipient the full interiors before asking for a verdict spends the only thing they contribute, since a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).
