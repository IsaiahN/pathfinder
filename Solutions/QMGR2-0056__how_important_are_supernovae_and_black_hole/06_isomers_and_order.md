## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."**
- As the literature writes them, SN feedback and AGN feedback share few atoms: massive stars and core collapse versus accretion onto a supermassive black hole. They are therefore **not isomers** as usually described. They are two members of the same functional group, "a channel that ejects gas", where the group fixes the kind and the qualifier fixes the member.
- The stronger form: if both are written over the full shared atom set {gas, energy injection, momentum coupling, gravitational potential, metals, time}, differing only in which structure mediates which state transition, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either channel can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, AGN outflow with no metal enrichment coupling, or SN outflow with no gravitational potential term), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. Single-source on the figure definition, triangulated with the two-currency rule in Fig 12 :126.

### Q2 - What order does the dependency chain take?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- The chain for this question reads: perceive gas (D4) → have terms for outflow and mass-loading (vocabulary) → say what counts as "important" (objective) → choose the discriminating observation (planning) → verify and carry (learn and carry).
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The attribution link is the middle link here, and Fig 3 :25-26 says exactly why it attracts the least attention: it has neither the obvious failures nor the obvious tests of perception and planning.
- The world literature confirms the order is usually truncated. Most outflow studies establish perception (an outflow exists) and vocabulary (mass loading) but stop before the objective (what would count as attributable importance). Buchner (2024) is an exception that names the objective. **Mode `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 3 structural order plus the literature's own admission).
- **Falsifier:** a study that measures channel attribution while skipping the perception and vocabulary links (it cannot define its own observable).

### Q3 - What is the union, and why is one lever insufficient?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- SN and AGN overlap: SN injects metals that change cooling, cooling changes the inflow that feeds the SMBH, AGN entrains the SN fountain, and fountain gas falls back (Biernacki and Teyssier 2018, sections 4.2 and 5.2). A lever that removes AGN clears the AGN-only region but leaves the coupled SN plus AGN intersection standing.
- Ecology provides a low-overlap cross-check: Crain et al. (2008) found *"cumulative effects in individual studies were additive (26%), synergistic (36%), and antagonistic (38%)"*, and Birk et al. (2020) note the combined effect is frequently more or less than expected from individual effects. The union structure is not a physics-only artifact. **Triangulated** (Fig 9 plus ecology), `specified` for galaxies.
- **Falsifier:** a dataset in which removal of one channel reduces total gas removal by exactly its independent contribution, with zero interaction residual, across all halo masses.

Their comparison (`⋛`) is then a second-order composition: (settled SN molecule) compared with (settled AGN molecule), and the comparison is a structural relation at a threshold, not a new feedback mechanism.

## CONCLUSION

The two channels are **not isomers as the literature writes them**; they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124). Over the full shared atom set the stronger isomer reading becomes a real, checkable claim with a stated falsifier. Their dependency order is the standard chain of Fig 3, truncated in practice before the objective link, which is why attribution stays open. Their union is a compound with its own loop, and the comparison of the two settled channels is second-order rather than a new primitive. This rescues what is defensible in "feedback" without claiming a single dominant mechanism. The `⋛` operand type remains an open item.
