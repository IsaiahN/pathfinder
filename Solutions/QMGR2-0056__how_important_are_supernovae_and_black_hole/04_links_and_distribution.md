## Three things in the literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Attribution is the open problem, not detectability.** Buchner (2024) frames the three outflow channels (star formation, luminous AGN, radio jets) and asks their *"absolute and relative importance"*, while stating it is *"observationally difficult to distinguish the proposed feedback mechanisms and establishing causality for most systems."* This is the metameric reading of Fig 6 :32-35: the outflow is detected, the source is not discriminated. Use this to motivate the gap, not to claim the framework produced it.
2. **Non-additivity is the field's own finding.** Biernacki and Teyssier (2018) report *"a non-linear coupling"* when SN and AGN act together; Crain et al. (2008) and Birk et al. (2020) independently find combined effects frequently non-additive. These are low-overlap witnesses for Fig 9 :55, *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* They corroborate the shape; they do not decide it.
3. **The ranking flips with the slot.** The MDPI review assigns the flat low-mass luminosity end to stellar feedback and the high-mass end to AGN feedback; Heckman and Best (2014) say strong AGN feedback is seen only in the most powerful AGNs. This is world corroboration of Fig 11 :49, *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
