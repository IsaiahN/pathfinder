**Subject:** Check one feedback claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about galactic feedback that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how important are supernovae and black-hole activity in pushing gas out of galaxies" is not one question with one answer, and it is not a matter of weighting two independent causes. In effect terms, the gap between what a galaxy's gas model predicts and what is observed is indexed per galaxy, not averaged (Fig 1 :19). The two channels, supernovae and AGN activity, are a union of overlapping conditions: a single lever that removes one channel leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55). And an actor's importance is relative to the residual, not a property it carries (Fig 11 :49): the same supernovae that clear gas from a dwarf cannot do so in a massive halo, where AGN feedback takes over. So the shape I am offering is a per-galaxy attribution with a non-additive overlap and a valence that flips with the halo, not a cosmic percentage.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that supernovae are always dominant, or that AGN feedback always wins, or that either is unimportant. I am not claiming the framework returns a number such as "60 percent supernovae". It returns a shape, not a name, and the numerical attribution is a declared gap: an instrument that would discriminate the source of an observed outflow is not yet held. Observationally distinguishing the mechanisms and establishing causality is difficult (Buchner 2024), and in systems where star formation and an AGN coexist it is not clear from observations whether one or both drive the outflow (Melioli and de Gouveia Dal Pino 2015). Those are field sources, offered as corroboration, not as the authority.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single importance coefficient that predicts gas removal after galaxies are stratified by halo mass, redshift and gas geometry;
> 2. one channel alone accounting for all gas removal across all slots with no interaction term;
> 3. an invariant channel ranking across a documented change of halo mass or gas geometry;
> 4. a direct-contact-only census predicting gas removal as well as a cascaded enumeration of the circumgalactic medium;
> 5. a single observable that uniquely assigns an outflow to supernovae or to the black hole across halo masses, with the discriminating resolution demonstrated.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract or revise it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
