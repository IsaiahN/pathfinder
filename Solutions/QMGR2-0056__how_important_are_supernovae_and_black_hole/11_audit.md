## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's shape to a one-sentence abstraction. Result: *feedback importance is a relational property of channel and galaxy, so it must be measured per galaxy and the channels must not be added.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that abstraction into a specific observing situation. Result: *measure outflows in more galaxies and compare their mass-loading factors.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The gap is what the abstraction cannot hold:

1. The one-sentence form collapses the union into "do not add", which a reader hears as "just measure each separately". Coming back down, it tells an observer to collect two independent mass-loading columns, which loses Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Effort lands above the break (Fig 3 :29-30).
2. It loses the anchor rule. "Relational property" does not say that the gas reservoir, not the outflow reading, settles the question (Fig 2 :29, :35). Coming back down, it permits a frame to measure success with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :27).
3. It loses the valence flip. "Measure per galaxy" has no place for the possibility that the same channel changes sign when the residual changes (Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 12 :119).
4. It loses the source-discriminator requirement. The abstraction cannot say what instrument would distinguish SN from AGN, which is the actual `gap` in the answer. It therefore cannot distinguish "unreached at this budget" from "underivable in principle" (Fig 8 :40).

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). It is also why the outreach packet sends the generator, not the one-sentence version (Fig 4 :25). The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
