## The question restated as an effect

"Systems" is read broadly: any bounded frame with a library, so a person, an institution, a model, or a team (Symbols table :111). The question is not about how much a system knows about a culture. It is about what happens to the residual on each side of an interaction.

Let the system's belief about the other be `b`, its action in the interaction be `a`, its prediction be `Γ(b,a)`, and what actually happens for the other person be `o'`. Then `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` is measured per person and per occasion, never averaged into a cultural mean (Fig 1 :19).

- Option A, "respect is achieved by a shared model of the culture": leads to the prediction that once both sides use the same vocabulary, the interaction is respectful. In the figures this is the mutual-update and common-cause collapse (Fig 2), a final room (Fig 7), and a convention that has decided an outcome (Fig 10).
- Option B, "respect is triangulated against what the interaction actually does to the people in it": leads to the prediction that each frame ranges against a legitimate shared ground separately, that the channel to that ground must be maintained, and that a shared vocabulary is a convention (the product) rather than evidence that the verdict was right.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape naming the two conditions that must both hold for an interaction to remain respectful (per-slot reading plus independent ranging against a legitimate ground, with a maintained channel), plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one condition from a failure of the other. Until that shape is bound it is a preference, predicts nothing, and no internal check of the system can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The specific content of respect in any particular culture is declared a `gap`, not filled with a name.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles what respect is in any culture, and no claim that the framework is the only account of cross-cultural interaction.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for agreement is one witness wearing many names. The other half is recorded separately: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41).
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
