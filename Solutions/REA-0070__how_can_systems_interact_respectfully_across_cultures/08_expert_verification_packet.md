# Expert verification packet - the Tether on respectful cross-cultural interaction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural researcher or communication scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of an actual interaction is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A system interacts respectfully across cultures only while two conditions both hold: (C1) each frame is corrected separately by a ground that does not move when either frame pushes on it, and (C2) the channel to that ground is maintained per person and per occasion, with readings attributed rather than pooled into a cultural average. Because the question is constitutively about people, the right kind of ground is the lived outcome for the people involved, including their own account. A shared working vocabulary is a convention about meaning and is the product; using it as evidence that the interaction was respectful turns a convention into a verdict. The framework does not name what respect is in a particular culture. It returns a shape, an instrument still owed, and refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and conceptual; checkable by reading Fig 2

- **Statement:** alignment here is triangulation against a shared, non-updating ground, not negotiation between frames. A shared model reached by convergence is a convention, not a verdict about a person.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 - **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :45 - **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Fig 2 :37 - **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** Fig 2 :41 - **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**; Fig 10 :47 - **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**.
- **What to check:** is the union reading right, that is, are C1 and C2 two conditions that must both hold rather than one dimension? Does the valence distinction between a convention and a verdict hold in the reader's own cases?
- **Refuter:** two frames sharing one evidence pool and the same categories register a fresh per-person residual against a fixed anchor without importing anything (breaks the mirror reading); or a convention about shared meaning is also the ground's verdict on a person's experience, with no separate reading of that experience (breaks the valence split).

### C2 - per-slot indexing; checkable against any cross-cultural dataset or instrument

- **Statement:** the residual must be indexed per person and per occasion. A cultural average applied to an individual destroys the signal that says where to look.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 - **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 10 :26 - **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**.
- **What to check:** in the reader's own data, does a group-level parameter leave the per-person residual unchanged? Does it still predict each person's own account of whether the interaction respected what mattered? If yes, the aggregation claim fails.
- **Refuter:** a pooled group-level parameter that preserves every per-slot residual and still predicts each individual's own account. Also relevant: the stereotype-threat literature reports that a group cue changes an individual's performance even when the individual does not endorse it, which supports the harm mechanism but is not a measurement of respect.

### C3 - contact and order; checkable against field practice

- **Statement:** the other's closure cannot be derived from the system's own library; contact with a differing closure is required, and the description must travel in effect terms. The link that usually breaks is between having terms for a situation and being able to say what would count as progress in it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 6 :30 - **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :27-28 - **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Fig 3 :25-26 - **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
- **What to check:** in the reader's field, do practitioners actually have an instrument for "what would count as respect here", or do they stop at a vocabulary of traits? Is the source of each reading recorded?
- **Refuter:** a specific other's expectations derived entirely from the system's own closure with no contact, with the per-slot residual closing; or an instrument that reads the objective per slot, is used by someone who can act on it, and leaves the residual flat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal respect metric, a final etiquette, or a correct cultural model to install. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*.
- Not that a shared vocabulary proves respect; that is a convention deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that a group average, a trait list, or a national dimension score can stand in for a person.
- Not that the framework names what respect is in any particular culture. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. The slot is a declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of an actual interaction is asserted here.
- Not that the framework is the only account of cross-cultural interaction; no uniqueness claim is made.
- The author of this derivation has an interest in a favourable reading. That stake is declared here, per Seat Map :171: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Specifically: an instrument, used in an actual interaction, that registers the other's own account of what mattered and discriminates that from the system's self-report. The world literature states the same requirement in different words: Boas in *Science*, *"the object of study is the individual, not abstractions from the individual under observation. We have to study each ethnological specimen individually in its history and in its medium."*

The six reversal conditions are listed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4. Any one firing moves the claim to refuted; none has fired in this simulation.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :35, :37, :40, :41 and :45, Fig 6 :23, Fig 9 :27-28, and Fig 10 :47. All are short passages.
2. Check C1: in one case you know well, did agreement about a shared vocabulary coincide with a separate reading of what happened to the people? If not, the valence split is wrong.
3. Check C2: in one dataset you know, does a group average preserve the per-person residual and predict the person's own account? If yes, the aggregation claim fails.
4. Check C3: name one instrument in your field that measures what would count as respect per person, and say who reads it. If you cannot, the middle link is indeed un-instrumented.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement from a reader who has now read this packet is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
