# The Tether's answer on respectful cross-cultural interaction

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is not "how much does the system know about other cultures" but "what happens to the per-person prediction error when two frames interact". `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`, indexed by person and occasion, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The two named failures both destroy that signal: flattening hides it in agreement between frames that share an evidence pool; stereotyping hides it in a group average applied to an individual slot.

The problem is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55-57). Two conditions must both hold, and a single lever clears only the region where one holds:

- C1: each frame ranges independently against a legitimate shared ground.
- C2: the channel to that ground is maintained, per person, provenanced, not pooled or stale.

## 2. The shape

1. **Triangulate, do not negotiate.** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** A shared model reached by convergence is a convention; it is not evidence that the interaction was respectful.

2. **The ground is legitimate because the question is about people.** Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** Concretely: the lived consequence for the people in the interaction, including their own account. Fig 2 :37-38 adds the caveat that this judgment is an instrument on axes it does not measure, so one self-report is not the whole ground.

3. **Keep the convention and the verdict in separate columns.** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**; Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**; Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. Building a shared working vocabulary is the product. Using it as proof is the collapse.

4. **Index the residual per person.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Stereotypes are aggregation plus *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"* (Fig 10 :26).

5. **Contact with a differing closure is required.** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Whatever crosses is cast in effect terms (Fig 6 :30), with source provenance and a shadow test, debited against independence. No amount of introspection on the system's own library yields the other's expectations.

6. **Describe before you choose a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Ask what the other would expect to see if this went well, in terms anyone could check, before selecting an etiquette model.

7. **Bind the criterion before the run.** Fig 5 :65-66: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."** The four refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are that binding, written down before outreach.

8. **Attribute capability to the pair, not the system.** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Respect is maintained contact, not a solved property of a model.

9. **No final shared model.** Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*. Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** The target is a maintained practice, not a universal etiquette.

10. **The content of respect is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. The missing thing is characterized in effect terms: an instrument that registers the other's own account of whether what mattered was respected, and discriminates it from the system's self-report. It is not named here.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that there is a universal respect metric, a final etiquette, or a correct cultural model to install. That is the last-room error.
- Not that a shared vocabulary proves respect. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.
- Not that a group average, a trait list, or a national dimension score can stand in for a person. Those are aggregations and flat formulas.
- Not that this derivation is `grounded`, and not that it can certify any actual interaction as respectful. The human judgment anchor would have to do that work, in the situation.
- Not that the framework is the only account of cross-cultural interaction. It makes no uniqueness claim over all frameworks.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would reverse it

**What would settle the open slot:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument, in the actual situation, that registers the other's own account of what mattered and discriminates it from the system's self-report. Boas's requirement in the world literature is the same in effect: study the individual *"in its history and in its medium"*, not an abstraction from the individual.

**What would reverse the shape**, from the pre-registered list in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4:

1. a system-only change that improves ground-verified outcomes with no change in contact or habitat;
2. two frames sharing one evidence pool registering a fresh per-slot failure without importing anything;
3. a pooled group average preserving every per-slot residual and still predicting each person's own account;
4. a specific other's expectations derived entirely from the system's own closure;
5. a convention about shared meaning that is also the ground's verdict on a person's experience;
6. the other's own account, given before any theory, that the interaction failed to register what mattered, with the per-slot residual not shrinking over repeated interactions.

## 5. The honest stop

The shape stops where the instruments stop. The framework returns *"a shape, not a name"*, records the lineage, and reports `gap` where it cannot name. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A system that appears respectful because both sides use the same words may be below the observer's distinction horizon; that is a report about the observer, not a verdict about the person. This is why the answer keeps the slot open and sends the refuter ahead of the theory.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell a system what respect is in a given culture, and it does not offer a universal etiquette. It says that an interaction stays respectful only while each side is corrected by a ground that neither side can move, and while the reading of the other person stays per person and per occasion instead of being pooled into a cultural average. Because the question is about people, the right kind of ground is their lived experience and their own account of what mattered. A shared vocabulary is useful, but it is a convention about meaning, not proof about a person; treating it as proof turns a convention into a verdict. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the description of an instrument still owed. The slot for the specific content of respect stays open until that ground settles it.
