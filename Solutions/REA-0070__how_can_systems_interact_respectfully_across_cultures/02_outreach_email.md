# Outreach draft - validate the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape below survives the reader's own check. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about cross-cultural interaction before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a system interacts respectfully across cultures only when two conditions both hold. First, each side is corrected separately by a ground that does not move when either side pushes on it. Because the question is about people, the right kind of ground here is the lived outcome for the people in the interaction, including their own account of what mattered. Second, the channel to that ground stays per person and per occasion, with the source of each reading recorded, rather than being pooled into a cultural average.
>
> What this rules out is the two familiar shortcuts. A shared model of "the culture" is a convention about shared meaning, which is useful, but using it as proof that the interaction was respectful turns a convention into a verdict. A cultural average applied to an individual is a pooled reading; it buries the per-person signal that would have shown where to look.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names what respect is in any particular culture. It returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that any team, model or institution is respectful because it uses these terms, and I am not claiming this is the only account of cross-cultural interaction.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written down before this email:**
> 1. a system-only change that improves ground-verified outcomes of cross-cultural interactions with no change in contact or in the people involved;
> 2. two frames sharing one evidence pool that still register a fresh per-person failure against a fixed ground without importing anything;
> 3. a group-level average that leaves every per-person signal unchanged and still predicts each person's own account of being respected;
> 4. a convention about shared meaning that is also the ground's verdict on a person's experience, with no separate reading of that experience.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I can send the source links then.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** state your own ground and write your own refuter before you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, with the practical boundary

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if they are curious, send the three source links and note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person states their own ground and writes their own refuter first.

Later agreement from a stage-2 reader is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). This is one-to-one contact. Nothing is sent by this agent; the sending is yours to do.
