## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks how a system can interact respectfully across cultures. Put as an effect, not a virtue: a system holds a belief `b` about another frame, acts `a`, predicts `Γ(b,a)` about what the other will experience, and observes `o'`. The residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`, indexed per person and per occasion (Fig 1 :19).

Two named failures are two different ways of destroying the signal:

- **Flattening differences** is treating the other frame as the same closure as the system's own. In figure terms it is the mutual-update or common-cause collapse: the two frames converge on whoever had the most influence, or agree without talking because they share one evidence pool (Fig 2 panels 1 and 2). At the limit it is a mirror chain, *"same closure, handed along;"* *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :12-13).
- **Relying on stereotypes** is applying a pooled, saturated, low-resolution reading to an individual slot: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"* (Fig 10 :26), and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22). It is a recording carried as knowledge: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* (Fig 4 :25).

So the residual is not "the system lacks cultural knowledge." It is: the system's per-slot prediction of the other's experience fails, and the two named response strategies each hide that failure in a different way. Flattening hides it in agreement; stereotyping hides it in the average.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): the shape of an interaction in which (C1) each frame ranges independently against a legitimate shared ground and (C2) the channel to that ground stays per-slot, provenanced and reachable, plus an instrument that would show which condition failed. This shape is written down here before the WATSON run so it can accrue a residual like any other term (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** a three-place contact relation, at minimum (system frame, other frame, the shared situation they are both in contact with). It is not a two-place "self and other" problem, because without a third term that does not update on either frame there is no anchor and the two frames can only drift or mirror (Fig 2 panels 1 to 3).
- **Symmetry:** the relation "closure differs" is symmetric, but the checking relation is not. Each frame is corrected by the anchor separately; the anchor is *"not a third party to the disagreement"* (Fig 2 :27, :46). So the bond is not a symmetric identity `≡` (flattening) and not a symmetric conjunction `+` (blend). It is triangulation.
- **Scale:** single interaction (one loop), repeated interaction (a chain of rooms), and a community or institution (membrane between sizes). The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30).

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 System frame | the system's library Γ, belief b, weighting w (private history vs inherited prior), action a | per-slot residual reduction | its own prediction against `o'` | yes, provisionally, but it is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37-38) |
| D2 Other frame | the other person or culture, their vocabulary, expectations, own account | their own ranging against the ground | the lived consequence of the interaction for them | yes |
| D3 Shared ground and habitat | the situation, conditions, history, relations, the task, the people affected | what settles, the lived outcome, the thing that pays | the ground, which does not update | yes, and this is the legitimate anchor because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :37) |
| D4 Interaction channel | percepts, signals, the bracket across handoffs, the metrics used to read the other | contact, fidelity, provenance | the ground, kept reachable by a maintainer seat | yes; the channel decays while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24) |
| X1 "A universal respect metric everyone should adopt" | a proposed final standard | compliance | none that does not update on the frames | **no nameable anchor, so not a domain yet** |
| X2 "The system's own self-consistency" | the system alone | coherence | none; a mirror cannot correct you | refused as an anchor (Fig 2 :35) |

### Partition or union

This is a **union compound**, not one hard question and not a partition. Two conditions must both hold:

- C1: each frame ranges against a legitimate shared ground independently (triangulation, not negotiation).
- C2: the channel to that ground is maintained, per-slot, provenanced, not pooled or stale.

A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55) and *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). *Adding diversity data* clears C2-shaped ground without C1; *agreeing on a shared etiquette* clears a convention without either. The residue is smaller and categorically different: a case in two conditions.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. perception: register the other's slot-specific response as it differs from what was there before (Fig 3 :4-5);
2. vocabulary: have terms for what the other's actions afford (Fig 3 :6-7);
3. objective: be able to say what would count as progress or respect here, in its own terms (Fig 3 :9-10);
4. planning: a route to that objective under incomplete information (Fig 3 :12-13);
5. learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next interaction (Fig 3 :14-15).

The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* (Fig 3 :25-26). Having a vocabulary of cultural traits is link 2. Saying what would count as respect in this specific interaction is link 3, and it is the one without an instrument.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Flattening is the mirror collapse, not a different preference (decisive for C1)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Reading: when the system absorbs the other into its own categories, the two frames converge and their agreement is the system's own vocabulary talking. Nothing has ranged against a ground. This is `specified` when applied to systems, `general` as a structural claim. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 7 room chain, low overlap between the alignment rule and the no-last-room rule).

**Refuter:** show a case in which two frames sharing the same evidence pool and the same categories nonetheless register a fresh, non-zero per-slot residual against a fixed anchor, without importing anything. That would break the mirror reading.

### F2 - Stereotypes are aggregation plus a pooled reading, a notation failure

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 10 :26: **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Reading: a stereotype is a group-level compression applied to an individual slot. It does not merely oversimplify; it destroys the only quantity that says where to look. This also covers the weighting `w`: a belief leaning too far on the inherited prior is a pooled reading of history. `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 per-slot rule and Fig 10 channel-decay rule).

**Refuter:** a pooled cultural parameter that leaves every per-slot residual unchanged and still predicts each individual's own account of whether the interaction respected what mattered.

### F3 - The other's closure cannot be derived from the system's own

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

Reading: no amount of introspection on the system's own frame yields the other's expectations. Contact with a differing closure is required. What crosses is characterized in effect terms, with a source gate (it is the other's own account) and a shadow test (does it explain a gap in the system's prediction?), debited against independence (Fig 6, source gate and shadow test). `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 6 and Fig 8, distinct roles).

**Refuter:** derive a specific other's expectations entirely from the system's library with no import and no contact, and show the derivation closes the per-slot residual.

### F4 - The legitimate anchor for "respectful" is human judgment, because the question is about people

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**

Reading: the ground for "was this interaction respectful" is the lived consequence for the people in it, including their own account, not the system's coherence and not agreement between frames. This is `specified` for the binding to this question, `general` for the anchor rule. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor definition plus the legitimacy clause).

**Narrowing, kept visible:** Fig 2 :37-38 continues that human judgment *"is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* So a single person's report is the right kind of anchor here but is still an instrument on axes it may not measure. This is not a licence to treat one self-report as the whole ground.

**Refuter:** a system-internal metric that changes its verdict when frames push on it, yet still settles a dispute about whether a person was respected.

### F5 - Shared meaning is a convention; a convention that decides the verdict has stopped being one

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Reading: building a shared working vocabulary with another culture is legitimate and is the product; the correlation is the point. But using that shared vocabulary as proof that the interaction was actually respectful converts a convention into a verdict. The two must be kept in separate columns. This is the central strain in the everyday use of "respect": it names both a shared meaning and a fact about a person's experience. `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 valence rule and Fig 10 convention rule).

**Refuter:** a case in which agreement about a shared meaning also constitutes the ground's verdict on a person's lived experience, with no separate reading of that experience.

### F6 - Describe the effect before choosing the frame

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Reading: before selecting an etiquette model or a cultural framework, ask the other what they would expect to see if this interaction went well, in their own terms, and write it as an effect anyone could check. This is why a list of national traits fails: it names the frame before it describes the residual. `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 6, different figures and different failure modes).

**Refuter:** a case in which a genuinely effect-term description still selects a frame that was chosen first, with the choice recorded before the description.

### F7 - Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run

Fig 5 :65-66: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

Reading: "respect" is a preference until it is written down as a checkable prediction. Section 0 does that with C1 and C2. The pre-registered refuter in §4 is the same discipline. `general` for the discipline, `specified` for the C1/C2 binding. **Status: single-source** for the figure line (Fig 5), corroborated by the Formula "Direction" paragraph, which is the same framework document, so not an independent witness.

**Refuter:** a pre-committed criterion shown to have been written after the outcome, or a criterion that cannot be shown to have been held before the interaction.

### F8 - The middle link is where this question usually breaks

Fig 3 :25-26: **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Reading: "having terms for cultural difference" is link 2. "Being able to say what would count as respect in this particular interaction" is link 3. Most cross-cultural training builds link 2 and assumes link 3, so effort lands above the break (Fig 3 :29-30). `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 3 and the seat-map failure genus "produced, recorded, and not read", Seat Map :271, distinct documents).

**Refuter:** a working instrument that measures, per slot, what would count as progress in a specific intercultural interaction, is read by someone positioned to act on it, and still leaves the per-slot residual flat.

### F9 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; respect is maintenance, not a solved state

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Reading: you cannot make a system respectful by tuning the system alone; the people, the setting and the history are part of the capability. Actor valence is relative to the residual, so no fixed list of "what this culture is like" survives a change of what is at stake. Channel maintenance is permanent work. `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 and Fig 10, different figures and different quantities).

**Refuter:** a system-only change that improves ground-verified outcomes across repeated cross-cultural interactions with no change in contact, habitat, or the people involved.

### F10 - The arrangement is the substance; a trait list is the flat formula

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Reading: two cultures can share an atom set and differ in the bond, which is an isomer difference; the ingredient list cannot see it. Flattening is the limiting case of writing `≡` (identity: *"two names, one referent"*, Operators table :25) between two frames that are not the same. `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 bond pricing and the Operators table identity test).

**Refuter:** a complete trait list that reproduces the lived meaning and per-slot predictions of an interaction without recording any relation between the traits.

### F11 - The specific content of respect is a declared gap

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Reading: no figure line names what respect is in a given culture, and the framework says it returns a shape rather than a name. After a genuine census this stays `gap`. The missing thing can be characterized in effect terms but not named: an instrument that registers the other's own account of whether the interaction respected what mattered, and discriminates it from the system's self-report. **Status: triangulated** for the limit (Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 13). `general`.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that names a specific cultural practice as respect, with a verbatim citation. None was found.

### F12 - Strain and re-census: Fig 13 :75 does not govern frames (correction kept visible)

An earlier reading of this derivation wanted Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** applied as "two frames must not bond directly."

Strain test: Fig 13 governs the six substrate terms (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network). A frame is not one of those; the Symbols table defines it as *"A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model."* (Symbols table :111). Applying the cross-kind rule to frames is an analogue, not a literal derivation.

**Re-census:** the direct governance for frames is Fig 2 :45, **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** The no-direct-bond claim is therefore derived from Fig 2, not Fig 13. The cross-kind rule may still be offered as a `general` analogy because the cast changes across levels (Fig 4 :20, *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*), but no frame-level bond prohibition is claimed from it. This correction is recorded rather than erased.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the WATSON run)

Required by the seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* (Seat Map :287)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1:** a system-only change improves ground-verified outcomes of cross-cultural interactions with no change in contact or habitat (breaks F9).
2. **R2:** two frames with the same evidence pool and the same categories register a fresh per-slot residual against a fixed anchor without importing anything (breaks F1 and F2).
3. **R3:** a pooled group-level parameter leaves all per-slot residuals unchanged and still predicts each individual's own account of being respected (breaks F2).
4. **R4:** a specific other's expectations are derived entirely from the system's own closure with no contact and the per-slot residual closes (breaks F3).
5. **R5:** a convention about shared meaning also settles whether a person was respected, with no separate ground reading (breaks F5).
6. **R6:** the other's own account, given before any theory, says the interaction failed to register what mattered, while the system's per-slot residual does not shrink across repeated interactions (breaks the recommendation at the ground).

A figure re-census that moved the anchor inside a frame would also reverse the verdict, but it would contradict Fig 2 :33 (*"The anchor must not update."*) and so would be a strain requiring re-census, not a simple flip. Until one of R1 to R6 fires, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Replace "shared model of the culture" with "triangulation against a legitimate shared ground" (F1, F4, F5). Keep the convention and the verdict in separate columns.
2. Index the residual per person and per occasion, and treat any group average as a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (F2, Fig 9 :60).
3. Require contact with a differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test on whatever crosses (F3).
4. Write down before the interaction what would count as respect here, in effect terms the other can check (F6, F7, F8).
5. Attribute capability to system-and-habitat, and maintain the channel rather than claiming a solved state (F9).
6. Say `gap` where the figures give no name (F11). Do not fill the slot.
7. Send the generator, the shape and its refuter, not a recording of this derivation (Fig 4 :22-25). Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

### Final verdict of this audit

The shape survives: triangulate each frame against a legitimate ground, index the residual per person, keep the channel provenanced and reachable, bind the sought-for criterion before the run, and refuse a final shared model. The specific content of respect does not survive as a figure-derived claim; it is a `gap` owed to an instrument and to the other's own account. Strains after re-census: **one correction recorded (F12), one valence distinction recorded (F5), none hidden.** Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

**Cost if wrong:** if the anchor is mis-set to agreement, failures become invisible because the frames converge (Fig 2 panel 1), and the cost arrives three steps out as a relationship that appears fine while the person in it was not respected (Fig 11 :52). If the reading is pooled, the live per-slot signal is buried in an average (Fig 1 :22). Both costs are avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.

**Undoable:** yes. A refused binding is added to the record rather than deleted, so the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :41-43). The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
