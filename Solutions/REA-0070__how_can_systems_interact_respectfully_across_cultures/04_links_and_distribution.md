## What the wider literature bears on the question

These are secondary corroboration only. The figures decide; a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census.

1. **Against pooling (strongest low-overlap witness).** Boas in the *Science* article, as quoted above: *"the object of study is the individual, not abstractions from the individual under observation."* This predicts that a cultural average cannot register the per-person residual. It corroborates Fig 1 :19 and Fig 1 :22. Field: anthropology and history of science, low overlap with the framework's engineering vocabulary.
2. **Against the trait list.** Virginia Heyer (1948), quoted on the cultural-relativism page: *"Cultural relativity, to phrase it in starkest abstraction, states the relativity of the part to the whole. The part gains its cultural significance by its place in the whole, and cannot retain its integrity in a different situation."* This predicts that a part lifted out of its arrangement changes meaning, corroborating Fig 12 :5 (the bond is the missing half) and Fig 11 :51 (valence relative to the residual).
3. **The insider account is a distinct frame.** The emic/etic distinction predicts that the other's own account carries information the outsider's standardised measure does not, and that the etic measure can be wrong on axes it does not read. Corroborates Fig 6 :23 (contact with a differing closure) and the instrument caveat in Fig 2 :37-38. It also warns that an etic framework used as the sole verdict repeats the flattening failure.
4. **The cost of a pooled reading applied to a person.** Stereotype threat research predicts that a group-level cue changes an individual's performance even when the individual does not endorse it, and that reframing the task changes the outcome. This corroborates the mechanism behind Fig 1 :22 and Fig 10 :26, but it is a different field (social psychology) and measures performance, not respect. Single witness for the harm mechanism.
5. **Culture as a process, not an ingredient list.** The animal-culture literature defines culture through social transmission and calls it *"a process"*. This is a genuinely low-overlap field (ethology and evolutionary biology) and corroborates Fig 12 :117 and the habitat rule in Fig 11 :43-44 (enumerate contacts, do not compose a trait list).
6. **Thick description supports effect-term description.** Geertz's method insists on the actor's interpreted context and on speaking *to* rather than *for* subjects. Corroborates Fig 6 :30 and Fig 9 :30-31. The summary also states cultural analysis is never complete, which corroborates the `gap` verdict in F11.
7. **A caution, not corroboration.** Hancock and Dein's ToM review frames its topic within a universalist-relativist tension. It supports the claim that modelling others is a measurable capability and that its development varies with culture, language and parenting. It does not support the stronger claim that a system can acquire the other's perspective from its own library. Treated as secondary and single-source.

**Overlap warning:** sources 1 to 4 and 6 are Wikipedia encyclopedia summaries covering anthropology, social psychology and evolutionary biology. They are different fields but one broad secondary-pool host, so their agreement is one witness pool talking, not six independent confirmations (Fig 2 :40). Source 5 is hosted on a different domain (scholarworks.gvsu.edu). No world source was found that conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole derivation before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* - ask what they would expect to see if an interaction were respectful before giving them the theory.
- **Fig 2 :40 and :41** - keep the convention (shared meaning) and the verdict (what happened to a person) in separate columns. Selecting readers for likely agreement manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their verdict worthless.
- **Fig 10 :59** - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* - declare the author's stake in the claim; never present the derivation as disinterested.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuters; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, if they opt in, the source links, with the note that the figures and the opened sources, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, restated

Stage 1 is the residual and its refuter. Stage 2 is opt-in access to the sources. Stage 3 is the formula as a reusable method, with each user writing their own ground and refuter before running it. Stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration. No bulk send, no opt-out-free attachment, and no request for trust or agreement.

**Hosting note.** Nothing here is sent by this agent. If the packet is hosted, prefer an opt-in download page that lets a reader decline contact, which is closer to the figure's rule than an attachment pushed to a list.
