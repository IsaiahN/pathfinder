# Expert verification packet - the Tether on simple and threshold contagion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a contagion modeller can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No fitted threshold, transmission probability or real-system mechanism assignment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

In one system with two contagion rules, simple exposure contagion and threshold contagion are two different typed relations over one contact substrate, not one mechanism with a knob. The simple rule is disjunction, where one contact suffices; the threshold rule is comparison, where a count of exposures is measured against a threshold. A model that records only the contact graph and a transmission probability has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement, so nothing in that model can tell which rule ran. Their combination is a union compound: a single lever clears the region where one exposure suffices and leaves the intersection standing, because a second condition still binds there. Simple transmissions settle first and the threshold compares their count second. The residual is indexed per node, not as a global fraction adopted, and the bond cannot be identified from aggregate incidence alone. The framework returns this shape, not a threshold value or a mechanism name for an observed cascade.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 9

- **Statement:** the two rules are two bonds, and their combination is a union compound with a binding intersection, not a single law.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :62 "either suffices; not both required" (the simple rule); Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities" and Fig 12 :89 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." (the threshold rule); Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."; Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."; Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- **Refuter:** exhibit one bond passing one test from which both "one exposure suffices" and "which is larger" follow; or show a single lever clearing the intersection while the second condition still binds.

### C2 - modelling-facing; checkable by a contagion modeller against their own model

- **Statement:** the dependency order is exposure first, threshold comparison second; and the residual is per node. Over a shared graph substrate the two rules are Fig 12 isomers of the contact graph.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."; Fig 13 :88 "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."; Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** in a coupled model, does the threshold condition consume successful simple transmissions (so the order is fixed), and does a per-node exposure gap carry information that the final attack rate does not? Min and San Miguel (2018) write that the transmission probability *"acts as a simple contagion process triggering a process of complex contagion"*; Contreras, Cencetti and Barrat (2024) report that simple-contagion infection patterns are *"almost completely determined by the final attack rate"* while threshold patterns are not.
- **Refuter:** a working model in which the threshold fires before any exposure has settled; or a demonstration that the global attack rate predicts takeoff as well as any per-node exposure gap; or a case where one rule requires an atom the other does not (kills the isomer claim and leaves the weaker functional-group claim).

### C3 - evidence-facing; checkable against published results

- **Statement:** the bond is not identifiable from aggregate incidence alone. Two mechanisms can produce the same population-level curve, so a fitted parameter never witnesses a mechanism.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."; Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** Hebert-Dufresne, Scarpino and Young (Nature Physics 2020) show an exact mapping between interacting simple contagions and complex contagion in well-mixed populations: *"Every curve of Fig. 1 can be obtained by solving the differential equations for either interacting or complex contagions. The solutions are identical."* Andres et al. (Phys Rev Research 2025) say the two mechanisms *"often occur together in social contagion phenomena, yet their distinguishability based on the observable contagion dynamics is challenging."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit an observable in aggregate incidence data that identifies the bond without per-node or co-infection event data.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a value for θ, λ, adoptability distribution, or mean degree.
- Not that simple contagion is "threshold with θ = 1" in the record. That collapse holds at outcome level inside one model family; it does not collapse the two bonds. Both are recorded.
- Not that the mixed regime is a blend or a relabelling. It is a union compound with a binding intersection.
- Not that aggregate curves identify which mechanism ran in a real system.
- Not a new primitive, and not an import that moves the wall: Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement of a real cascade.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does a per-node exposure gap minus θ predict takeoff better than the global attack rate, on the same data and budget?
2. With both rules applied, is there residual left on the nodes requiring multiple exposures? If not, the union claim fails.
3. Does per-node co-infection event data separate simple transmission from reinforcement where aggregate incidence cannot?
4. Can a threshold condition fire before any single exposure has settled in a working model of the same system?
