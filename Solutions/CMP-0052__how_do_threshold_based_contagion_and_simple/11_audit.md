## Independent domain census, written from the residual line alone

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Two rules on one network: one exposure suffices, or a count reaches a threshold. Arity: two conditions. Symmetry: the simple rule is symmetric over donors (any one will do); the threshold rule is an ordered comparison of two quantities. Scale: one pairwise link versus a whole neighbourhood. Because the conditions differ in both symmetry and scale, they cannot be averaged into one law; this matches Fig 1 :19, which I reached from the residual before reading the primary's census.
- **Domain 1, contact substrate.** Actors: nodes, edges, weights. Currency: contacts and exposures. Anchor: measured contact data. Enumeration, not composition.
- **Domain 2, simple rule.** Actors: susceptible node, one infected neighbour, λ. Currency: independent transmission events. Anchor: an infection curve.
- **Domain 3, threshold rule.** Actors: node, k neighbours, θ. Currency: exposed-neighbour count. Anchor: an adoption curve.
- **Domain 4, coupled system.** Actors: heterogeneous nodes, seeds, both rules. Currency: final adopted fraction, phase boundaries. Anchor: measured adoption fraction and phase transitions. Union, because one node can satisfy both conditions.
- **Domain 5, observation frame.** Actors: analyst, model, data. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence zero in this simulation.

Order by dependency: substrate, then per-exposure event, then threshold comparison, then coupled system, then observation. Refuse a single-law reading before minting the two-bond reading (Formula step 2).
