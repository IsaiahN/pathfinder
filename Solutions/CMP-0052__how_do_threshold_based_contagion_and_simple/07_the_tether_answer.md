# The Tether's answer: how simple and threshold contagion interact in one system

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a fitted result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

One contact network, one set of nodes and edges, two transmission rules. Simple (exposure-based) contagion: one contact suffices with probability λ, and exposures act independently. Threshold contagion: a node adopts only when the count or fraction of exposed neighbours reaches θ. The residual is the shape of their interaction when both rules run in the same system. The question is not "what is the right θ", it is "what kind of thing is the interaction and what does it leave unresolved".

## 2. The shape

1. **They are two bonds over one substrate, not one mechanism with a knob.** The simple rule is disjunction: Fig 12 :62 "either suffices; not both required". The threshold rule is comparison: Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities", tested by Fig 12 :89 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Since Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance.", a model that records the contact graph and one λ has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement (Fig 12 :95). It cannot tell which rule ran.
2. **Their combination is a union compound with a binding intersection.** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A single λ clears the θ = 1 region and leaves the mixed region standing. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." That compound has its own loop and its own ground.
3. **Simple transmission is upstream of the threshold comparison.** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 13 :88 "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." The successful exposures settle first; their count is what θ compares. Reversing the order is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :19).
4. **The residual is per node, not a global average.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The node one exposure short of θ is the live slot. A global attack rate hides it.
5. **The bond is not identifiable from the aggregate curve.** Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Interacting simple contagions and social reinforcement can give identical population-level dynamics. The bond is a property of the record, not of the curve.
6. **Threshold contagion is a second-order effect over the habitat.** Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." neighbourhood width and overlap, not only path length, govern it.
7. **Over a shared graph substrate the two rules are isomers.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The weaker, always-available reading is a functional group: Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a value for θ, λ, the fraction of complex nodes, or the mean degree. Those are filters for the budget: Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not that the aggregate incidence curve identifies which mechanism ran. That is F5 and the exact mapping in Hebert-Dufresne et al. (2020).
- Not that simple contagion is "just threshold with θ = 1" as a matter of the record. That collapse holds at outcome level inside one model family; it does not collapse the two bonds. Both statements are kept.
- Not that the mixed regime is a blend or a relabelling. It is a union compound with a binding intersection.
- Not a new primitive, and not an import that moves the wall: Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this derivation settles any bond assignment for a real system.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Per-node exposure gap:** does a node's current successful-exposure count minus θ predict takeoff better than the global attack rate? If no, finding F4 fails.
- **Co-infection event data:** with two contagions interacting, does the per-node event record separate simple transmission from reinforcement? If yes, F5 is bounded to aggregates only; the aggregate claim still stands.
- **Single-lever test:** with the simple rule fully applied, is there residual left on the θ > 1 nodes? If no, the union claim fails.
- **Order test:** can a threshold condition fire before any single exposure has settled in a working model of the same system? If yes, F3 fails.
- **Standing falsifiers:** one bond passing both tests; aggregate time series identifying the bond; a direct first-order model reproducing neighbourhood-dependent cascades without any composition.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The bond set and the six-term substrate are visible choices, not nature's own list. *They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The framework stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: two bonds can give one curve, and the curve does not decide between them. The answer stops at the shape and hands the naming to the ground. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether says that simple exposure contagion and threshold contagion are not one mechanism with a knob when they run in the same system. They are two different relations over one contact network: one where a single contact suffices, and one that compares a count of exposures against a threshold. Because the relation differs, a model that records only the network and one transmission probability has lost the information that decides which rule ran. Their interaction is a union, not a single law: a single lever clears the nodes that need only one exposure and leaves the mixed region standing, where a second condition still binds. Simple transmissions settle first and the threshold compares their count second, and the residual that matters is per node, not the global fraction adopted. From the population curve alone the mechanism cannot be identified, because two different mechanisms can produce the same curve. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not give you the threshold value or name the source of an observed cascade. That is for the ground.
