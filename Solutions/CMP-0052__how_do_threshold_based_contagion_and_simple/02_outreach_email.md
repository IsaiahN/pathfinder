# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mixed-region claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F2 and the refuter below are stated clearly enough for a reader to fire them. The stage-1 output must be checkable on its own.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about simple and threshold contagion in the same system

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** in a system where both rules run on the same nodes and edges, simple exposure contagion and threshold contagion are not one mechanism with a knob. They are two different typed relations over one contact substrate. Simple contagion is the rule where one contact suffices. Threshold contagion is a comparison, the count or fraction of exposed neighbours against a threshold. Because the relation is different, the arrangement carries information the contact graph does not. A model that records only the graph and one transmission probability has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement, so nothing in that model can tell you which rule is running.
>
> The interaction is a union, not a single law. A single transmission probability clears the region where one exposure suffices and leaves the intersection standing, because in that intersection a second condition still binds. The mixed region is not a harder simple case; it is a specific compound with its own threshold and its own ground. Simple transmissions settle first and the threshold compares their count second, so the order is not arbitrary.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you a value for the threshold or the transmission probability. I am not claiming that aggregate incidence curves identify the mechanism. Two different mechanisms can produce the same population-level curve, so the bond is a property of the record, not of the curve. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A single per-edge transmission probability, with no threshold comparison, reproduces the full coupled behaviour at the same budget, so no second relation is owed.
> 2. A single lever clears the intersection, that is, nodes needing multiple exposures have no residual left after the simple rule is applied.
> 3. Aggregate incidence time series alone identify which rule is running, without per-node or co-infection data.
> 4. A per-node exposure gap predicts cascade takeoff no better than the global attack rate.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract used for every citation): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Competing contagion processes: complex contagion triggered by simple contagion, Min and San Miguel, Scientific Reports 2018: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6039514
> - Infection patterns in simple and complex contagion processes on networks, Contreras, Cencetti and Barrat, PLOS Computational Biology 2024: https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1012206
> - Macroscopic patterns of interacting contagions are indistinguishable from social reinforcement, Hebert-Dufresne, Scarpino and Young, Nature Physics 2020: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8247125
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim and refuter. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, the three source links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode.
