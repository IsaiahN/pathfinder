# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the method is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the residual in your own case is described in effect terms and your own refuter is written down. The offer below only works if the stage-1 output can survive without the theory behind it.

---

**Subject:** One question about comparing two historical series, and what would refute my answer

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about historical data comparability that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the method first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. A failure is more useful to me than an endorsement.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Two readings of the same stretch of the past exist. The observers, categories and instruments that produced the second reading are not the ones that produced the first. Someone reads the difference between the two numbers as a change in the world. What is being decided is whether that difference is a change in the world or a change in the channel that measured it. The two options lead to very different histories: one writes a real trend, the other writes an artifact.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-case predictions, not `grounded`).** The question is a bundle, not one question. It splits into three conditions that overlap: is the referent the same, is the channel calibrated or bridged, and is there a record that distinguishes a label reached independently from a label inherited. So there are two branches. If the quantity exists independently of the category (temperature, height, a yield), then its referent must act as an anchor that does not move when the category is renamed, and a comparison is licensed only by an overlap or parallel measurement whose discrepancy is measured, not declared. If the quantity is constituted by the category (an official rate, a legal status, a diagnostic threshold), then changing the rule changed the thing the series describes; the honest comparison is of the conventions, not of a world trend. In both branches the answer is per category, never one verdict for the whole dataset. A shared label carries no corroboration until the record shows whether it was reached twice or borrowed once. If no bridge was ever recorded, the result is "not comparable on this evidence", with a description of what an overlap would have to look like. That is a verdict, not a silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that all historical data are incomparable. Not that a careful recode can always fix a break. Not that a significant correlation or a large sample makes two series comparable. Not that expert agreement calibrates a series. I am not claiming a ground reading: no dataset was measured in producing this.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. Two readings from demonstrably different channels, with no overlap and no fixed anchor, shown by a source-discriminating instrument to differ exactly as the recorded trend predicts, with the result surviving replication.
> 2. A shared label shown by an independent ground reading to have exactly the same extension and intension in both epochs, with a record proving the later users did not inherit it.
> 3. A comparison of drifted categories whose residual trends down under repeated recoding with no bridge, so the channel difference was depth rather than a break.
> 4. A round trip returning the original reading within a stated tolerance on held-out data, so the discrepancy between the two category schemes is negligible.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run it on a comparison you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
