# The Tether's answer: can historical data be compared reliably after the measuring arrangement changed?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No dataset was opened.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two readings of one stretch of the past, produced by different observers using different categories and instruments, are read as a change in the world. The decision is whether the difference is a ground change or a channel change, and what would have to be shown before calling it a ground change. The two options lead to different histories: one writes a real trend, the other writes an artifact. The framework does not answer yes or no for all data. It returns the shape a reliable comparison must have and the split that must be made first.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question is a union, not one question.** It bundles same referent, a calibrated or bridged channel, and a record that distinguishes a label reached twice from a label borrowed once. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* One harmonisation clears only the label region; the intersection where the referent also changed remains.

2. **The changed thing is the channel.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Observers, categories and instruments are the channel. When the channel moves, the first thing to suspect is lost contact, not a changed world. Fig 10 :27: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*, a report of lost contact rather than a trend.

3. **Two branches, decided by the anchor rule.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
   - **Branch 1, quantities whose referent exists independently of the rule** (temperature, height, yield): the referent is the anchor. A comparison is licensed only if both readings range against it and an overlap measurement exists.
   - **Branch 2, quantities constituted by the classification** (an official rate, a legal status, a diagnostic threshold): Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Here changing the rule changed the thing described. The honest comparison is of the conventions, where Fig 2 :41 says agreement *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*, not of a world trend.

4. **A declared category is not a grounded category.** Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* What must be installed is something that can be violated: an overlap, a crosswalk with error bounds, a check that could fail.

5. **The bridge must already be returning something.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The analogue is a parallel measurement under both rules on the same referent. Fig 6 :35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Where no overlap exists, the honest output is `unreached` or `unreachable at this budget`, with a description of what the missing instrument would have to look like. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

6. **Measure the round trip.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` is the cost of carrying an old reading up to the new category and back down. Fig 4 :31: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."*

7. **A shared label is not identity.** Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Until provenance discriminates the two, the shared label carries zero corroboration. Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

8. **Per slot, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* One drifted category can hide inside a clean overall series.

9. **Agreement is not calibration.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all historical data are incomparable. That is a verdict of exhaustion no frame can certify (Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*).
- Not that a careful recode can always repair a break. Re-coding is step 3 work and cannot rescue a broken referent step (Fig 3 :19).
- Not that significance, sample size or a large correlation makes two series comparable. Those are filters, not witnesses.
- Not that two experts using the same inherited category verify each other. If they share an evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a ground reading. No series, archive or crosswalk was opened in this run, and no verdict is asserted as settled.
- Not a name for the missing bridge in a specific archive. The framework returns a shape, not a name.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A bridge and a source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The checks that would fire are:

1. **Parallel measurement.** Both arrangements read the same referent on a held-out overlap. If the discrepancy is within a stated tolerance, the comparison is licensed. If not, measure it and report `R_T`.
2. **Convergence versus borrowing.** A record showing whether two shared labels were reached separately or one was inherited from the other. Only the former is corroboration.
3. **Per-slot residual.** The drifted categories named. A global trend is not an acceptable substitute.
4. **Branch decision.** An argument, checked against the referent, for whether the quantity is constituted by the category. If it is, report the convention comparison, not a world trend.
5. **Pre-registered refuters.** The five in `01` section 4, run before any conclusion is accepted. Any one that fires amends the corresponding claim.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the general instrument requirement: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* For this question the discriminating instrument is the overlap, not more commentary on the series.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The archive may still contain a parallel measurement that nobody searched for (Formula step 7 asks SEARCHED first). Where none exists, the framework can characterise the missing thing's shape and stop there. Fig 13 :139: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: a harmonised series that nobody can distinguish from a continuous one is still a harmonised series, and the record of the route is what keeps that visible.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that historical data are simply incomparable, and it does not say that a good recode makes them comparable. It says that the question is a bundle, so you must first ask whether the quantity exists independently of the category that names it. If it does, the referent must be an anchor that does not move when the label changes, and the comparison is licensed only by an overlap in which old and new methods both read the same thing, with the round-trip gap measured rather than declared. If it does not, then changing the category changed the world the series describes, and the legitimate comparison is of the conventions, not of a trend. In both cases a shared label is no evidence until the record shows whether it was reached twice or borrowed once, the check is per category rather than global, and agreement among historians is not a calibration. Where no overlap was recorded, the answer is "not comparable on this evidence", with a description of what a bridge would have to look like. That is a verdict, not a silence.
