## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the answer

The canonical limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (figures README). Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for HIS-0046 a **shape** is not "yes, comparable" or "no, not comparable". It is:

- a two-branch split (referent-independent quantities versus constitutionally category-dependent quantities);
- three conditions that must hold and can be checked separately (same referent, bridged channel, distinguishing record);
- a per-slot rule instead of a global one;
- a named missing thing when the bridge is absent, rather than a silence.

That is a kind of thing. It does not tell you whether any particular pair of census series is comparable. Only a ground reading of that pair could do that, and none was taken.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: describe the effect, not the borrowed word

*Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*

Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"* (continued at :31).

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the reason directly: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is why the residual in `01` is stated as three terms (ground, channel, reading) rather than as "the 1971 coding scheme changed". The former can be checked by anyone; the latter only by someone who already knows the scheme.

*Library of Babel* also names the exact structural error in this question: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is Fig 9 :54 in different words, and it is why F1 of the audit refuses a single yes or no.

## 3. Why a shared number is not a shared meaning

This is the heart of the question, and *How Intelligence Actually Works* states it in a thought experiment about a video frame and a photograph:

> *"The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which."*

Two historical series can carry the same number with the same apparent warrant, where one was measured under the old rule and the other was recomputed under the new one. The artifact does not record the route. The same essay adds: *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* And on provenance: *"often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."*

This is Fig 8 :51 verbatim in another vocabulary: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. The shape therefore always includes a provenance condition. It cannot be replaced by a statistical test on the numbers, because the numbers do not carry the distinction.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the instrument requirement: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* In this question the instrument that distinguishes the paths is the overlap measurement, the parallel reading under old and new rules. Without it, the comparable-looking data are the green reading that cannot comment on the source.

## 4. Why drift is invisible from inside, and why the anchor must be outside

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* is the most directly relevant essay in the set:

> *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."*

> *"The reference point both sides check against has to stay put. If it moves, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

> *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."*

That is Fig 2 :33-35 and Fig 2 :40, and it explains why a community of historians using the same inherited category cannot certify that the category did not drift. The third sentence also states the legitimate branch: agreement about shared meaning *is* the product when the question is constitutively about the convention (Fig 2 :36, :41).

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* gives the clock analogy, which is almost a literal description of a spliced time series:

> *"You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."*

And the self-validation limit: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. What licenses a name, and what this question may name

The category is a declared convention until something can violate it (Fig 10 :18, :19). *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* sharpens this:

> *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot. But visibility is not neutrality, and revision is still bounded by what can be conceived to revise toward."*

So naming a category as "the same category across time" is a choice made visible, not a discovery. The question's own vocabulary is borrowed from the institutions that produced the data. A bridge, a crosswalk with error bounds, or a parallel measurement is what licenses saying the same label refers to the same thing. Failing that, the framework returns a shape: what such a bridge would have to look like.

The same essay on imports: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* In this question the imported thing is not a new code for an old category; it is the overlap measurement that adds a new axis, the ability to see the relation between the old rule and the new one at all. Where no overlap was ever recorded, no amount of searching the existing series can manufacture that axis (Fig 8 :32, *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*).

## 6. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid saying which comparison is reliable? ANSWERED

No, and the restriction is narrower than it first appears. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. It governs a primitive not held in the library. Here the missing thing is the bridge, the overlap instrument. The framework can characterize its shape (parallel readings of the same referent under both channels, with a measured discrepancy and a stated tolerance) but cannot name a particular archive where it must be found. That is an empirical search, not a derivation.

What the framework *can* do is narrower but still useful: bind the ordinary terms already held (referent, channel, record, anchor, round trip, slot) and let each accrue a residual. That is what `01` does. The honest output is a rule for deciding, not a decision about a case.

## 7. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of eminent historians agreeing that two series are comparable gives the claim a budget to be tried. It does not settle the comparison. The public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are chosen to give the claim a budget, not to gather endorsements.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So every substantive file in this folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle whether any historical dataset is reliable, and it says so on every claim.
