# Expert verification packet: the Tether on historical data comparability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian, archivist, statistician or measurement scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** No dataset was opened. This is a rule for deciding, not a decision about a case.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

"Can these historical data be compared?" is a union of three conditions, not one question. The decision splits on the anchor. When the quantity exists independently of the category that names it, the referent must be an anchor that does not move when the label changes, and the comparison is licensed only by an overlap in which old and new arrangements both read the same referent, with the round-trip gap measured rather than declared. When the quantity is constituted by the classification, changing the category changed the thing the series describes, and the honest comparison is of the conventions, not of a world trend. In both branches a shared label is no corroboration until the record distinguishes convergence from borrowing, the check is per category, and agreement among experts is not calibration. Where no overlap was recorded, the answer is "not comparable on this evidence" together with a description of what a bridge would have to look like. That is a verdict, not a silence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 9

- **Statement:** the question is a union, and the first thing to decide is which of two anchor branches applies.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** does the figure actually make the convention a legitimate anchor when the question is constitutively about it? If yes, the two-branch split is figure-derived; if no, F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is wrong.
- **Refuter:** a case assigned to branch 2 shown to have a referent that survives the definition change, which moves it to branch 1 and reopens the bridge requirement; or a demonstration that the three conditions form a partition, so the union claim is too strong.

### C2: method-facing, checkable by a historian, archivist or measurement scientist

- **Statement:** a bridge must already be returning something. A declared crosswalk is not a bridge. The analogue of the proto-instrument is an overlap or parallel measurement on the same referent; where none exists, the result is `unreached` or `unreachable at this budget`, with the missing instrument's shape described.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :35 *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** does the world practice match this? The climate field records non-climatic jumps from instrumentation changes and treats parallel measurement as a guideline; the occupational-classification literature publishes correspondence tables and measures cross-country coding inconsistency; the HISCO standard was built precisely to code historical descriptions. Does any of these count as a bridge in the figure's sense, or only as a declaration?
- **Refuter:** a bridge built from a pure description with no prior returning instrument that passes a held-out check; or a round trip returning the original reading within a stated measurement tolerance on held-out data, so `R_T` is negligible and the harmonisation is licensed.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with an empirical edge

- **Statement:** a shared label is not identity. Same tokens under different coding acts are isomers, and only the record separates convergence from borrowed import. The report must be per category.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 10 :65 *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
- **What to check:** is the isomer reading faithful to Fig 12? Does provenance in fact license an audit but not a pass? And does a global trend in a real series conceal per-category drift, as Fig 1 predicts?
- **Refuter:** independent evidence that two shared labels were reached by convergent derivation with separate histories and no borrowing, plus a ground reading confirming the referent; or a case where the per-slot residual and the aggregate agree, so the aggregation warning does not bite.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all historical data are incomparable. A search that finds no bridge is not a proof that none exists.
- Not that a recode, a crosswalk or a statistical adjustment automatically repairs a break.
- Not that significance, sample size or correlation makes two series comparable. Those are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that expert agreement calibrates a series (Fig 7 :35).
- Not `grounded`. No series, archive or crosswalk was opened in this derivation.
- Not a name for the missing bridge in a specific archive. The framework returns a shape.
- Not a claim that the category is always the problem. In branch 2 the category is part of the referent and the convention comparison is legitimate.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A parallel measurement on a held-out overlap, with a stated tolerance and a measured `R_T`.
2. A record establishing convergence or borrowing for the shared labels.
3. A per-category residual report naming the drifted slots.
4. A checked decision about which anchor branch the quantity belongs to.
5. The five pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4, run before the conclusion is accepted.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :36 and Fig 9 :54, three short passages. Decide whether the two-branch split is figure-derived.
2. Read Fig 6 :32 and :35 and Fig 10 :18. Decide whether a declared crosswalk can count as a bridge in the framework's sense.
3. Name one real overlap or parallel measurement from your own field, or say why none could have been recorded.
4. Look at one series you know well. Is the drift global or per category? Does a shared label in your field represent convergence or inheritance?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
