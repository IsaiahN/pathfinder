## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure lines on anchor, channel decay, union split, proto-instrument, round trip and record.
2. **Abstraction (`T_A`):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: comparability is a union, it decides at the anchor, branch 1 needs an overlap and a measured `R_T`, branch 2 compares conventions, labels need provenance, the report is per slot.
3. **Concretisation (`T_E`):** the concrete claim a user would make about a specific pair of series, for example "these two occupation series from 1911 and 1998 measure the same thing, so the trend is real".

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **which branch applies** to the specific quantity (the shorthand states both; the case needs one);
- **what counts as a returning proto-instrument** in that archive (a crosswalk, a parallel campaign, an unchanged coding instruction, or nothing);
- **which categories are the live slots** (the global series conceals them);
- **whether a shared label is convergence or borrowing** for that pair of coders.

One thing survives cleanly: a channel change is not automatically a world change, and a declaration is not a bridge. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` states.
