# Expert verification packet: the Tether on first codes and protocol emergence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in protocol emergence, language evolution, coordination or information theory can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` for the structural shape, `specified` for the protocol application, not `grounded`.** No measurement or protocol trace is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The first shared code between two systems is not agreed into existence in the absence of a code. Mutual updating with no anchor is a mirror chain, not a mechanism. The first terms are enumerated from a shared habitat, arranged by typed bonds so that the relation carries information the bare symbols do not, installed as a convention that can be violated and does not decide an outcome, and carried across when one system meets another whose closure differs. Agreement is the product for the convention and disqualifying as a witness that the code tracks a ground. Repetition stabilizes the code; it does not certify it. The framework returns the shape, the written refuters and the instruments that would name the particular code. It does not name the code.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Anchor and habitat, checkable by reading Figures 2 and 11
- **Statement:** there is no code-from-nothing. The first shared terms come from a contact neither frame authors, so the question "without an agreed-upon starting code" describes a contact, not a void.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :48 - *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
- **Refuter:** two frames with no shared anchor and no contact with a differing closure reliably converge on a stable shared code across independent pairs. Also: a protocol whose first terms are demonstrably uninfluenced by any shared contact, replicated across pairs.

### C2 - Bond and provenance, checkable by reading Figures 12 and 8
- **Statement:** a protocol is a molecule, not an inventory. The relation between terms carries information the terms alone do not, and a shared symbol does not tell you whether the two frames converged or one imported.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 8 :50 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*
- **What to check:** in a protocol you know, do two arrangements over the same signal inventory produce different behaviour? Is the shared record sufficient to tell independent convergence from adopted import?
- **Refuter:** identical inventories under different arrangements produce indistinguishable behaviour with adequate instrumentation and a discriminating ground. That would make a protocol just its inventory and falsify the bond claim.

### C3 - Convention guard and the budget/witness split, checkable by reading Figures 10, 2 and 9
- **Statement:** the first protocol is a convention that must remain violable and must not decide an outcome. Repetition and more agreeing frames are budget filters, not witnesses.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 10 :18 - *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** a convention neither frame can violate which also settles a world-verdict, still counted as a convention; or agreement among high-overlap frames that changes the ground's verdict on whether the code works.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a code can be generated from nothing. There is always a lower substrate already registering (Fig 6 :34).
- Not a universal first protocol or a final code; no last room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not that a shared symbol is a shared meaning, and not that convergence proves import or vice versa.
- Not that agreement settles truth. Agreement is the product for conventions only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a trace, experiment or measurement.
- Not a name for the first code. The slot stays `???`; unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. (1) A source-discriminating trace that distinguishes contact-enumerated terms from imported terms. (2) A controlled bond test over a fixed inventory with a discriminating ground. (3) A guard test showing the convention remains violable after it has coordinated action.
