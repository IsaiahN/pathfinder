## QUESTION

How do systems negotiate new meanings or protocols without an agreed-upon starting code? Restated in effect terms: two frames need a composition both can admit so each can predict the other's observable action, but any negotiation seems to presuppose a code in which to negotiate. Option A: a code is created from nothing by mutual agreement. Option B: the first code is found at a shared contact, bonded into a molecule, installed as a violable convention, and carried across by import from a differing closure. A leads to coherent drift; B leads to a checkable shape with an open slot for the particular code.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two frames plus one ground, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: mixed, since agreement about a shared meaning is symmetric and is the product of a convention (Fig 2 :41), while agreement about the world is not settled by agreement at all (Fig 2 :40). Scale: a single exchange, repeated interaction, and a population of pairs; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :22). Mixed symmetry signals a union compound, not one hard question.
- **D1 Contact / habitat.** Actors: both frames plus the world. Currency: residual per slot. Anchor: the ground, a shared contact neither frame authors (Fig 2 :29, Fig 11 :43). Admitted, triangulated.
- **D2 Convention / protocol.** Actors: the two frames. Currency: agreement and revisability. Anchor: the public, violable convention (Fig 10 :45-49). Admitted, triangulated.
- **D3 Code as molecule.** Actors: atoms and bonds. Currency: cost(φ) and the price of bonds. Anchor: settlement against the ground (Fig 12 :5, :46). Admitted, single-source for the price rule.
- **D4 Closure / import.** Actors: frames with differing closures. Currency: an atom crossing the boundary. Anchor: provenance and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-51). Admitted, triangulated.
- **D5 Direction / discipline.** Actors: the searcher. Currency: the pre-bound shape. Anchor: the written-down prediction (Fig 5 :65, Fig 9 :27-28). Admitted, single-source.
- **X1 A code created from nothing by mutual agreement.** Actors: the frames only. Currency: mutual expectation. Anchor: none nameable, because the anchor would update with the frames (Fig 2 :13-15). **Not a domain yet**, rejected by the anchor test.
- **X2 The first code is simply innate.** Real in biology, but it is an import from a differing frame (evolution), not a derivation. Absorbed into D1 as inherited contact.

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D4 form the ground-contact compound; D2 and D3 form the convention-composition compound. A code that decides a world-verdict is a category error, not a harder case (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order (Fig 3): perceive an already-registering anchor, build vocabulary for the residual, state the objective in each frame's own terms, plan which closure to import from, then settle and carry a reversible convention. The middle link is the one with the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: reading "negotiate" literally strains Fig 2 :45 and :43, so it was re-censused to X1; reading "no starting code" as "no contact" strains Fig 6 :34, so it was re-censused to absence of a code, not absence of registration. No strain remains after re-census except the two-currency cost in D3, which is named, not summed.

Quote: "a solution that people tend to choose by default in the absence of communication in order to avoid coordination failure". Prediction: coordination without a prior exchange uses salience from a shared situation. **Corroborates** D1 and X1 rejection. Overlap: medium, shares the coordination literature with the next two rows, so treat as one witness for the mechanism shape.
- **Game theory / voluntary standards.** Edna Ullmann-Margalit (1977, *The Emergence of Norms*), reported in Wikipedia "Coordination game", 2026 accessed. Quote: "a voluntary standard (when characterized also as de facto standard) is a typical solution to a coordination problem". Prediction: a protocol is a coordination solution, so agreement is the product. **Corroborates** D2. Overlap: medium, same coordination pool.
- **Philosophy / signaling conventions.** David Lewis (1969, *Convention*), reported in Wikipedia "Lewis signaling game", 2026 accessed. Quote: "both the sender and receiver prefer the same outcomes as one another, this game is a game of perfect common interest". Prediction: shared interest plus a shared state space, not a prior dictionary, supports a signaling system. **Corroborates** D1 and D2. Overlap: medium, same pool.
- **Cognitive science / sound symbolism.** Uznadze 1924, Köhler 1929, Ramachandran and Hubbard 2001, Cwiek et al. 2022, reported in Wikipedia "Bouba/kiki effect", 2026 accessed. Quote: "some sound–shape mappings precede language learning, and may in fact aid in language learning by establishing a basis for matching labels to referents". - **Anthropology / origin of language.** Wikipedia "Origin of language", 2026 accessed. Quote: "Language is ruled out because the best way to guard against being deceived is to ignore all signals except those that are instantly verifiable." Prediction: arbitrary first symbols need reliability and trust, which are habitat properties, not a code. **Corroborates** D1 and D2; also supplies a live objection (cheap signals are unreliable) that was returned to the census and left in D1 as a contact condition. Overlap: low.
- **Philosophy / public criterion.** Ludwig Wittgenstein (*Philosophical Investigations*), reported in Wikipedia "Private language argument", 2026 accessed. Quote: "justification consists in appealing to something independent". Prediction: a code usable by only one frame is incoherent; correctness needs an independent criterion. **Corroborates** D1 and C3. Overlap: low.
- **Linguistics / contact languages.** Wikipedia "Pidgin", 2026 accessed. Quote: "a grammatically simplified form of contact language that develops between two or more groups of people that do not have a language in common". Prediction: a first code emerges under sustained contact with no shared interlanguage. **Corroborates** D1 and D4. Overlap: low to medium, shares the contact-language literature with the origin-of-language row.
- **Engineering / protocol bootstrap.** Ralph Droms, DHCP, IETF RFC 2131 (1997), reported in Wikipedia "Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol", 2026 accessed. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and is triangulated: there is no code from nothing; first terms are enumerated at a shared contact, bonded into a molecule whose relation carries meaning, installed as a violable convention that does not decide an outcome, and carried across by import from a differing closure after R was described in effect terms. Agreement is the product for the convention and disqualifying for the verdict; repetition is a budget filter. Mode `general` for the shape, `specified` for protocols, nothing `grounded`. The specific first code is a declared `gap`, owed to a discriminating instrument. The two-currency cost of a code (bits and actions) is a named strain, not a summed figure.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. A protocol designer would spend budget manufacturing conventions where no shared contact exists, or would treat repetition as certification and miss drift. The real cost is spending an outside checker's independence by sending the finished framework before the effect description. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its five refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. Fig 6 :42-43 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." A refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was struck out. The shape and its refuters are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Independent pairs converging with no anchor and no differing closure; first terms demonstrably independent of shared contact; identical inventories under different arrangements with no behavioural difference; an unviolable convention that decides an outcome counted as a convention; recognition of a correct code with no prior written characterization. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that shared codes are fully predicted by repeated interaction alone would also reverse it. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
