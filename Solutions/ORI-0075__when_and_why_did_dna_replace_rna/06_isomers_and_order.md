# Structure, order and analogues for the RNA to DNA role change

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond classification. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

"DNA replaced RNA" packs three claims: a chemical difference, an ordering, and a class membership. This file unbundles them and states a falsifier for each. No date is derived here.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- Atoms held in the chemistry frame: ribose, deoxyribose, phosphate, adenine, guanine, cytosine, uracil, thymine, the 2'-hydroxyl group, the methyl group.
- Bonds held: phosphodiester linkage, base pairing (A to U or T), sequence `→`, functional-group membership.
- Anchors: measured hydrolysis behaviour and prebiotic synthesis (Wikipedia, Lazcano 1988, Wächtershäuser 2014); the figures for the bond classification.
- Overlap: the chemistry frame and the figure frame share only the classification vocabulary, not the measurements.

## Q1 - What exactly differs in structure, and why does it matter?

Wikipedia, *RNA world*: *"The major difference between RNA and DNA is the presence of a hydroxyl group at the 2'-position of the ribose sugar in RNA"*, and *"This group makes the molecule less stable because, when not constrained in a double helix, the 2' hydroxyl can chemically attack the adjacent phosphodiester bond to cleave the phosphodiester backbone."*

The same source adds: *"Chemically, uracil is similar to thymine, differing only by a methyl group"*, and *"Uracil is, however, one product of damage to cytosine that makes RNA particularly susceptible to mutations."* Lazcano 1988 adds the UV argument and the fidelity argument; Wächtershäuser 2014 gives the half-life contrast: *"DNA has a half-life on geologic time scales, while catalytic mRNA has a half-life on metabolic time scales."*

So the structural difference is small in atom count and large in consequence. This is exactly Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance"* applied one level down: a methyl and a hydroxyl change what the polymer can do over time.

**Falsifier:** a measurement showing that the 2'-OH does not materially increase cleavage under the habitat conditions reconstructed for the transition, or that thymine's methyl group does not reduce the cytosine-deamination ambiguity. Then the structural explanation fails and a different selection pressure must be named.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Wikipedia (tertiary), Lazcano 1988 and Wächtershäuser 2014. The three draw on overlapping chemistry literature, so this is one evidence pool, not three witnesses.

## Q2 - Is "replace" a sequence, and what does the arrow commit to?

Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. The Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*. So writing "RNA → DNA" commits to an ordering the evidence does not automatically give.

Re-census from `01` F2 splits two objects:

- **Molecule origin.** No sequence is licensed. Wächtershäuser 2014 argues the DNA/RNA functional dichotomy *"existed since the dawn of the genetic machinery"*. Wikipedia reports Forterre's viral account with an RNA-based LUCA. Both conflict with a clean temporal succession.
- **Role occupancy.** A sequence is licensed. In every extant lineage the long-term hereditary role is occupied by DNA, and RNA's hereditary role is transient or viral. The role moved even if the molecules coexisted.

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Once the deoxy polymer is settled against the ground, it can occupy the hereditary slot as an atom at the next level. That is the recursion, not an import of a seventh term.

**Falsifier:** a reconstructed lineage in which the long-term hereditary role is jointly held by RNA and DNA with no displacement of one by the other.

**Status:** `general` on the re-census, `specified` on the role sequence. Triangulated (Fig 12, Operators table, two conflicting world sources that agree on coexistence even though they disagree on chronology).

## Q3 - Are DNA and RNA isomers? (retraction of my first reading)

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :97: *"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"*, with Berzelius naming isomerism in 1830.

DNA and RNA do **not** meet the definition. Their atom sets differ: deoxyribose versus ribose (one oxygen), thymine versus uracil (one methyl). They are not variants of one formula; they are different substances with different atoms. My first reading, that they are isomers of a hereditary polymer, is **retracted** and kept visible here.

The correct figure classification is Fig 12 :123: *"The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* They are two members of a **functional group**: long-lived hereditary polymer, with different chemical qualifiers (deoxy/thymine versus ribo/uracil). Fig 12 :119 gives the neighbouring structural term: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* RNA retains the catalytic part after the role move, which is why it is not eliminated.

**Falsifier:** a synthesis showing both polymers can be written over one identical atom set with only a bond change, which would restore the isomer claim.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition, retraction recorded. This matches the retraction pattern in the exemplar's `06`.

## Q4 - What is the order of the union?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The union is:

```
D1 chemistry (hydrolysis, UV, fidelity)
   ↓ selected in
D4 habitat (unmaintained, Fig 11 :36)
   ↓ bounded by
D3 geological record (when), a lookup with its own anchor
```

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Chemistry and fidelity are upstream of selection; the date is a parallel lookup, not downstream. A claim that combines them into one linear story commits the blend that Fig 9 :55 names: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**Falsifier:** an instrument that settles timing and mechanism in one observation, collapsing D3 into D1/D4.

**Status:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 3, the domain census in `01`).

## Q5 - Do pre-RNA analogues settle the "when"?

Wikipedia lists *"peptide nucleic acid (PNA), threose nucleic acid (TNA) or glycol nucleic acid (GNA)"* as suggested pre-RNA candidates, and adds that *"the chemically plausible generation of 'simpler' nucleic acids under prebiotic conditions has yet to be demonstrated."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

These analogues are further members of the same functional group (hereditary polymer), not missing primitives that would move the wall (Fig 6 :21). They widen the shape, not the date. *They're Scanning Books* warns that *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*, so a vocabulary that admits only RNA and DNA is already a visible choice.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that one analogue was the actual ancestral hereditary polymer and that it was replaced by RNA and then DNA in a dated sequence. This would supply the missing ground, not refute the shape.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source (Wikipedia, tertiary; the underlying primary literature was not opened here). Recorded as weak evidence.

## CONCLUSION

The structural difference is tiny in atoms and decisive in behaviour (Fig 12 :94). "Replace" is a sequence bond that licences role occupancy but not molecule origin (Fig 12 :72, re-census in Q2). DNA and RNA are **not isomers**; they are two members of a hereditary-polymer **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), a retraction of my first reading. The union is ordered by dependency, with the date a parallel lookup, not a downstream step. None of this yields a date; each sub-claim carries its own falsifier.
