## Restatement in effect terms

A sender has a finite capacity budget in bits and can repeat a signal. Two outcomes are observed across systems: the repetition lowers the error rate, or the repetition spends capacity without lowering the error rate. The residual is the gap between the predicted gain from adding a copy and the observed gain, indexed per copy slot, not averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). An answer must name the quantity that flips the sign of that gain, state what an answer would look like before running the search, and bind it as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual.

## What an answer would look like (Direction)

A bound prediction of the form: *redundancy improves reliability iff the repeated signals are independent draws against the same ground, and the capacity spent on them is less than the error removed; where the repeats draw on one evidence pool, their agreement compounds while evidence does not.* That shape is written down here before the derivation and is refusable by the tests in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, a room does not add an anchor: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." (Seat Map :283). Second, selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." (Seat Map :163). Third, showing the full interior before asking for a verdict spends the checker's independence: Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
