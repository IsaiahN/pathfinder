**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the one-paragraph shape claim in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §6 has been read against the refuter below and any error corrected. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One prediction about redundancy that you can refute in two minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in communication: what decides whether a repeated signal improves reliability or just spends capacity? I have one specific prediction I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the prediction does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the sign of the gain from adding a redundant copy is decided by whether the copies are independent draws against the same ground. If they draw on one evidence pool, their agreement compounds while their evidence does not, and the added capacity buys agreement rather than reliability. If they are independent, each copy can carry a new observation, and the union has a surplus neither had alone. Crucially, copies whose correlation is fixed at origin cannot be refreshed by using them more; replacement with a different source is the only refresh. The second condition is a bargain: the capacity cost of the copy plus the error it leaves unexplained must be less than the error before the copy. Bits and the actions needed to check the copy are different currencies and do not add.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. A system with independent noise draws and low evidence overlap whose error rate does not improve at any capacity cost.
> 2. A system whose copies are correlated at or near one, sharing one cause and one evidence pool, whose *verdict* accuracy improves without any new contact with the ground.
> 3. A redundancy scheme carrying no new information about the ground that lowers the combined cost in bits and actions.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage discipline

- **Stage 1:** send only the shape claim and its three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
- **Stage 2 (opt-in):** if they are curious, send the source links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 (opt-in):** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names their own ground, writes their own refuter, and states their own mode.

No stage asks the recipient to trust the framework. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
