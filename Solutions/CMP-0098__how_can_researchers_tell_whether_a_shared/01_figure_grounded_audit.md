## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table :38-40). Here:

- **b** is what a research frame believes about what produces a mathematical pattern, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is the act of attributing: naming a mechanism, fitting a model, running an intervention, importing an explanation from another field.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what that frame's library of terms and bonds predicts should accompany the pattern if the attribution were right.
- **o′** is what the comparison actually returns when a second domain, an intervention, or a source-discriminating instrument is consulted.
- **s** is the pattern-slot: one named mathematical form observed in one named pair of domains. R is indexed per pattern-slot, never aggregated into a single cross-science verdict.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"** (Fig 1 :13)

The phenomenon to decide is not whether the two patterns look alike. That is given. It is whether the same reading has the same source. Observed: the same power-law exponent, the same network motif, the same scaling relation or the same differential form recurs in domains with no obvious shared stuff. A frame that answers "same pattern, same mechanism" by resemblance alone, and a frame that answers "same pattern, coincidence" by difference of vocabulary alone, both leave a residual. The effect description names no theory, no "universality", no "analogy", no "isomorphism". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** before the run (Formula, "Direction"): the answer would be a conjunction of conditions whose joint presence licenses "shared mechanism", an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of another, and a failure condition written in advance. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** The same line names the candidates: **"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** For this question the legitimate anchors are a held-out measurement of the pattern in each domain and an intervention that changes a candidate mechanism while the pattern is re-read. **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30). Aesthetic judgment of elegance, the prestige of the field that first reported the pattern, and agreement among researchers who read the same review all move when pushed; they are instruments or pooled opinions at best. Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a four-place relation: pattern P, domain D1, domain D2, candidate mechanism M, indexed by pattern-slot. It carries a **symmetric** bond of identity at the level of the reading (**≡**, *"two names, one referent"*, Operators :24-26; the test is *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*, Operators :27), and an **ordered** bond of production at the level of the mechanism (**→** sequence or **⇒** production, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :76). The question's ordinary phrasing slides from the first bond to the second without paying for the change. Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**
- **Symmetry.** "Same pattern" is symmetric: exchanging D1 and D2 loses nothing. "Same mechanism" is not: a mechanism produces a pattern; the pattern does not produce the mechanism. Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."** The observed equality is a reading, not a verdict about source.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view. The pattern is read inside one domain (one stratum). The mechanism claim is about a generator at another stratum. The cross-domain comparison runs at a third. The membrane between them is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21) and **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The reading domain** (pattern in each domain) | instruments, fits, exponents, motifs, datasets | description cost in bits `\|φ\|`; the pattern as a reading | yes: the held-out measurement in each domain, which does not move when a theorist renames the mechanism | admitted |
| **D2 The generator domain** (candidate mechanisms) | candidate atoms and typed bonds in Γ, interventions | actions needed to verify which bond holds (Fig 12 :125-127) | yes: an intervention or source-discriminating instrument, when one exists | admitted, conditional on an instrument |
| **D3 The cross-domain comparison domain** | two frames with different closures | independence: how little they draw on the same evidence (Fig 2 :31) | yes: two independent instruments, each ranging against its own ground | admitted |
| **D4 The provenance domain** | dated records, routes, lineage | provenance and shadow test | yes: the record of where each atom came from (Fig 8 :50-52; Fig 13 :126-130) | admitted |
| **X1 "Mathematical elegance makes the mechanism self-evident"** | the pattern alone | aesthetic force | **no** anchor independent of the reading itself; a frame would score itself with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :27) | not a domain yet, excluded |
| **X2 "Same equation, therefore same mechanism"** | the pattern and its name | resemblance | this is the claim under test, not an anchor; already inside D2 as a candidate that must pay | absorbed, not separate |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that elegance never guides discovery.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: the reading is produced by a generator, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D3 and D4 overlap: independence is read from a provenance record, so that overlap is a second compound. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1 Source discrimination.** A discriminator exists, at some resolution, and returns different readings for different sources. This is the cheapest and first question, and the other three assume its answer.
- **C2 Independence.** Two frames with differing closures, low evidence overlap, each ranged separately against a ground that does not update on the other's report.
- **C3 Composition and provenance.** The candidate mechanism is a priced composition of atoms and typed bonds, and the record separates convergent derivation from adopted import.
- **C4 Habitat re-check.** Capability is agent-and-habitat, so the mechanism must be re-tested in each domain's habitat rather than assumed to travel with the formula.

A pattern that passes C3 and fails C2, and a pattern that passes C2 and fails C1, are not different degrees of one failure. They need different instruments. The essay *The Library of Babel* states the same stop: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the pattern be read reliably in each domain?) → 2 vocabulary (do we have terms for the **bond**, not only the ingredient list? Fig 12 :5) → 3 objective (what would count as a failure of "same mechanism"?) → 4 planning (design the discriminator or intervention) → 5 learn and carry (record the route and the refusal). The middle link, having a term for the source of a reading and saying what would count as discriminating it, is the one with no instrument in most cross-domain work. Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** Most effort lands above the break, where pattern-matching tools already exist.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about cross-domain research, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1 - A shared reading is not a shared source (decisive, structural)

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 8, low topical overlap: composition notation vs provenance).**

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)
> **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."** (Fig 12 :95)
> **"This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."** (Fig 12 :97)
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** (Fig 8 :51)
> **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."** (Fig 13 :128)

The mathematical pattern is a reading of a composed term. The bond that produced it is not recoverable from the operands. The ingredient list, that is, the equation written on the page, records half of the thing. So "same pattern" licenses only an identity claim at the level of the reading: **"two names, one referent"** (Operators :24). "Same mechanism" is a claim about which bond and which route produced it, which is an ordered, provenance-bearing claim. Moving from the first to the second without an instrument is the exact error the figures name.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the mathematical form alone determines which intervention will break it, with no ground reading and no lineage. If such a form-to-intervention map exists and is checked, F1 is false.

**World bearing (secondary only).** *Convergent evolution* (Wikipedia, 2026) defines the analogous case: **"Convergent evolution is the independent evolution of similar features in species of different lineages."** The same form, different route. *Spurious relationship* (Wikipedia, 2026) names the statistical case: association without causal relation due to a common unseen factor. Both corroborate F1 from genuinely low-overlap fields. Neither is a figure line.

### F2 - The question slides from a symmetric bond to an ordered one

**Mode: `general`. Domain D2. Status: single-source for the bond definition (Operators table, Fig 12), triangulated with the physics of critical phenomena only at a different stratum.**

The observation "the exponent is the same in both domains" is symmetric: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* for identity (Operators :15 style test applied to ≡ at :27). The attribution "the same generator produced it" is ordered: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** (Fig 12 :76); the test is *"is there a B before A fires? No."* (Fig 12 :78). Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** A frame that treats the two claims as one has silently replaced a symmetric bond with an ordered one. The price of that replacement is an intervention, because only an intervention can show that B was not waiting before A fired.

**Refuter:** a case where the pattern itself carries a timestamped route from generator to reading, so the ordered claim is readable off the symmetric one without any intervention. No such reading is held here.

### F3 - The decision is a union of four conditions, not one test

**Mode: `specified`. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, but the essay is framework-internal, so one witness).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 (quoted in section 1.3) are the governing lines. The four unbundled conditions have different instruments:

- A C1 failure shows up as two candidate mechanisms that stay indistinguishable at every available resolution; the honest report is `unreachable`, not "same mechanism" (Fig 6 :34, Fig 8 :39).
- A C2 failure shows up as agreement among frames that read the same report; the agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :18-21).
- A C3 failure shows up as two mechanisms with the same atom list but no recorded bond, or as an import with no source gate (Fig 12 :5; Fig 8 :12-13).
- A C4 failure shows up as a mechanism that works in one habitat and not the other, with no change in the formula (Fig 11 :39-40, :66-69).

A single lever, for example "fit the same model in both domains and compare fits", clears the region where only C1 holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). F3 is why the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Refuter:** a single instrument that discriminates source, guarantees frame independence, prices the bond and reproduces the habitat in all domains at once, with no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### F4 - A discriminator descends from an instrument already returning something

**Mode: `general` for the instrument rule, `specified` for its application. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 6 and the source essay, low overlap between instrument theory and the metamerism example, though the essay is framework-internal).**

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** (Fig 6 :32)
> **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."** (Fig 6 :33)
> **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."** (Fig 6 :34)

From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened directly: **"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"** and **"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."** The instrument that resolves source is not an argument about the pattern. It is a discriminator built from a reading that already fails to resolve. In practice: a perturbation that changes the exponent in one candidate mechanism but not the other; a knockout that removes the proposed generator and leaves the pattern; a dated lineage that shows the two frames reached the same term separately.

**Refuter:** a case where no reading at any resolution registers a difference between the two sources, and yet a correct source attribution is later confirmed by an instrument that was not built from any prior reading. That would break the descent rule in Fig 6 :32-34.

### F5 - Independence decides whether agreement means anything

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 8, low overlap: anchor rule vs union surplus).**

> **"collapse 2: common cause"** (Fig 2 :6) and **"(hidden shared source)"** (Fig 2 :7)
> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** (Fig 2 :31)
> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** (Fig 2 :31)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."** (Fig 8 :50)

A shared pattern found by two teams using the same dataset, the same review and the same fitting convention is one witness wearing two names. The common cause is literally the figure's collapse 2. When two instruments with different evidence pools reach the same term separately, that is **"corroboration at maximum independence"** (Fig 8 :50), but even then only the record of where each came from separates convergence from import (Fig 8 :51). **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** (Fig 2 :42).

**Refuter:** a case in which two frames demonstrably drawing on one evidence pool produce a correct source attribution that a low-overlap pair gets wrong, with the shared-pool success replicated. That would break F5.

**World bearing.** The *Spurious relationship* page names the common-cause structure; the *Universality class* page supplies a positive case where two different microscopic models genuinely share a coarse generator. Both corroborate the distinction from different fields.

### F6 - Same atoms, different bond: isomer, not identity

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 13, low topical overlap: bond definition vs chemistry precedent).**

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."** (Fig 13 :122)
> **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** (Fig 13 :121)

Two candidate mechanisms built from the same parts, joined by different typed bonds, are isomers under Fig 12 :117: the difference is invisible in the ingredient list and decisive in behaviour. Two mechanisms that reach the same substance by different routes are convergence under Fig 13 :121. Both produce a shared pattern. They are opposite verdicts on "shared mechanism", and only the bond record plus the lineage tells them apart. If the candidates share only a `state → structure → state` shape while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :123).

**Refuter:** two mechanisms with identical atoms and identical bonds that nevertheless diverge in their interventions with no habitat difference and no unmeasured bond. That breaks the isomer claim and moves the explanation to habitat (D4/C4).

### F7 - A mechanism that travels is a method, not a recording

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 4 and Fig 11, low overlap: membrane vs habitat substitution).**

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22)
> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** (Fig 4 :25)
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** (Fig 4 :30)
> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** (Fig 11 :66)

When a mechanism is carried from one domain to another as a finished equation, what crosses is a recording of one occasion. It can be replayed, but it cannot generate a new reading in the second habitat unless the bond and the conditions it required were carried too. The test is the round trip: carry the method up, bring it down into the second domain, and measure R_T. A zero assumed gap is dead reckoning. **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** (Fig 11 :70).

**Refuter:** a mechanism transferred as a bare formula that generates new ground-verified readings in a substituted habitat with no bond record and no measured gap, repeatedly. That would break Fig 4 :22-25 and Fig 11 :66-70.

### F8 - Cost is in two currencies and does not decide the verdict

**Mode: `general`. Domain D2. Status: figure-derived.**

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** (Fig 12 :125)
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** (Fig 12 :126)
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** (Fig 12 :130)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

A pattern that is cheap to describe (a short exponent, a simple power law) and a mechanism that is expensive to verify (a decade-long intervention) are priced in different units. A more parsimonious equation is a budget filter, not a witness for "same mechanism". Deciding the verdict by fitting cost alone is the named error.

**Refuter:** a case where description cost alone predicts which of two mechanisms will survive an intervention, prospectively and per pattern-slot, with no independent ground reading. Then F8's methodological consequence is false.

### F9 - The standing list of "universality rules" is refused

**Mode: `general` for the refusal, `specified` for the separator. Domain D1. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 9).**

> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)

The question asks how researchers can tell, in general, which patterns indicate mechanism. Fig 11 :51 structurally refuses a standing list of tell-tale features: a feature that signals shared mechanism for one residual signals resemblance for another. What survives is the shape of a check, not a catalogue. **"what it returns is a shape rather than a name"** (Fig 13 :133).

**Refuter:** a standing feature list that correctly classifies shared-mechanism versus superficial-resemblance across a change of residual, prospectively, with no ground readings used in its construction.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. The mathematical form alone determines the intervention that will break it, with no ground reading and no lineage (breaks F1, Fig 12 :5, :95).
2. The pattern carries a readable route from generator to reading, so the ordered claim follows from the symmetric one without an intervention (breaks F2).
3. Two frames demonstrably drawing on one evidence pool produce a correct source attribution that a low-overlap pair gets wrong, replicated (breaks F5, Fig 2 :31, :40).
4. Two mechanisms with identical atoms and identical bonds diverge in interventions with no habitat difference and no unmeasured bond (breaks F6, Fig 12 :117).
5. A bare formula repeatedly generates new ground-verified readings in a substituted habitat with no bond record and no measured round trip (breaks F7, Fig 4 :22-25).
6. A source-discriminating instrument returns one reading for two mechanisms and still identifies which produced it (breaks the metamerism claim in *The Hard Problem is About Source*).
7. A figure line's claim is derived by a system that holds no ground (breaks Fig 2 :29).

A single firing retracts the corresponding finding. No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a source-discriminating instrument has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator and its refuter, not with a theory of universality (Fig 10 :52, **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**).
2. Index the claim per pattern-slot and per domain. A single "cross-domain mechanism score" is a pooled reading (Fig 1 :22; Fig 10 :26).
3. Ask, at each comparison, which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat the equation as Γ, a candidate library whose bond and route must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 12 :5; Fig 1 :27).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Two teams with one dataset are one witness.
6. Record the lineage: which atom arrived by convergence, which by import, with dates (Fig 8 :50-52; Fig 10 :62-64).
7. Measure the round trip at every transfer (Fig 4 :30). A pattern that survives the trip unchanged is a candidate mechanism; a pattern that does not is a recording.
8. Use the world literature as a set of low-overlap witnesses and budget filters, never as the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
9. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
10. Stop at the shape. Do not name which mechanism produced a given pattern without a discriminator; report `unreachable` if none exists (Fig 13 :134, **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**; Fig 8 :39, **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**).
