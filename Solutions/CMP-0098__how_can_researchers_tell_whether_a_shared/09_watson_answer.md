## QUESTION

How can researchers tell whether a shared mathematical pattern reflects a shared mechanism or only a superficial resemblance? Restated in effect: when one form P is read in domains D1 and D2, what would make the attribution "one generator produced P in both" pay, and what would make "different generators produced the same reading" pay? R is the gap between what a proposed attribution predicts and what a discriminator or a second domain returns, indexed per pattern-slot and never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). The sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term before the run: a separator of conditions, an instrument per condition, and a failure condition written in advance.

Symmetry: "same pattern" is a symmetric identity claim, "same mechanism" is an ordered production claim; the two are not interchangeable. Scale: pattern read within a domain, generator claim at another stratum, cross-domain comparison at a third, with a lossy membrane between them (Fig 4).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives: **a shared pattern is a reading and cannot decide its own source; the answer is a four-condition separator (source-discriminating instrument, independent frames ranged against unmoved grounds, priced composition with provenance, habitat re-check with a measured round-trip gap), with verdicts kept per pattern-slot and per domain and never averaged.** Shared mechanism and superficial resemblance are both real outcomes, separated by what breaks the pattern and by the recorded route, not by the form. The universality case is the positive boundary; convergent evolution, common cause and equifinality are the negative cases. Which mechanism produced a given pattern in a given pair of domains is a declared `gap` of the source-discriminator kind, reported as unreached rather than silence. Mode `specified` on the separator, `general` on the reading-versus-source structure, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected method. A team failing C1 would be told to import another theory when it owes a discriminator; a team failing C2 would be sold more agreement when it owes independence; a team failing C3 would be given a formula with no bond record; a team failing C4 would replay a success in a substituted habitat. The cost is real but bounded and reversible, because the packet states the failure conditions in advance. The larger cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a separator, which Fig 2 :42 prices by asking us to buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added, not because anything was struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The separator and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-65).
- **reversed by:** any of the seven pre-registered falsifiers in `01` §4, in particular a form that determines its own breaking intervention, a shared-pool pair that beats a low-overlap pair at attribution, a bare formula that generates new readings in a substituted habitat, or an instrument that distinguishes sources while returning one reading. A ground reading settles the `gap`: an intervention that removes a proposed generator and changes the pattern in one domain but not the other would name the source. Agreement, prestige and fit statistics are not such instruments.
