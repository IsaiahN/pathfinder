# Solution CMP-0098

**Question:** How can researchers tell whether a shared mathematical pattern reflects a shared mechanism or only a superficial resemblance?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 119

**Section:** 10. Intervention, control, and cross-domain theory

**Mode:** `specified` on the four-condition separator and on every per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape that a reading cannot decide its own source, `gap` on which mechanism actually produced a given pattern in a given pair of domains. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms.** Researchers observe one mathematical form P in two domains and must decide between two attributions: a shared generator produced P in both, or different generators produced the same reading. The residual is the gap between what a proposed mechanism predicts about P and what the cross-domain comparison returns, indexed per pattern-slot and never averaged. The sought-for answer was bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a separator of conditions, an instrument that discriminates them, and a pre-registered refuter. It is not a catalogue of patterns and not a universal test that works without a ground.

**Key verdict (in one line).** A reading cannot decide its own source: the figures return a four-condition separator (source-discriminating instrument, independent frames ranged against unmoved grounds, priced composition with provenance, and habitat re-check), not a decision procedure; genuinely shared mechanism and superficial resemblance are both real outcomes, distinguished only by what breaks the pattern and by the lineage, and the mechanism actually responsible for a given pattern is a declared `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the separator.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: a list selected for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Seat Map :163; Fig 2 :39-41); a room cannot substitute for a ground (Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*); and a recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict can no longer supply an outside check (Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*). Legally, unsolicited bulk outreach to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Suggested next step

Take `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 to one public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv` at a time. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure of the separator. Record the answer before showing any theory. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
