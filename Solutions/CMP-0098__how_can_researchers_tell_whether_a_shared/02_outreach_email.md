# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have a public institutional or opt-in route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` separator with no measured source attribution, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the separator, never endorsement of it. If no failure condition can be shown that the recipient can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about cross-domain pattern matching before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about cross-domain research that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when the same mathematical pattern appears in two domains, the pattern itself cannot tell you whether the same mechanism produced it. A reading does not carry the bond or the route that made it. So a decision needs four conditions, and a failure of one is not a failure of the others:
>
> 1. **A discriminator.** Can anything, at any resolution, produce different readings for the two proposed sources? If nothing has ever registered a difference, the honest answer is that the source is out of reach at this budget, not that the mechanisms are the same.
> 2. **Independent frames.** Do the two groups drawing on the pattern use different evidence, or are they reading the same report? Two people reading one report are one witness. Agreement from a single pool is not a check.
> 3. **Composition and provenance.** Is the proposed mechanism a priced arrangement of parts and bonds, with a record showing whether the two fields reached it separately or one borrowed it from the other? Same parts under different bonds are different mechanisms even when the formula looks the same.
> 4. **Habitat.** Does the mechanism still pay when the surrounding conditions change? A formula that works in one habitat and not another may be a real mechanism in both, with different contacts. The arrangement has to be carried as a method, and the loss across the transfer measured.
>
> The claim is that these are four different failure modes, not four degrees of one problem, and that a single "same pattern" test clears only one of them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that shared patterns never indicate shared mechanisms. Universality classes are a real positive case where different microscopic models share exponents because a common coarse generator can be identified. I am not claiming that my method can name the mechanism behind a given pattern, that it supplies a catalogue of which patterns are diagnostic, that it is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of cross-domain transfer. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a mathematical form that by itself determines the intervention that will break it, with no ground reading and no lineage;
> 2. a case where two groups drawing on one evidence pool get the source right and a genuinely independent pair gets it wrong, repeatedly;
> 3. two mechanisms with identical parts and identical bonds that behave differently with no habitat difference and no unmeasured bond;
> 4. a bare formula that keeps producing new ground-verified readings after being moved into a substituted habitat, with no bond record and no measured transfer loss;
> 5. an instrument that gives the same reading for two mechanisms and still identifies which one produced it.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers: the canonical figure extract kept in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a cross-domain comparison you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Four failure modes kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended "transfer score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No universal diagnostic list | *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* (Fig 11 :51) |
| Positive case admitted openly | *"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms"* (Fig 13 :122); refusing the universality case would be a strain, not a check |
| Links only after a verdict | *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read 2026-11-22) |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check what it is looking for; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* (Fig 10 :59)
