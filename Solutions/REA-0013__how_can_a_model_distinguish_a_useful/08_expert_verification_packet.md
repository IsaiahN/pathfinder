# Expert verification packet: distinguishing a useful regularity from a coincidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a machine learning researcher, statistician or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the shape, `general` for the structural claims, not `grounded`.** No experiment at new contact is claimed here. This is a discriminating test shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A training-fit score cannot by itself discriminate a useful regularity from a coincidence, because it is produced by the same frame it is asked to grade. A discriminator has the shape of a union of three conditions: (i) the candidate pays a compression bargain against the whole corpus and the residual is read per object slot, so a term that fits a single occasion fails; (ii) the candidate is exposed to contact not produced by the frame and the anchor is shown to be reachable, since a gate is not the ground and a held-out split is a substituted habitat that proves wiring, never capability; (iii) every confirming frame carries provenance, since agreement from one evidence pool is the pool talking. The conditions do not average into one score, and description cost and testing cost are two currencies that do not add. A particular candidate is settled by a ground reading, not by further agreement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the one-occasion failure is the figure's own definition of a bad term

- **Statement (framework-internal plus statistics-facing):** a candidate that fits one narrow subset perfectly is the term the acceptance test refuses, and the refusal is computed over the whole corpus with the per-slot leftovers counted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43-44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."
- **World-facing check:** the minimum description length literature reaches the same one-occasion failure from a different pool. In the biased-coin example, "there is not enough evidence to support the hypothesis of the biased coin, even though the best element of the second model class provides better fit to the data." (W1, Wikipedia summary of Rissanen/Grunwald). Check whether the fitted hypothesis class, not just its best member, is paid for.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a candidate that fits one occasion perfectly, pays `cost(phi) + left(R,phi) < cost(R)` over the full corpus **and** survives ground contact without a drop. Then the one-occasion clause does not discriminate.

### C2: a gate is not the ground, and a held-out split is a substituted habitat

- **Statement (framework-internal):** clearing the three mint guards and the MDL bargain is necessary and not sufficient. Settlement requires contact the frame did not produce.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :32: "then check it against the ground, then carry it up". Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."
- **World-facing check:** cross-validation "only yields meaningful results if the validation set and training set are drawn from the same population and only if human biases are controlled" (W7). The same source reports that swap sampling with blinded modelers predicted external validity better than ordinary cross-validation. Does the proposed discriminator include a condition that would fail on a substituted habitat?
- **Refuter:** a recorded experiment in which a term that cleared all internal gates and the bargain was settled as useful by ground contact alone, with no new instrument and no independent frame. Then the gate/ground distinction collapses.

### C3: agreement from one evidence pool is not corroboration

- **Statement (framework-internal):** two frames drawing on the same corpus add no evidentiary weight to a world claim. A confirming frame must differ in closure and carry provenance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **World-facing check:** inductive bias and no-free-lunch results mean a test carries its own assumptions (W3, W6). Two implementations of the same biased test do not make the bias disappear. Does the design record which confirmations came from a different closure?
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that two frames drawing on the same evidence pool add evidentiary weight to a world claim. Since Fig 2 :31 defines independence as "how little they draw on the same evidence", this would force a re-census of the anchor rule itself.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named universal discriminator, not a threshold value, not a product recommendation.
- Not that MDL, cross-validation, or any other method settles the question. Each is a gate or a substituted habitat until the ground pays.
- Not that a large reader list or a prestigious reviewer is evidence. A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35).
- Not that a search inside the same corpus can prove a pattern is not a coincidence ("Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room.", Fig 9 :44).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle a candidate

1. Name the new contact: a habitat not produced by the training frame, with the conditions the goal requires enumerated rather than assumed (Fig 11 :43-44).
2. Report the per-slot residual there, not just a global mean (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. Record the provenance of each confirming frame, and state its closure difference (Fig 8 :51, Fig 2 :31).
4. State the mode of the result: `specified` until the ground pays, `grounded` only after it does.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :43-44, Fig 11 :70 and Fig 2 :40, three short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against a case you know: did a one-occasion fit ever survive genuinely new contact? If yes, send the case, it fires refuter 2.
3. Check C3 against your review experience: did agreement among teams sharing a dataset ever function as independent corroboration? If yes, send the case, it fires refuter 3.
4. Name one instrument or design that would change contact rather than the metric. Or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
