# Outreach draft: check the discriminating test first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked by a ground you can name. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One testable claim about regularity versus coincidence, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A model holds a pattern extracted from a corpus. Two options: compose over it as a useful regularity, or withhold it as a coincidence. The question is what could tell these apart that is not just the fit that produced the pattern. A training-fit number is produced by the same frame it is asked to grade, so by the framework's own rule it cannot be the metric that settles the question. This is a structural claim, not a claim about your favourite method.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The distinction is a union of three conditions, not one score:
>
> 1. **A bargain on the whole corpus.** A coincidence is exactly the term that fits one occasion perfectly. The term must cost less to state, plus what it still leaves unexplained, than the confusion it removes across all object slots. A global average is not enough; the residual is indexed per slot.
> 2. **Contact not produced by the frame.** Clearing the internal gates is necessary and not sufficient. The ground settles the term, and a gate is not the ground. A held-out split from the same corpus is a substituted habitat: it proves wiring, never capability.
> 3. **Provenance for any confirmation.** Two models trained on the same pool are one witness spelled twice. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.
>
> This is a shape any answer must have. It is not a measurement and it does not name your favourite regulariser as the answer.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that a particular codelength or cross-validation scheme settles the question; that a good validation score proves a regularity is real; that a large committee of readers provides the missing ground. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is the pool talking, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this email):**
> 1. A recorded ground reading in which a quantity produced only by the model, with no outside contact, settles a regularity/coincidence distinction.
> 2. A candidate that fits one occasion perfectly, pays the compression bargain over the full corpus, and then survives ground contact without a drop.
> 3. Two frames drawing on the same evidence pool shown to add evidentiary weight to a world claim.
> 4. A substituted habitat that reproduces every condition the goal required, with no unintended imports, whose synthetic solve then transfers to capability.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs can be simulated rather than derived. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
