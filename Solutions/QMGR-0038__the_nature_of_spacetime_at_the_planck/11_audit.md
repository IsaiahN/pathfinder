## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 13 :41, :43-44, :74-75, :104, :124-125, :134-135, Fig 9 :27, :32, :57, Fig 4 :27, :30 and Fig 7 :6.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` §9 and `07`: spacetime is a derivative with a structure in the slot; continuity and discreteness are residues; no privileged bottom level; the microstructure is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the physical claim the question asks for: spacetime is discrete at 10^-35 m, or continuous there, or has a minimum length.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **primitive/residue distinction**: both horns of the dichotomy become "a primitive" in the concretisation, which is the error Fig 13 :43-44 removes;
- the **level/length distinction**: "no bottom" becomes "no minimum physical length", which F3 re-census separates;
- the **unbundling**: the three conditions are recombined into one question, contrary to Fig 9 :57;
- the **import gate**: a candidate microstructure is treated as if it were derived, contrary to Fig 13 :115-118 and Fig 8's source gate.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a structure must sit between space and time, and the slot is not selected by the six. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
