## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are continuity and discreteness isomers, or members?

- Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." A seventh member
  must be irreducible to the six.
- Fig 13 :43-44: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a
  spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what
  its own members produce."
- Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular
  composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."
- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the
  formula and decisive in the behaviour."

So continuity and discreteness are **not** members and **not** isomers in the strict sense either,
because an isomer is a composition of the same atoms under different bonds (Fig 12 :117) and both
are *properties of a composition*, not compositions themselves. The accurate term is **residue**:
what a turn leaves behind. A smooth continuum is what a gapless spectrum over space and time leaves;
a discrete order is what a locally finite network of relations leaves. They differ at the level of
the bond arrangement, not the atom list. This is triangulated across Fig 13 :43-44, Fig 13 :124-125
and Fig 12 :117 (low overlap between the minimality argument, the derivatives list and the bond
definition).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if an instrument shows that continuity or discreteness is
irreducible to the six, the residue reading fails and a seventh member is owed. Until then both are
`specified` residues, never primitives.

### Q2 — Does a smallest scale introduce an order?

- `→` is sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :30, via the exemplar). Fig 13
  :75 writes the composition with commas, not arrows, so the figure does not introduce an ordering
  at the level of the kinds.
- Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is
  bonded to is part of what it is." So a directedness can be attached to a state, but it is a bond
  choice, not a primitive.
- In the world frame, causal sets take "spacetime is fundamentally discrete" and relate events by a
  partial order, with Sorkin's "Order + Number = Geometry" (Wikipedia, *Causal sets*, read
  2026-11-23). This is a binding from the physics frame onto Spectrum (ordering), Network (relation)
  and Energy (finiteness of counting), all atoms already held. It is **not** a seventh atom and it
  does not move the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" adds none here). If asserted as a derivation
  rather than a binding, it is an apophenia error: frame chosen first, residual second (Fig 9 :27).
- Loop quantum gravity similarly reports a granular structure of space at the Planck length
  (Wikipedia, *Quantum gravity*, read 2026-11-23), a binding onto Network plus Spectrum with Energy
  as the countable quantity. Again unproven as a binding.

**Falsifier:** if any candidate requires an ordering that cannot be written as a bond among held
atoms, the binding fails and an import with a source gate is owed.

### Q3 — What order is the union of conditions?

- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7:
  "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- The three conditions (composition, continuum/discreteness, bottom) are processed in this order:
  1. composition: a structure must sit between space and time (Fig 13 :75), slot unselected;
  2. continuum/discreteness: both are residues (Fig 13 :43-44, :124-125), so the debate is
     mis-posed as a primitive question;
  3. bottom: no privileged level of irreducibility (Fig 13 :104), but the physical minimum length is
     a ground question and stays `gap`.
- This is a dependency order, not a temporal one. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom
  for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
  Each settled first-order result becomes an operand for the next; nothing here composes across
  scales without a round trip (Fig 4 :27).

The cognition analogue is the only low-overlap witness. By Fig 2 :40 ("agreement about the world is
worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"), its independence is real but its
relevance is structural only. It corroborates the *possibility* of a continuous residue from discrete
steps; it says nothing about 10^-35 m. Using it as physical evidence would be a strained reading, and
strain sends us back to the census, not to a softer verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Continuity and discreteness are residues of composition, not members of the six and not strict
isomers of one another. A smallest scale introduces an ordering only as a bond choice among held
atoms, never as a new primitive. The union of the question's conditions has a dependency order:
settle the composition, then classify the residues, then leave the floor to the ground. The one
low-overlap analogue (phi phenomenon) corroborates the shape of the residue claim; the high-overlap
physics analogues describe candidates that this derivation neither confirms nor refutes.

Both falsifiers are stated in advance. If either fires, the structure claim is withdrawn. Nothing
here is `grounded`.
