## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether a general theory is elegant, but whether the residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| can stay small in each domain while one kernel is shared across domains. R is indexed per domain-slot and never aggregated: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

- Option A, "generality is a shared cast": the same entities, mechanisms and quantities are asserted in every domain. This leads to predictions fitted to the source domain and failing at the target domain's ground. Domain-specific differences are erased by construction.
- Option B, "generality is a shared kernel with a per-domain cast": the loop and the rule for naming what worked transfer; the actors, currency and anchor are supplied by each domain; each domain's ground settles its own predictions. This leads to testable predictions whose residual is reported per domain and whose round-trip gap is measured.

The shape an answer would have, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): two conditions that must both hold, C1 (a method that crosses) and C2 (a per-domain anchor that can refuse), plus an instrument that measures what the crossing dropped, the round-trip gap R_T. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B, conditional in substance, with C1 and C2 kept separate as a union and never averaged. The factual question of whether any actual theory satisfies both in all domains is a declared `gap`, reported as a verdict and not silently filled.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the question, and no claim that the Tether framework is the only true general theory of complex systems.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." and Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting readers for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" A list selected for likely agreement is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.

A fifth reason is specific to this question: the framework is itself a candidate general theory of complex systems, so it cannot be a neutral witness for the possibility claim. Fig 8 :34 "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe." This is stated explicitly in `01`, `05`, `09` and `11`.
