## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's conclusions, in WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a general theory's prediction in a domain and that domain's ground return. Arity: one kernel related to N domains, each indexed by domain-slot, so a family rather than a single two-place prediction. Symmetry: ordered; the kernel applies to a domain, not conversely. The cross-domain claim is a threshold comparison (⋛), not a symmetric join. Scale: method level and instance level with a lossy membrane, Fig 4 :19-20.
- **D1 Kernel level.** Actors: loop, bonds, negation, six-term substrate, naming rule. Currency: reach and settlement. Anchor: figures for design questions, the instance's ground for a measurement. Admitted.
- **D2 Source domain.** Actors: the entities the kernel was distilled from. Currency: its predictions. Anchor: its instrument. Admitted.
- **D3 Target domain.** Actors: different entities. Currency: different predictions. Anchor: its own ground, which must not be movable by the theory. Admitted.
- **D4 Verification frame.** Actors: readers in both domains. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement; independence must be low overlap. Admitted.
- **X1 groundless theory**, no nameable anchor, not a domain. **X2 averaged differences**, the disqualified operation at Fig 1 :22. **X3 terminal theory**, excluded at Fig 7 :6.

**Union, not partition.** The two conditions C1 (method crosses) and C2 (local ground refuses) overlap. A theory with only C1 produces untestable assertions; a theory with only C2 is not general. Dependency order: describe R, name the anchor, state the objective, plan the transfer, accept or refuse, measure the round trip (Fig 3). My census matches the primary's four domains and union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 4 and Fig 10, I derived from Fig 1 slot indexing and Fig 3 dependency order. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)), measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** Fig 4 :19-20 (kernel versus cast), Fig 2 :29/:45 (anchor and ranging), Fig 4 :23-25 (method not recording), Fig 9 :30-32 (effect description, shape not a name), Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand C1 and C2 as a union plus M; transfer the method, leave the cast in the domain, measure what the crossing dropped, never average the residual.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim "kernel K predicts domain D" with D's actors, currency and anchor filled in.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down without extra work:

- the **name of D's anchor**, which no kernel supplies and which must be found in D (Fig 2 :29);
- the **semantics of D's terms**, which cannot be carried unchanged from the source domain (Fig 9 :30-31; Marks et al 2019);
- the **evidence that the transfer was a method and not a recording**, which requires a provenance record (Fig 8 :51-52).

One thing survives cleanly: the kernel must be re-applied against a ground that can refuse, and what is shared is the loop rather than the cast. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
