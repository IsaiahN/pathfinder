# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 claim below must be offered as a shape with its refuter attached. Do not lead with the framework or with any claim that it proves the answer.

---

**Subject:** Check one output on cross-domain prediction before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** can a general theory of complex systems make testable predictions across domains without erasing the differences that matter in each domain?
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the application, not `grounded`):** yes, but only under a two-part condition. What transfers between domains is the method, the loop and the rule for naming what worked. What does not transfer is the cast: the actors, the currency and the anchor belong to each domain. A prediction is testable in a domain only if that domain supplies a ground that can refuse it and does not move when the theory pushes on it. The two conditions overlap, so they cannot be averaged into one score. What the transfer gave up is measured by a round trip, reported per domain and never pooled.
>
> The failure mode is specific. If a general theory carries the record of a success in one domain into another instead of a re-applicable method, it replays rather than predicts. Agreement between its users is then a chain of mirrors, not corroboration.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any existing framework, including the one I work on, has actually done this in every domain. Whether some theory fills the shape is an open question, and I report it as open rather than filling it with a name. I am also not claiming that domain differences are reducible to a common substrate, that a general theory can replace domain-specific instruments, or that this is a theory of everything. The framework explicitly has no last room, and it cannot certify its own possibility: it is a candidate instance of the very class the question asks about, not a neutral witness.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a theory that transfers the source domain's cast as if it were the kernel and still gets an accepted ground reading in the target domain;
> 2. user agreement shown to be sufficient for a test in a domain whose ground cannot refuse the prediction;
> 3. replaying one domain's recorded success independently confirmed in the second domain with no re-specification;
> 4. a transfer whose round-trip gap is demonstrated to be zero at the relevant resolution;
> 5. an averaged cross-domain score that still detects the failing domain without a per-slot instrument;
> 6. reach in a target domain extended by pure internal composition, with no new atom, differing frame or provenance record.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG and markdown): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative and the cross-domain derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every claim | "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate." |
| Self-application declared | Fig 8 :34 "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe." The framework cannot witness for the possibility claim it answers. |
| The factual open question left open | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". |
| Per-domain residual, never average | Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." |
| Method offered, recording withheld | Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." |
| No prestige ask | "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Ask users to write their own refuter | Formula "Direction": a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so the sought-for shape must be made an ordinary term before the run. |
