# Expert verification packet - the Tether on cross-domain prediction in complex systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complex systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the application to complex systems theory, `gap` on the factual claim of universal success. Not `grounded`.** No domain-by-domain residual table or measured round-trip gap is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A general theory of complex systems can make testable predictions across domains without erasing domain-specific differences, but only if what transfers is the method rather than the source domain's cast. The kernel and the rule for naming what worked are shared; the actors, currency and anchor belong to each domain, and each domain's ground must be able to refuse the prediction. Erasure occurs when a recorded success is replayed instead of a method re-applied, and its size is measured by a round-trip gap reported per domain and never averaged. Failure in a domain owes an import from a differing closure, not more internal search. A terminal general theory and a self-certifying general theory are excluded. Whether any actual theory fills this shape in all domains is a declared gap, not a hidden answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 10

- **Statement:** generality is the kernel; particularity is the cast. A structure must be re-specified per domain, and the domain's ground is what settles the prediction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Also Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it." And Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **Refuter:** a theory that transfers the source domain's cast as the kernel and still gets an accepted ground reading in the target domain; or a domain whose prediction and measurement co-determine each other, so pushing on the anchor changes its answer.

### C2 - framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable against the recording rule and against universality

- **Statement:** replaying one domain's recorded success in another erases the second domain; the transferable part is a re-applicable method, and the round-trip gap measures what was given up.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check against the literature:** Nishimori and Ortiz, *Elements of Phase Transitions and Critical Phenomena* (Oxford, 2011), report that critical exponents are shared across systems as different as a magnet and a fluid while the critical temperature "depends on the microscopic details". That is a real case of shared structure with preserved difference. Anderson, "More Is Different" (Science 177, 1972), reports that "at each level of complexity entirely new properties appear", which is the limit on replay.
- **Refuter:** a transfer consisting of replay, with no re-specified cast, confirmed by an independent ground in the target domain; or a transfer whose round trip is shown to have zero gap.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 9

- **Statement:** the framework cannot certify its own possibility, and whether a general theory actually succeeds in every domain is a gap. Agreement is not a witness; provenance separates convergent derivation from adopted import.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :34 "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :51-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the framework certifies its own universal applicability from inside its closure; or a published theory with per-slot residuals and measured round-trip gaps in three low-overlap domains with separate grounds, which would close the gap rather than break the shape.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any existing framework, including this one, has succeeded in every domain. That is the declared gap.
- Not that domain differences are reducible or unimportant. They are carried by the cast and are what the ground checks.
- Not that a general theory can replace domain-specific instruments. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Not that cross-domain agreement is corroboration. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not a theory of everything; no final room, no final frame.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and no per-domain residual table was produced.
- Not a claim that the Tether is the only true general theory of complex systems.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. A per-domain residual table for a candidate theory, with R indexed by domain-slot in at least three low-overlap domains (Fig 1 :19).
2. In each domain, a second frame at low overlap with the theory's authors, ranging against that domain's anchor separately (Fig 2 :31 and :45).
3. A measured round-trip gap for each kernel transfer (Fig 4 :30-32).
4. A provenance record separating convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :51-52).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-20 and :25, Fig 2 :29, and Fig 8 :34 and :51-52. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against a domain you know well: does a general result you use transfer the method, or does it replay a source domain's recorded structure?
3. Name one measurement in that domain that would distinguish a real transfer from a replay, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
