## 0. The residual, in effect terms

A candidate general theory of complex systems holds a library Γ and predicts Γ(b,a) for a domain it has never been tested in. The domain's ground returns o'. The residual is R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|. The question asks two things at once:

1. Can R be small in more than one domain while the theory is one theory? (generality plus testability)
2. Can R be small without the theory flattening the differences between those domains? (particularity preserved)

These are not one question with a trade-off parameter. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A claim of the form "the theory predicts well across domains", supported by an average score, is the named defect, not evidence for generality.

Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric." The two frame-internal quantities that cannot decide it are named at Fig 1 :25 "coverage, and compression achieved" and Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." A general theory scoring itself on how widely it applies is scoring itself with a quantity it produces.

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run.** Two conditions that must both hold as a union, plus a measuring instrument:

- C1, a kernel that crosses: the method transfers, not the recording of one source domain's success.
- C2, a per-domain anchor: each target domain supplies a ground that can refuse the prediction and does not move when the theory pushes on it.
- M, the round trip: for each transfer, the gap between what was carried up and what came back down is measured and reported (R_T).

This shape is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else. It is the sought-for shape, not a result.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** A relation between one kernel and N domains, each with its own actor set, currency and ground. It is not a two-place prediction; it is a family of predictions indexed by domain-slot.
- **Symmetry.** Ordered, not symmetric. The kernel applies to a domain; the domain does not apply to the kernel. The cross-domain claim itself is a threshold comparison (⋛), not a symmetric join of two operands at the same level. Fig 9 :22 names the three descriptors "arity · symmetry · scale".
- **Scale.** Two levels with a lossy membrane between them: the method level (the kernel) and the instance level (each domain's prediction). Fig 4 :19-20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

### 1.2 One line per domain

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The kernel / method level | the loop, the seven bonds and negation, the six-term substrate, the rule for naming what worked | reach and settlement | the figures for design questions; the ground of each instance for a measurement | admitted |
| D2 The source domain | the entities and mechanisms the kernel was distilled from | its own predictions | its instrument, experiment, dataset or board | admitted |
| D3 The target domain | different entities, different mechanisms | different predictions | its own ground, which the general theory must not be able to move | admitted |
| D4 The verification / comparison frame | expert readers in both domains | residuals and agreement | the ground, never agreement; low overlap between frames | admitted |
| X1 A theory that predicts with no ground | none | none | none nameable | not a domain yet |
| X2 Domain differences as noise to average away | the statistics of the average | a pooled score | none, because the anchor was replaced by a filter | not a domain; this is the disqualified operation at Fig 1 :22 |
| X3 One final theory that ends inquiry | none | none | a terminal room with no outside | excluded by Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room" |

### 1.3 Partition or union

Union, not partition. C1 (a kernel that crosses) and C2 (a per-domain ground that refuses) are overlapping conditions. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A theory that transfers a kernel but has no local ground produces untestable assertions; a theory with local grounds but no shared kernel is not general. Neither lever clears the compound. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The compound has its own loop and its own ground. Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Dependency order

Fig 3 orders by dependency, not importance: perception of each domain's residual, then vocabulary for what that domain affords, then the objective (what counts as progress there), then planning the transfer, then learn and carry. Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The break is expected in the middle links. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." For a general theory of complex systems the unlooked-at link is: having terms for the kernel and being able to say what would count as preserving the target domain's structure. Reporting R_T per transfer is the instrument for that link.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Generality lives in the kernel, particularity in the cast (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 4 :19-20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

These are three low-overlap routes: the membrane rule (Fig 4), the ground-maintainer level rule (Fig 10) and the anchor definition (Fig 2). They converge on the same split. A general theory of complex systems, under these figures, is the kernel plus the rule for naming what worked. The domain-specific differences are not a residue the theory fails to explain; they are the actors, currency and anchor that the theory must leave in the domain. The two halves of the question therefore do not trade off: they are assigned to different parts of the theory.

Mode `general` for the split, `specified` for the complex-systems application.

**Refuter.** Show a theory that transfers the source domain's cast (its actors, currency and anchor) as if it were the kernel and still obtains a ground reading in the target domain that the target domain's practitioners accept. Or derive the general rule solely from one domain's cast with no remainder. Either breaks the split. This is triangulated, so a single figure recanted would not be enough.

### F2 - Testability requires a per-domain anchor that cannot be moved (triangulated)

Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Fig 2 :46 "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

A general theory makes a testable prediction in a domain only if that domain supplies something that can refuse the prediction and does not change its answer because the theory is popular there. Agreement among the theory's users is not a test. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Where the cost of a wrong general theory is high, the second domain's frame must be bought at low overlap with the first, not selected for receptiveness.

Mode `specified`: the figures govern any frame; the application to complex systems is the specification.

**Refuter.** Identify a domain in which the general theory's prediction and the measurement co-determine each other, so pushing on the anchor changes its answer, and the failure is invisible to the domain's own practitioners. That is panel 1 of Fig 2 (coherent drift), not a test.

### F3 - What erases differences is replaying a recording, not carrying a method (triangulated)

Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."

Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."

Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"

This names the precise failure the question is worried about. If a general theory spreads by carrying the record of a success in domain A into domain B, it does not predict in B; it replays A. The next room is a mirror of the last. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." The safeguard is that the next room must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced.

Mode `general` for the failure mechanism, `specified` for the complex-systems case.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a cross-domain transfer that consists of replaying domain A's successful description in domain B, with no re-specified cast and no new ground, and still receives an independent confirmation from domain B's ground. That would falsify the claim that replay cannot settle.

### F4 - The round-trip gap is the instrument that measures erasure (single-source in the figure set, corroborated by the Symbols table)

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Fig 4 :31-32 "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."

The Symbols table defines R_T as "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold." (Symbols :75-78).

The "without erasing important domain-specific differences" clause is therefore not a promissory note; it is a measurement obligation. A general theory must report, for each transfer, what the shared description could not hold. Setting R_T to zero by declaring the kernel identical to the domain is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. This finding is `single-source` within the figure set (Fig 4 plus the Symbols table, which is the same document family), with low-overlap world corroboration from Marks, Gerrits and Marx 2019 (see `09` WORLD).

Mode `general`.

**Refuter.** Show a transfer whose round trip is demonstrated to have zero gap at the relevant resolution: a shared description that holds every domain-specific reading without loss. Then M is unnecessary and this finding fails.

### F5 - Per-slot reporting, never an average (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

A generality claim averaged over domains is the disqualified operation. A single domain where the theory fails must be visible as a live slot even when the global score is near zero, exactly as Fig 1 :13 permits "a global R near zero with one live slot residual" as "a legal state".

Mode `general`.

**Refuter.** A cross-domain evaluation that averages residuals, reports a good global score, and still detects the failing domain with no additional instrument. Under Fig 1 :22 it cannot; if it can, the figure is wrong about averaging.

### F6 - When the theory fails in a domain, an import is owed, not more internal search (single-source in the figure set)

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."

Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"

When a general theory's prediction fails in a domain, the remedy is not to search harder inside the theory. It is to characterize the failure in effect terms and find the frame in that domain whose closure already predicts its shape, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 import rules). Fig 8 :39 also forbids reading a failed search as proof that no domain-general theory is possible. This finding is `single-source` within the figure set (Fig 6 and Fig 8 are one argument about closure), with independent world corroboration from the no-free-lunch and negative-transfer literatures.

Mode `general`.

**Refuter.** Extend the general theory's reach for the target domain by pure internal composition with no new atom, no differing frame and no provenance record, verified by the target domain's ground. That would falsify idempotence as applied here.

### F7 - Structure travels; semantics do not (single-source in figures, triangulated in the WORLD)

Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"

Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

Fig 8 :51-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

The transferable part is the structure of the residual, described in effect terms (arity, symmetry, scale), not the source domain's vocabulary. Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." This is the figure-side half of the syntactic/semantic distinction that Marks, Gerrits and Marx 2019 establish empirically. Within the figure set the finding is `single-source` (Fig 9 and Fig 6 are one instruction about description); across figure and world it is triangulated at low overlap.

Mode `general`.

**Refuter.** A cross-domain transfer that carries the source domain's semantic interpretation unchanged into the target and is then confirmed by the target's ground. Under Marks et al this should fail; if it succeeds, the description rule needs narrowing.

### F8 - The framework cannot certify its own possibility (triangulated, and load-bearing for honesty)

Fig 8 :34 "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe."

Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality." The same essay says plainly: "Metatheories cannot validate themselves. This is Godel's insight, and it still holds here. You need an external validator."

The Tether is itself a candidate general theory of complex systems. It therefore cannot be the witness for the possibility claim it answers. If this audit said "yes, the Tether is such a theory, therefore such theories are possible", it would be a frame certifying itself. The verdict below is about the shape any such theory must have and the instruments that would check it, not an existence proof. This is triangulated between a figure line (Fig 8 :34), a figure line about closures (Fig 9 :44) and an external essay by the framework author, which is high overlap with the figures and is counted as one witness, not three.

Mode `general`, with the self-application declared as a stake (Fig 10 :59 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it.").

**Refuter.** A demonstration that the framework certifies its own completeness or its own universal applicability from inside its own closure. That would break Fig 8 :34 and would also break the author's own stated limit.

### F9 - A terminal general theory is excluded (triangulated)

Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room"

Fig 7 :30-31 "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"

Fig 13 :90 "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."

A general theory that ends inquiry, a final frame with no outside left to import from, is forbidden. The "general" in the answer is a generator of shapes applied until the residual stops registering, not a terminal explanation. Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Mode `general`.

**Refuter.** A candidate general theory of complex systems for which no further domain could require an import, with that closure proved from inside the theory. Under Fig 7 and Fig 13 that proof cannot be given; if it were, both figures would be wrong.

### F10 - The factual "can it in fact" is a declared gap

No figure line settles whether some actual general theory succeeds in every domain. That is a ground question about particular theories, not a metatheoretical constraint. After a genuine re-census the following lines govern the *reporting* of the gap, not its content:

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The gap is of the shape-not-a-name and unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not underivable-in-principle. Fig 8 :40-43 distinguishes them and requires different instructions. The honest answer is: the figures constrain the shape such a theory must have; whether any theory fills it in all domains is unreached, and is reported as such.

Mode `gap` on this point.

**Refuter for the gap itself.** Produce a general theory with a published, domain-by-domain, per-slot residual record across at least three low-overlap domains, each with its own ground, and a measured round-trip gap per transfer. Then the factual claim moves from `gap` toward `grounded` in those domains, one domain at a time.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A general theory transfers the source cast (actors, currency, anchor) as the kernel and still gets an accepted ground reading in the target domain (breaks F1).
2. Agreement among the theory's users is shown to be sufficient for a test in a domain whose ground cannot refuse the prediction (breaks F2).
3. Replaying one domain's recorded success in another is confirmed by the second domain's independent ground with no re-specification (breaks F3).
4. A transfer's round-trip gap is demonstrated to be zero at the relevant resolution (breaks F4).
5. An averaged cross-domain score is shown to detect the failing domain without an additional per-slot instrument (breaks F5).
6. Reach in a target domain is extended by pure internal composition with no new atom, no differing frame and no provenance record (breaks F6).
7. A theory transfers source-domain semantics unchanged into a target domain and is then confirmed by that domain's ground (breaks F7).
8. The framework certifies its own universal applicability from inside its own closure (breaks F8).
9. A terminal general theory with no outside left to import from is proved complete from inside itself (breaks F9).
10. A published theory supplies per-slot residuals and measured R_T in three low-overlap domains, each with its own ground (closes F10 and moves the factual claim toward `grounded`).

If any of 1 to 9 fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. If 10 fires, the gap is narrowed, not silently erased. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer the question as a conditional shape, not a yes or no verdict about a particular theory. The condition is C1 and C2 as a union, never averaged.
2. Report R per domain-slot, never as a cross-domain average. Report R_T for each transfer as the honest measure of what the shared kernel could not hold.
3. When a domain fails, describe the failure in effect terms and look for the frame in that domain whose closure predicts its shape. Do not spend budget searching harder inside the general theory.
4. Never present agreement from users of the theory as corroboration. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
5. Declare the self-application explicitly: the framework is a candidate instance, not a neutral witness.
6. Send the generator (the shape and the refuter), not a recording of this derivation. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

## 8. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. This audit ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :19-20 (kernel versus cast), Fig 2 :29 and :45 (anchor), Fig 4 :23-25 (method not recording), Fig 9 :30-32 (describe the effect, shape not a name).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in this folder, C1 and C2 as a union plus M: transfer the method, leave the cast in the domain, measure what the crossing dropped, refuse averaged scores.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete general-theory claim of the form "kernel K predicts domain D" with D's actors, currency and anchor specified.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down without extra work:

- the **name of the domain's anchor**, which no kernel supplies and which must be found in D (Fig 2 :29);
- the **semantics of D's terms**, which cannot be carried unchanged from the source domain (Fig 9 :30-31; Marks et al 2019);
- the **evidence that the transfer was a method rather than a recording**, which requires the provenance record of Fig 8 :51-52.

One thing survives cleanly: a kernel must be re-applied against a ground that can refuse, and what is shared is the loop rather than the cast. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## 9. What would settle it

Not more agreement. A source-discriminating instrument, in the terms of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a spectrometer, not an argument, resolved which source produced a green reading. Here the corresponding instruments are:

- a published per-domain residual table for a candidate general theory, with R indexed by domain-slot (Fig 1 :19);
- a second frame in each domain at low overlap with the theory's authors, ranging against that domain's ground separately (Fig 2 :31, :45);
- a measured round-trip gap for each kernel transfer (Fig 4 :30);
- a provenance record separating convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :51-52).

Until those exist, the shape is `specified` and the factual universal-success claim is `gap`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument: *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."
