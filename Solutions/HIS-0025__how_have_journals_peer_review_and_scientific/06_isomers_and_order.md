# Isomers, unions and order in the institution question

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the historical binding. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading** (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how three institutions changed two effects. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) is this one problem or a union of overlapping ones, and (2) in what dependency order do the changes sit? A third follows: are the historical institutions isomers of one another (same actors, different bond) or different kinds of thing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01`, not re-derived here)

- Atoms held: frames, a ground, a channel, a membrane, a habitat, a library, a reviewer pool. Bonds held: triangulation (Fig 2), directed filter (Fig 4), import (Fig 6), room selection (Fig 7), settlement (Fig 10).
- No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23).
- Domain 1 evaluation, Domain 2 circulation, Domain 3 community. An overlap exists: a journal is both a membrane and a certifier, so the split is a union, not a partition.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The intersections here are real: editorial review changes both circulation (it decides what is published) and evaluation (it decides what counts as vetted). A single lever that clears evaluation leaves the circulation intersection standing.

**Conclusion:** union compound. Three loops, not one. Verdict `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and the residual's own arity.

**Falsifier:** a historical case where one lever changed both effects in lockstep and no intersection remained. That would make it a partition and this derivation wrong.

### Q2 : What order do the changes sit in?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."* The five links map as follows:

| Fig 3 link | institutional form | can it be read? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | a claim becomes distinguishable from prior claims | yes, obvious tests |
| 2 vocabulary | the field has terms for what the claim affords | yes, dictionaries and methods |
| 3 objective | what would count as progress or validity here | **the usual break** |
| 4 planning | the review and publication procedure | yes, obvious tests |
| 5 learn and carry | publication, indexing, citation | yes, counting |

Fig 3 :25-27: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* The institutional change that matters is at link 3: refereeing is an attempt to state what counts as progress. Circulation (link 5) can only carry a claim as settled after link 3 exists. The society habitat supplies the frames that keep link 3 honest over time.

**Order: E -> C -> S.** Effort spent on link 5 (more journals, higher impact factors) cannot rescue a broken link 3 (no independence). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

**Falsifier:** a documented case where circulation expanded first and evaluation standards followed, with the expanded circulation itself providing the standard. That reverses the order and breaks this derivation.

### Q3 : Are the institutions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Two pairs are genuine isomers under this definition:

1. **Reviewer as colleague versus reviewer as outside frame.** Same actor (a frame with a library), different bond to the claim. As a colleague who shares the author's evidence pool, the reviewer is a mirror; as an outside frame ranged separately against the ground, the reviewer is a verifier. Same atom, different bond, different substance. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
2. **Journal as membrane versus journal as ground.** Same institution, different bond to the claim. As a directed filter it keeps a channel open (Fig 4 :21, Fig 10 :24); as a self-certifying authority it has become an interested ground (Fig 10 :93). The substance changes even though the institution's name does not.

**Not isomers:** journals, peer review, and societies themselves. They differ in atoms as well as bonds (a membrane, a checking arrangement, a habitat). Calling all three "institutions" is the flat-formula error Fig 12 :5 names: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information."*

**Falsifier:** a historical case where the same actor under two bonds produced behaviour indistinguishable in every respect that matters to the ground. That would collapse the isomer claim.

### Q4 : Serial handoff versus mirror chain

Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 7 :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* A citation chain where each paper cites the last is a mirror chain: it compounds agreement. A room chain where each new venue is selected against the residual the last one surfaced is progress. Progress per handoff is two factors (Fig 7 :37-39): the bracket's fidelity across the handoff and the ground's pressure on the room. Zero either and the chain runs in place.

The institutional implication: a conferences-and-journals system is a room chain only if the next room differs from the last. A society that keeps booking rooms like the last one has stopped travelling (Fig 7 :42).

**Falsifier:** a citation chain where evidence demonstrably compounded without any new frame entering and without a ground pressure changing. That breaks Fig 7 :35.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound (Q1), ordered E -> C -> S (Q2). Two historical pairs are isomers under Fig 12's definition (Q3), and a citation system is a room chain only when the next room is selected against the residual (Q4). This framing rescues what the institution story can support without claiming a chronology the figures do not hold. The ground question (which change happened when) remains `gap` (F6 of `01`).
