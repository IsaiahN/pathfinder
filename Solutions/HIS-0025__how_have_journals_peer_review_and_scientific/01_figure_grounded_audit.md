## 0. The question restated as a residual

> How have journals, peer review, and scientific societies changed the circulation and evaluation of claims?

Stated in effect terms, before any figure is chosen:

- **R (the residual):** the gap between what a claim predicts about itself when it leaves its maker and what a community can rely on when it arrives. Two couplings need separating: **claim to community** (circulation) and **claim to ground** (evaluation).
- **Arity:** two effects x three institutions = six slots, but structurally only two couplings. The question is therefore a **union compound**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Unbundle before searching.
- **Symmetry:** circulation is directed, a filter with a direction (Fig 4 :21). Evaluation is not a symmetric agreement among peers, it is triangulation against an anchor (Fig 2 :35). Treating them as one "communication" quantity is the first available error.
- **Scale:** a single claim (first order) against an institution that persists across many claims and over time (second order). Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

**Binding of the sought-for shape, written down before the run.** "Changed" means a relocation of seats (anchor, maker, verifier, outside frame) and filters, not an increase in the number of claims. If this binding is wrong, the refuter in section 3 fires. This is the Direction discipline: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 evaluation** (peer review) | author as maker, reviewers as outside frames and interior auditors, editor as ground maintainer, reproducibility and experiment as ground | verdicts and residuals | **yes:** experiment, replication, a reading that can fire | partition of who may hold which information; ignorance is load-bearing |
| **D2 circulation** (journals) | manuscripts, editors, readers, indexes and citation databases | claims, citations, attention, methods | **yes:** the ground, via the channel that stays reachable; also the reach, what becomes composable | a directed membrane plus an import gate |
| **D3 community** (societies) | peers, societies, meetings, journals-as-rooms | agreement, novelty, selection | **yes:** the ground, which votes between rooms | habitat enumeration, not composition |

**Partition or union.** The three domains overlap: a journal both publishes (D2) and certifies (D1); a society both supplies reviewers (D1) and hosts rooms (D3). This is a union compound (Fig 9 :55). Each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict. "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing" (Fig 9 :55-56).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain is: perception of the claim (link 1) -> vocabulary to state it (link 2) -> objective, what counts as progress or validity here (link 3) -> planning, the review procedure (link 4) -> learn and carry, publication and citation (link 5). Fig 3 :25-27 names link 3 as the usual break: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Therefore evaluation must be settled before circulation can carry anything as settled. A claim cannot circulate as reliable before the community has a notion of reliability. The dependency order is E -> C -> S: evaluation, then circulation, then the society habitat that refreshes outside frames.

## 3. Findings (one verdict per domain, never averaged)

### F1 (D1 evaluation) : peer review installed a checking arrangement, not a vote

**Claim, mode `general`:** the structural change attributable to refereeing is that evaluation moved from a single authority who knows the maker to independent outside frames ranged separately against a ground. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 9 :51: *"evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

**Mode `specified`, ungrounded, from world sources:** the archival sequence reported by Wills (2024) from Moxham and Fyfe's research is that the Royal Society took financial and editorial control of *Philosophical Transactions* by committee in 1752, formalized the written report of fitness by 1832, and *Nature* made refereeing standard practice in 1973 to *"raise the journal above accusations of cronyism and elitism."* Wikipedia's *Scholarly peer review* records that *"referees in scientific publication do not act as a group, do not communicate with each other"* and that *"the goal of the process is explicitly not to reach consensus or to persuade anyone to change their opinions, but instead to provide material for an informed editorial decision."* That is the seat geometry the figures predict, arrived at historically. I did not open Moxham and Fyfe's original article; I cite Wills's report of it. Ground status: archive unread by me.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 9, and Fig 10, which govern different quantities (anchor, admissible evidence, verifier access). Low conceptual overlap. All three are one framework evidence pool. The world corroboration is a second pool (history of science), but it is mostly encyclopedic reporting.

**Refuter:** a documented historical case where evaluation became more reliable while the same small group that authored the claim also judged it, with no independent outside frame introduced. That breaks Fig 2 :35 and Fig 10 :42.

### F2 (D1 evaluation) : independence is the load-bearing quantity, and prestige is a stake

**Claim, mode `general`:** peer review only evaluates when the reviewers do not draw on the same evidence pool as the maker or each other. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

**Mode `specified`, ungrounded, from world sources:** Wikipedia's *Mertonian norms* records Mitroff's counter-norm *"Scientists assess new knowledge and its applications based on the reputation and past productivity of the individual or research group."* Wikipedia's *Scholarly peer review* records that peer review is *"strongly biased against studies with null results"* and quotes Richard Smith calling it *"ineffective, largely a lottery, anti-innovatory, slow, expensive, wasteful of scientific time, inefficient, easily abused, prone to bias, unable to detect fraud."* From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* Journal prestige is a budget, not a verdict.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 2 and the Seat Map (both framework, different documents) with two distinct world fields (sociology of science, metascience). The cross-pool agreement is weak but not circular, because the failure mode was derived before the world sources were read.

**Refuter:** a case where a journal's prestige reliably settled a claim against the ground and its keeper was not disqualified. That breaks Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*

### F3 (D2 circulation) : a journal is a directed membrane, not a neutral pipe

**Claim, mode `general`:** circulation has a direction and a filter. Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A published paper that is a record of one occasion does not by itself grow what a community can compose over: Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The only operator that adds an atom is an import from a frame whose closure differs, Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Mode `specified`, ungrounded, from world sources:** Wikipedia's *Science Citation Index Expanded* describes a *"rigorous selection process governed by 24 quality criteria and four impact criteria"*, a *"seal of quality"*, and *"de-listing"* for journals that fail standards. That is a membrane with a direction and a valence, not a passive conduit. Wikipedia's *Republic of Letters* says *"Periodicals and printed publications remained the most successful however, when the primary goal was to spread information and scientific discovery to the masses."* The historical function was circulation through a filter, exactly the membrane shape.

**Triangulation:** single-source for the membrane claim within the figures (Fig 4); triangulated with Fig 6 for the "only import moves the wall" half; corroborated from two low-overlap world fields (bibliometrics, history of the book). Verdict kept separate from F1: circulation is not evaluation.

**Refuter:** a documented case where the volume of claim-records grew and the community's reach (what it could compose over and settle) grew in direct proportion without any method crossing or import being admitted. That breaks Fig 4 :22-25 and Fig 6 :21.

### F4 (D2 circulation) : the second function of a journal is keeping the channel to the ground reachable

**Claim, mode `general`:** a journal's other effect is not to be the ground but to keep the route to it open. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A chain of journals that cite one another is a mirror chain: Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*, and Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

**Mode `specified`, ungrounded, from world sources:** Wills (2024) reports that from 1752 to 1957 every *Transactions* carried an Advertisement saying the Society could not *"answer for the certainty of the facts, or propriety of the reasonings"* of its papers; all that *"must still rest on the credit or judgement of their respective authors."* That is direct historical evidence that the journal was a membrane handing judgment back to authors, not an anchor. It confirms the derivation rather than straining it. Wikipedia's *Scientific integrity* records Broad and Wade's finding that scientific forgers had *"often for extended periods of time"* successfully defied logic, replication, and peer review. A channel can stay green while contact is lost.

**Triangulation:** single-source within the figures (Fig 10 and Fig 7 govern different things: channel decay and room selection, so this is weak triangulation rather than one line). Corroborated by two world sources with low overlap with each other (a history essay and a metascience article, though both eventually hosted on encyclopedic or magazine platforms).

**Refuter:** a case where a journal that explicitly disclaimed responsibility for its contents nevertheless settled disputes by its own authority over a sustained period. That breaks Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :24.

### F5 (D3 community) : societies supply the habitat from which outside frames are drawn

**Claim, mode `general`:** societies change evaluation by maintaining the pool of independent peers. Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The pool must be unauthored: Fig 10 :73-74: *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 7 :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

**Mode `specified`, ungrounded, from world sources:** Wikipedia's *Learned society* states that societies' activities *"typically include holding regular conferences for the presentation and discussion of new research results, and publishing or sponsoring academic journals in their discipline"* and that *"Scholars in the sociology of science argue that learned societies are of key importance and their formation assists in the emergence and development of new disciplines or professions."* Wikipedia's *Republic of Letters* records that the Royal Society's *"weekly meetings... provided the opportunity to read and discuss scientific publications and recent letters in a formal setting"* and that it became a *"legitimizing body"*. That is a maintained habitat that supplies outside frames and rooms.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 11, Fig 7, and Fig 10 (habitat, room selection, authored pool: three different quantities). Corroborated from sociology of science and history of science. The society's own stake is declared: Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

**Refuter:** a case where a society selected its own reviewers from a pool it authored and the resulting verdicts remained reliable against the ground over time. That breaks Fig 10 :73-74 and Fig 11 :43.

### F6 (all domains) : the literal historical "how" is a `gap` at this budget

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*

It is a ground question, and the ground here is archives, catalogues, and datasets that this run did not open.

**Verdict: `gap` for the chronology, `general` for the shape.** This is a verdict, not a silence. The honest report is: here is the shape any historical answer must have (F1-F5), and here is what would settle the chronology (section 5 of `07_the_tether_answer.md`). ### F7 (budget vs verdict) : volume is a filter, never a witness

**Claim, mode `general`:** impact factors, citation counts, number of journals, and number of papers are filters for where to look. They are not witnesses for whether a claim was evaluated. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Wikipedia's *Science Citation Index Expanded* records that SCIE metrics are *"frequently misused as a proxy for the quality of individual research articles rather than the journal as a whole."* That is the named error, in the world's own words. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"A library that grows is not a reach that grows."*

**Triangulation:** single-source within the figures (Fig 9), corroborated by one bibliometrics source. The corroboration is weak because it is the same claim reached from a field that itself uses the metric, so a stake is declared.

**Refuter:** a case where a citation count settled a disputed claim against an experiment that contradicted it. That breaks Fig 9 :60.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any verdict, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The derived shape is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A documented historical case where evaluation became more reliable while the maker and the verifier shared the same evidence pool and no independent outside frame was introduced (breaks F1, F2, Fig 2 :35 and :40).
2. A documented case where the volume of circulated claim-records grew and the community's reach grew in direct proportion without a method crossing or an import (breaks F3, Fig 4 :22-25, Fig 6 :21).
3. A case where a ground with a reputation to protect reliably corrected claims and its keeper was not disqualified (breaks F2, Fig 10 :93).
4. A case where a society authored its reviewer pool and the verdicts stayed reliable against the ground (breaks F5, Fig 10 :73-74).
5. A verbatim figure line that governs a dated historical transition, which would withdraw F6.
6. A direct archival ground reading that reverses the dependency order E -> C -> S (breaks Fig 3).

Until one of these is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Strain test and re-census

- **Fig 4 :25 ("a recording carried upward") versus published claims.** A settled paper is a record, yet it seems to enlarge what a community can rely on. Re-census: Fig 4 :25 governs a record carried across a **scale change**, where it becomes a description of one occasion. Fig 12 :104 governs a **settled molecule admitted as an atom** at the same level: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Different objects, different boundaries. No strain after re-census.
- **Fig 7 :13 ("agreement compounds, evidence does not") versus multiple referees.** Re-census: independent referees who do not communicate are separate frames, so their agreement is triangulation; a mirror chain is journals citing one another inside one closure. Different objects. Named, not hidden.
- **Fig 2 :29 (anchor) versus the 1752-1957 *Transactions* disclaimer.** Re-census: the disclaimer is evidence that the journal had **not** taken the anchor seat. It confirms the derivation. Not a strain.
- **Fig 9 :51 (verifier denied maker-only evidence) versus open peer review.** Re-census: open identities change who knows whom, not whether the verifier can reconstruct the claim. The admissibility rule governs evidence, not names. If a named reviewer's reputation is allowed to settle the verdict, that is F2, not a figure conflict.
- **After re-census: zero hidden strains.** One open item remains, carried into `06_isomers_and_order.md`: whether "differs from the last room" (Fig 7 :36) is measurable without an instrument that names the residual. That is a question for a ground, not an argument.
