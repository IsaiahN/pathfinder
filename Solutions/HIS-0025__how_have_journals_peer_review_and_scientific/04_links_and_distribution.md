## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **Institution-building rather than truth-settling.** Wills (2024), reporting Moxham and Fyfe, says the refereeing practices were meant to *"disarm specific attacks upon the eighteenth-century Society; sometimes, to protect the Society's finances; and, by the later nineteenth century, to award prestige to members of the nascent profession of natural scientists."* That is Fig 10 :59 in historical clothes: an interest in the reading is disqualifying, and where it cannot be removed it must be declared.
2. **The disclaimer as evidence of an unoccupied anchor seat.** From 1752 to 1957, *Transactions* said it could not *"answer for the certainty of the facts, or propriety of the reasonings"* of its papers; that *"must still rest on the credit or judgement of their respective authors."* Under Fig 2 :29 this is not a weakness of the journal; it is evidence that the journal was a membrane and not yet an anchor.
3. **Correlation as the standing failure.** Mitroff's counter-norm particularism and the replicated reports of prestige bias and publication bias are the same failure Fig 2 :40 names: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
4. **The filter's valence.** *Peer review* records that editorial review is *"strongly biased against studies with null results."* A membrane has a direction, and therefore a bias (Fig 4 :21).
5. **Metrics as budget, not verdict.** The SCIE's *"seal of quality"* and the misuse warning are the Fig 9 :60 error made visible in a real institution.
