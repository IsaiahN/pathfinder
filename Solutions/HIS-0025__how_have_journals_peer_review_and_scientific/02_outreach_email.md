# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the history

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the `specified` label in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is understood by the sender. The historical narrative is not evidence; the shape and its refuter are the offer.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what peer review actually changed

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded account of what journals, peer review, and scientific societies changed about the circulation and evaluation of claims. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the history, not `grounded`):** these three institutions did not simply move more claims faster. They relocated the checking arrangement. Journals are a directed filter plus a kept route to whatever would settle a claim; refereeing put independent outside frames between the author and the verdict; societies supply the pool from which those independent frames are drawn. The claim is about seat geometry, not about volume. That is a prediction of a shape, not a measurement and not a chronology.
>
> This predicts specific failures. If reviewers draw on the same evidence pool as the author, their agreement is one pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. If a journal's reputation settles a claim, its keeper has become an interested ground. If a society authors its own reviewer pool, it has built a room rather than a habitat. If citation counts are used as the verdict, a filter has been handed a witness's job.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that refereeing caused any particular historical change; that any journal is the ground of a claim; that prestige is a form of proof; that the figures record dates. The framework gives the shape any historical answer must have. The dates are owed from archives, and I have not read them. Until a ground reading exists, the historical part stays `specified`.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A documented case where evaluation became more reliable while maker and verifier shared the same evidence pool and no independent outside frame was introduced.
> 2. A documented case where the volume of circulated records grew and the community's reach grew with it, with no method crossing and no import.
> 3. A case where a journal with a reputation to protect reliably corrected claims and its keeper was not disqualified.
> 4. A direct archival reading that reverses the order: evaluation before circulation before the society habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. Please tell me if you would prefer no further contact.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I am interested in what you would expect to see if the shape claim were true, before you look at them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

> - A sourced history of peer review (JSTOR Daily, 2024): https://daily.jstor.org/the-history-of-peer-review-is-more-interesting-than-you-think/
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
