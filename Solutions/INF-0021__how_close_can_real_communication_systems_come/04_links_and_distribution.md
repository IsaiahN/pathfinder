## Wider literature relevant to the question

Read as search result listings and abstracts above. Each was opened; only the quoted/abstracted material is used. The overlap between the fading-channel papers and the adversarial-channel papers is low (different models, different authors, different venues), so where they agree it is not one evidence pool talking. The arXiv cs.IT listing and taxonomy are infrastructure pages, not independent witnesses.

A further relevant result seen in the search listing but not opened in full: Hosseinigoki and Kosut, *"Capacity of Gaussian Arbitrarily-Varying Fading Channels"* (arXiv:2106.07189, 2021), whose abstract states capacity depends on *"whether the transmitter and/or adversary have access to the fading gains"* and can *"drop to zero"* with a strong jammer. Because only the listing was read, it is used as context in the audit and marked accordingly in the ledger, not as a primary quote.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what limit they would expect before naming a benchmark.
- **Fig 7 :13** - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a wrong answer about how near a real system is to its limit, which can mislead engineering effort.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim, its mode and its pre-registered refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure, and what benchmark they would use; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then offer the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2:** Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction:** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A reader's agreement after absorbing the theory is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted:** Fig 4 :22 - methods cross up. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter.
- **Hard precondition:** stage 1 is the re-censused limit claim, not a "percent of capacity" number. If the latter is sent first, the benchmark convention decides the outcome before the ground speaks.
