## Independent domain census (written from the residual line before reading the primary's conclusions)

I redid the census from the question alone, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is `C - R` where `R` is achieved rate and `C` a claimed limit, under a channel that changes unpredictably. Arity: transmitter, channel, receiver, plus whoever measures; at least four positions. Symmetry: broken. The limit changes with who holds channel state, with latency and with whether variation is adversarial. Scale: per channel use, per block, per adaptive episode. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* I read the compound as a **union** of short blocks, moving channel and unknown statistics before looking at the primary's F7.
- **Domain 1, channel / ground.** Actors: sender, receiver, channel, noise. Currency: bits per use and error probability. Anchor: measured reliability under a stated state process. Nameable only once the state process is stated. This is where "the limit" must be re-censused.
- **Domain 2, coding frame.** Actors: encoder, decoder, codebook. Currency: rate, blocklength, encode/decode effort. Anchor: measured error at stated rate and blocklength. A composition inside closure(Gamma), not a new atom.
- **Domain 3, knowledge / adaptation frame.** Actors: CSI holders, estimator, adversary. Currency: state information and regret. Anchor: the state sequence and the reading instrument. If no estimator exists, this is not yet a domain.
- **Domain 4, verification / outreach frame.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence is zero here because all seats read the same pool.

Order by dependency: perceive channel, build a vocabulary of state-dependent codes, state the objective under a declared benchmark, plan, then learn and carry. The likely break is at perception or learn-and-carry, not at coding (Fig 3).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. ## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29-34 (anchor test), Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual), Fig 6 :23, :27 (import and two kinds of unreachability), Fig 4 :30 (round trip), Fig 9 :55 (union).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: "the limit is a family indexed by CSI, latency, blocklength and threat model; report per-slot and `R_T`; short blocks are depth, missing CSI is absence."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim a user might make, for example "at SNR X, blocklength n, with full CSI, the system is within V bits/s/Hz of the outage capacity."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three constraints the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **list of side conditions** (CSI, latency, blocklength, threat model) that the concrete claim must state before the benchmark is meaningful;
- the **benchmark dependence** itself: the abstract "family" becomes one chosen capacity in the concrete claim, and that choice is a convention that can silently decide the answer (Fig 10 :47);
- the **two-currency split**: the shorthand keeps bits and actions apart, but a throughput number re-merges them.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the gap must be reported per realisation, and depth and absence take different remedies. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no measurement was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "capacity formula equals implemented rate" would set `R_T = 0` and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
