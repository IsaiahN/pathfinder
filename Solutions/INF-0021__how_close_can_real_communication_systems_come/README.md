## What this package decides, in effect terms

A transmitter sends at rate `R`. A claimed limit `C` (channel capacity) is offered as the ceiling. The residual is the persistent gap `C - R`. The question asks how small that gap can be made when the channel changes unpredictably.

Two readings are possible. Reading A treats `C` as one fixed anchor and asks how closely engineering can approach it. Reading B re-censuses first: when the channel moves, a single fixed-channel capacity no longer settles the question, so the limit splits into a family indexed by what the frames hold (channel state information, latency budget, blocklength, adversarial power). The derivation below finds that Reading B is the figure-licensed one. The honest answer is a shape (a family of limits with a measured round-trip gap), not a single number.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words.

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence."* Adding readers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. All venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are public submission or contact channels.
