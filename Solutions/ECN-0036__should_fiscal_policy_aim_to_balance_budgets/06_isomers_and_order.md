## Q1 - What bond does each candidate use?

> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** (Fig 12 :46)
> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"** (Operators table :51)
> **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."** (Operators table :50-51)

All three candidates are `⋛` comparisons, so all three are goals in the operator sense. But they bind different operands at different levels:

| candidate | operands | level | bond | what the bond hides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| balance the budget over the cycle | revenues, outlays, averaged across periods | flow, cross-scale abstraction | `⋛` at equality with a `→` sequence underneath | the state of the slot in each period |
| stabilize the debt ratio | accumulated debt, GDP | stock, one level | `⋛` against a threshold | the response function that keeps it there |
| employment and price stability | measured employment, measured inflation, two targets | ground-facing, two conditions | two `⋛` comparisons | the union between them |

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* A fiscal charter that lists three targets without recording which bond attaches to which operand has recorded the ingredients and not the molecule. That is the flat-formula collapse in Fig 12 :14-17.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two fiscal regimes with identical operands (same revenues, same debt, same measured outcomes) and different bonds (one that lets the surplus respond, one that fixes the balance) nevertheless show identical persistence across a shock, the bond distinction is empty and the seat assignment fails.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Operators table, different figures), single-source for the fiscal application.

## Q2 - Are two countries with the same fiscal rules isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two countries with the same formal rule set (a debt rule, a balance rule, a council) have the same atoms. If one composes them as a repeated sequence applied identically in booms and busts and the other as a network that imports from other closures and changes with the state, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the rulebook. This is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that it is not the rule on paper that matters but how it is wired into the economy.

If the countries share only the pattern (a target compared with an outcome) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The IMF dataset's four rule types are a functional-group classification: it says what kind of rule, not which arrangement will pay.

**Falsifier:** if two countries with identical formal rules and identical internal wiring nevertheless diverge across a shock with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the explanation out of the rule layer.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the country application. The IMF dataset corroborates at the classification level only, same policy pool.

## Q3 - Stock versus flow: why a balanced flow is not a stable stock

> **"energy"** / **"conserved, finite, directionless alone"** (Fig 13 :13-14)
> **"Progress is a subtraction."** (Fig 12 :112)
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."** (Fig 12 :112)

A flow balance is a comparison between two quantities over a window. A stock is a state that accumulates the remains of past comparisons: debt is conserved and finite, and it has no direction until a gradient (an interest rate above growth, a primary deficit) is bonded to it. Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* Re-census caution: Fig 13 governs the six physical primitives, not fiscal variables (strain S1 in `01`). The licensed reading here is structural only: a stock cannot be steered by a comparison alone; a route (sequence) and a response (subtraction) are also needed. That is exactly Fig 12 :112, and it is why Bohn's finding that the primary surplus *"is an increasing function of the debt-GDP ratio"* is a response rule, not a fixed balance.

**Falsifier:** a country whose debt stock is stable across a shock with a primary surplus that does not respond to the ratio at all, and whose balance is fixed every period. That would show a comparison alone steering a stock and break this derivation.

## Q4 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for an authority facing a changed state of the world:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the cycle and the debt service at all? Failure here has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ distinguish a structural from a cyclical deficit, and does it have terms for what the changed situation affords? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state the comparison that counts as progress, and at which level? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem, and import when the closure runs dry.

The three conditions map onto this chain: C1 (channel) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (stock reachable) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the authority names what worked but never lets the surplus respond; C3 (renewal) is also link 5, because no atom enters from a differing closure. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear all three (Fig 9 :55).

**Falsifier:** an authority whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit.

## Q5 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tax smoothing** (Barro 1979 via QuantEcon: smooth tax rates by issuing debt in surges) | the route, a sequence of tax collections | close to Fig 12 :112, a plan as sequence | It assumes the expenditure path and does not say which state of the world the authority is in; no per-slot residual |
| **Debt sustainability / fiscal reaction function** (Bohn 1998: surplus increasing in debt) | C2, the response that keeps the stock reachable | Fig 10 :29, keep the anchor reachable | It is one country's history, a filter for where to look, not a verdict for another habitat (Fig 9 :60) |
| **State-dependent multipliers** (Auerbach and Gorodnichenko 2012) | the reason the cycle-average balance is the wrong instrument | Fig 1 :22, per-slot indexing | Estimated in one broad evidence pool; it corroborates the mechanism, not the seat assignment |
| **Indeterminacy under balanced-budget rules** (Abad et al. 2020) | the instability of a rule that cannot borrow | Fig 7 :36, chain of mirrors | A model result, not a ground measurement; it cannot name a working rule design |
| **IMF fiscal rule taxonomy** (four rule types, 123 economies) | D4, the rule layer as Γ | Fig 6 :21, minting cannot add an atom without import | Same policy pool as the above; no source gate for the fiscal question |
| **Adaptive cycle and rigidity trap** (Resilience Alliance; Allison and Hobbs 2004) | the turnover of a rule and the cost of over-connectedness | Fig 7 :36, Fig 6 :23, Fig 11 :49-51 | Low-overlap analogue at a different stratum; no fiscal ground |
| **Luck / favourable terms of trade** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40, *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first five are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the two low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the fiscal fact.

## Q6 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the tax-smoothing emphasis on debt as a buffer, as the route (sequence) that a flow balance cannot express;
- the sustainability emphasis on a reactive surplus, as C2 and Fig 10 :29;
- the stabilization emphasis on state-contingent action, as Fig 1 :22 and the state-dependent multiplier result;
- the rule-design emphasis on escape clauses and correction mechanisms, as C3 and Fig 6 :23;
- the rigidity warning, as Fig 7 :36 and the resilience analogue.

It cannot generate the *name* of the right numerical target or the optimal rule design. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

The three candidates all use the `⋛` comparison bond, so all are goals in the operator sense, but they bind different operands at different levels: a flow averaged across scale, a stock with a threshold, and two ground-facing comparisons. Countries with the same formal rules but different wiring are isomers under Fig 12 :117; countries sharing only the target-comparison pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. A balanced flow cannot steer a stock, because a comparison is not a sequence or a subtraction. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary and objective link and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1, C2 and C3 need different instruments. The standard explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim, the stock-flow claim and the order claim each have a written falsifier; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
