# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no prospective measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about fiscal rules before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about fiscal policy that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the familiar question, should fiscal policy balance the budget over the cycle, stabilize the debt ratio, or prioritize employment and price stability, treats three different things as if they were rival goals at one level. They are not. They occupy different seats:
>
> 1. employment and price stability are the outcomes the question is about, so realized measurements and the market's verdict on solvency are what settle it. They are also two conditions, not one, and a lever that helps employment may leave the price condition binding;
> 2. the debt ratio is a threshold on an accumulated stock. It is a constraint to be kept reachable, and it has to be met by a surplus that responds to the state of the world, not by a fixed number that sits still while the denominator falls;
> 3. a balanced budget is a rule, a convention. It is useful for containing overspending in booms, but a convention that is allowed to decide the outcome has stopped being a convention. Balancing over the cycle nets the bust against the boom and hides the state that needed action.
>
> The further claim is that the rule itself has to keep meeting situations that differ from the ones it was written for. A fiscal authority that only re-runs its own rule at a larger scale is agreeing with itself, not checking itself.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the right inflation target, the natural rate of unemployment, or the right debt threshold. That a balanced budget is never useful. That any particular country is a case in point. That this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of fiscal policy. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a strict balanced-budget rule leaves employment and prices unchanged when the market stops paying for what the government does;
> 2. a fiscal authority keeps the same rule across two regimes and keeps passing the outcome test without taking in anything new from outside;
> 3. a fixed debt-to-GDP target dominates a surplus that responds to the debt ratio and to the cycle, with no loss of stabilization;
> 4. one instrument brings both employment and prices to target in every state and leaves nothing categorically different behind;
> 5. two forecasting groups with a genuinely shared evidence pool deliver a prospective rule failure that neither predicted alone, and their agreement is what found it.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Three seats kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended fiscal score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map :287) |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No name for the targets | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A direct reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Seat Map :171).
