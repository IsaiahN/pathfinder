## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which number to put in a fiscal charter. It is which of three candidates occupies which seat in the loop: the thing that settles the question (the ground), the constraint that keeps the sovereign reachable (a path condition on an accumulated state), or the instrument used to steer (a convention). In effect terms:

- A fiscal authority holds a library Γ, its model of the economy and its existing rules. It takes actions `a` (spending, taxation, borrowing). The economy returns `o′` (measured employment, prices, output, debt service). `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` is indexed per state of the world and per period, never averaged across the cycle (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).
- **Option A, balance budgets over the cycle:** leads to a comparison of two flows with the threshold set at equality, averaged across boom and bust. It hides which state needs action and turns a convention into a verdict.
- **Option B, stabilize the debt ratio:** leads to a comparison against a threshold on an accumulated stock. It is a keep-reachable condition, not the aim, and it must be state-contingent to survive a shock.
- **Option C, prioritize employment and price stability:** leads to the outcomes the question is constitutively about, but they are a union of two conditions with different instruments, and the framework returns a shape, not numerical targets.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): an assignment of each candidate to ground / constraint / instrument, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one from a failure of another. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 4 of the audit can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is that the three are not rival aims at one level: Option C is closest to the ground, Option B is a reachability constraint on a state, and Option A is an instrument that must not be allowed to decide the outcome. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the fiscal-policy question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of fiscal policy.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Seat Map :163).
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
