## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "balance the budget over the cycle, stabilize the debt ratio, or prioritize employment and price stability" treats three different things as rival goals at one level. They occupy different seats. Realized employment, prices and solvency are the outcomes the question is about, so measurement and the market's verdict are what settle it; they are also two conditions, not one, with different instruments. The debt ratio is a threshold on an accumulated stock, a constraint to be kept reachable by a primary surplus that responds to the ratio and to the state of the world, not by a fixed number. A balanced budget is a rule, a convention useful against overspend in booms, but a convention allowed to decide the outcome has become an unpaid verdict; balancing over the cycle nets the bust against the boom and hides the state that needed action. The rule itself must keep meeting closures that differ from its own. The claim is a shape any candidate answer must have; it does not name the numbers or the rule design.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 1 and 10

- **Statement:** realized outcomes are the anchor and the ledger is an instrument; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; aggregating across states hides the live signal.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
  - Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
  - Fig 10 :26 - *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- **Refuter:** a strict balanced-budget rule that leaves measured employment and prices unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment for government action; or an instrument that discriminates in real time, from the deficit reading alone, a shock-driven shortfall from structural overspend. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one economics-facing check

- **Statement:** the debt ratio is a keep-reachable constraint on an accumulated stock; the surplus must respond to it; a rule that never meets a differing closure cannot renew its reach.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :29 - *"→ keep the anchor reachable"*
  - Fig 13 :13-14 - *"energy"* / *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Bohn 1998 reports that *"the U. S. primary surplus is an increasing function of the debt-GDP ratio"* and that the ratio shows mean reversion once wars and cycles are controlled. Is that not a response function of exactly the kind C2 describes, rather than a fixed target? A "no", with evidence that a fixed ratio dominates, is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a fixed debt/GDP target that dominates a state-contingent primary-surplus reaction function with no loss of stabilization across a shock; or a fiscal authority that keeps one rule across two regimes and keeps passing with no import from a differing closure. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Union and structure

- **Statement:** employment and price stability are two conditions with different instruments, so the instruction is a union that must be unbundled; the balanced flow and the stable stock use different operations; countries with the same rules but different wiring are isomers, so a rulebook alone cannot predict success.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :112 - *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **Refuter:** one instrument that brings both employment and prices to target in every state and leaves no categorically different residue; or two authorities with identical formal rules and identical internal wiring whose outcomes diverge with no difference in contact or imported atoms, which would move the explanation out of the rule layer and into habitat alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about public finance, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of variance attributable to fiscal policy, luck or the cycle.
- Not that fiscal discipline, rules or councils are irrelevant. They are Γ, the library operand; the claim is that Γ alone does not settle the outcome.
- Not that a balanced budget is always wrong. It is an instrument against overspend in good times; what is refused is its promotion to the aim.
- Not that any specific numerical target or rule design is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the macro-fiscal literature independently corroborates this. Bohn, Auerbach and Gorodnichenko, Abad and coauthors and the IMF sources share one broad evidence pool. The resilience sources corroborate the mechanism, not the fiscal fact.
- Not that this packet, produced with an LLM, is submittable or publishable as authored work. See the AI-policy note in section 6.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-state study that measures C1 and C2 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, and reports which condition failed before the outcome worsened. Concretely:

1. **Channel test:** does the authority's reading of the economy degrade while the market keeps paying someone? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Anchor test:** are realized employment, prices and debt service measured, and is the ledger treated as an instrument rather than the thing settled (Fig 2 :29, :36)?
3. **Response test:** does the primary surplus respond to the debt ratio and to the state of the world, or is it fixed? Bohn's reaction function is the benchmark.
4. **Union test:** can the study separate an employment failure from a price failure with two instruments, rather than one blended fiscal score (Fig 9 :57)?
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can it tell a recession shortfall from structural overspend from a reclassification when all three produce the same deficit reading? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29 and :36, Fig 10 :24, :29 and :47, Fig 1 :22, Fig 6 :23, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 12 :112. Nine short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of fiscal reaction functions: is there a documented case of a fixed balance target that stabilizes debt across a shock without a responsive surplus? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a recession shortfall from structural overspend using observable data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
