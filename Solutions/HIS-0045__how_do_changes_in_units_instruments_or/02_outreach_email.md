# Outreach draft - check the separator before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union framing and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact. The output has to survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about apparent trends in historical measurement records

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in the history of measurement: when a long series shows a trend, how much of the trend is the world and how much is a change of unit, of instrument, or of definition? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the three kinds of change are not three versions of one error. They are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors. A unit change is a convention: two names, one referent. It changes the code or the realisation, not the quantity. An instrument change is a change in the channel: the ground does not decay, the channel does. A definition change re-slices the slots, so the same case can move from one category to another while the underlying thing does not move. Because the conditions overlap, a single uniform correction clears only the regions where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
>
> What follows from that is a separator rather than a number: segment at every documented change, keep the provenance of each segment, re-index cases across definition changes, build the correction from parallel measurements of the old and new instruments, measure what the harmonisation dropped, and keep the reference outside the community doing the correcting.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you what percentage of any named trend is instrumental or definitional. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the numerical share needs a source-discriminating instrument that I do not have here. I am also not claiming that all instrument corrections erase a real trend; the ocean heat content literature shows a case where correcting the channel changed the decadal structure but left the long-term slope similar. That boundary is part of the claim, not an exception to it.
>
> **What would refute the shape:** (1) one uniform correction that reduces the residual on every interval and every intersection without a frame-specific term; (2) a unit redefinition that changes the quantity itself while an independent anchor holds it fixed; (3) an instrument change where the ground moved first and correcting the channel erases a real trend; (4) a definition change that leaves slot membership identical for every case; (5) a break whose cause is recoverable from the values alone, with no station history or revision date; (6) a harmonisation with a demonstrated inverse at every point.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - The figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - BIPM, historical perspective on the metre: https://www.bipm.org/en/history-si/metre
> - WMO Guidelines on Homogenization (2020): https://dgm-meteo.github.io/wmo-clino/Docs/WMO2020_1245_en_Guidelines%20on%20Homogenization.pdf
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a record you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]

## What is deliberately not in the email

- No request for trust, belief, or endorsement. Prestige is not a validator: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*.)
- No personal email address of a named individual. Public institutional and opt-in channels only, per `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
- No "bombshell", "true answer", or promise that the framework settles the history of measurement.
- No bulk-send language and no opt-out suppression. One recipient, one checkable claim, a clear permission to decline.
