# Expert verification packet - the Tether on apparent trends in historical measurement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of measurement, a metrologist or a climate-record specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical share. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of any historical series was taken here. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A change of unit, instrument or definition does not move the quantity; it moves the name, the channel or the category through which the quantity is read. The three are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, so a single uniform correction clears only the regions where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. A unit change is a convention (two names, one referent) and must not decide the trend verdict. An instrument change is channel decay while the ground does not decay. A definition change re-slices the slots, so cases move between categories while the underlying thing does not. The discriminator is provenance plus an instrument that separates sources, and harmonisation is a round trip whose gap must be measured. Over a fixed atom set the before and after series are isomers; where the definition changed the counted world they are members of one functional group. The framework returns this shape, not a percentage.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1, framework-internal plus metrology-facing; checkable by reading the Operators table and Fig 10

- **Statement:** a unit redefinition is a convention (identity: two names, one referent), not a move of the quantity. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators :25 "two names, one referent"; Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"; Fig 10 :46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."; Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"; Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world."
- **What to check:** is the case one where the quantity is held fixed and only the code, scale or realisation changed? BIPM's history of the metre is the documentary test: the 1960 krypton definition and the 1983 light definition changed the realisation while custodians strove to preserve the length. If a case changes the quantity itself while an independent anchor holds it fixed, the sub-claim fails.
- **Refuter:** exhibit such a case. Also: exhibit a case where the series cannot be re-expressed by a two-names-one-referent identity at all.

### C2, instrument-facing; checkable by a climate-record or instrumentation specialist against their own cases

- **Statement:** an instrument change is a change in the channel, not the ground. The ground does not decay, the channel does. Corrections must be built from the old instrument's residual, which in practice means parallel measurements, not a description of the quantity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :25-26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"; Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."; Fig 6 :33 "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."; Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** in the XBT record, the 1975 to 1985 fall-rate minimum appears as a warm period in uncorrected thermosteric sea level (Wijffels et al. 2008). In the USHCN, time-of-observation and electronic-thermometer changes produce opposite biases on maximum and minimum (Williams et al. 2012). Is the correction in each case derived from a collocated comparison, and does it preserve a real ground trend where one exists?
- **Refuter:** show an instrument change where the ground moved first and the channel change was a consequence, such that correcting the channel erases a real trend. Also note the boundary: Levitus et al. (2009) found that correcting the channel changed interdecadal variability but left the long-term slope similar. The sub-claim is "can open a gap", not "always creates the trend".

### C3, framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 8, Fig 13 and Fig 4

- **Statement:** a break in a series cannot be attributed from the values alone; the discriminator is provenance plus a source-separating instrument, and harmonisation carries a measurable round-trip gap R_T.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."; Fig 13 :130 "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."; Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** has anyone recovered the cause of a documented break from values alone, with no station history and no revision date? Has anyone demonstrated a harmonisation that is information-preserving at every point, with a working inverse? If so, the provenance and R_T requirements are too strong for that case.
- **Refuter:** either of the two exhibits above. Also: a figure line that returns a numerical share for a named cause would close the declared `gap` and revise this packet.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a percentage attributed to unit, instrument and definition. That number needs a ground reading and a source-discriminating instrument; the framework returns a shape.
- Not that every apparent trend is an artefact. Real ground trends exist.
- Not that instrument correction always changes the slope. The ocean-heat-content boundary case is recorded in C2.
- Not that a convention is false. It is true as shared meaning and silent about the world.
- Not a derivation of one theory from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement taken in this derivation.
- Not a claim that the figures name the cause. Naming a cause is a provenanced binding onto an atom the record already holds; the correction to my own earlier overstatement is kept visible in `05` §5b and `06` Q1.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A parallel-measurement record: old and new instrument observing the same quantity across the change.
2. Complete metadata: revision dates, station histories, screen and sensor types, coding rules.
3. A re-indexing test across the definition boundary showing whether the trend survives.
4. An anchor that did not update on the correcting community's own reports.
5. The six refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` and the eight in `01` §5, written before this packet was compiled.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Operators :25 and Fig 10 :18, :46, :47 (four short passages). Decide whether your case is a convention or a ground change.
2. Read Fig 10 :24-26 and Fig 6 :32-33. Check whether the correction in your case was built from the old instrument's residual.
3. Read Fig 8 :51 and Fig 4 :30. Ask whether the break's cause came from provenance or from a value-only inference, and whether the harmonisation gap was reported.
4. Name one discriminator in your field that would separate unit, instrument and definition causes, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not send agreement as corroboration; by Fig 2 :40 agreement from one evidence pool is one witness.
