## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The field already has the "synthetic solve" failure in its own record.** The theory-of-mind page reports that very simple reflex-only robots can pass ToM tests and that the tests may not measure cognitive ability. Read with `Fig 11 :70`, "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability", this is the strongest reason not to accept a benchmark win as an answer to the question as posed. It corroborates a figure line from a low-overlap field, but it is corroboration only.
2. **The three named phenomena are theoretically disunified in their own fields.** Humour studies report no consensus across relief, superiority and incongruity; pragmatics treats implicature as context-dependent and defeasible; speech-act theory treats an indirect request as a relation between literal content and an unstated goal. Read with `Fig 9 :54-57`, this supports unbundling the union before search. The headings were picked from different fields with little overlap with each other, so this is one of the few genuinely multi-witness points in the run.
