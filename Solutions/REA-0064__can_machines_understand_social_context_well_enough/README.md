## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

Before the run, the sought-for shape is written down so it can accrue a residual like any other term. A sufficient answer here must: (1) name the ground that settles "understands social context" and show it does not move when a frame pushes on it; (2) give a per-slot verdict for indirect speech, humor, and implied meaning separately, never a single averaged score; (3) state whether the machine's library can reach the intended meaning by composition alone or whether contact with a social habitat must be imported; and (4) state what measurement would reverse the verdict. Any answer failing these four is not an answer to this question but to a different, easier one. This is the "Direction" discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** `Fig 2 :35` "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation (`Fig 2 :40`).
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** `Fig 10 :42` "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
3. **Prestige is not action.** The author's own essay: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action." A campaign that gathers famous names to agree spends the ground this question needs.

Legally: scraping personal addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
