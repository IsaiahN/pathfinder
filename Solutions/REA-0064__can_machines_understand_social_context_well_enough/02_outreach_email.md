# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four-part shape in the README is stated in your own words and the refuter below still reads correct to you. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about "machines understanding social context"

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the closure claim, not `grounded`):** "Can machines understand social context well enough to interpret indirect speech, humor, or implied meaning?" is not one question. Those are three conditions with different bonds. Indirect speech produces an action from a question; humor resolves a comparison; implied meaning is the literal reading subtracted from the intended one. Under the framework I am using, a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions, not a harder single case. So the question has to be split before it is searched, and a single accuracy score cannot settle it.
>
> What an answer would have to look like, written down before the run: (1) a ground for "understands" that does not move when a lab or a model pushes on it, where the ground is the coordination outcome (did the window get closed, did the reply fit); (2) a separate per-slot verdict for each of the three phenomena, never an average; (3) a statement of whether the machine's library reaches the intended meaning by composition alone or needs contact with a frame whose closure differs; and (4) the measurement that would reverse the verdict.
>
> One further claim, stated as a reach result rather than a proof: a library built only from text cannot compose a social primitive it does not already hold by searching that same library more. The relevant figure line is "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs" (Fig 6 :23). This is not "no machine ever"; it is "not reachable from this closure", which is a different fact and has a different remedy.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the threshold called "well enough" has been measured; that any particular system has or lacks a theory of mind; that a benchmark failure proves incapacity; or that a named primitive such as embodiment is required. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence. A synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before sending this note:**
> 1. one typed relation, one ground, and one falsifier that govern all three phenomena without loss;
> 2. an instrument for intended meaning that does not move when pushed, and that settles a disputed case;
> 3. a text-only library composing a pragmatic primitive that changes a per-slot coordination outcome;
> 4. an agent-only change that moves the coordination ground without changing contact.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the loop is in the third link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and report the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape, and refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode.

A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. By `Fig 2 :40`, if the readers share this packet as their evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking.
