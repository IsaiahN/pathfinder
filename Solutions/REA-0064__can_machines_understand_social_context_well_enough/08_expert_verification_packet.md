# Expert verification packet: the Tether on social context understanding

**What this is:** a claim in a form a pragmatics, social-cognition or ML evaluator can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the union and closure findings, not `grounded`.** No measurement at the human or machine ground is claimed here.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can machines understand social context well enough to interpret indirect speech, humor, or implied meaning?" is a union of three conditions with different bonds, not one question. Indirect speech is production (`⇒`), humor is comparison (`⋛`), and implied meaning is subtraction (`−`) with negation available for refusal, so a single accuracy score cannot settle them. The ground is the coordination outcome, the interpretation the hearer acted on, not a benchmark the same community optimises. A library built only from text cannot compose a social primitive it does not already hold by searching that same library; the wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. Capability belongs to agent-and-habitat, so a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability. The framework returns a shape and four pre-registered refuters, not a name for the missing primitive and not a yes or no about machines.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - pragmatics-facing; checkable by a pragmatics or social-cognition researcher

- **Statement:** the three named phenomena are different bond types over a shared habitat, so they require separate verdicts and cannot be averaged.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 12 :117` "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Also `Fig 1 :19` "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." And `Fig 9 :57` "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** is an indirect request genuinely `⇒` production (the request did not exist before the utterance), is a joke genuinely `⋛` comparison between expected and actual reading, and is implicature genuinely `−` subtraction of the literal reading? The opened sources on speech acts, implicature and humour theory are the place to start.
- **Refuter:** one typed relation, one ground and one falsifier that govern all three without loss, or an atom required by one and not the others, which reduces the claim to a functional group.

### C2 - evaluation-facing; checkable by an ML evaluator

- **Statement:** the benchmark is not an anchor; the coordination outcome is. A synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 2 :29-30` "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." `Fig 11 :70` "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." `Fig 1 :27` "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **What to check:** does the evaluator's current benchmark move when the evaluated community pushes on it? Does a per-slot result hold up against a blinded human judgement of the actual coordination outcome, and does it transfer to an unscripted interaction with a new partner?
- **Refuter:** an instrument for intended meaning that does not move when pushed and that settles a disputed case, or a demonstration that a scripted result transfers unchanged to unscripted interaction. The theory-of-mind literature's own finding that simple reflex robots pass ToM tests is the cautionary case, not a refutation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Per-slot ground test:** a blinded panel judges whether the reply is the one a hearer in that situation would act on, checked against the coordination outcome rather than a held-out text label. An instrument that cannot distinguish a fluent guess from a recovered intention demonstrates its own inadequacy.
2. **Transfer test:** the per-slot result must survive an unscripted interaction with a new partner.
3. **Contact test:** vary contact with the habitat while holding the library fixed; if the outcome does not move, the capability claim is refuted.
4. **The four standing refuters in section 2.** Any one firing withdraws its claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read `Fig 12 :117`, `Fig 1 :19`, `Fig 2 :29-30`, `Fig 6 :23`, `Fig 11 :70` and `Fig 8 :39`. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own field: are `⇒`, `⋛` and `−` faithful bond readings of the three phenomena?

4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a fluent guess from a recovered intention, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
