# The Tether's answer on social context understanding

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the union and closure findings, not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A machine is given an utterance and context and must recover the meaning the speaker expected a hearer to act on. Literal composition succeeds on the words and fails on the intended meaning. The residual is per slot: indirect request, joke, implicature. As posed, the question bundles three conditions with different bonds and asks for one yes or no. `Fig 9 :57` "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." It is a union compound with its own loop, not one hard question.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split before you answer.** Indirect speech is production (`⇒`), humor is comparison (`⋛`), implied meaning is subtraction (`−`) with negation (`¬`) available for sarcasm. `Fig 12 :117` "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." One score cannot carry three bonds.
2. **The anchor is the coordination outcome.** `Fig 2 :29-30` "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Did the window close, did the reply fit, was the false reading corrected? A benchmark the same community optimises is not that anchor (`Fig 1 :27`).
3. **Per-slot verdicts, never an average.** `Fig 1 :19` "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A model can be strong on requests and fail on irony while the mean looks healthy.
4. **State the reach, not just the score.** `Fig 8 :32` "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." A text-only library cannot compose a social primitive it does not hold by searching that same library. The wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs (`Fig 6 :23`).
5. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** `Fig 11 :39` "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." `Fig 11 :70` "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A benchmark win is wiring until it transfers to an unscripted interaction.
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** `Fig 13 :135` "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." So the answer does not say that a machine must have a named module. It says what contact the missing thing must provide and what would reverse the verdict.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a blanket "yes" or "no" about machines. That would be a verdict in `general` mode with no ground, the output-layer failure the exemplar names.
- Not that any named architecture (theory of mind module, embodiment, world model) is required. That names a missing primitive.
- Not that a failed benchmark proves incapacity. `Fig 8 :39` "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that more text of the same kind is useless. It is a budget filter, not a witness (`Fig 9 :60`).
- Not `grounded`. No instrument settled whether any system recovered an intended meaning here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

1. **Per-slot ground test.** For each slot, a blinded human panel judges whether the system's reply is the one a hearer in that situation would act on, with the score checked against the actual coordination outcome, not against a held-out text label. An instrument that cannot distinguish a fluent guess from a recovered intention "demonstrates its own inadequacy" (essay, read this session).
2. **Transfer test.** Move from the scripted harness to an unscripted interaction with a new partner. If the per-slot result does not transfer, the synthetic solve was wiring (Fig 11 :70).
3. **Contact test.** Vary contact with the habitat (shared attention, correction, turn-taking history) while holding the library fixed. If the outcome does not change, F5 is refuted and the capability claim falls (Fig 11 :40).
4. **Standing refuters from the audit:** one typed relation governing all three slots; a non-moving instrument; a text-only composition that adds a pragmatic atom; an agent-only change that moves the ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** `Fig 13 :138-139` "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides." The six-term substrate and the habitat atoms are a visible choice, not nature's own list. The loop stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is characterised in effect terms as "contact with a frame whose closure differs"; it is not named. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether machines understand social context. It says the question is three questions wearing one coat. Indirect speech produces an action, humour resolves a comparison, and implied meaning subtracts the literal from the intended, so they need separate verdicts, not one score. Any answer must name a ground that does not move when a lab pushes on it, and that ground is the coordination outcome, what the hearer actually did, not a benchmark the same community optimises. A library built only from text cannot mint a social primitive it does not hold by searching that same library; the wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. A synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability. What the framework offers is the shape an answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
