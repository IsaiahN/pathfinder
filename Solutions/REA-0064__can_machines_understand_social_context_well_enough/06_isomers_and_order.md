## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What bond does each phenomenon use?

- **Indirect speech.** An utterance of one illocutionary force performs another. The speech-act source opened this session gives "Peter, can you close the window?" as a question that also requests, and "I'm cold" as a request that relies on unstated shared facts. That is `⇒` production: the request did not exist before the utterance fired (Operators table: "A produces B; B did not exist before"). `[specified]`
- **Humour.** Incongruity theory, read this session, says humour relies on "anything contrary to expectation according to some norm", and the joke resolves when the incongruity is put into a real relation. That is `⋛` comparison: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table), where the quantities are the expected and actual readings. `[specified]`
- **Implied meaning.** What the speaker suggests though not literally expressed (implicature source, read this session). The intended reading is the literal reading with the gap accounted for; it is cancellable and context dependent. That is `−` subtraction of the literal from the intended, with `¬` available when the literal reading must be refused (sarcasm). `[specified]`

They therefore share atoms and differ in bond. `Fig 12 :117`: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any two of the three can be shown to use the same typed bond over the same operands with the same ground, they are variants of one term, not isomers, and F1 in the audit weakens to a vocabulary distinction.

### Q2 - Are they isomers over the full atom set?

- The weaker reading: each phenomenon recruits a different subset of habitat atoms (humour leans on norm and timing; indirect speech on relationship and shared physical context; implicature on the cooperative principle). If the subsets differ, they are not isomers even under Fig 12; they are a functional group at best.
- The stronger reading: write all three over the full shared atom set `{speaker, hearer, utterance, context, history, convention, correction}` and let the bond differ. Then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the claim worth testing, because it is checkable and needs no new primitive.
- **Falsifier:** if one phenomenon requires an atom the others do not (for example, humour requires a norm that indirect requests do not), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3 - What is the order of the interpretation chain?

`Fig 3 :4-15` gives five links: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Mapped to this question:

| link | in this domain | instrument status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | register utterance and context, including gaze, prosody, shared setting | strong (transcription, gaze tracking, annotation) |
| 2 vocabulary | terms for what an utterance affords: request, irony, implicature | moderate (pragmatic annotation schemes) |
| 3 objective | say what counts as progress: the hearer acted on the intended meaning | weak, the middle link with no instrument |
| 4 planning | a route from literal to intended reading | strong (models propose readings readily) |
| 5 learn and carry | name what worked, verify, carry to the next interaction | weak, because verification is link 3 |

`Fig 3 :25-27` "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." `Fig 3 :19` "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

So the chain breaks at link 3. Effort on stronger perception or more fluent planning arrives at a step that cannot use it. BIG-bench, read this session, calls the corresponding failure "brittle metrics" and notes that "social bias typically increases with scale in settings with ambiguous context". That is what a missing objective looks like from inside a benchmark. `[specified]`

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a validated instrument for pragmatic coordination progress and show that gains in links 1 and 2 now propagate into link 3. If they do not, the break is elsewhere and the census must be redone.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order for repair?

1. Refuse the bundled question before minting any term (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2; `¬` is not a bond).
2. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (`THE_FORMULA.md` Direction).
3. Name the ground per slot, in effect terms, before choosing a theory (`Fig 9 :27-28` "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
4. Settle link 3 (the objective) before links 4 and 5. A system that cannot say what counts as progress cannot verify what worked.
5. Only then decide whether an import from a different closure is owed (`Fig 6 :23`), with provenance and a shadow test.

Order matters: `Fig 3 :20` "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Doing step 5 before step 3 is how a field imports an expensive primitive to solve a problem it has not stated.

## CONCLUSION

The three phenomena are best read as **isomers over the full shared habitat atom set**, differing by bond (`⇒`, `⋛`, `−` with `¬` available for refusal), provided the strong reading survives the stated falsifier. As commonly studied they are a **functional group**, because each recruits different atoms and a different ground. The machine's interpretation chain breaks at link 3, the objective, not at perception or fluent planning; the field's measurable successes sit above the break, which is why they do not settle the question. The repair order is refuse the bundle, bind the shape, name the ground per slot, settle the objective, then decide on import.

This rescues what is defensible without importing a named primitive. "Indirect speech is production, humour is comparison, implied meaning is subtraction" is a checkable structural claim with a falsifier. "A machine needs a theory of mind module" is a name where the framework returns a shape, and is not derived here.
