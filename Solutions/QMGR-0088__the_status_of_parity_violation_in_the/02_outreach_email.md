> Hi [name],
>

> **Two separate claims, with their modes stated:**
>
> 1. `grounded`: the weak interaction violates parity. This is settled by experiment, not by any framework. The Co-60 beta asymmetry, pion and muon decay, neutrino helicity and the kaon results are the ground here. If you know a controlled weak measurement in which a process and its mirror image return the same rate, I would like to hear about it.
> 2. `specified`, with part of it `gap`: the historical event was a **refusal** of a held term, not the discovery of a missing force. Parity conservation was bound and predicting before 1956; the experiment removed its standing to be composed over. Whether this reflects a deeper principle is **not** settled. What the figures return is a shape: a deeper principle, if one exists, would be a term that predicts the handed asymmetry as a consequence rather than a postulate, and is settleable by an instrument that discriminates among sources. I am not naming it. Mirror matter and left-right symmetric models are candidate bindings from the physics frame, recorded with provenance and carrying no verdict weight until something settles them.
>
> **What would refute or close the second claim:**
> 1. an instrument that discriminates among sources at the relevant scale, for example a reproducible right-handed-current signal at a stated mass or a mirror-nucleon oscillation;
> 2. a bound ordinary term that predicts the handedness as a consequence and whose residual trends down under such an instrument;
> 3. a derivation of parity as a seventh primitive of the six-term substrate, or a state-state composition admitted by the framework that cannot be refused.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
