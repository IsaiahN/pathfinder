## 0. The question under audit

> "The status of parity violation in the weak interaction and whether it reflects a deeper principle" (QM & GR Questions.txt line 113, question 88).

This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54 says to unbundle a union before searching again, and the Library of Babel essay puts the same instruction plainly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" The two conditions have different anchors and must keep separate verdicts.

- **Condition A (status).** Did the ground return a parity asymmetry in weak processes? This is settled by instruments, not by any frame's preference.
- **Condition B (deeper principle).** Is the violation terminal, or the derivative of a symmetry not yet held? This is not settled, and the figures say what an answer to it would have to look like.

Averaging A and B would throw away the only live signal in the question. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 1. The residual in effect terms

- **Arity:** two conditions (A and B). Union, not partition. The overlap is the historical fact that the same experiments are used both to settle A and to motivate B, so the compound needs its own loop.
- **Symmetry:** parity is a discrete Z2 reflection on a spatial state. The residual is not "is there a symmetry" but "what is the standing of a symmetry that was held and then refused." That distinction decides which bin of the formula applies.
- **Scale:** subatomic (beta decay, kaon and B-meson decays, neutrino helicity). Condition B asks about a different level, a UV or hidden-sector closure, so the answer composes across levels under Fig 12 :103-105.

**Effect description, written before any frame was chosen:** "An instrument comparing a weak process with its mirror image returns a nonzero asymmetry, and a frame that once held mirror symmetry as a term no longer holds it as composable. Whether some other term restores symmetry at a different level is not decided by that reading." This is a description of effects, so it travels (Fig 9 :30-31).

## 4. Findings

### F1 - Condition A is settled by the ground, not by agreement (grounded)

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Wu, Ambler, Hayward, Hoppes and Hudson (1957) aligned Co-60 and found more beta electrons opposite the nuclear spin. Later experiments in pion, muon and neutrino decay returned the same handedness. The Wu experiment article: "By the end of 1957, further research confirmed the original results of Wu's group, and P-violation was firmly established." The Parity (physics) article: "All fundamental interactions of elementary particles, with the exception of the weak interaction, are symmetric under parity transformation, a phenomenon known as P-symmetry."

These are different instruments pointed at the same quantity, so the agreement is triangulation, not one pool talking (Fig 2 :31-32). Verdict for domain 1: `grounded`, triangulated.

**Refuter:** an experiment in which a weak process and its mirror image return the same rate after polarisation, detector acceptance and background are controlled. That would return the status to `open`. It has not fired.

### F2 - Parity conservation was REFUTED, not found missing (specified, triangulated)

Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". The formula's route bin names the same fifth kind: "REFUTED a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation", and says the first four bins sort R by what is MISSING while the fifth sorts by what is WRONG.

Before 1956 parity conservation was a bound, predicting term. It was not absent from the library; it was held and generating predictions. The Wu result did not add a new atom. It added a term that refuses. Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

Fig 6 :42-43: "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." So "parity is violated" has a precise structural reading: the record keeps parity conservation, stamps it as refused, and the reach falls because terms composed over it no longer have standing. This is why the weak interaction looks like a subtraction from what could be composed, not an addition of a new force.

This is the single most important correction to the naive reading of the question. Asking "what force causes parity violation?" assumes a NOVEL bin. The figure census says the bin is REFUTED. A NOVEL reading strains; re-census puts it in REFUTED. Triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6 and the Operators table, which have low internal overlap on this point (pipeline, reach, operator arity).

**Refuter:** show that parity conservation was never bound and predicting in pre-1956 physics, so the residual was MISSING rather than WRONG. That would move the finding to F3-style analysis and break this verdict.

### F3 - Symmetry is a derived quantity, so parity is not a primitive (specified, triangulated)

Fig 13 :124: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Fig 13 :29-30: "A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what? A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."

Parity is a spatial reflection, so it attaches to the state space, not to the six-member set as a seventh member. The six are closed (Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."). A continuum was already removed for failing this test (Fig 13 :43). Symmetry fails it the same way: it is what a turn leaves behind.

**Consequence for condition B.** A "deeper principle" that merely restores a symmetry is not a new primitive under this rule. It would be a different arrangement of held atoms, or an import of a genuinely new axis. The distinction matters: the framework can characterise the shape of either, but only the second moves the wall.

**Strain resolved by re-census.** Fig 13 :62 ("no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything") initially looked like a direct figure line for this question. It strains: that line governs what is lost if the space primitive is removed from the set, whereas parity is a discrete operation on space, not the removal of space. Re-census sends it to the Fig 13 :124 line, which governs derived symmetries. This retraction is kept visible in §7.

**Refuter:** derive parity as a seventh member from the other six, or show that a symmetry appears as a primitive in the substrate table. Either breaks this finding.

### F4 - The deeper principle is a PREFERENCE, not a term (gap, triangulated)

Fig 5 :61-62: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term." Fig 5 :63-64: "Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION". Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM".

This governs condition B directly. "Deeper principle" is the shape the searcher wants. It is unbound, predicts nothing, and accrues no residual, so no guard in the mint pipeline can see it. The framework's remedy is a discipline, not a step: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, let it accrue a residual. That binding is done in §5 as the pre-registered refuter, so the preference becomes refusable.

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." So the deliverable for B is a shape with a stated falsifier, not a named theory. Candidate names (mirror matter, left-right symmetry, a grand unified gauge group) are bindings from the physics frame, provenanced as such, and carry zero figure weight until they are settled or imported with a source gate.

**Refuter:** bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term and show it accrues a residual that trends down under an instrument that discriminates among sources. If that happens, the gap closes and this finding is withdrawn. Until then the `gap` stands.

### F5 - The existence question is a metaframe question (gap, triangulated)

Fig 8 :34: "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe. Godel's second theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency." Fig 8 :37: "A frame cannot certify its own limit." Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

Whether the Standard Model's chiral structure reflects something deeper is a question about the frame from outside the frame. A Null result for right-handed currents at a given energy does not prove there is no deeper principle; it proves the frame has not reached one. Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom".

This is why condition B cannot be answered by more data inside the same closure. It needs an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."), with a source gate and a shadow test, and debited against that frame's independence.

**Refuter:** a derivation inside the current frame that settles the existence or nonexistence of a deeper principle without importing a new atom. Under Fig 8 this is not available in principle, so a claimed derivation should be re-censused as an import that has not declared its provenance.

### F6 - Depth and absence look the same from inside (gap, single-source for the figure)

Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 6 :28-31 keeps the instruments opposed: INWARD extends an instrument already returning something; OUTWARD casts the residual in effect terms and finds a frame whose closure predicts its shape. They are two tracks and must not be read against each other.

For this question, INWARD asks whether an instrument at higher energy or higher precision can resolve a right-handed current or a mirror partner. OUTWARD asks whether a different closure already predicts the handed asymmetry as a consequence. A failure of INWARD is not evidence against OUTWARD, and vice versa. The Mirror matter article supplies the candidate shape: "parity symmetry can be restored as a fundamental symmetry of nature if the particle content is enlarged so that every particle has a mirror partner." It also records that parity "can also be spontaneously broken depending on the Higgs potential", so restoration and spontaneous breaking are both live shapes, not one answer.

**Refuter:** an instrument that discriminates a depth explanation from genuine absence, for example a positive signal for right-handed currents at a specified mass scale, or a null result with a sensitivity that covers the whole predicted range. Either would collapse the two tracks into one verdict.

### F7 - The residual is per channel, never an average (general, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 and :22 (quoted in section 0) govern the method. Parity violation is not a global property read off an averaged sample. Wu measured Co-60 beta asymmetry; Garwin, Lederman and Weinrich measured pion and muon decay; the Goldhaber experiment settled neutrino helicity; CP violation was found in neutral kaons and later in B mesons, D mesons and baryons. Each is a slot. The CP violation article reports that "CP violation is only observed in the weak interaction", and that in 2025 LHCb discovered it in baryons. Aggregating these into one number would hide which channel carries the live residual.

This also sets a caution on outreach: a single dramatic asymmetry is not the whole ground record.

**Refuter:** show that the asymmetry exists only in an aggregate across channels and vanishes in every individual channel. That would break the per-slot reading.

### F8 - Imports need a source gate and a shadow test (specified)

Fig 6 :23 (quoted in F5) and Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

If a deeper-principle candidate is offered, the record must say which closure it came from, what gap it explains, and what it costs in independence. The Mirror matter article is an import candidate with a declared provenance (Lee and Yang 1956, Kobzarev, Okun and Pomeranchuk 1966, Foot, Lew and Volkas 1991), which is exactly the kind of record the figure asks for. It is still `candidate`: the same article notes that most neutron-to-mirror-neutron searches "found no signal and hence gave limits" and one paper claimed a signal. Treating it as settled would spend the frame's independence for nothing.

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The shape was described in section 1 before mirror matter was named here.

**Refuter:** an import that explains no gap, or whose source is the same evidence pool as the frame it is imported into. That is the common-cause collapse of Fig 2, disqualifying as a verdict.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The two conditions are refuted separately.

**Condition A (status):** refuted if a controlled weak measurement returns equal mirror rates after polarisation, acceptance and background are corrected. Then the status reverts to `open` and the whole package stops.

**Condition B (deeper principle):** the shape claim is refuted, or closed, by any of the following:
1. An instrument discriminates among sources at the relevant scale: a reproducible right-handed-current signal at a stated mass, a mirror-nucleon oscillation, or a neutron EDM at the sensitivity that separates candidate closures. That names the slot and moves B from `gap` to `grounded` or `refuted` for specific candidates.
2. A bound ordinary term is written down that predicts the handed asymmetry as a consequence rather than a postulate, and its residual trends down against an instrument. That closes the Fig 5 preference limit.
3. The substrate is broken from the other side: parity is derived as a seventh member of the six-term set, or a composition of two states with no intervening structure is admitted and cannot be refused.
4. A source-discriminating argument shows that depth and absence are no longer indiscernible from inside the current frame.

If none fires, the verdict on B remains `gap`. No amount of agreement among readers fires any of them.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Ship the measured status as `grounded` and keep it separate from the principle question. Do not let one bleed into the other.
2. Describe parity violation as a refusal of a held term, not as a missing force (F2). This is the figure-grounded correction that changes what could be composed.
3. Offer condition B as a shape with a falsifier, never as a named theory (F4, F8). Mirror matter and left-right models are candidate imports with provenance, not verdicts.
4. Ask every outside reader what would refute stage 1 before offering any framework material (Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source").
5. Record the round trip gap rather than calling the formula identical to the figures (Fig 4 :30).
