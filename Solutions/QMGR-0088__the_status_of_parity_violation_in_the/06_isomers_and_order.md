### Q1 - Refusal comes before mint (order)

- The formula's route step: "REFUTED a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation", and the first four bins sort by what is MISSING while the fifth sorts by what is WRONG.
- Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
- Fig 6 :42-43: "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."
- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- Hence the order is: parity conservation held and predicted -> Co-60 and the confirming experiments returned a residual that did not trend down -> the refusal term was added -> the standing of parity conservation as a composable term fell -> only then could a chiral gauge theory be settled and become an atom at the next level.
- The hex sign for this is negation, not subtraction. Subtraction is "A with B removed or absent" (Operators table :42-45); negation is "this does not hold" (Operators table :55). The weak interaction does not remove the mirror sector from a list; it refuses the claim that the laws are mirror invariant.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a frame can mint a deeper principle and settle it while still composing over parity conservation as a live term, the order claim fails. Equivalently, if a consistent theory predicts the observed handedness while retaining parity as an exact symmetry of the same fields with no hidden sector, the refusal was misclassified and this derivation is withdrawn. Triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6 and the Operators table; no ground test was run here.

### Q2 - Are the two readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- A parity-conserving electroweak theory and a chiral (parity-violating) one can be written over the **same gauge and matter content** with a different assignment of representations: vector-like doublets versus left-handed doublets and right-handed singlets. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers** if the atom set is held fixed: same fields, different bonding, different observed behaviour. The difference is invisible in an ingredient list and decisive in a beta-decay asymmetry.
- If instead one theory requires an atom the other does not (a right-handed neutrino, a mirror partner, an additional gauged B-L symmetry), they are **not** isomers. They are two members of the same **functional group** (chiral gauge theories), a claim that fixes only the kind, not which member. The Mirror matter article gives exactly this qualifier distinction: mirror symmetry can be exact with a partner for every particle, or spontaneously broken depending on the Higgs potential. Same group, different members.
- **Falsifier:** if one of the two readings cannot be written over the same field content without adding an atom, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead. The mirror-matter example already exercises this falsifier: adding a mirror partner adds content, so that candidate is a functional-group variant, not an isomer of the Standard Model over a fixed set.
- Status: `specified` for the figure definition (single-source, Fig 12), unmeasured for the physics claim that the field contents can actually be held fixed.

### Q3 - What is the second-order composition?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"
- The measured refusal is settled against the ground (F1), and the chiral gauge construction is settled against the same ground. Each can serve as an atom at the next level. The comparison of a theory with a candidate deeper closure is therefore a **second-order composition**, licensed by the settled-molecule rule rather than by a new primitive.
- This is also where the mirror-matter and spontaneous-breaking shapes live. They are not competitors to the measurement; they are compositions built on a settled measurement.
- **Falsifier:** show that a settled result cannot serve as an atom for a new composition, or that "settled" here is only a gate (internal consistency) and not the ground. Formula step 5: "A gate is not the ground." If the chiral construction were only internally consistent and not experimentally settled, the second-order step is not licensed. The world sources say it is experimentally settled, so this falsifier has not fired.

### Q4 - Why the deeper principle stays a shape

- Fig 5 :62: "a frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term." The figures cannot see a preference, so they cannot certify that a deeper principle exists.
- Fig 8 :34: "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe. Godel's second theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency."
- Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."
- ⇒ Held atoms can be arranged into candidates, but the existence of an atom outside the closure is not decidable from inside. The output is a shape with a refuter, not a name. The instrument that would settle it is described in `08` section 5.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that names the source, or a bound ordinary term that predicts the handedness and whose residual trends down. Either closes the gap.

## CONCLUSION

The order is fixed: refusal before mint, at first order; the settled chiral construction becomes an atom for a second-order comparison. Over a fixed field content, a parity-conserving and a parity-violating theory are Fig-12 isomers, same atoms and different bond, with different behaviour. If a candidate requires extra fields, it falls to the weaker functional-group reading. The deeper principle itself is not named: it is a preference held as a shape, and the figure's metaframe limit plus the depth/absence indistinguishability keep the verdict at `gap`. What rescues the question from vagueness is the set of falsifiers written above, not a candidate name.
