**Subject:** Check one output about causal representation before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question bundles three conditions that should be kept separate. (1) Representation: causal direction is carried by an ordered bond and a refusal operator, not by the ingredient statistics of the joint distribution. A vocabulary that can only name states and dependences cannot name a route. (2) Identification: a frame searching inside a closed library adds no new atom, so a direction atom that is not already held cannot be found by more search over passive observations. (3) Usefulness: a representation counts for something only when the ground pays for it, an intervention outcome or a downstream decision, not when the model scores itself.
>
> The load-bearing word in the question is "alone". Remove it and my framing predicts what your field already reports: observational data plus an imported structural assumption, an environment label, temporal order, an auxiliary variable, or an intervention, can identify. The framing does not deny causal representation learning. It prices the import that makes it work.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that causal representation learning from observations is impossible in the world. Fig 8 says a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence. I am claiming something narrower and checkable: in a closed frame with no import and no ground contact, the direction atom is unreachable, and the result is a shape and a boundary, not a name. Which concrete latent variable is causal is left open. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
>
> **What would refute the output (written before this message):**
> 1. derive a direction term in the frame's closure from passive observations alone, with no imported atom and no new instrument, and show it predicts unseen interventions above a correlation baseline;
> 2. show a frame settling "useful" with a metric it produces itself, where the score does not move when an intervention changes the ground outcome;
> 3. write a directional, refusable causal claim in a vocabulary with only symmetric bonds and no negation, and show it still distinguishes cause from effect.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
