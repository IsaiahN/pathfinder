The main audit returns a shape in four slots but leaves two questions open. (1) Is the relation between observationally equivalent causal graphs a case of Fig 12's isomer, or merely a loose analogy? (2) What is the dependency order of representation, identification and settlement, and does an import cross that order correctly? ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are observationally equivalent DAGs isomers? (`general`, triangulated)

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- The three two-variable structures X→Y, X←Y, and Z→X with Z→Y share the observable variables (and, for the first two, the same dependence). Wikipedia's Causality article states that Type 1 and Type 2 triplets "represent the same statistical dependencies ... and are, therefore, indistinguishable within purely cross-sectional data." Schölkopf et al. state that without additional assumptions "we cannot distinguish these three cases using observational data" and that the class of observational distributions is "the same in all three cases."
- Map: same atoms (the variables), different bond (the direction), identical formula (the joint distribution), different behaviour (the result of do(X)). This satisfies all four clauses of Fig 12 :117 without strain.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two observationally equivalent DAGs give identical intervention distributions under every intervention, the "decisive in the behaviour" clause fails and the isomer claim is a loose analogy, not a figure-derived fit.
- Verdict: accepted as a structural fit, `general`. Triangulated across Fig 12 (bond definition) and two low-overlap world sources (Wikipedia Causality, Schölkopf et al.). It is a retrodiction of a known case, not a forward-tested prediction. Recorded as corroboration, never as a new law.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order? (`general`, triangulated)

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces" (Fig 3 :20-21).
- Map: perception (passive o') → vocabulary (ordered bonds present?) → objective (does the system's success criterion distinguish correlation from intervention?) → planning (search or mint) → learn and carry (settle at ground). The middle link is the one with no instrument. Fig 3 :26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
- Formula step 2 adds a prior step: refuse before mint. The term held is "observational data alone suffices", so the residual is REFUTED, not NOVEL. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- **Falsifier:** if a system with no ordered-bond vocabulary and no intervention-sensitive objective nevertheless produces intervention-valid causal representations at scale, the order is wrong and the break is elsewhere.
- Verdict: accepted, `general`, triangulated (Fig 3 and formula step 2, different artefacts).

### Q3 - Does the import cross scales correctly? (`specified`, single-source for the round trip)

- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- An imported structural assumption (auxiliary variable, environment label, intervention protocol) is a head start coming **down** from another frame, not a recording of that frame's success. If a team copies another team's learned latent names without the provenance of how those names were settled at a ground, it has carried a recording upward and will replay one occasion. The round trip gap R_T is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, recorded qualitatively in `01` §6.
- **Falsifier:** show an imported assumption that is a recording of one occasion and that still transfers across environments with zero measured gap. That breaks Fig 4 :22-25.
- Verdict: accepted, `specified`, single-source (Fig 4 only), with the two-currency caveat from Fig 12 :126-130 held separately: bits price the description, actions price finding out whether it holds.

### Q4 - Where does refusal fit? (`general`, single-source for the operator, open price)

- Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"
- Operators table :77-78: "What a unary operator costs is open: an atom slot, nothing, or the choice of what to negate."
- A counterfactual claim is a refusal of an actual-world claim, so `¬` is required in addition to the ordered bonds. It is not a seventh or eighth bond; it joins nothing. The price is explicitly open, so no number is asserted here.
- **Falsifier:** derive the cost of ¬ from the seven bond tests, or show a counterfactual claim written with bonds alone and no unary operator. Either would close the open item.
- Verdict: structural requirement accepted, `general`, single-source (Operators table). Price left open, not estimated.

## CONCLUSION

The observationally equivalent DAGs are Fig 12 isomers, not an analogy: same variables, different ordered bond, same observable formula, different intervention behaviour. The dependency order is perception → bond vocabulary → intervention-sensitive objective → search → settlement, with a refusal of "alone" before any mint. An import is a head start coming down from a differing closure and must carry a round-trip gap; a recording copied upward will replay a past success. Refusal needs the unary `¬`, whose price the figures leave open. All four claims are simulated and checked for internal consistency, none is `grounded`.
