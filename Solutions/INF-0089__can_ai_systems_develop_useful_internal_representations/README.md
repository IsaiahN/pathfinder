## The answer in one line

The figures return a **shape and a boundary**, not a yes or a no. A system whose library holds ordered bonds can *represent* the shape of causal structure from passive observations, but it cannot *settle* that representation as useful or correctly directed from observational data alone: closure is idempotent, no search adds the direction atom, and there is no anchor to range against. The word "alone" is load-bearing. Remove it and the framework's import rule predicts exactly what the causal representation learning literature reports: observational data plus a provenanced structural assumption or an intervention can identify. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
3. Sending the framework before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
