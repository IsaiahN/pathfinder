## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (opened): *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, *"the answer is per slot, with a currency and a non-updating anchor named for each slot"* is a shape. *"Tariffs cost 0.27% of GDP"* is a reading from one instrument, not the shape, and *"tariffs are bad"* is a verdict stated in the wrong mode.

The same essay supplies the second move that decides this question: if the final search fails, *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* That is Fig 9 :54-57 in narrative form, and it is the exact move F1 makes.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (opened): use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Applied here: describe what you would expect to see in the protected sector, in consumer prices, in export volumes and in supply continuity, then let the reader check those expectations against data.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping (opened): you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* In this solution the finish line is the per-slot separator: having built it, the question no longer asks for a single verdict.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Here: an employment series, a price index, a bilateral trade flow, a supply-continuity measurement.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Here: the ecology insurance literature supplies the shape of resilience as response diversity; the trade literature supplies the three-channel decomposition. Neither is a gift from the framework; both are bindings with provenance.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried): *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

So the five slots in `01` are a visible guess at where this residual makes contact, not nature's own list. Naming a slot as *the* answer to "jobs" or "supply chains" would turn a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why a pooled national number is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; it could be one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

A national "jobs" number reads the projection. Protected-sector gains, downstream input-cost losses and retaliatory export losses can produce the same aggregate reading from different sources. The reading does not determine which source acted. What is owed is the three-channel discriminator (Flaaen and Pierce's decomposition is exactly such an instrument), not the aggregate verdict.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened): *"The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* A framework cannot certify that its separator is the right one; the ground must pay for it.

## 5. What licenses the gap

*Library of Babel* (opened): if the exhaustive search fails, the possibilities are that the question was unanswerable or malformed, possibly a bundle. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :133: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The universal "do tariffs protect jobs" asks for a conversion across incommensurable currencies. No figure supplies that conversion, so the honest report is a **gap characterized in effect terms**: a single non-updating ground that returns one signed effect for jobs, prices and resilience at once. That is a shape, not a name, and it is why the gap is not filled with a compromise verdict such as "tariffs help some workers but hurt consumers." That sentence is true but it is not the answer; it is the union restated.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 and §4 (see `06` Q1)

I initially wrote "shape, not a name" as if it forbade naming jobs, prices or resilience. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive: *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Jobs, prices, networks and retaliation are bindings from the trade-policy frame onto atoms the library already holds (an employment count is a spectrum-like ordering, prices are a gradient-like difference that can be spent, supply chains are the held network atom, retaliation is a cycle). Naming them is provenance, not the invention of a seventh term. What remains wrong is claiming that naming them moves the wall: Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*

My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted. A national jobs reading still cannot comment on which slot produced it, and the aggregate remains a lossy projection. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held atoms. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened): *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous economists who agree that tariffs are good (or bad) is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the separator against data, with the ground settling each slot.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay (opened): *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of tariff policy and must be labelled as such. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
