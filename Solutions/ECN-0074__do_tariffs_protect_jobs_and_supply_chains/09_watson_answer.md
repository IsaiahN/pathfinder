## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that tariffs protect jobs and supply chains versus the claim that they raise costs and invite retaliation. Option A: read it as one binary with two opposite answers and pick a side. Option B: treat it as a union of overlapping conditions, return per-slot verdicts with named currencies and non-updating anchors, and declare the universal aggregate a gap. What each leads to: A produces a national verdict in the wrong mode, averages away the protected-sector gain and the downstream loss, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B produces a checkable separator, a retaliation cycle, a bond-versus-structure distinction, four refuters written in advance, and an honest stop at the aggregate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the promised effect of a border price change and its measurement. Arity: five conditions, so a **union**, not a partition: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Symmetry: the "protect / raise" opposition is rhetorical; the effects are in different currencies, so no symmetric sign is defined. Scale: one membrane action, effects registered at firm, sector, consumer and national frames, with retaliation a cross-frame loop. Fig 9 calls for unbundling before searching.
- **Domain 1, protected-sector frame.** Actors: import-competing domestic firms, their workers, the tariff authority. Currency: sector output and employment. Anchor: measured sector employment. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 2, consumer and downstream frame.** Actors: consumers, importing firms, downstream producers. Currency: prices, real income, input costs and downstream employment. Anchor: price and cost indices. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 3, trading-partner frame.** Actors: foreign exporters and governments, domestic export sectors. Currency: export volumes, foreign and domestic welfare. Anchor: measured bilateral flows. Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the cycle and triangulated on independence.
- **Domain 4, network / supply-chain frame.** Actors: suppliers, producers, logistics nodes. Currency: continuity and resilience of supply. Anchor: observed continuity under disruption. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 5, verification frame.** Actors: researchers, advocates, journals. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all five domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero. Verdict: `general`, triangulated.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the border action, name the slot vocabulary, fix the objective (which residual, which currency, which anchor), then plan the lever. Refuse before mint (formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame. Settle each slot before the second-order comparison.

Strain test applied: reading "tariffs protect jobs" and "tariffs raise costs" as opposite answers to one question strains because the currencies differ; re-census assigns them to different domains. No strain remains after that re-census, except the live type question of whether a welfare total is a ground or a filter (recorded in `11`).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The union survives; the binary does not. A tariff is one border action whose effects register per slot, in currencies that do not add, against anchors that must not move when frames think about them. Import protection can help the protected sector; input tariffs and retaliation are the larger drag. Retaliation is a cycle from a differing frame, not a side effect on a mirror. A tariff reprices a bond and does not rebuild a supply network; reshoring is habitat substitution with two silent failures. The universal "protect or raise" verdict is a declared gap, because no figure supplies the cross-currency conversion. This is `specified` and ungrounded; the world literature corroborates the shape and supplies magnitudes, but this derivation measured nothing.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, but real. The corrected packet is mode-labeled, states four refuters before the conclusion and records provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict instead of a separator, which the figures price as buying agreement in a shared evidence pool. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A wrong policy conclusion drawn from this shape would be a category error, since the shape explicitly refuses the aggregate it would need.
- **undoable:** yes. The binary framing, the aggregate verdict and the conflation of bond with structure are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no atom has to be withdrawn; a refusal adds a term and removes standing to compose over it, so the reach falls safely.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. One non-updating measurement returning the same signed effect for all five slots, or a constructed cross-currency conversion, closes the gap and breaks the union verdict; a measurement showing a tariff raised supply continuity with the supplier and route set fixed breaks the bond-versus-structure distinction; a large-tariff episode with no response from any differing frame empties the retaliation domain; an instrument at the objective link carrying signal at all slots dissolves the dependency finding. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
