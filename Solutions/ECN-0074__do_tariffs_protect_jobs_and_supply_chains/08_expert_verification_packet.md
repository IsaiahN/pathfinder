# Expert verification packet — the Tether on tariffs, jobs and supply chains

**What this is:** a claim in a form a trade economist or economic historian can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal aggregate. Not `grounded`.** No tariff effect was measured in this derivation. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do tariffs protect jobs and supply chains, or raise costs and invite retaliation?" is a union of at least five overlapping conditions, not a binary (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). A tariff is one border action whose effects must be read per slot, each in its own currency against a non-updating anchor: protected-sector output, consumer and input prices, downstream employment, export volumes under retaliation, and supply-network continuity. Import protection can raise the first; input tariffs and retaliation, a cycle from a differing frame, are the larger drag in the others. A tariff reprices a bond at the border; it does not rebuild the network. Reshoring is a separate habitat substitution with two silent failures. No figure converts the five currencies into one signed national verdict, so the universal answer is a declared gap, not a hidden compromise.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — the union and per-slot indexing; checkable by an empirical trade economist

- **Statement:** the residual must be split before it is judged; aggregating slots hides the signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 — *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** does the 2018 tariff literature actually find opposite-signed effects in different slots from one action? Flaaen and Pierce report a small protection gain offset by larger input-cost and retaliation drags; Autor, Beck, Dorn and Hanson report no employment gain in newly protected sectors and clear losses from retaliation; Javorcik et al. report no positive effect of protection on job postings. One evidence pool, not three witnesses.
- **Refuter:** a single non-updating ground returning one signed effect for all five slots with a conversion each instrument accepts. If it exists, the union collapses and C1 is false.

### C2 — retaliation as a cycle from a differing frame; checkable by an economic historian or trade theorist

- **Statement:** retaliation is the tariff output re-entering as input from a frame whose closure differs, not a side effect on a passive variable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 — *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :31 — *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **What to check:** is the historical record consistent with an independently chosen partner response? Mitchener, Wandschneider and O'Rourke (Economic Journal 2022) find US exports to retaliators fell 28-32 percent and retaliators' welfare gains from trade fell 8-16 percent after Smoot-Hawley. The US Office of the Historian records world trade down about 66 percent between 1929 and 1934. Retaliation is **single-source on the figure side** for the cycle itself; independence is triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7.
- **Refuter:** a measured large-tariff episode with no response from any differing frame and unchanged exports against a fixed anchor. Then the retaliation domain is empty for that episode.

### C3 — bond versus structure in supply chains; checkable by a supply-chain or network economist

- **Statement:** a tariff changes the price of one bond at the border; it does not by itself restructure the supply network. Reshoring is habitat substitution with two silent failures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :24 — *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*. Fig 12 :5 — *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 11 :66 — *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68 — *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*, and :69 — *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.
- **What to check:** did the 2018 tariffs change the set of suppliers and routes, or only their prices? Amiti, Redding and Weinstein (JEP 2019) report dramatic supply-chain reconfiguration and roughly 165 billion dollars of trade redirected annually to avoid tariffs. They do not report a resilience gain from the tariff itself.
- **Refuter:** a measurement showing a tariff raised supply continuity under disruption with the supplier and route set held fixed and a non-updating anchor.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that tariffs never help any worker; the protected-sector gain is a per-slot reading.
- Not that retaliation is automatic in every episode; its magnitude and occurrence are empirical.
- Not that a national welfare total is the ground for the jobs question. It is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 — *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not that a specific tariff rate is optimal, and not that the framework can name one (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that "shape, not a name" forbids naming jobs, prices or resilience. It governs missing primitives; these are bindings onto held atoms (`05` §6, `06` Q1).
- Not grounded. The whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot employment discriminator separating import protection, input costs and retaliation against sector series.
2. A price discriminator (customs unit values before tariff) distinguishing domestic tariff-inclusive prices from foreign export prices.
3. A retaliation test with partner response treated as an independent frame and bilateral flows as the anchor.
4. A supply-continuity test with supplier and route set held fixed.
5. One ground and one sign for all five slots, or a constructed cross-currency conversion, which would close the declared gap and force retraction of C1.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 12 :121/:5, Fig 11 :66/:68-69 (six short passages).
2. Check C1 against the employment channels you know: does a single national jobs number hide opposite-signed slot effects?
3. Check C2 against one retaliation episode you know: did the partner behave as a frame with a different closure?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a bond-price change from a network change, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
