# The Tether's answer on tariffs, jobs and supply chains

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal yes/no. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No tariff, price or employment observation was taken here.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for one verdict on a bundle. The residual is the gap between the promise ("protect jobs and supply chains") and the measurement, and it is indexed per slot: protected-sector output, consumer and input prices, downstream employment, export volumes under retaliation, and supply-network continuity (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The question is a union, so one lever cannot clear it and verdicts must be kept per domain.

## 2. The shape

1. **The either/or is a false partition.** A tariff can raise output in the protected sector and lower it downstream from the same action. The two packages overlap at "jobs", and that shared fraction is unassignable (Fig 9 :56-57). The union must be unbundled before it is searched.
2. **Every verdict is per slot, in its own currency, against a non-updating anchor.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The tariff's valence flips with the residual: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another"* (Fig 11 :50).
3. **Import protection can help the protected sector; the measured 2018 gain was small and was swamped by the other two channels.** This is a `specified` world-corroborated reading, not a figure verdict. The figures fix only that the gain must be read at its own slot.
4. **Input tariffs and retaliation are the larger drag.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Retaliation comes from a frame with a different closure; a mirror frame cannot supply it (Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).
5. **A tariff reprices a bond; it does not rebuild a network.** A supply chain is the network atom (Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*). Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Reshoring is habitat substitution with two silent failures (Fig 11 :68-69).
6. **Jobs, prices and resilience are incommensurable currencies.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A welfare total is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
7. **The universal verdict is a gap.** No figure line converts the five slots into one signed number. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :133: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.
8. **Order matters.** The three channels are a sequence (protection), a production (input-cost state) and a cycle (retaliation), not an ingredient list (`06` Q2; Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*). Over a fixed atom set, protection-only and reciprocal-escalation regimes are isomers (Fig 12 :117).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that tariffs never help any worker. A protected-sector gain is possible and is a per-slot reading.
- Not that retaliation is automatic in every episode. It is a real response from a differing frame; its occurrence and magnitude are empirical.
- Not that a national welfare total settles the jobs question. It is a figure in one accounting, not a cross-currency ground.
- Not that a tariff is the same thing as reshoring, or that a price change is a network change.
- Not that a particular tariff rate is optimal. No figure returns a rate (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not grounded. This is a simulated derivation; the world sources corroborate the shape and supply the magnitudes, but they were read, not measured here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminator per slot, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Per-slot employment discriminator:** the three-channel decomposition (import protection, input costs, retaliation) already separates the sources of a national jobs reading. Read each channel against its own sector series.
- **Price discriminator:** customs unit values before tariff application distinguish a tariff-inclusive domestic price from a foreign export price; both Amiti et al. and Fajgelbaum et al. used this and found complete pass-through.
- **Retaliation test:** bilateral flows against partner response indicators, with the partner treated as a differing frame rather than a mirror. The Smoot-Hawley record supplies the long-loop version.
- **Supply-continuity test:** a measurement of continuity under disruption with the supplier and route set held fixed. If a tariff improves it without a network change, F5 is refuted.
- **Standing refuters from `01` §3:** one ground and one sign for all five slots; bond becomes structure; empty retaliation domain; objective instrument at all slots; constructed cross-currency ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached at the shape, unreached at the universal aggregate.** The five slots are a visible guess at where this residual makes contact; declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral (*They're Scanning Books*, carried). The framework stops where instruments cannot distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The cross-currency ground the question implicitly asks for has not been built; search inside the trade-policy closure does not build it (Fig 8 :39). A gap is reported rather than filled with a compromise slogan.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say tariffs are good or bad. It says the question is a bundle, not a binary. A tariff is one action at one border, and its effects register in five different slots, each with its own currency and its own non-updating anchor: protected-sector output, consumer and input prices, downstream jobs, export volumes under retaliation, and the continuity of supply networks. Protection can raise output in the first slot; input costs and retaliation, a real loop from a different frame, are the larger drag in the others. A tariff reprices one bond at the border; it does not rebuild a supply network, and reshoring is a separate habitat substitution with two silent failures. The framework returns that shape and a refuter written in advance, not a national verdict. The single "do tariffs protect jobs" number is a declared gap, because jobs, prices and resilience are currencies that do not add. The ground, not agreement, decides each slot.
