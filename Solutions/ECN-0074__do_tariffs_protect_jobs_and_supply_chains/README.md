**Section:** 8. Trade, globalization, and industrial policy.

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal yes/no. Nothing here is `grounded`: no tariff was imposed, no employment or price series was measured in this derivation.

## Restating the question as an effect

The ordinary term hides two different questions. Option A, "tariffs protect jobs and supply chains", names a policy lever and two promised outcomes. Option B, "tariffs raise costs and invite retaliation", names the same lever and two unwanted outcomes. The effect version is: a border price change is applied at one membrane, and we ask what residual it reduces and what residual it creates, per slot (sector, factor, horizon, trading partner), against a ground that does not move when a frame thinks about it. What an answer would look like: a per-slot separator naming the currency and the anchor for each outcome, with a failure condition written before the run. Bind that as an ordinary term and the "either/or" becomes a testable union rather than a rhetorical choice.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with a protectionist or a free-trade position.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words. A room does not upgrade a label (Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*). Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). And asking a reader to accept the framework before they check the output spends the only independence they contribute (Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
