## Q2 — What order are the three channels?

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A list of three channels is not a route. Reading the operators (Operators table; Fig 12 :70-89):

1. **Import protection** is `→` sequence: the border price changes, then domestic output rises because a previously imported good is now relatively dearer. Fig 12 :71: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. The domestic output was already present; it expanded. Refutable by a sector where output does not move after protection.
2. **Input-cost channel** is `⇒` production: the tariff fires and a *new* cost state exists that was absent before, because imported intermediates are priced up. Fig 12 :76-77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* and the test *"is there a B before A fires? No."* This is why it cannot be written as subtraction: the higher input cost is not an absence, it is a newly produced condition.
3. **Retaliation** is a cycle: Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The domestic tariff output re-enters as an input to the partner's decision, whose output re-enters as an input to domestic export sectors (Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* across the membrane).

A chain of three independent effects would be a flat formula. The figure forbids that: Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* The world literature's three-channel decomposition is the empirical counterpart of this reading; it is corroboration, not the source of the order.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the three channels can be written as three independent additive terms with no ordering or loop, the bond reading fails and the flat formula stands. The literature's need to estimate them jointly would then be a statistical convenience rather than a structural fact.

## Q3 — Are two tariff regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Over the fixed atom set {domestic producer, foreign supplier, domestic consumer, border, export relation}, consider two regimes:

- **Protection-only:** the border bond is a tariff on imports; the export relation is left untaxed.
- **Reciprocal escalation:** the same atoms, but the border bond is tariff-on-imports **and** the export relation acquires a retaliatory tariff bond.

Same atoms, different bond arrangement, different behaviour. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not variants of one policy. This is the structural reason degree-style reasoning (a higher or lower tariff rate) misses the point: changing the bond type changes the substance. Fig 12 :40: *"same two atoms, three substances"* applies directly: a tariff, an input tariff and a retaliatory tariff over two of the same atoms are three different substances.

**Falsifier:** if a regime can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, retaliation without a distinct export relation or a differing frame), the isomer claim fails and the regimes are merely different points on one scale. `01` F4 requires a differing frame, so this falsifier is live, not rhetorical.

## Q4 — Functional group, catalyst and the silent failures of reshoring

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The three-channel structure (protection, input cost, retaliation) is a functional group: its presence fixes *what kind* of tariff episode this is; the particular sectors and partners say *which member*. This is why F1 can call the union a union without claiming every episode is identical.

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A policy authority that imposes a tariff and survives its own use is catalyst-like: it can impose again. A protective industry that lobbies to renew the tariff is a regenerating part; an industry that disappears after the protection ends is a spent part. Nothing in a headlineemployment number records which. Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A government can introduce tariff protection; it cannot subtract the trading partners or the supply network that the introduction acts on.

Fig 11 :66-69 govern reshoring: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another"*, with *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*. Over the fixed atom set, protection-only and reshoring-plus-protection are isomers (same atoms, different arrangement of the network bond and the policy bond). The second carries two silent failures the first does not.

## Q5 — The two currencies of a tariff regime

Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* and *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

- **Bits:** how much has to be stated to specify the regime (which sectors, which rates, which partners, which exemption rules). A detailed exemption schedule is expensive to describe.
- **Actions:** what it costs to find out whether the regime actually protected the slot (an employment series, a price index, a supply-continuity measurement, a trade-flow dataset).

Fig 12 :127: *"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."* A policy announced in a short press release (cheap in bits) can require an expensive empirical program to settle. Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* A welfare total that adds tariff revenue, producer gains and consumer losses is a figure in one accounting; it cannot price the actions needed to know whether supply continuity improved.

**Falsifier:** a single instrument that prices both the description length of a regime and the measurement cost of its effects in one unit, with both currencies' instruments agreeing. Absent it, Q5 stands as a priced distinction.

## CONCLUSION

The three tariff channels are not an ingredient list. They are an ordered structure: a sequence for protection, a production for the input-cost state, and a cycle for retaliation. Two tariff regimes over a fixed atom set are isomers, so the operative variable is bond type rather than rate. The three-channel structure is a functional group, and a protection authority is a catalyst whose regenerating or spent character a headline number does not record. Reshoring is habitat substitution with two silent failures. A regime costs in two currencies that do not add. All of this is `specified` and ungrounded; it fixes shape and order, not coefficients.
