## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual

The question arrives as an either/or:

> Tariffs protect jobs and supply chains, OR tariffs raise costs and invite retaliation.

Stated in effect terms, a tariff is an action `a` at a border membrane. The library `Γ` predicts an observation `o′`. The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, and the figures require that it be read **per slot** (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The relevant slots are not two; they are at least five:

| slot | predicted effect of `a` | currency | candidate ground (anchor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 protected import-competing sector | output and employment rise | jobs/output in that sector | sector employment and output series |
| S2 domestic consumers and import-dependent firms | prices rise, real income falls | prices, real income | consumer and producer price indices |
| S3 downstream users of imported inputs | input costs rise, downstream employment falls | input costs, downstream jobs | input-output tables and downstream employment |
| S4 export sectors facing foreign tariffs | exports fall, retaliation loss | export volumes, foreign welfare | bilateral trade flows |
| S5 supply-chain structure | networks reconfigure or reshore | continuity and resilience of supply | supply-continuity under disruption |

The residual is the gap between the promise and the measurement at each slot. The question's either/or silently aggregates S1 through S5 into two rhetorical packages. That aggregation is the defect the audit finds first.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** The answer we are searching for would look like: *a per-slot verdict that names the currency and a non-updating anchor for each outcome, separates the conditions rather than averaging them, and states what would refute each verdict before the run*. Written down here and treated as a candidate with its own residual, so the preference can be refused like any other term (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-66).

## 1. Figure census

Before choosing any line: describe R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A nameable anchor is required before a region counts as a domain.

**R by arity / symmetry / scale.** Arity: at least five conditions, so a **union**, not a binary partition (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Symmetry: "protect" and "raise costs" are written as opposite ends of one dial, but they are measured in different currencies against different anchors, so the opposition is rhetorical, not structural (Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*). Scale: a tariff acts at one membrane (the border) and its effects register at firm, sector, consumer and national frames, with retaliation a cross-frame response (Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*).

**Domain census.** One line per domain:

- **D1, protected-sector frame.** Actors: import-competing domestic firms, their workers, the tariff authority. Currency: sector output and employment. Anchor: measured sector employment, which does not move because a frame approves of protection. A domain exists here.
- **D2, consumer and downstream frame.** Actors: consumers, import-dependent firms, downstream producers. Currency: prices, real income, input costs. Anchor: measured price and cost indices. A domain exists here.
- **D3, trading-partner frame.** Actors: foreign exporters and governments, domestic export sectors. Currency: export volumes, foreign and domestic welfare. Anchor: measured bilateral flows. A domain exists here, and it is a **different closure** (Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."*).
- **D4, network / supply-chain frame.** Actors: suppliers, producers, logistics nodes across borders. Currency: continuity and resilience of supply. Anchor: observed supply continuity under disruption. A domain exists here (Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*).
- **D5, verification frame.** Actors: researchers, policy advocates, journals. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). A domain exists here, and in this simulated run its independence is zero because all frames read one evidence pool.

**Partition or union:** union, not partition. S1 and S3 both register as "jobs" while pointing in opposite directions, so the two packages overlap; the shared fraction is unassignable (Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (a tariff is a border price change) precedes vocabulary (terms for jobs, costs, retaliation, bonds), which precedes the objective (which residual are we reducing, in which currency, against which anchor), which precedes planning (tariff yes/no). The question as posed jumps to planning before the objective link is fixed. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The either/or is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (decisive)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

A tariff can raise output in S1 and lower it in S3 at the same time, from the same action. Those are not two answers to one question; they are two conditions in a union with a shared fraction (both are "jobs") that no single number can assign. The world literature converges on exactly this decomposition: Flaaen and Pierce decompose into import protection, higher input costs and retaliation, finding *"a small boost from the import protection effect of tariffs is more than offset by larger drags from the effects of rising input costs and retaliatory tariffs"* (Federal Reserve FEDS 2019; full text via search integration, direct PDF fetch unsupported). Autor, Beck, Dorn and Hanson (2024) report that *"import tariffs on foreign goods neither raised nor lowered US employment in newly-protected sectors; retaliatory tariffs had clear negative employment impacts, primarily in agriculture"* (MIT PDF, search integration).

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 (union logic) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual), with low overlap between the two routes and world corroboration from a trade literature that independently uses the same three channels. This is a figure verdict; the world sources corroborate, they do not decide.

**Refuter:** show that a single scalar ground measures S1 through S5 and gives one sign for all of them, against an anchor that does not update on the measurement. If such a ground exists, the union collapses to one question and F1 is withdrawn.

### F2 — Jobs are indexed per slot; the same tariff is benefactor to one and malfactor to another

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The tariff is an actor whose valence flips with the slot. In S1 it is a benefactor; in S2, S3 and S4 it is a malfactor. The political phrase "protect jobs" names S1 while speaking as if it named all jobs. Javorcik, Stapleton, Kett and O'Kane (World Bank PRWP 10249, 2022) find *"we do not find any evidence of positive impacts of import protection on job openings"* while input and retaliatory tariffs reduced postings. The point is not that protection never helps a protected firm. It is that the help is slot-indexed and cannot be reported as a national jobs verdict without throwing away the only signal (Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*).

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot indexing) and Fig 11 (valence relative to residual). The two figures have low overlap: one governs measurement granularity, the other governs actor-valence. World corroboration from three independent empirical designs is one literature pool, not three witnesses.

**Refuter:** a measured national employment effect in which the protected-sector gain and the downstream loss are exactly cancellable and the residual at every sector slot is zero. That would make aggregation lossless and F2 false.

### F3 — Costs and jobs are different currencies; the "trade-off" is the summing error

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

"Tariffs protect jobs but raise costs" sounds like a trade-off on one scale. It is not. Employment in S1 is one measurement with one anchor; prices in S2 are another with a different anchor. Amiti, Redding and Weinstein (Journal of Economic Perspectives, 2019) report that *"the full incidence of the tariffs has fallen on domestic consumers and importers so far"* and that the United States experienced *"dramatic changes to its supply-chain network"* alongside higher prices. Fajgelbaum, Goldberg, Kennedy and Khandelwal (QJE 2020; NBER WP 25638) report *"complete pass-through of tariffs to duty-inclusive prices"* and losses to consumers and importing firms of *"$51 billion, or 0.27% of GDP"*. Those are price-currency readings. They do not convert into job-currency readings by arithmetic, and adding them to a jobs number is the named error.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 (two currencies) and Fig 1 (ground the only metric), with world corroboration. The "net welfare" number in the literature is itself a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

**Refuter:** exhibit a single, non-updating ground whose reading is simultaneously the jobs effect and the price effect, with a declared conversion that both currencies' instruments accept. Absent that, the summing stays refused.

### F4 — Retaliation is a cycle from a frame whose closure differs, not a side effect on a mirror

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Retaliation is the tariff output re-entering as input from another frame. That frame must have a closure that differs; if the trading partner were a mirror that merely echoed the first frame, no retaliation would register as new information. The historical record shows a real, independently chosen response: Mitchener, Wandschneider and O'Rourke (Economic Journal 2022; Cato research brief 2021) find that *"U.S. exports to retaliators fell by 28%–32%"* and that the retaliators' *"welfare gains from trade fell by 8%–16%"*. The Smoot-Hawley episode is the same structure with a longer loop (US Office of the Historian: *"world trade declined by some 66% between 1929 and 1934"*). Treating the partner as a passive variable is the coherent-drift collapse of Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

**Verdict:** `specified`. On the figure side this is **single-source** for the cycle itself (Fig 12 :121); the independence condition is triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7 (low overlap between triangulation logic and room-chain logic). World corroboration is strong but from one historical and one contemporary trade pool, counted as one witness.

**Refuter:** a measured episode in which a large tariff produces no response from any trading partner and export volumes are unchanged against a non-updating anchor. That would make the retaliation domain empty and F4 false for that episode.

### F5 — "Protect supply chains" conflates a structure with a border-price bond

Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* (the network atom).

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*

Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*

A supply chain is a network, one of the six structural atoms. A tariff changes the price of one bond at the border; it does not, by itself, add, remove or rewire the network's nodes and relations. Calling that "protecting supply chains" treats a bond change as a structure change, which Fig 12 forbids: the bond is not recoverable from the operands, and a list of ingredients (suppliers, factories, routes) is not the thing. Reshoring, if that is what is meant, is habitat substitution: it reproduces some of what the old habitat supplied, inherits actors that were not chosen, and carries two silent failure modes. Amiti, Redding and Weinstein (2019) report *"dramatic changes to its supply-chain network"* and estimate that roughly *"$165 billion of trade per year will continue to be redirected in order to avoid the tariffs"* (JEP PDF via search integration). That is reconfiguration under a price shock, not demonstrated resilience.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 13 (network atom) and Fig 12 (bond carries the information), with Fig 11 supplying the substitution law. World source corroborates the reconfiguration, not the resilience claim.

**Refuter:** a measurement showing that a tariff, with no change in the set of suppliers or routes, increases supply continuity under a disruption against an anchor that does not move. That would make a bond change a structure change and F5 false.

### F6 — The chain breaks at the objective, and the question plans past the break

Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The vocabulary link works: everyone can name jobs, costs, retaliation and supply chains. The perception link works: tariffs and trade flows are measured. The objective link does not: the question never says which slot's residual is being reduced, so "protect" and "raise" have no shared definition of progress. Effort spent arguing about tariff design lands above the break and cannot be used by a step whose input never arrives. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 3), corroborated by the literature's own need to define three exposure channels before estimating anything.

**Refuter:** a policy analysis that states a single objective with one instrument covering all five slots and shows the objective link carrying signal. Then the break is elsewhere and F6 is withdrawn.

### F7 — The universal yes/no is a declared gap, not silence

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 13 :134: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

Fig 13 :133: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line returns "tariffs protect jobs" or "tariffs do not" as a universal verdict. The framework returns a shape: per-slot verdicts with named currencies and anchors. It cannot return the single aggregate the question asks for, because the aggregate is not a term in `closure(Γ)`; it would require a new currency that converts jobs, prices and resilience into one number. That is a **gap of the shaped kind** (the sought-for thing is characterized in effect terms: a cross-currency conversion with a non-updating anchor). It is not `unreachable-at-this-budget`: more searching the same trade literature will not produce the conversion. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

**Verdict:** `gap`, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and Fig 8 (three different routes to the limit), and explicitly reported rather than hidden.

**Refuter:** the construction of the cross-currency ground described in F3 and F1. If it exists, the gap closes and a universal verdict becomes possible. Until then, "unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" governs.

### F8 — Agreement cannot decide this question; independence is the parameter

Fig 2 :13-15: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"* (collapse 1, mutual update).

Fig 2 :18-21: *"one evidence pool / they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, / not two frames confirming each other"* (collapse 2, common cause).

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Fig 7 :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

The question is politically charged, so the tempting move is to assemble a list of agreeable authorities. That manufactures the disqualifying correlation. In this simulated derivation, the second seat in `11_audit.md` reads the same figure text, the same essays and the same world sources as the primary seat, so its agreement is one pool talking. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The trade sources likewise share data and methodology (Fajgelbaum et al., Amiti et al., Flaaen and Pierce, Javorcik et al. all study the same 2018 tariff waves), so their convergence is one evidence pool, not four independent witnesses.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7; the world-source overlap is declared, not hidden.

**Refuter:** a genuine second frame with a different closure and a different anchor that returns the same per-slot verdict. That would be corroboration; its absence is not a refutation of the verdict, only a limit on its witness weight.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **One ground, one sign.** A single non-updating measurement is shown to return the same signed effect for protected-sector jobs, consumer costs, downstream jobs, export volumes and supply resilience, with a declared conversion accepted by each slot's instrument (breaks F1, F2, F3).
2. **Bond becomes structure.** A tariff is shown to increase supply continuity under disruption with no change in the supplier or route set, so a price-bond change is a structure change (breaks F5).
3. **Empty retaliation domain.** A measured episode shows no response from any differing frame and unchanged export volumes against a non-updating anchor (breaks F4).
4. **Objective instrument exists.** A policy analysis states one objective with one instrument carrying signal at all five slots (breaks F6).
5. **Aggregate is reachable.** A cross-currency conversion is constructed and settled against the ground, closing the declared gap (breaks F7).

Until one fires, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Drop the either/or. Replace it with a per-slot table: currency, anchor, observed residual, and a verdict per domain kept separate and never averaged (F1, F2).
2. State the objective before designing the lever: which slot's residual, in which currency, against which non-updating anchor (F6).
3. Report retaliation as a response from a differing frame with a source gate and a declared measure, not as an externality footnote (F4).
4. Treat supply-chain resilience as a network property. A tariff is a bond change; reshoring is a habitat substitution with two silent failures, and a source gate plus shadow test is owed for the imported productive capacity (F5).
5. Use the literature's welfare totals as filters for the budget, never as witnesses for the verdict (F8, Fig 9 :60).
6. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the separator and its refuter, not the conclusion that tariffs are good or bad.

### Which of the three step-7 questions applies

- **SEARCHED first:** is the cross-currency ground already in the trade-policy closure and unfound? Partly. Welfare totals exist, but they are filters, not a ground for all five slots. More searching the same literature does not close F7.
- **INWARD:** sharpen our own instrument. A per-slot exposure decomposition (Flaaen and Pierce's three channels) is exactly a sharpened instrument already returning something. It returns per-slot sign and magnitude. It does not return the aggregate.
- **OUTWARD:** import a frame whose closure predicts a cross-currency ground. The ecology insurance literature (Mori et al. 2013; Loreau et al. 2021, low overlap with trade economics) supplies a shape for resilience as response diversity in a network, not a price aggregate. It does not supply the missing conversion. Source gate and shadow test recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

INWARD and OUTWARD are kept distinct and never read against one another (Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a per-slot separator with named currencies, non-updating anchors, a cycle for retaliation, a bond-versus-structure distinction for supply chains, and a declared gap for the universal aggregate. Every specific claim that trade policy can be reduced to a single "protect" or "raise" verdict fails. Strains after re-census: one live type question (a welfare total is a filter, not a ground), named and kept visible in `06` and `11`; none hidden.
