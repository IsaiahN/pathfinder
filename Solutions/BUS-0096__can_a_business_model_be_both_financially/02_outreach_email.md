# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 8 is read as a `specified` structural claim and the refuter in section 4 is attached. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the mission-versus-profit question is settled

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about sustainable business models. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can a business model be both financially sustainable and genuinely accountable to a social mission?" is a union of two conditions, not one dial. Financial return and beneficiary outcome are two different grounds, measured by two different anchors, in two different units. The framework's own rule is that money and outcome are "two currencies" that "do not add" (Fig 12 :125, :130). So the "trade-off" framing performs a forbidden sum before the question is even asked. What the derivation returns is a shape: both residuals can stay small only when contact with each ground is maintained separately, the mission ground is measured by an instrument the firm cannot move, and the reporting conventions never decide the outcome. Which certification, legal form or metric occupies the mission slot is not returned; that is a declared gap.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular certification proves accountability, that shared value automatically resolves the tension, or that a mission clause in a constitution changes contact. Those are bindings from the business frame, and convergent derivation and adopted import "look identical in the contents" (Fig 8 :51). Until provenance is shown they carry no evidentiary weight. I am also not claiming to have measured a single firm.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single prospective per-model score that does not pool the two slots and predicts which one will fail next, surviving a change of habitat;
> 2. a self-authored mission rating shown to be an anchor that does not move when the reporting incentive is reversed;
> 3. a commensurable unit in which a marginal euro and a marginal beneficiary outcome are the same quantity for the decision at hand;
> 4. a model that loses mission accountability while independent measures of bracket fidelity and ground pressure both stay high;
> 5. a report that keeps describing the mission without any imported reading from a frame whose closure differs from the firm's own.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text and line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project folder
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the source essay warns that an output that cannot be distinguished from its source "demonstrates its own inadequacy." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two practical notes

1. **Disclose the method.** The accompanying derivation is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Say so in the first sentence of any cover note. Several journals now require LLM use to be documented in a Methods section; the Springer Journal of Business Ethics instructions state that *"Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section"* and that LLMs *"do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria."* Submitting simulated work as empirical findings would be a category error as well as an ethical one.
2. **One to one.** Do not send the same note to a mailing list of people likely to agree. A list selected for receptiveness is one evidence pool wearing many names (Fig 2 :31, :40).
