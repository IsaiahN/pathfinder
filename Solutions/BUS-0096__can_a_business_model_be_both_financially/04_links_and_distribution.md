## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **A strong "both is possible" claim exists and is not naive.** Porter and Kramer's shared-value proposal is a first-order claim about the market slot and the existence of joint value. It is a contact claim, not a firm-attribute claim, so under re-census R1 it corroborates F5 rather than answering the union question.
2. **The strongest objection is about the instrument, not the ambition.** Schwartz 2024 accepts that shared value addresses *"sustainable net societal value"* but says it fails to incorporate balancing and *"demonstrating sufficient accountability."* That is exactly the missing second anchor. It is a conflict only if both are read as answers to one question; re-census R2 places them at different strata.
3. **The empirical literature supplies channel-failure mechanisms, not a verdict.** Connolly and Kelly find legal-obligation reporting in place of constructive accountability; Zhuang et al. find governance structure changes the drift relation; Jones et al. find *"tremendous variation in the strategies"* used to avoid drift; Silva et al. find weak measurement consistency in a large certification sample. Together they are one broad management-and-measurement pool, so they count as a cluster of witnesses about failure modes, not independent corroboration of the figure verdict.
4. **The low-overlap witnesses are the useful ones.** Goodhart and Strathern come from monetary policy and audit studies; Carson's DDT case comes from ecology. Neither shares an evidence pool with management strategy, and both predict the same figure-shaped failure: a reading that becomes a target, and a residual three steps out that first-order enumeration misses.
5. **The "double bottom line may be mutually exclusive" paper (Kar 2013) is kept with reduced weight.** Its abstract was seen in the search index and its bibliographic record opened, but the full text returned 403. Its title states the tension in effect terms; it is not used as a substantive quote in the verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27**: *"describe it before you go looking, or"* *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (Fig 9 :28). Ask what they would expect to see if the two-slot shape were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59**: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare that this derivation is simulated and that the author has a stake in the framework being useful.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure on each slot; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure on the financial slot and on the mission slot. Record both verdicts *before* showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local figure links and the public repository. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. It is `specified`, not `grounded`, and the named instrument is a declared gap. Do not present it as a measured result about any firm.

## AI-policy caveat

Several target venues have explicit rules for simulated work. The fetched Journal of Business Ethics guidelines state that *"Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section"* and that LLMs *"do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria."* This solution is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not an empirical study. Offer it as a method and a checkable shape, disclose the method, and do not submit it as findings. Check each venue's current AI policy before any submission.
