## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of a firm's profit or the *sincerity* of its mission statement. It is whether two residuals can stay small at the same time, over time, against two anchors that do not move when the firm reports success.

- **b** is the business model's belief about what customers will pay for and what beneficiaries need, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what it does: price, sourcing, governance, measurement, hiring, distribution.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what its library of routines, assets and promises predicts will happen.
- **o′₍fin₎** is what the market actually returns: revenue, cost, survival.
- **o′₍soc₎** is what the social domain actually returns: the outcome in beneficiaries' lives, measured outside the firm.
- **s** is the firm-slot, indexed separately on the financial ground and the mission ground. R is never averaged across the two slots.

So the residual is: *why would the gap between what a business model predicts and what either ground returns stay small on both slots at once, and what would make one slot's small residual a mask for the other slot's large one?*

- Option A, "it is a single dial, financial return traded against mission": leads to the prediction that there is one optimum and that accountability is a cost subtracted from profit.
- Option B, "it is a union of two conditions with different anchors": leads to the prediction that both residuals can be small only when contact with each ground is maintained separately and the mission ground is measured by something the firm cannot move. A failure of one is not a weaker case of the other.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the two conditions that must both hold, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of financial contact from a failure of mission contact, and that detects when a small mission residual is produced by relabeling a reading rather than by a change in the world. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The named instrument, legal form and metric are a declared `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of sustainable business models.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
