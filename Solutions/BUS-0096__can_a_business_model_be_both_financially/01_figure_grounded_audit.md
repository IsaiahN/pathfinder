## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here the question forces a second reading of o′, and that is the whole difficulty.

- **b** is the business model's belief about what customers will pay for and what beneficiaries need, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what it does: price, sourcing, governance, measurement, hiring, distribution, legal form.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what its library of routines, assets, promises and metrics predicts will happen.
- **o′₍fin₎** is what the market returns: revenue, cost, survival, willingness to pay.
- **o′₍soc₎** is what the social domain returns: the outcome in beneficiaries' lives, measured by something other than the firm's own statement about it.
- **s** is the firm-slot, and it is indexed **twice**, once against each ground. The two are never averaged into one "overall performance" number.

> Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
> Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The phenomenon to explain is not a level of profit and not a declared intention. It is **the duration for which both residuals stay small**: call it `D₍both₎`. Observed: some models keep both small for years; more commonly one is small while the other grows, and the growing one is hidden by a metric that pools the two. A frame that predicts "mission is a cost that reduces profit" has a residual here, because it cannot represent a case where both stay low. A frame that predicts "a certified mission is automatically carried out" also has a residual, because it reads the artifact rather than the outcome.

State the residual as: *why does the gap between what a business model predicts and what each ground returns remain small on both slots, and what makes one slot's small residual a mask for the other slot's large one?* This is an effect description. It names no legal form, no certification, no "shared value", no "triple bottom line". Those are candidate names withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a pair of conditions whose conjunction keeps `D₍both₎` large, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of financial contact from a failure of mission contact, and that detects relabeling of a reading as a change in the world. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29 names the candidates directly:

> Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
> Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

The market is a named anchor for the financial slot. A "patient outcome" is a named anchor for the mission slot: an outcome in a person's life, not a claim about it. Management opinion, analyst consensus, an impact score the firm designs, and a certification badge all move when the firm pushes on them; they are instruments at best. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* The question is constitutively about returns to customers and to beneficiaries, so both anchors are legitimate here. Fig 2 :37-38 warns that *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure"*: a profit figure says nothing about beneficiary outcome, and an output count says nothing about whether the outcome occurred.

**A forced choice is refused.** The question is phrased as "can it be both?" That invites a yes or a no on one scale. Under Fig 9 :4-5 a disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each rule works on a different subset, is not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Splitting is required before searching. The answer is not a point on a dial; it is a union with two instruments. This refusal is recorded as a correction in section 6.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a four-place relation: model Γ, action a, financial return o′₍fin₎, mission outcome o′₍soc₎, indexed by slot s and ordered by time t. It carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) because settling the financial slot before composing the union is not the same as doing it afterwards. It carries a **comparison** bond (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87) for "sustainable" and "genuine", which are thresholds, not amounts. It is not symmetric: customer and beneficiary are not interchangeable, even when they are the same person.
- **Symmetry.** Financially sustainable is a threshold relative to cost of capital and survival, under the comparison bond of Fig 12 :85-89. Genuinely accountable is a threshold relative to an external outcome, not a symmetric exchange of money for virtue. The two thresholds are not on one axis.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single model's step-by-step loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel), the population of models with a shared library and *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), and the time channel across years. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The business-model loop** (one agent, one scale) | decision makers, routines, promises, metrics | actions and residual reduction on both slots | yes: realized market return and realized beneficiary outcome | admitted |
| **D2 The market habitat** (population) | customers, competitors, entrants, suppliers, regulators | willingness to pay, share, survival | yes: the market, named in Fig 2 :29 | admitted |
| **D3 The mission habitat** (population) | beneficiaries, communities, workers, regulators, independent auditors, affected non-customers | the social outcome in people's lives | yes: an external outcome measure, "a patient outcome" in Fig 2 :29; not the firm's own report | admitted |
| **D4 The reporting channel** (across time) | impact metrics, audits, certifications, disclosures | contact with the ground versus readings of contact | yes: the ground, "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 Mission as brand identity or narrative** | the firm, its marketing | prestige, signaling | **no** anchor independent of the firm's own claim; frame-internal (Fig 1 :27) | not a domain yet |
| **X2 Profit as the only real bottom line** | the firm, owners | financial return | measurable, but it collapses D2 and D3 into one slot, which is the pooling error of Fig 1 :22 | absorbed into D4 as a named failure mode, not a separate domain |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that narrative never matters. X2 is admitted only as a possible failure of D4, because "profit is the only bottom line" is precisely the move that makes one slot's residual disappear into an average.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D2 and D3 have different anchors. They cannot be a partition of one quantity, because there is no single quantity that both measure (Fig 12 :125-130). D1 overlaps with both: Γ is inside the model and is exercised against both grounds. D4 overlaps with both: the channel is how each ground is read. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
> Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
> Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1, financial contact maintained:** the market still pays for what the model does, and the firm can read that payment without pooling it into a blended score.
- **C2, mission contact maintained:** an external outcome still changes when the model changes, and the firm can read that change without authoring the reading.

A model failing only C1 and a model failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure. They are different compounds and need different instruments. A single lever, for example "raise the price to fund the mission", clears the region where only C1 holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55). This is why the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
> Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
> Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the model read both grounds?) → 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what customers and beneficiaries afford?) → 3 objective (can it say what progress would count as on both slots?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what each situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Mission is habitually stated as an intention rather than a residual, which is exactly the un-instrumented objective link. Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* Most diagnostic effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

Fig 12 :113-115 says a vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it, and that *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about business models, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: The question is a union of two slots, not one dial

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap: per-slot indexing vs union/unbundle).**

> Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
> Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
> Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
> Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The common "trade-off" framing treats profit and mission as two readings of one value pool. That is an average across slots. It throws away the only signal that says which ground has lost contact. The figure-grounded form keeps two residuals and two instruments. The world corroborates at the level of method, not verdict: Schwartz 2024 reports that the shared-value proposal *"currently fails to adequately incorporate the equally important notions of (i) appropriately balancing stakeholder interests with those of the corporation's shareholders, as well as (ii) demonstrating sufficient accountability"*, which is a complaint about collapsed slots, but it is one business-pool witness.

**Refuter:** a single prospective per-model score that predicts, without pooling, which named model will lose financial contact and which will lose mission contact next, and that survives a habitat substitution. If one exists, F1's unbundling is false.

### F2: Both anchors must be external and must not update

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, low overlap: anchor definition vs channel maintenance).**

> Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
> Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
> Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
> Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

A mission that is "genuine" only when the firm says it is genuine has no anchor. A certification score, an impact report and a stated purpose are all instruments, and instruments can be moved. Fig 2 :37-38: *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* The world supplies the same failure in a different vocabulary. Connolly and Kelly 2020 find that social enterprise organizations *"do not provide constructive and voluntary accountability information to their stakeholders, at least through the annual report, and that their focus is on satisfying legal obligations"*, and that their *"accountability disclosure practices are inconsistent with the social objectives on which they are based."* That is a channel reading that has replaced the ground.

**Refuter:** a model whose stated mission outcome does not change when its own reporting incentives are reversed, with an independently measured beneficiary outcome held constant. If the stated outcome moves with the incentive, F2's anchor requirement is confirmed, not refuted; F2 is refuted only if a self-authored reading is shown to be an immovable anchor, which contradicts Fig 2 :30.

### F3: The "trade-off" is a unit error, not a curve

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for method. Domains D2, D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 9, low overlap: composition pricing vs union structure).**

> Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
> Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"
> Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."
> Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"

Money and beneficiary outcome are in different units. Asking how much mission one should buy with a unit of profit is the error Fig 12 names, not a deep insight. The `⋛` comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities, so it can say that a slot clears a bar; it cannot convert one slot into the other. The consequential prediction: any report that presents a single "blended value" or "double bottom line score" has already performed the forbidden sum, and its low mission residual is not evidence of mission contact.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated commensurable unit in which a marginal euro and a marginal beneficiary outcome are the same quantity for the decision at hand, with an instrument that measures both. If one exists, F3 narrows to a warning about existing practice rather than a structural law.

### F4: Mission drift is channel decay, not the mission fading away

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 7, low overlap: ground maintainer vs room chain).**

> Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
> Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
> Fig 10 :28 "you have lost touch"
> Fig 10 :29 "→ keep the anchor reachable"

A model loses mission accountability while beneficiaries still need what it does. The named decay modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings. "Pooled readings" is the business-model version of aggregating across slots (Fig 1 :22). Fig 7 adds the two-factor test:

> Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"
> Fig 7 :38 "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"
> Fig 7 :39 "the ground's pressure on the room"

A decade of accountability is a chain of handoffs: each reporting cycle must carry a bracket that matches the current residual and must be selected against a ground that still pays. The world corroborates: Zhuang, Wang and Li 2025 report that financial motivation and prior business experience *"respectively facilitate and restrain the occurrence of mission drift"*, and that a comprehensive governance structure changes both relations. Jones, Krasynska and Donmoyer 2020 found *"tremendous variation in the strategies"* used to avoid drift, naming *"structured ignorance, problem reframing, and defensible trade-offs"*. That variation is the figure's point: drift is a failure mode of a channel, with more than one way to keep it reachable.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which a model loses mission accountability while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining, with no saturated or pooled metric. If both stay high, F4's mechanism is not the cause.

### F5: Accountability is a property of model-and-habitat, not of the model alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
> Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
> Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

The habit of answering "is this model accountable?" with a list of its internal features (legal form, board clause, mission lock, certification) is an agent-only improvement that may not change contact. What changes contact is the relation to customers, beneficiaries, regulators and independent auditors. From the strategy pool, Porter and Kramer define shared value as *"Corporate policies and practices that enhance the competitive advantage and profitability of the company while simultaneously advancing social and economic conditions in the communities in which it sells and operates."* That is a contact claim, and it **conflicts** with a firm-only reading. The conflict sends us back to the census (section 6, re-census R1), where "shared value" is mapped to D2 and D3 relations, not to a firm attribute. Under that mapping it corroborates F5, recorded as one business-pool witness.

**Refuter:** a controlled case in which a model's accountability is unchanged across a habitat substitution that demonstrably removes the ground's payment for its mission work. If it survives, F5 is false.

### F6: Renewal of reach requires contact with a differing closure

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13, three figures with distinct roles).**

> Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"
> Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
> Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
> Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
> Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

A model can keep optimizing its impact metrics forever and never add an atom. To stay accountable when the residual changes, its reach must be expanded by contact with frames whose closures differ: beneficiaries who are not customers, regulators, independent researchers, affected communities outside the value chain. Fig 11 :52 supplies the cascade warning: first-order enumeration missed the effect that mattered, *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Carson's *Silent Spring* (Wikipedia record, 1962) documents the same structure: pesticide use was read at the field, the residual arrived at the eagles. A mission model that enumerates only direct beneficiaries can miss third-order harm in exactly that way.

**Refuter:** a model that renews its mission reach solely by composing inside its own closure, with no new atom entering from a differing closure, and that still passes an external outcome test across a change of habitat. That breaks idempotence and F6 with it.

### F7: A convention that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D4, D1. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and the measurement literature, low overlap: framework rule vs economics of metrics).**

> Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
> Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
> Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
> Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

A metric that begins as a convention for describing mission contact becomes a constant the firm authored when it decides which outcome counts and what passes. The world has a name for this, and it is a low-overlap witness: Goodhart's law, recorded as *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"* (Wikipedia entry, citing Strathern 1997). The same page records the stronger original: *"Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes"* (Goodhart 1975). Silva et al. 2022 find in 2,262 B Corp assessments that *"there are apparently no minimum values established for each of the parameters evaluated, which may cause imbalances in the sustainable development process of B Corp companies"*, so a high aggregate label can coexist with a weak slot: a pooled reading, exactly Fig 10 :26.

**Refuter:** a metric that remains an undeciding convention after it has been made the basis of pay, certification or public ranking. Under Fig 10 :47 that should be impossible; if observed, F7 is wrong about the threshold at which a convention flips.

### F8: An impact reading cannot name its source without a discriminator

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4, D3. Status: triangulated (framework source discipline plus an independent instrument argument, with the business-pool measurement study as one witness).**

> Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"
> Fig 9 :31 "rather than the cause"
> Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*, fetched 2026-11-22, states the instrument rule in effect terms: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and of the colour case, *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* A reported social outcome is a reading. Several underlying causes, including genuine delivery, clever attribution, selection of easy cases and plain relabeling, can create the same reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not a better argument. The framework itself returns a shape, not a name.

**Refuter:** a reading that uniquely determines its source across all known alternative generators, with no instrument. Under Fig 9 :32 and the metamerism argument that should be impossible; if demonstrated, F8 is false.

### F9: The world conflict is returned to the census, not used to flip a verdict

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

Two world sources genuinely conflict. Porter and Kramer 2011 say a company's *"success and social progress are interdependent"* and that shared value *"is at the core of the business strategy."* Schwartz 2024 says the same proposal *"currently fails to adequately incorporate the equally important notions of (i) appropriately balancing stakeholder interests with those of the corporation's shareholders, as well as (ii) demonstrating sufficient accountability (i.e., taking responsibility) by properly reporting on and addressing any negative impacts resulting from the firm's activities."*

Under the conflict rule, a conflict sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the verdict. Re-census R2 in section 6 resolves it: Porter and Kramer make a first-order claim about the market slot and the existence of joint value; Schwartz makes a second-order claim about the accountability instrument and the missing refusal term. They are not answering the same question. Both are kept, with their provenance, and neither is promoted to the verdict. This is Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

**Refuter:** a single measure on which both claims are directly comparable and one side is simply false. If produced, the union reading collapses and F9 is withdrawn.

### F10: Two models over a shared atom set can be isomers, but the common case is a functional group

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D1, D3. Status: single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the atom assignment unmeasured.**

> Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
> Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write a social enterprise and a conventional firm with a mission clause over the atom set `{price, cost, beneficiary relation, governance, measurement, horizon}`. If both use all six atoms and differ only in which bond connects beneficiary to revenue and whether the mission atom is a functional group or a qualifier, they are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. If one model omits an atom the other requires, they are not isomers; they are two members of a functional group with different qualifiers. The isomer claim is the stronger, checkable form and is developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either model can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A single prospective per-model score that does not pool the two slots and predicts which one will fail next, surviving habitat substitution (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :57).
2. A self-authored mission reading shown to be an immovable anchor under Fig 2 :30 (breaks F2).
3. A demonstrated commensurable unit between money and beneficiary outcome for the decision at hand (breaks F3, Fig 12 :125-130).
4. A model that loses mission accountability while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining (breaks F4, Fig 7 :37-39).
5. A model that renews mission reach with no import from a differing closure and still passes an external outcome test across habitat change (breaks F5 and F6, Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32).
6. A reported reading that uniquely determines its source with no discriminating instrument, or a metric that stays a convention after it decides outcomes (breaks F7 and F8).
7. Fig 13's six terms derived from five, or Fig 6's idempotence failing inside a closed set (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until both grounds have paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of purpose (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep the two conditions separate in every report. A blended "overall sustainability score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Ask at each handoff which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat any list of model attributes as Γ, that is, as a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
5. Use the business literature as a budget filter and as witnesses of different closures, never as the verdict.
