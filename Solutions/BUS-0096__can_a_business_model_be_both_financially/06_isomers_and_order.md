## DERIVATION

### Q1: Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Write model A (mission-led) and model B (conventional with a mission clause) over the six atoms.

- **Strong form, isomer:** both use all six atoms, and differ only in which bond connects `beneficiary relation` to `price` and whether `measurement` is bonded to `governance` or to `horizon`. Same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers, and a report that lists only the six atoms cannot tell them apart.
- **Weak form, functional group:** one model omits an atom the other requires, for example a conventional firm with no `beneficiary relation` atom. They are then two members of a `state → structure → state` family with different qualifiers, not isomers. Fig 12 :123 fixes the kind; the qualifier fixes the member.

The two forms have different instruments. The isomer claim needs a bond census: enumerate which typed relation connects each pair. The functional-group claim needs only an atom census: list what is present. The world literature mostly reports atom censuses (a mission clause present or absent, a certification held or not), which is why it systematically misses the isomer question. That is Fig 12 :14-17: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either model can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. This is the same falsifier recorded in `01` F10.

**Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the atom assignment unmeasured. Triangulated only in the sense that Fig 12 :5 and :117 come from one figure with low topical overlap (notation vs chemical analogue); it is not a second evidence pool.

### Q2: What is the order of settlement?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"* (the extract continues with a clause about no top in Fig 4).
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

A combined claim such as "this model is both profitable and accountable" is a second-order composition. It is legal only if each first-order molecule is settled on its own ground first:

1. settle `market contact` for the financial slot against the market anchor;
2. settle `outcome contact` for the mission slot against an external outcome anchor;
3. only then compose the union, and mark it as a second-order term whose operands are settled molecules-now-atoms.

Composing the union before either slot is settled produces a phrase that says "both" without either ground paying. That is Formula step 5: *"SETTLE: the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*

**Falsifier:** a case in which the union is settled by a single measurement that does not settle either slot separately. If one exists, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q3: The two currencies and the comparison bond

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
- Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*

The `⋛` comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities. It can say that a financial slot clears a survival bar, or that a mission slot clears an outcome bar. It cannot convert one bar into the other. Treating "financial sustainability versus mission" as one `⋛` over one axis is the summing error Fig 12 names. This is strain S2, resolved by re-census into the two-currency reading; it is preserved here rather than erased.

At second order a further type question remains: whether two settled molecules, now serving as atoms, count as "quantities" for `⋛`. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom; it does not state that every atom is a quantity. This needs an instrument, not an argument. Named as strain S5, not smoothed.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that the operands of a threshold comparison must be bare quantities at every level, with no level-relative promotion. If shown, the second-order union cannot use `⋛` as written and must be re-composed under a different bond.

### Q4: Catalyst, cycle and the reporting routine

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

An impact metric is a catalyst if it regenerates across reporting cycles and still reads the world, and a spent part if it becomes a constant once it decides outcomes (Fig 10 :47). Nothing in the metric's description records which. A reporting loop is a cycle, and its sign decides whether it closes: a measurement that re-enters as an incentive can either improve contact or seal the channel. Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

**Falsifier:** a metric whose measured contact with an external outcome is invariant after it is used to set pay or certification for three consecutive cycles. Under Fig 10 :47 and Fig 12 :119 that should be impossible; if observed, the claim that a deciding convention flips is false.

## CONCLUSION

The two models are **isomers only in the strong form**, where both use the shared six-atom set and differ in the typed bonds. As usually described in the literature, one atom is missing from one side, so they are members of a functional group with different qualifiers. The union is second-order and must wait for each slot to settle. The comparison between the two slots is a threshold relation, not a conversion, and the two currencies are held apart. A reporting metric is a catalyst or a spent part depending on its bond, and nothing in its label records which.

This rescues what is checkable in the question without importing a new primitive, a new legal form or a single blended score. Which specific atoms and bonds actually occupy the two models remains unmeasured: that is a `gap`, not a silence.
