# Expert verification packet - the Tether on ecological network observation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or methods researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the method name. Not `grounded`.** No network measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Observation error is indexed per interaction slot, so a global coverage score can hide a live slot. Rare, hidden, and hard-to-measure are overlapping conditions, not one problem with one method: rare owes distributed independent search inside the current closure; hidden owes an imported instrument whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test; hard to measure owes a price in two non-additive currencies. The interaction list is read off the habitat and enumerated outward to second order, never taken from a standing catalogue. Methods are ranged separately against a ground that does not update on what a reviewer thinks, and what crosses a scale is a method, not a recording of one field season. The framework does not name the winning method: it returns a shape, not a name. Falsifiers were written before this run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and data-facing; checkable by reading Fig 1 and a real detection table

- **Statement:** residual per interaction slot; a global detection coverage number can hide a live slot; the three named reasons are a union with intersections.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13 - "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **What to check:** take one of your own networks. Is there a species pair whose non-detection was averaged away in a published completeness or connectance value? Do rare, hidden and hard-to-measure slots overlap in your data, so that a single method leaves an intersection standing?
- **Refuter:** show that one global coverage number preserves every live slot, or exhibit a partition of the three conditions with no residual compound at the intersections.

### C2 - instrument reachability; checkable by a methods researcher against their own detection limits

- **Statement:** rare is a SEARCHED problem (more independent search, no new atom); hidden is a reachability problem (import an instrument from a differing closure, with a source gate and a shadow test).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :49 - "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change." Fig 6 :50 - "Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent." Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :32 - "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** for a slot you genuinely cannot see, has any instrument at any resolution ever registered it? If yes, it is rare and more independent search is owed. If no, what worse instrument was already returning something from which the new one could be improved? Can you show the new method's provenance and the gap it closes?
- **Refuter:** resolve a hidden interaction by search alone at the current closure, or build an instrument from a pure description with no prior worse instrument returning anything. Either breaks the reachability split.

### C3 - verification and habitat; checkable by a field ecologist against the site

- **Statement:** the anchor is the interaction web, not agreement between observers sharing a pool; the habitat and its second-order effects are enumerated from the world; network properties are a lossy crossing from per-slot detections.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :31 - "two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 11 :43 - "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48 - "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." Fig 11 :52-53 - "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 4 :30 - "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** do your two detection methods share a sample, a primer, or a detection threshold? If so, their agreement is one pool, not two witnesses. Did the observation design enumerate beyond direct contact, and did a second-order effect matter? Take a per-slot detection vector to a network property and back to a site recommendation: how much did the crossing drop?
- **Refuter:** show that agreement among methods sharing one evidence pool changed the interaction web rather than the report, or recover a network property losslessly from per-slot detections so the round-trip gap is zero.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal protocol, a final method list, or a claim that there is a last room. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room."
- Not that more sampling always helps. It helps rare slots, not hidden ones (Fig 6 :32), and not where action cost binds (Fig 12 :125-126).
- Not that non-detection is absence: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that connectance, nestedness or robustness directly measure observation quality. They are second-order compositions with a round-trip gap.
- Not a derivation of metabarcoding, camera trapping, isotope analysis, or any named instrument. Each is a candidate binding from the methods frame, provenanced as such, and the specific winner is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not `grounded`. No slot was settled by a measurement in this package.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The discriminator instruments to name:

1. For a rare slot: does distributed independent search drive its per-slot residual down, with searchers drawn from separate evidence pools?
2. For a hidden slot: does an imported instrument register it at some resolution, and can the import's source gate and shadow test be shown?
3. For a hard-to-measure slot: what are the description cost and the action cost, held apart, and which binds?
4. Does enumerating to second order reveal an effect that a direct-contact design missed?
5. Round trip: measure the gap when a per-slot detection vector is carried up to a network property and back down to a site recommendation.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :13/:19/:22 and Fig 9 :54-57. Four short passages.
2. Open one detection table from your own work. Find a slot that a global average could hide. If none exists, say so; that is C1's refuter.
3. Pick one interaction you cannot see. Decide whether any instrument at any resolution has ever registered it. If yes, say what independent search you would run; if no, name the worse instrument your new one was improved from. That checks C2.
4. Check whether your two methods share a sample, primer, or threshold. If they do, their agreement is one witness. That checks C3.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
