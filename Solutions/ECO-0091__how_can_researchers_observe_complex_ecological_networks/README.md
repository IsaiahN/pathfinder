# Tether outreach - figure-grounded package

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity of any particular method that fills a slot. No measurement taken here settles a verdict, so nothing is `grounded`.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

**Question:** How can researchers observe complex ecological networks when many interactions are rare, hidden, or difficult to measure?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 112.

**Section:** 10. Measurement, prediction, and ecological theory.

## The question restated as an effect

The observer predicts an interaction web. The observation returns a different web. The gap, `R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|`, is indexed per interaction slot (species pair x interaction type), not per study. Most slots return nothing; a few live slots carry the rare, hidden, or hard-to-measure interaction. The three adjectives name three different reasons a slot fails to register, and each leads to a different instrument or a different budget. Option A treats them as one problem and searches harder with one method. Option B splits the union first, then routes each condition to its own loop. This package derives B and records what would refute it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not add an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
2. **Independence is bought, not assumed.** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A recipient list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
3. **The verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Stage 1 therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk mail to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and will get a sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
