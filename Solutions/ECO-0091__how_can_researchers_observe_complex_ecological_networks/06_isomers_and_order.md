# Method isomers, dependency order, and the two currencies

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how to observe interactions that are rare, hidden, or hard to measure. `01` splits this into three conditions with an instrument owed for each. Two supporting questions remain: (1) are the candidate methods different substances over one atom set, or just different names for one method, and (2) what is the order in which an observation chain breaks. Both are answerable from Figs 12 and 3 without naming a winner.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are observation methods isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Written as ingredient lists, camera trapping, diet metabarcoding, eDNA and stable isotope analysis all contain a consumer, a resource, a channel and a detection event. A list of ingredients cannot tell them apart.
- They differ in the bond from consumer to resource: a direct image, a swallowed molecule, a degraded trace in water, an assimilated isotope ratio. The bond decides what each can settle. A camera cannot see a fully digested soft-bodied prey; a DNA read cannot by itself give prey abundance (the diet-DNA page reports that sequence-count abundance estimates "have been unsuccessful"); an isotope ratio gives trophic position without needing a filled stomach (the isotope page).
- ⇒ Over the fixed atom set they are **isomers**, and the difference is decisive in the behaviour (triangulated: Fig 12 :117 plus the two low-overlap method pages, which are one ecology-methods pool rather than two witnesses).
- The weaker form: as a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.") all four are "consumer - channel - resource" detectors. The group fixes the kind; the bond fixes the member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two methods with the same atom set and the same bond give different answers on a settled interaction, the isomer claim fails. If they give the same answer on every settled interaction, the bond carries no information and the difference is only spelling. Single-source on the figure definition, with the world check still unmeasured.

### Q2 - What is the order of the observation chain?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Order: perception (has anything registered at any resolution?) -> vocabulary (is there a term for what the interaction affords: trapped, sequenced, traced, sampled?) -> objective (is progress a drop in R on a named slot?) -> planning (which probe to which slot?) -> learn and carry (which method, not which occasion, crosses?).
- The break usually sits between perception and vocabulary. A hidden interaction has no instrument, and the literature already has excellent instruments for the links that work. Effort lands above the break (Fig 3 :29-30), which is why more sampling is spent on visible links.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a study for a supposedly hidden slot whose chain demonstrably continues through objective, planning, and verified carrying. Then the break was assumed, not real.

### Q3 - Catalyst or spent part?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."
- A camera trap and a primer protocol are catalysts: they survive their own use and are redeployed across sites. A consumptive gut-content sample is a spent part: it is destroyed in the assay. Nothing in a method inventory records which a term is (Fig 12 :119: "Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is.").
- This matters for repeat sampling. A rare interaction needs many independent searchers (Fig 6 :50); if the detector is a spent part, every repetition pays the detection cost again. If it is a catalyst, the method carries and only the site visits are re-earned.
- **Falsifier:** show a consumptive assay that regenerates itself, or a reusable detector that cannot be redeployed without being rebuilt from scratch. Either breaks this reading.

### Q4 - Does the loop close?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Observation is a cycle when a detected interaction updates the next survey's target list. The sign decides whether it closes: a positive loop refines the search toward a live slot; a negative loop (a saturated detector that keeps returning the same common species) suppresses the rare slot from the schedule and converges to the mean.
- Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." The next instrument is summoned by the residual it surfaced, not composed from a standing protocol.
- **Falsifier:** show an observation schedule that re-enters its output with the sign fixed so that a surfaced rare slot can never be selected. Then the loop is a chain with a recorded outcome and the cycle claim fails.

### Q5 - Two currencies, held apart

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- A method with high sensitivity but a prohibitive field cost is cheap to describe and expensive to settle. A long chain of cheap detections may be expensive to describe and cost one action to refute. "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind" (Fig 12 :129).
- Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A sensitivity threshold is a budget filter. It must not decide that an interaction is absent.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a ground-derived exchange rate between bits and actions for this question. Then the two-currency claim narrows to a bookkeeping convenience rather than a structural constraint.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed detection atom set the candidate methods are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference decisive in which interactions register. As a weaker claim they share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123). The observation chain is ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20) and usually breaks before an instrument exists for the hidden slot. Catalysts survive reuse; spent parts do not (Fig 12 :119). The loop closes only if its output can re-enter with a sign that keeps the live slot selectable (Fig 12 :121, Fig 7 :35). Cost is read in two non-additive currencies (Fig 12 :125-126). This framing rescues the useful part of the method question without naming a universal winner. Each named method stays a provenanced binding, and the specific winner is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
