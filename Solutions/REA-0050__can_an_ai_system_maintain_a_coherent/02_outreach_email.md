# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this email carries a `specified`, ungrounded shape, not a result. Do not let a reader convert it into a claim that a particular system does or does not have a self-model. The two-stage offer collapses if stage 1 is oversold.

---

**Subject:** A check on what "coherent self-model" could even mean for a changing system

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about self-modelling systems. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can a system keep a coherent model of itself as it changes?" bundles two conditions that have different anchors. A self-account that stays internally consistent by being updated on the system's own recent behaviour is a named failure in the figures, called coherent drift. Coherence there is the symptom of losing the reference, not evidence of having one. The other condition, staying in contact with a reference that does not change because the system changes, is a different thing. The figure rule is: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35)
>
> What survives is a four-condition separator, not a design:
> 1. the residual is indexed per slot, so a global coherence score is not a signal (Fig 1 :19);
> 2. the reference for "what I am" is outside the model and does not update on the model's report (Fig 2 :33);
> 3. the channel to that reference is maintained with an instrument, because "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24);
> 4. the check is made by a verifier that cannot reconstruct the claim (Fig 10 :42).
>
> I make no claim about which representation, architecture or metric fills those slots. The framework returns a shape, not a name or a number. A standing list of self-model contents is not something I can derive, and I am flagging that as a gap rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. a self-account shown correct against an external, non-updating anchor at each slot across learning and interaction, where the anchor was not authored by the system;
> 2. a coherence metric produced by a mechanism distinct from the one it scores, with the two shown not to share an evidence pool;
> 3. a verbatim figure line that governs the contents of a self-model or licenses a coherence metric;
> 4. a system whose reach grows exactly with its record across genuine refutations.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the separator. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
> - Continual learning review, opened and verified 2026-11-23: `https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07569`
> - Animal self-recognition evidence, opened and verified 2026-11-23: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_test`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a self-modelling problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
