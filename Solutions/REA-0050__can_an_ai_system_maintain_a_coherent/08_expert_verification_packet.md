**What this is:** a claim in a form a self-modelling researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on self-model content and any universal coherence metric. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of any system is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. ## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can an AI system maintain a coherent model of itself as it changes?" bundles two conditions with different anchors. A self-account kept consistent by being updated on the system's own recent behaviour is coherent drift, a named failure in the figures (Fig 2 :4-5, :34), not a success. What can instead be maintained is triangulation: the self-account stays in contact with a reference that does not update on it, measured per slot, through a channel that is kept readable, with the check performed by a verifier that cannot reconstruct the claim. Persistence of the record is not persistence of the reach (Fig 6 :42). Over a fixed atom set the before and after self-models are isomers (Fig 12 :117); learning that mints or refuses breaks the isomer reading. The framework returns the shape and refuses to name the representation, the metric, or the self-model's contents. That is a declared `gap`, not an oversight.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** a self-account that is coherent by mutual update is the named collapse, not evidence of contact. Coherence must be re-grounded as triangulation against a reference that does not update.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :34, "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Also Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." And Fig 2 :33, "The anchor must not update."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a self-account that stays correct against an external, non-updating anchor at each slot across learning and interaction, where the anchor was not authored by the system and the system cannot reconstruct the verifier. Or exhibit a coherence metric produced by a distinct mechanism with no shared evidence pool. If either holds, C1 falls.

### C2: world-facing; checkable against self-modelling and continual-learning work

- **Statement:** stability of a stored self-description and change of reachable capability are different quantities. Replay of one occasion is a recording, not a generator, and continual acquisition from non-stationary streams tends to overwrite or saturate the channel.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25, "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 6 :42, "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **What to check:** in a system you know, does the self-description transfer to a task it was not recorded on (generator), or is it replayed verbatim (recording)? Does the residual at the self-slot keep arriving after N updates, or do saturated metrics and pooled readings take over? Does the field literature treat stability and plasticity as competing demands? Parisi et al. 2019 (Neural Networks) state that continual acquisition from non-stationary distributions "generally leads to catastrophic forgetting or interference"; the catastrophic-interference account names "the stability-plasticity dilemma".
- **Operational test from a low-overlap source:** Ha and Schmidhuber 2018, "World Models" (arXiv:1803.10122), "We can even train our agent entirely inside of its own hallucinated dream generated by its world model, and transfer this policy back into the actual environment." Transfer back is the generator test. A dream that cannot transfer is a recording.
- **Refuter:** a self-model that guides successful action in a novel task with a measured round trip, plus an update that increases stability and reach together without a new atom or anchor. Either would collapse the union and fall against C2.

### C3: framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** a self-check is a frame certifying its own limit. An outside seat with non-access is required, and self-agreement is not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :37, "A frame cannot certify its own limit." Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
- **Refuter:** a verifier that cannot produce what it checks, with a shown separation of evidence pools and a non-updating anchor. Zero-knowledge proof systems are the figure's own example of sound non-access, but a mechanism must be shown rather than named. If such a verifier is shown inside the system, C3 narrows to the outside-check case only.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Slot discriminator:** does a reading distinguish actual self-state from self-report, where several accounts produce the same reading? *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source"; "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."
2. **Non-updating reference test:** is there a reference at the slot whose answer does not move when the report moves?
3. **Channel test:** does the residual keep arriving after N updates? Fig 10 :25-26 names "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings".
4. **Verifier non-access:** can the verifier produce the self-account it checks? If yes, it cannot verify it.
5. **Full falsifier list:** `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, written before the conclusion.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :4-5, :33, :34, :35 and Fig 10 :24, :42 (six short passages). Decide whether "coherent drift" is a fair reading of the figure's panel 1.

3. Name one instrument that would distinguish self-report from actual capability at a slot, or say why none is possible.
