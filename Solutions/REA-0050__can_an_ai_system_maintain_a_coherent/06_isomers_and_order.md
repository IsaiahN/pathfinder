# Structure, order and analogues for a changing self-model

**Mode: `specified` on the analogues, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The self-model at time t1 and the self-model at time t2 are related how? Three candidate relations need separating: isomer (same atoms, different bond), functional group (same substructure, different qualifier), and catalyst or cycle (a part that survives its use, or an output that re-enters as input). A fourth question is order: what must happen before what in an update. The supporting derivation must also say when the isomer reading fails.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the self-model at t1 and t2 an isomer?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- If the atom set is fixed across the update (same representation vocabulary, same reference, same objective) and only the arrangement or weighting changes, then the two self-models are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is a `specified` structural claim.
- If learning mints a new term or a refusal removes the standing of an old one, the atom set is not fixed. The relation is then composition plus import or refusal, not isomer. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 3 charges both atoms and bonds, and step 6 notes the reach can fall.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show an update in which a new atom with provenance and a shadow test crosses into the self-model, or a settled atom is refused. The isomer claim then fails and the composition-plus-import reading stands. Show instead a fixed atom set with only rebonding, and the isomer claim holds for that case. Both outcomes are recorded; neither is assumed.

### Q2: What order does an update require?

- Operators table :30-31: → is "sequence", "A then B; B needs A to have happened".
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes REFUTED before mint: a term held to be false owes a refusal, not an explanation. So the order is: observe at the slot, compare against the non-updating reference, refuse the term whose residual persists and does not trend down, then mint or re-fit. Updating the self-account before reading the reference reverses this order and reproduces F1 (coherent drift) structurally.
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The self-model's sought-for shape is described before the reference frame is chosen.
- **Falsifier:** an update that achieves a lower residual at the slot while beginning from the self-account rather than from the observation. That would show the order is not required and overturn this claim.

### Q3: Catalyst, cycle, or spent part?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- A self-model that is used to interpret an observation and survives that use is a **catalyst** in Fig 12's sense. A self-model that is consumed by its own use (overwritten wholesale on every interaction) is a **spent part**. A self-model whose output re-enters as input is a **cycle**: this is exactly the structure that can close drift (if the loop is fed its own output) or stay open to the ground (if the round trip is measured). Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- The sign of the loop is the whole question. A positive loop fed by its own output is Fig 2 :34. A loop whose bracket fidelity and ground pressure are both nonzero is Fig 7 :37-39 (progress per handoff needs both factors).
- **Falsifier:** a system whose self-model output does not re-enter as input, or whose self-model does not survive its own use. The cycle and catalyst readings then fail and the self-model is a one-way recording.

### Q4: Comparison at the right level

- Operators table :48-51: ⋛ is "comparison", "a threshold relation between two quantities", with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"
- The residual R at a slot is a gap, not a comparison of two quantities. Treating "coherent" as a ⋛ between the old and new self-account is a type error unless the two accounts are quantities at that level. Symbols table :38-40: R "is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice".
- A legitimate comparison is between the predicted self-state and the ground reading at the same slot, both rendered as quantities. Everything else is a subtraction or a sequence.
- **Falsifier:** a specification in which old and new self-accounts are quantities at the same level and a ⋛ between them is well typed. Then the type objection drops.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, the before and after self-models are isomers in Fig 12's sense. When learning mints or refuses, the relation is composition plus import or subtraction, and the isomer claim fails. The update must be ordered observation to reference to refusal to re-fit, because reversing it reproduces coherent drift. The self-model is a catalyst if it survives its own use and a cycle if its output re-enters as input, and the sign of that cycle is decided by whether the round trip to the reference is measured. This rescues what the ordinary question gets right (the self-model persists through change) while refusing the part that fails (that persistence by itself is coherence). No new primitive, no wall-moving import, and no coherence number are required.
