## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the separator and its refuter, not a finished self-image.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :35** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." No mass send selected for likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a false yes to a question about self-modelling systems, which is high enough to justify an outside check.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 carries only the residual, the four-condition separator and the refuter. Stage 2 is opt-in. Stage 3 offers the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each reader writes a fresh refuter and states a mode. Stage 3 is permitted because a method crosses up; it still needs a ground per problem, and a self-model output "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula" is the standing warning from the framework's own essay.

**Hard precondition:** the separator is `specified` and ungrounded. If a reader treats it as a measured fact about deployed systems, the two-stage offer has failed and must be withdrawn.
