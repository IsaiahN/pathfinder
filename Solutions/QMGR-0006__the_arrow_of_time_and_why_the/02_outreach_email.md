# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the `gap` in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact in the text below. If the low-entropy "why" is presented as answered, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one figure-grounded output before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the structure, `gap` on the open part):**
> the framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and
> structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule that composition runs across the kinds
> and never within one. Time is "the ordered succession of differences"; energy is "conserved,
> finite, directionless alone." Directedness is not in the energy term. It is in the gradient,
> "a difference that can be spent," and the framework's own argument is that neither an ordering
> nor a span of space has directedness on its own. So the thermodynamic arrow is a gradient over
> time, a composition of held terms, not a seventh primitive and not a new field.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that this explains why the early universe had such low
> entropy. That is a separate slot, and after a genuine search I record it as a `gap`, unreached
> at this budget. There is a measurement telling us the early state *was* smooth and far from
> equilibrium, but no instrument I can find returns the reason for it. The framework's own limit
> is that it returns a shape, not a name. So I am not putting forward inflation, a bounce, a
> no-boundary proposal or a fluctuation as the answer. Each would be an import that needs a
> provenance and a shadow test before it can move anything.
>
> **The three slots are kept separate:** (1) why entropy increases toward one direction, (2) why
> the initial state was low entropy, (3) why the physical and psychological arrows align. (1) is
> ground-supported by the second law and by statistical mechanics; (3) is a maintained-contact
> relation, since records can form in only one direction; (2) is the open one. I am not averaging
> these into a single "arrow of time" score.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim**, written down before this message:
> 1. deriving directedness from energy alone, or exhibiting a state–state or structure–structure
>    bond that carries the arrow with no opposite-kind term between;
> 2. measuring entropy decreasing in a genuinely isolated macroscopic system, or deriving
>    irreversibility from time-symmetric dynamics with no boundary condition and no import;
> 3. producing a source-discriminating cosmological instrument that closes the low-entropy slot,
>    or deriving that boundary from the held frame with no import;
> 4. showing the psychological arrow is independent of entropy production and contact.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is
> more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to check the wider literature:** the two sources that bear most directly are the
> Stanford Encyclopedia entry on thermodynamic asymmetry in time
> (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/time-thermo/) and Carroll and Chen's 2004 paper on
> spontaneous inflation and the origin of the arrow of time (https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0410270).
> They disagree about how much cosmology explains, and I have recorded that disagreement rather
> than resolving it by choosing a side.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem
> you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before*
> you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a
> reported success.
>
> — [your name]
