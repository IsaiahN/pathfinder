## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Statistical mechanics and the direction problem.** The SEP entry covers Boltzmann's phase-space argument, Loschmidt's time-reversal objection, Zermelo's recurrence objection, the Minus First Law of Brown and Uffink, and the subsystem worry. This is the ground-supported half (s1), corroborating F1/F4.
2. **Cosmology and the boundary condition.** Carroll and Chen review the "unitarity critique" of inflation from Penrose, Unruh, Hollands and Wald, and Dyson, Kleban and Susskind, then propose spontaneous eternal inflation with entropy unbounded above and a universe statistically time-symmetric on ultra-large scales. The SEP judges that these scenarios do not make the asymmetry nomologically necessary. The disagreement is recorded, not resolved by a side pick (F5, F8).
3. **Measure and typicality.** Both sources agree that a reliable measure on the space of cosmological initial states is missing. Carroll and Chen: *"we do not have a reliable understanding of entropy in the context of quantum gravity."* This is part of why the `gap` is an instrument gap, not a lack of imagination.
4. **Nonequilibrium statistical physics and neuroscience.** Lynn et al. show the arrow is compositional and interactional at the scale of retinal circuits, with pairwise correlations dominant. Low overlap with the cosmological discussion; corroborates mechanism shape only.
5. **Cognition and language.** Gu et al. show the mental front/back mapping of past and future is language- and culture-sensitive. Low overlap; corroborates the claim that the *representation* of the arrow is a binding, so the psychological arrow must be aligned by contact, not by identity (F7).

Overlap declaration: the two SEP entries plus Wikipedia share a broad philosophy and physics evidence pool and count as one witness for the conceptual framing. Carroll and Chen is a distinct cosmology source. Lynn et al. and Gu et al. are genuinely low-overlap witnesses (statistical physics/neuroscience and cognitive science respectively) but speak only to mechanism shape and representation, not to the cosmological boundary. The eight essays carried in `05_what_shape_means.md` share the framework author's pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.
