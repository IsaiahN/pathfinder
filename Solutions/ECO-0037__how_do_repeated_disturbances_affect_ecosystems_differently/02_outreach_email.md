# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used anywhere in this folder.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output has survived a check against a real dataset or experiment. The output is a shape claim, not a measured result; if it cannot survive a check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about repeated disturbance before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a claim about disturbance regimes and I would like you to check one output against what you already know from disturbance ecology. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Two regimes with the same cumulative disturbance intensity are not necessarily the same disturbance. One delivers the dose as a single event; the other delivers it as a sequence of smaller events with recovery intervals between them. The claim is that "total intensity" is an ingredient count, not a description of the regime, and that the arrangement of the dose over time is a separate quantity that carries information the total does not. The two regimes stand to each other as isomers over one disturbance budget: same atoms, different bond, different outcome. The recovery interval is the important part, because what decides the outcome is whether the part that was spent regenerates before the next pulse.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that small repeated disturbances are always better than one large one, or that any particular frequency is optimal. I am not claiming a number, and I am not claiming this has been measured. It is a prediction of a shape, not a dose-response curve. Whether the sign of the difference is positive or negative for any given ecosystem is an empirical question I cannot answer from the framework.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. in a matched experiment at fixed total dose, a descriptor that states only total intensity predicts the outcome as well as one that states dose, frequency and return interval;
> 2. the outcome is invariant to the spacing and ordering of pulses across the tested range;
> 3. the proposed regenerative part is shown not to survive its own use and outcomes still depend on schedule;
> 4. third-order contact enumeration adds nothing over first-order mortality at fixed dose.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the sources behind it. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority. The wider literature I have read on this is listed in `04_links_and_distribution.md`, including a microcosm experiment showing that disturbance frequency and intensity interact, and a materials-science analogue in which load sequence breaks a linear cumulative-damage rule.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
