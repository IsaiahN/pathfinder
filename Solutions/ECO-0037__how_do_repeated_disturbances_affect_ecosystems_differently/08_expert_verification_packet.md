# Expert verification packet: the Tether on repeated disturbance

**What this is:** a shape claim in a form a disturbance ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Two disturbance regimes with the same cumulative intensity are not thereby the same regime. Total intensity is an ingredient count; the arrangement of the dose over time is a separate quantity carried by the bond. One pulse and a spaced series of smaller pulses share the disturbance budget but differ in bond, so they stand to each other as isomers over that budget, or at minimum as members of one functional group with a different qualifier. The recovery interval is the load-bearing part: what decides the outcome is whether the part spent by one pulse regenerates before the next, a catalyst and cycle question that the dose readout does not record. Outcomes must be read per species, patch or functional group and out to third-order contacts. The framework returns that shape and its refuters. It does not return a sign, an optimum or a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12
- **Statement:** the bond carries information the ingredient total does not. At fixed dose, one pulse and a spaced series are different terms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Refuter:** in a matched experiment at fixed total dose, a descriptor stating only total intensity predicts per-slot outcomes as well as one stating dose, frequency and return interval; or the outcome is invariant to spacing and ordering across the tested range.

### C2: ecology-facing; checkable by a disturbance ecologist against experiments and datasets
- **Statement:** the recovery interval is a catalyst and cycle question. Whether the spent part regenerates between pulses decides whether the regime closes a loop or ratchets toward another state. This is why the schedule can matter at fixed dose.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119, *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** name the candidate regenerative part in a system you know (seed bank, resprouting tissue, soil mutualist). Is it spent and regenerating on the timescale of the return interval? Hall et al. 2012 report that *"the form of the DDR depends on an interaction between disturbance frequency and intensity"*; does their mechanism, or yours, match the catalyst and cycle description? Note the limit of the engineering analogue: in materials *"Damage is irreversible. Materials do not recover when rested"*, so that field cannot supply the regeneration term.
- **Refuter:** the candidate part demonstrably does not regenerate and outcomes still depend on schedule, or it regenerates identically across schedules and outcomes still differ. Either result makes the catalyst term idle here.

### C3: framework-internal with a world-facing tail; checkable against the habitat
- **Statement:** the residual must be read per slot and out to third-order contacts. A whole-system average and a first-order mortality count both discard the live signal. The regime composes from settled events, so no new primitive is needed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :52, *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*.
- **Refuter:** per-species or per-patch residuals are zero wherever the whole-system residual is nonzero, and third-order contact enumeration adds no predictive power over first-order mortality at fixed dose.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that total intensity is irrelevant. It is incomplete, not false.
- Not that repeated disturbance is always gentler or always more damaging. No figure line ranks the bonds.
- Not an optimal frequency, a threshold dose, a dose-response curve, or a number.
- Not the intermediate disturbance hypothesis. It is a contested world hypothesis and is used as corroboration only.
- Not a mapping onto the six-term substrate. That mapping was declined as a strain (F10 in `01`).
- Not grounded. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.
- Not corroborated by this packet's agreement with the solution. The second seat shares the evidence pool; Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

## 5. What would settle it

A matched-schedule experiment in a named system. The four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are written so that any of them can fire. Fig 2 :29 to :30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Agreement among ecologists is not such an anchor. A null result at an instrument that cannot resolve the recovery gap is not a verdict either: Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :94, :117, :119, :121 and Fig 1 :19. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a system you know: can you name the part that is spent and regenerates between pulses, and the timescale on which it does so?
3. Look at one dataset or experiment where total dose was matched across schedules. Did the descriptor that included return interval beat the dose-only one? If you do not know, say so; that is useful too.
4. Name one instrument or design that would discriminate regeneration from simple dose accumulation in that system.
5. Send the refutation, or say which sub-claim survived. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
