## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Frequency and intensity interact, so dose alone is not a sufficient statistic.** Hall et al. 2012 manipulated both frequency and intensity in the same bacterial system and obtained monotonically increasing, unimodal or flat diversity curves depending on which aspect was considered. Their discussion: *"different DDRs can arise, even within a single empirical system, depending on an interaction between the frequency and intensity of disturbances."* This is the closest direct corroboration of the figure claim that the bond carries information the ingredients do not. It is a low-overlap ecological source and carries real independent weight.
2. **Compound disturbance is a named phenomenon.** The disturbance ecology page describes events in quick succession creating *"a new situation which is more than the sum of its parts"*, which is the ecological vocabulary for Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."* It also notes ecosystems maintained by periodic disturbance, so repeated disturbance is not automatically damage.
3. **History dependence is already inside the standard hypothesis.** The IDH page says the classification of a disturbance depends on previous history and on which component is evaluated. This corroborates the claim that "total intensity" is an incomplete descriptor, and it also warns that "intermediate" is not an objective slot. Fox 2013's call to abandon IDH is recorded as a genuine conflict inside ecology, not as a figure verdict.
4. **Resilience supplies the recovery loop vocabulary.** Walker et al.'s definition keeps function, structure, identity and feedbacks through reorganization, and "latitude" names the distance to a threshold before recovery becomes difficult or impossible. This corroborates Fig 12 :121 (cycle) and :119 (catalyst) at the level of ecological practice.
5. **Spacing is a low-overlap analogue from human cognition.** With material and total study time held fixed, spaced presentation beats massed presentation in the overwhelming majority of comparisons. The same shape (arrangement over time changes the outcome at equal total input) appears in a field with no stake in ecology.
6. **Cumulative damage is a low-overlap analogue from engineering.** Miner's rule is exactly the "same total dose" assumption, and the standard limitation is that sequence matters. The analogue is partial and the limit is important: in materials *"Damage is irreversible. Materials do not recover when rested"*, whereas ecosystems do regenerate between pulses. That difference is the reason the figure's catalyst term is needed here and not in the fatigue case. Record the limit; do not stretch it.
7. **No source settles the sign.** None of the above says whether repeated disturbance at fixed dose will increase or decrease diversity, biomass or recovery speed in a specified ecosystem. The framework does not either. That is the honest stop.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. No theory, no links, no request for trust. Record the verdict before anything else is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three most relevant links: the Hall et al. experiment, the disturbance ecology page and the resilience page. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they have seen the frame they share its evidence pool.

**Stage 3, method only.** Offer the formula as a generator to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The formula is a method, so giving it away does not give away an answer, but each new problem still needs its own ground.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output can be checked. The claim here is a shape with four stated refuters, not a measured result, and it must be presented that way.
