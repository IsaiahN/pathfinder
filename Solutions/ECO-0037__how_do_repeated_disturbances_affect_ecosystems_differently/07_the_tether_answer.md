# The Tether's answer: repeated versus single disturbance at equal total intensity

**Mode: `specified` on every substantive claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two regimes share a cumulative disturbance dose. One delivers it as a single event; the other as a sequence of smaller events with recovery gaps. The residual is the systematic difference in outcome that a dose-only descriptor fails to explain. It is measured per slot, species, patch or functional group, not as one whole-system average. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The comparison is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54): the intensity condition and the recovery-interval condition overlap, and matching the first leaves the second binding. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Equal total intensity is an ingredient count, not a description of the regime.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* The difference the question asks about is carried by the bond, so it is not noise around a dose-response curve.
2. **The regimes are isomers over one disturbance budget, or at minimum members of one functional group.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Repetition is an ordered sequence: Fig 12 :70, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."* So spacing and ordering are part of the term.
3. **The decisive variable in the gap is whether the spent part regenerates.** Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A positive sign closes recovery; a negative sign makes the series a ratchet toward another regime. Total-dose accounting records the spend and not which kind of part was spent.
4. **Third-order contacts can dominate the outcome.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50), so the same repeat disturbance benefits one species and harms another. There is no standing list of good or bad pulses.
5. **The regime is second order out of settled events.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom."* Each event's mortality and regrowth are countable, so a series composes from settled events. No new primitive is needed.
6. **Which arrangement wins is not in the shape.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework says the arrangement matters and that the recovery term is load-bearing. It does not say that small repeated disturbances are better, that an intermediate frequency is optimal, or that any threshold exists.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that total intensity is irrelevant. It is an incomplete descriptor, not a false one. Dose still matters.
- Not that repeated disturbance is always gentler, or always more damaging. No figure line ranks the bonds.
- Not a dose-response curve, an optimal return interval, or a number. No measurement was taken here.
- Not the intermediate disturbance hypothesis. That is a contested world hypothesis (`04_links_and_distribution.md`), and IDH's "intermediate" depends on prior history and on which component is measured. It is corroboration at most, never a figure verdict.
- Not a mapping of the regime onto the six-term substrate. That mapping was considered and declined as a strain (F10 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- Not grounded. Nothing here was settled by a plot, a microcosm or a dataset.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A matched-schedule experiment in a named system, not a further argument. Fig 2 :29 to :30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

The discriminating questions are:

- At fixed total dose, does a descriptor stating dose, frequency and return interval beat a dose-only descriptor in out-of-sample prediction of per-slot outcomes? If not, refuter 1 fires.
- Is the outcome invariant to spacing and ordering across the tested range? If yes, refuter 2 fires and the regimes are not isomers here.
- Does the candidate regenerative part survive its own use and regenerate between pulses, and does removing it change the schedule effect? If not, refuter 3 fires.
- Does a third-order contact census add predictive power over first-order mortality? If not, refuter 4 fires.
- Underivable is not the same as unreached. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A null result at a coarse instrument is not a verdict about the shape until the instrument is shown to resolve the recovery gap.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set narrows the shape to a bond, a catalyst and a cascade, then stops at the limit of what this derivation can distinguish. The stop is recorded so it is checkable: *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*). This solution cannot settle the ecological question it reports on. A second seat that shares this evidence pool adds no witness (Fig 2 :40). The answer moves to `grounded` only when an experiment pays for it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that one large disturbance and several small ones with the same total intensity are the same, and it does not say which is worse. It says that total intensity is an ingredient count, not a description of a regime. The two regimes share their atoms and differ in their bond, so they stand as isomers over one disturbance budget, and the spacing of the pulses is part of the term rather than free spelling. What matters in the gaps is whether the part that was spent regenerates before the next pulse: that is a catalyst and cycle question, and a dose readout does not record which kind of part was spent. The outcome has to be read per species or patch and out to third-order contacts, because the average and the first-order effect both discard the live signal. The framework returns that shape and four refuters written in advance. It does not return a sign, an optimum or a number. Those stay open until an experiment settles them.
