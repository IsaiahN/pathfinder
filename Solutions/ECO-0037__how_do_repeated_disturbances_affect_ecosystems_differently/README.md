## Restating the question as an effect

Two disturbance regimes share one cumulative dose. One delivers it as a single event, the other as a sequence of smaller events separated by recovery intervals. The question asks what differs in the ecological outcome when the total intensity is held equal. In effect terms it asks whether "total intensity" is a sufficient description of a disturbance regime, or whether the arrangement of that intensity over time is a separate quantity that carries information the total does not. Option A: treat the two as the same dose and expect the same outcome, so any difference is noise. Option B: treat them as two arrangements of the same ingredients, expect a systematic difference, and say in advance what would refute that expectation. The derivation below takes B as a `specified` prediction and records the ground that would settle it.

## The one-line result

The shape that survives is that equal total intensity is an ingredient count, not a description of the regime. Repeated and single disturbances share the atoms but differ in bond and arrangement, so they stand to each other as isomers over the same disturbance budget. The recovery interval is a catalyst and cycle question about which parts survive their own use and regenerate, and total-intensity accounting does not record which. This is a `specified`, ungrounded shape, not a measured ecological law.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, a room does not become an anchor: Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Second, selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation: Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Third, showing a recipient the full interior before asking for a verdict spends the only independence they contribute: Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
