## RESIDUAL

The two regimes (one pulse of dose D, or n pulses of dose D/n with recovery gaps) are being compared as if they were one quantity at two settings. The residual is the part of the outcome that the single quantity cannot explain. Two questions are owed: (1) are the two regimes isomers under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what does the ordering of the operands add that an ingredient list cannot say? A third question follows: which order is the union, and by what dependency?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this derivation: the disturbance budget (dose), the recipient organism or patch, the regenerative part, and the third-order contacts. Bonds held: sequence (`→`), conjunction (`+`), comparison (`⋛`), and the cycle and catalyst structures named in Fig 12. No new primitive is available without import. The comparison between regimes is itself a threshold relation: which outcome crosses a damage or recovery threshold first.

Arity and symmetry: the regime is a one-operand object under a single pulse and a k-operand object under repetition. The bond that joins the pulses is ordered, so permutations are different terms. The dose sum is symmetric under permutation, which is exactly why it cannot carry the regime's identity.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the two regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117:
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :95:
> **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

Fig 12 :97 names the chemistry precedent: silver fulminate and silver cyanate share a composition and differ in properties, which is what forced structure into chemical notation. That is the structural analogy, not an ecological measurement.

- Under a single pulse the disturbance budget is one operand. Under repetition it is k operands joined by sequence with gaps.
- The ingredient set (total dose) is identical. The bond is not.
- Therefore, on the figure's definition, the two regimes are isomers over the same disturbance budget. The difference is invisible in the dose formula and decisive in the behaviour.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the outcome is invariant to frequency and return interval at fixed total dose, or if a symmetric bond maps the two schedules to one term with no residual, the isomer claim fails and the flat, dose-only reading stands. This is refuter 2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3.

### Q2: What does order add?

Fig 12 :70:
> **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**

Fig 12 :74:
> **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."**

The sequence bond is ordered. A pulse that removes a dominant competitor changes the resource conditions under which the next pulse acts. A pulse that removes a mutualist removes the condition for recovery from the next one. Swapping or spacing the operands therefore makes a different term, not a re-spelling of the same one. A dose-only descriptor charges for the sequence as if it were free, which Fig 12 :102 forbids: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."*

The ordered-bond reading is triangulated across Fig 12 :70, :74 and the world analogues below, which is not the same as being measured in an ecosystem.

### Q3: Where does the recovery loop sit?

Fig 12 :119:
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Fig 12 :121:
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A single pulse spends the part once and succession may restore it. Repetition tests whether restoration completes before the next spend. If the sign of the loop is positive, the regime is a closing cycle and the system can absorb the series. If the sign is negative, the series is a chain that cannot close by construction and each pulse spends more than the last restored: a ratchet toward a different regime. The dose formula has no slot for the sign, so it cannot name the difference.

**Falsifier:** measure the candidate regenerative part between pulses. If it demonstrably does not regenerate and the schedule still changes the outcome, the catalyst term is idle here and another bond governs. If it regenerates identically across schedules and the outcome still differs, the cycle term is idle. Both results are live alternatives, not rhetorical objections.

### Q4: Are the regimes isomers or members of a functional group?

Fig 12 :123:
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Fig 12 :111:
> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

If the two regimes are described only as "a disturbance of intensity D", they share every ingredient and differ in nothing: they are the same term and the question cannot be asked. If they are described as "a disturbance regime delivered over time", they share a functional group (the state to structure to state route from dose to recipient to outcome) and differ in the qualifier (one operand or a sequence). So the weaker claim is a functional-group claim: same kind, different member. The stronger isomer claim requires both to be written over the full shared atom set, varying only the arrangement. That stronger form is a checkable structural claim, still `specified`.

This is the exact move Fig 12 :111 names: a vocabulary with only dose can name a state and cannot name a route to one.

### Q5: What order is the union?

Fig 12 :104:
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

Fig 12 :125:
> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Each disturbance event is settled against the ground (mortality and regrowth are counted). A series of events therefore composes at the next level out of settled events, not out of raw dose. The regime is second order relative to an event, and the compound of dose condition plus recovery condition is third order relative to the ingredients. Dependency order: settle the event response, then the recovery loop, then the regime comparison. Optimising the dose estimate cannot rescue a missing recovery term (Fig 3 :19).

The two currencies matter: describing the schedule is cheap in bits, and finding out whether it holds in a given ecosystem costs experiments. A system holding only the bit accounting will propose schedules it cannot afford to test. That is why the pre-registered refuter comes before the claim.

### Q6: Low-overlap analogues (world, corroboration only)

These are not figure lines. They are independently arrived at in fields with no stake in the framework, and they are used as corroboration, never as verdicts.

- **Human cognition, spacing effect.** *"more information is encoded into long-term memory by spaced study sessions ... than by massed presentation ('cramming')"*, and Cepeda et al. found spaced practice better in *"259 out of 271 cases"*. Material and total study time held fixed, arrangement changed, outcome changed. Predicts the same shape here. Corroborates.
- **Engineering, Miner's rule.** *"The sequence in which high vs. low stress cycles are applied to a sample in fact affect the fatigue life, for which Miner's Rule does not account."* Linear cumulative damage is the engineer's "same total dose" assumption, and it fails for the same structural reason. Predicts dose-only accounting will miss the schedule term. Corroborates, with a limit: *"Damage is irreversible. Materials do not recover when rested."* Ecosystems do regenerate, which is why the catalyst term is needed here and not there.
- **Ecology, Hall et al. 2012.** *"the form of the DDR depends on an interaction between disturbance frequency and intensity."* Same system, both aspects manipulated, different curves. Direct corroboration at low overlap with the framework.

Agreement from three fields does not make a fourth witness if they draw on one evidence pool. Here they do not: memory research, material fatigue and microbial microcosms are distinct closures. They still corroborate a shape, not a sign.

## CONCLUSION

The two regimes are isomers over one disturbance budget under Fig 12's definition, or at minimum members of one functional group with a different qualifier. The sequence bond is ordered, so spacing and ordering are part of the term rather than free spelling. The recovery gap is a catalyst and cycle question whose sign decides whether the loop closes or ratchets. The union is second order out of settled events. All of this is `specified` and unmeasured here. The falsifiers are stated in Q1 and Q3 and are the ones sent in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.
