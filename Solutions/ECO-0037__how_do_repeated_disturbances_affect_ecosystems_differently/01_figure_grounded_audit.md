## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

> Question: How do repeated disturbances affect ecosystems differently from a single disturbance of the same total intensity?

Stated as an effect, the residual R is the systematic difference in ecological outcome between two regimes that have the same cumulative intensity. Write the two regimes as operands of a comparison: one pulse of dose D, or n pulses of dose D/n separated by recovery intervals of length t. The aggregate "total intensity" records D in both cases and discards n and t. R is what the discarded quantities still explain.

R by arity, symmetry and scale, before any figure line is chosen:

- **Arity:** two operands under comparison (one regime against the other), each of which is itself a composition. The comparison is at least binary; whether it is a symmetric join or an ordered relation is the first thing to decide, not to assume.
- **Symmetry:** time ordering is not symmetric. A pulse at t0 followed by a pulse at t1 is not the same term as the same two pulses reversed if the first changed the state the second acts on. A sum over intensity is symmetric under permutation; the regime is not. This is the seam the audit has to explain.
- **Scale:** first order is the individual event and the organism or patch response; second order is the regime, the community trajectory across events, and the recovery loop; third order is the legacy carried in soil, seed banks, trophic partners and landscape structure.

**No nameable anchor yet.** A domain is not a domain until an anchor can be named. Here the anchors are available: experimental microcosms and field plots that measure mortality, diversity, biomass and recovery time after a defined disturbance schedule. Fig 2 :29 to :30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* So the residual is ground-testable in principle. Whether this folder has tested it is another matter: it has not.

## 2. Findings

### F1: Equal total intensity is an ingredient count, not a description (governing)

Fig 12 :5:
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :94:
> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :95:
> **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

Fig 12 :46:
> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**

"Same total intensity" is a recipe that names ingredients. One event of dose D and n events of dose D/n have the same ingredient total but different bonds: one operand versus an ordered series with gaps. The difference the question asks about is carried entirely by the bond, so it is not an anomaly to be averaged away. It is the substance.

**Refuter:** a regime descriptor that states only dose predicts the measured outcome as well as one that states dose, frequency and return interval, across the same system and the same total dose. Then the bond carries nothing here and F1 is withdrawn for this domain.

### F2: The two regimes are isomers over one disturbance budget (governing)

Fig 12 :117:
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :102:
> **"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."**

Fig 12 :70:
> **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**

Fig 12 :74:
> **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."**

The sequence bond is ordered, so swapping or spacing the operands makes a different term. Where the ingredients are the same disturbance budget and the bond differs, the regimes meet Fig 12's definition of isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is a structural claim, not a measured ecological law, and it is stated as `specified`.

**Refuter:** show that the ecological outcome is invariant to frequency and return interval at fixed total dose in a system where both are varied, or that the two schedules can be mapped to one another by a symmetric bond with no residual. Then the isomer reading fails and the flat, dose-only reading stands.

### F3: Recovery is a catalyst and cycle question, and dose accounting does not record which (governing D2)

Fig 12 :119:
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Fig 12 :121:
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :123:
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Between two pulses, the system either regenerates the part that was spent (seed bank, resprouting tissue, microbial mutualist) or it does not. Regeneration is a cycle whose sign decides whether the loop closes; loss of the regenerative part turns a series of pulses into a ratchet. Total-intensity accounting records the spent dose and, in the figures' own phrase, *"nothing records which a term is."* This is the structural reason the same dose can have different outcomes: the accounting variable does not carry the state of the catalyst.

**Refuter:** in a matched experiment, measure survival and regeneration of the candidate part between pulses. If the part is demonstrably spent and never regenerates, and outcomes still depend on schedule, then F3 is not the mechanism and another bond governs. If regeneration is identical across schedules and outcomes still differ, the catalyst term is idle and should be removed.

### F4: The aggregate erases the live slots (governing D1)

Fig 1 :19:
> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single "ecosystem response" averages over species, patches and trophic positions. A pulse that removes a dominant competitor and a pulse that removes a foundational mutualist can have the same immediate biomass loss and opposite later trajectories. Fig 1 says the signal that says where to look is thrown away by the average.

**Refuter:** if per-species and per-patch residuals are zero wherever the whole-ecosystem residual is nonzero, the aggregate is sufficient at this scale and F4 is withdrawn. That would require the live signal to be genuinely at the whole-system slot rather than in its parts.

### F5: The residual that matters is steps out (governing D3)

Fig 11 :52:
> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :43:
> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :50:
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :63:
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state. And in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author."**

The disturbance acts first on biomass, then on competitive release, then on mutualists and soil structure. Scoping the comparison to first-order mortality misses the third-order effect, which is the one that decides recovery. Valence is relative to the residual, so the same repeat fire can benefit a fire-adapted species and harm a late-successional one. There is no standing list of good or bad pulses. And a managed regime is permanent work, not a solved state, which is what repeated disturbance means in practice.

**Refuter:** enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering. If the measured outcome at third order adds nothing to the first-order prediction at fixed dose, the cascade claim is idle here.

### F6: This is a union compound, not one hard problem (governing the split)

Fig 9 :54, quoted in §1. Fig 9 :27 to :28:
> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 9 :32:
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

If the question is framed as "how strong is disturbance", any dose-response frame will seem to fit. Describing the residual first, as a compound of intensity arranged in time with a recovery interval, selects the frames that can hold it: experimental disturbance ecology, memory and spacing phenomena, and cumulative-damage engineering. What comes back is a kind of thing (an arrangement-gated response), not a numeric answer.

**Refuter:** show that intensity alone is a complete sufficient statistic for the outcome across schedules in the target system. Then the union splits and the compound reading is unnecessary.

### F7: The dose-only vocabulary is the broken middle link (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :19 and :27, quoted in §1. Fig 12 :111:
> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

"Total intensity" names a state (how much disturbance). It cannot name a route (how that disturbance was delivered across time). A vocabulary with only ingredients can say what a regime contains and cannot say what would count as recovery from it. The missing term is the return interval and the state of the regenerative part between pulses, not more precision on dose.

**Refuter:** provide a dose-only descriptor that states the route as well as the state, for example a descriptor whose parameters include the full schedule and that predicts outcome without a separate bond term. Then the vocabulary objection is satisfied.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. In a matched experiment at fixed total dose, a descriptor that states only total intensity predicts the outcome as well as one that states dose, frequency and return interval.
2. The outcome is invariant to the spacing and ordering of pulses across the tested range (the flat, dose-only case).
3. The proposed regenerative part is shown not to survive its own use, and outcomes still depend on schedule, so the catalyst term is idle.
4. Third-order contact enumeration adds no predictive power over first-order mortality at fixed dose.
5. A symmetric bond maps the two schedules to one term with no residual, so they are not isomers.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
