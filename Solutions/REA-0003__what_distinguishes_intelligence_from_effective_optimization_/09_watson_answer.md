## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what distinguishes intelligence from effective optimization of a narrow objective. Option A: intelligence is a higher degree or wider scope of the same optimization, so more tasks, more compute and a higher score move toward it. Option B: intelligence is a different relation to a ground, a habitat, a closure and an objective, so a perfect proxy score can fail to qualify. What each leads to: A builds bigger optimizers and treats a ceiling score as the goal; B maintains the contact channel, keeps import available, makes the objective refusable, and treats scores as filters for the budget rather than witnesses for the verdict.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Two systems with the same observable competence on a narrow task are called different things. Arity: at least a three-place relation (system, objective, ground), indexed per slot and per habitat (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: "intelligent" is used as a threshold over a class of systems (Fig 12 :87); the agent-to-habitat relation is asymmetric (Fig 11 :61, introduction available, exclusion not). Scale: one loop, a chain of rooms, and a habitat cascade three steps out (Fig 11 :52).
- **D1 Narrow-optimizer loop.** Actors: agent Γ, belief b, action a, fixed proxy. Currency: proxy score, residual reduction. Anchor: none nameable, because the proxy moves when it becomes a target. Status: admitted as a defect, not as a standalone domain.
- **D2 Ground contact and habitat.** Actors: agent, habitat actors, conditions, relations, seat. Currency: verified contact and settlement. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24). Status: admitted, triangulated.
- **D3 Closure and reach.** Actors: atoms(Γ), bonds, imports. Currency: reach, cost in bits and in actions. Anchor: settlement and a differing closure as witness. Status: admitted, triangulated.
- **D4 Direction and objective revision.** Actors: a frame holding a sought shape as a preference, the seat. Currency: refusal (¬) and import. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Status: admitted, single-source on the operator itself.
- **X1 "intelligence as an intrinsic scalar of the agent" and X2 "more compute equals more intelligence".** No anchor that survives a habitat change; both absorbed into D1.

Union, not partition: D1 can run perfectly while D2 through D4 are absent, so the compound needs its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54, :57). Dependency order: perception to vocabulary (Fig 3 :7) to objective (Fig 3 :10) to planning to learn and carry. The objective is link 3, the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :27), which is where the distinguishing intervention sits.

Strain test: Fig 11 :36 appears to deny intelligence where nothing maintains anything, while Fig 11 :39 makes capability a general agent-and-habitat property. Re-census: :36 governs the **label** in unmaintained settings; :37 to :40 govern the **practice** where a seat exists and the capability relation in both modes. Different quantities, no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The distinguishing property is not a higher score on a fixed objective. It is a four-part relation kept separate per domain: capability belongs to the agent and its habitat; contact with a ground is maintained and the channel stays readable; reach is renewed by import from a differing closure because search inside a closed set adds no atom; and the objective itself is refusable because negation is not a bond. A narrow optimizer can run D1 perfectly and still lack D2 through D4. The single-test request is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. Verdicts are never averaged across domains. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected effort. A system failing D2 would be sold more compute, a system failing D3 would be given more search, and a system failing D4 would be given a better score to optimize. The cost is real but bounded, because the six refuters are published with the claim, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The avoidable part is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework before the output; Fig 2 :42 prices that ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. A refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its refuters carry provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered items, in particular an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; an objective metric that remains an anchor under optimization pressure; reach renewed by internal composition with no import; a system without negation that refuses its own objective; or a case where the label does work with no ground contact. A publication that closes F6 with a real discriminating instrument would change the `gap` to a result and would be a welcome reversal. Agreement from the same evidence pool would not reverse anything (Fig 2 :40).
