## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a distinguishing property. Restated as an effect: R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Fig 1 :19). A narrow optimizer drives R down on one proxy. An "intelligent" system is supposed to do something more. What is the difference in the contact structure, such that (i) a perfect score on a fixed proxy need not qualify, and (ii) an imperfect score on a live ground can qualify?

Bound the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running the census, per Formula step "Direction": an answer would look like a **relation** between a system and its ground and habitat, not a scalar property of the system, and it would name what failures are owed when that relation breaks. That shape is now refusable by the same test as everything else.

- **Option A:** intelligence is a higher degree or wider scope of the same optimization (more tasks, more compute, higher benchmark score). Leads to: build bigger optimizers; measure skill; a ceiling score is the goal.
- **Option B:** intelligence is a different relation to ground, habitat, closure and objective. Leads to: maintain the channel, enumerate the habitat, keep import available, make the objective refusable; a score is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

What an answer would look like and be answerable to is fixed before the run. The verdict below comes from the figures; the world sources are secondary.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least a three-place relation (system, objective, ground), indexed per slot and per habitat (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: "intelligent" is used comparatively, a threshold over a class of systems (Fig 12 :87, comparison); the agent-to-habitat relation is explicitly asymmetric (Fig 11 :61, introduction available, exclusion not). Scale: one loop (one agent, one task), a chain of rooms (Fig 7), and a habitat cascade where the residual that mattered is three steps out (Fig 11 :52).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Narrow-optimizer loop | agent Γ, belief b, action a, fixed objective/proxy | proxy score, residual reduction | the proxy, which moves when pushed | admitted **as a defect**: its metric is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30) |
| D2 Ground contact and habitat | agent, habitat actors/conditions/relations, seat | verified contact, settlement | the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24) | admitted, triangulated |
| D3 Closure and reach | atoms(Γ), bonds, imported primitives | reach, cost in bits and actions | settlement plus a differing closure as witness | admitted, triangulated |
| D4 Direction and objective revision | frame holding a sought shape as preference, seat | refusal (¬), import | the ground, never agreement | admitted, triangulated |
| X1 "intelligence as an intrinsic scalar" | the agent alone | talent / g | none nameable that survives a habitat change | not a domain yet; absorbed into D1 |
| X2 "more compute equals more intelligence" | the same loop, larger budget | terms minted, skill | the proxy again | absorbed into D1; a filter for budget (Fig 9 :60) |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 is a loop that can run perfectly while D2 through D4 are absent (specification gaming is the case in point). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57), and when the split lands on a union it must be unbundled before it is searched again (Fig 9 :54). The compound has its own loop and its own ground.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** perception → vocabulary (what things afford, Fig 3 :7) → objective (Fig 3 :10) → planning → learn and carry. The objective is link 3, the middle link with no instrument and the fewest eyes (Fig 3 :24-27). An optimizer that is excellent above the break cannot rescue a break at link 3: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19). Dependency order, not importance, decides the census.

**Anchor test applied to D1.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* A proxy that becomes a target ceases to settle the question (Goodhart, Strathern 1997, world source W1). So D1 has a metric but no nameable anchor. By the standing rule, no nameable anchor means it is not a domain on its own; it is a loop whose metric is not an anchor. That is the finding, not a reason to drop it.

## 2. Findings F1 to F6

### F1 - The question is replaced; capability is agent-and-habitat (decisive, triangulated)

- Fig 11 :34 *"What is intelligence?"*
- Fig 11 :35 *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."*
- Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- Fig 11 :41 *"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."*

Triangulation: Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* (membrane, domain-agnosticism) are two figures with low conceptual overlap. One says capability lives in the relation; the other says the rules do not change with size, only the cast. **Status: triangulated.** Mode: `general` for the structure, `specified` for the AI application.

Refuter: an agent-only improvement that moves the ground. Fig 11's own closing caveat permits this: *"can still come back wrong: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."* (Fig 11 :72). If that is demonstrated, F1 is withdrawn.

### F2 - The label is downstream of ground contact; selection runs where no seat maintains (triangulated)

- Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
- Fig 11 :37 *"Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."*
- Fig 11 :38 *"That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice."*
- Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Reading: where there is no maintainer, the word "intelligence" is a post-hoc label on a lineage that met its ground; the work was selection. Where a seat exists, the word names a practice (enumerate, rank, introduce, build, read). In neither case is it an intrinsic scalar carried by the agent. Triangulation: Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 2 (ground/anchor) govern different quantities, one about where actors come from, one about what settles. **Status: triangulated.** Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a case where the label attaches to an agent with no ground contact and the word does real explanatory work there, such that removing the label loses a prediction.

### F3 - A narrow objective's score is frame-internal, not a ground (triangulated)

- Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Fig 5 :48 *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*
- Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Reading: the optimizer's reward is a quantity the same frame produces, so it cannot score that frame. Under pressure the metric saturates (Fig 10 :26) or is gamed (world sources W1, W2, W5), which is Fig 2 :30's test in action: if it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor. Optimizing harder can therefore increase skill while decreasing contact. That is exactly the effect the question is asking us to distinguish. Triangulation: Fig 1 (which quantities are disqualified), Fig 5 (output is not success) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are three distinct figures. **Status: triangulated.** Mode: `general` moving to `specified` for the AI case.

Refuter: a narrow objective metric that remains a valid anchor under arbitrary optimization pressure, with a published test showing it does not drift when it becomes the target. If Goodhart and Campbell are wrong in this case, F3 weakens.

### F4 - A fixed-objective optimizer cannot cross its closure or re-aim its question (triangulated)

- Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
- Fig 5 :62 *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."*

And from `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" (a formula line, not a figure line): *"It cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* The remedy there is a discipline: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"* (Fig 5 :66, where the figure carries it).

Reading: a narrow optimizer searches inside closure(Γ). More search visits more of the same space and adds no atom (Fig 8 :32). What distinguishes the other case is not a better search but an import from a differing closure (Fig 6 :14, :23) and the ability to make the sought shape refusable, which is the direction-of-question move the loop cannot perform on itself. Where nothing returns a reading there is no edge to extend from: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* (Fig 6 :34). Triangulation: Fig 6 and Fig 8 have different roles (what moves the wall versus why nothing else can). **Status: triangulated.** Mode: `general` for idempotence, `specified` for the AI application.

Refuter: a demonstrated renewal of reach by internal composition alone, with no imported atom and no new measurement. That would break the definitional claim in Fig 8 :32 and the whole finding falls.

### F5 - A fixed objective has no negation; the vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate (single-source for the operator, triangulated with Fig 12)

- Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond. A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."*
- Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
- Operators table :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*

**Quote correction, kept visible.** The first line above was first drafted with the words "what would count of getting somewhere". The verbatim figure reads *"what would count as getting somewhere from it."* The corrected form is used in the files that ship; the draft error is recorded here rather than silently erased. (Checked again against `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` line 112.)

Reading: a narrow objective is a goal (comparison) plus progress (subtraction) plus a plan (sequence), all of which the bond vocabulary can state. What it cannot state, without the unary operator ¬, is that the objective itself is wrong. A system that can only accumulate cannot carry a correction to its own aim. This is the structural reason a perfect optimizer cannot, from inside its own vocabulary, refuse the objective it was given. Triangulation: Fig 12 and the Operators table are the same operator set stated twice, so they are **not** two independent witnesses. The line is `single-source` for the operator claim; the Fig 12 versus Fig 3 link (Fig 12 :115) is a separate route to the same importance of the vocabulary link. Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a system with no negation operator that correctly refuses a held term. If it can, ¬ is not needed for refusal and F5 is withdrawn.

### F6 - One number that discriminates intelligence from optimization: `gap`

No figure line governs a single test that separates the two in one score. Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 says *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 8 :39 says *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* What is owed is an instrument shaped in effect terms: something that registers maintained ground contact and cross-closure import rather than skill on a fixed proxy. Fig 6 :34 says such a sensor cannot be specified from an account of Mars; it must already be returning something that fails to resolve.

**Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.** The gap is declared, not hidden, and it is the honest stopping point for the measurement question. Mode: `general`, status `gap`. Refuter (a welcome one): a published instrument that discriminates maintained contact from proxy optimality in one score on a shared benchmark. That would close the gap rather than threaten the shape.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

1. An agent-only improvement, with no habitat or contact change, that moves the ground or increases verified capability. Breaks F1 and the capability claim at Fig 11 :40.
2. A narrow objective metric that stays a legitimate anchor under optimization pressure. Breaks F3.
3. Reach renewed purely by internal composition with no imported atom. Breaks F4 and the definitional claim at Fig 8 :32.
4. A system with no negation operator that correctly refuses a held term. Breaks F5.
5. The label "intelligence" attaching to a lineage with no ground contact where the word still does explanatory work. Breaks F2.
6. A single general instrument that separates the two cases in one score. Closes F6 (this is a successful result, not a danger).

If any of 1 through 5 fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the replacement question and the shape: the difference is a relation to ground, habitat, closure and objective, not a degree of optimization. Do not offer a definition of intelligence in one number.
2. State the mode on every substantive claim. `general` for the structure, `specified` for the AI application, `gap` for the one-test request.
3. Treat scores as filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). A benchmark can decide what to try next; it cannot settle what the word means.
4. Ask the outside frame what they would count as a failure before showing any theory. Record their verdict on stage 1 first.
5. Keep the refuters in front of the links. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. Verdict of the audit

The distinction survives as a shape. Intelligence is not a higher score on a fixed objective. It is the compound in which (1) capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, (2) contact with a ground is maintained and the channel is kept readable, (3) import from a differing closure keeps reach live, and (4) the objective itself is refusable. A narrow optimizer can run every step of the inner loop and still lack all four. Verdicts are kept per domain, never averaged. One `gap` is declared for the single-test request. Strains after re-census: none hidden. The result is `specified` and ungrounded, candidate until a ground settles it.
