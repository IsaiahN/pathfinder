# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No trust request, no agreement request, no prestige appeal.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against the verbatim figure text by the sender. The offer collapses on contact if a quoted line has drifted.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about wildlife trade, habitat disruption and disease emergence

> Dear [name],
>
> I am writing with one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line failure is more useful to me than endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Wildlife trade and habitat disruption are not two strengths of one pressure. They are two different operators on the contact field.
>
> 1. **Habitat disruption changes the enumeration of actors, conditions and relations in contact with the pathogen.** The framework's rule is that the habitat is enumerated, never composed, and that you list what is in contact with the thing you are tracking, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. A first-order change can therefore surface as a third-order effect. An actor's valence is relative to the residual being tracked: the same host can dilute one pathogen and source another.
> 2. **Wildlife trade is an import.** It carries an actor and its pathogens from one host community into another whose closure differs. Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone, and that surplus is where new transmission routes become reachable. No new primitive is added to the world; a frame gains access to something already reachable elsewhere.
> 3. **Emergence is a term minting event, and sometimes a refusal of a held term; spread is a route, not an ingredient list.** A reservoir is a part that survives its own use. A sustained chain closes a cycle; a chain of one-off spillovers does not.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not naming a species, a pathogen or a place as *the* answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that biodiversity loss always raises disease risk, or that high biodiversity is always protective: the sign depends on scale and on the pathogen, and I record that dependence rather than smoothing it. I am not claiming that any published review has settled this, since most draw on overlapping pathogen lists and the same hotspot papers. I am not claiming a measurement. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **The shape claim I want checked:** the two drivers are categorically different operators, and a single risk index that merges them loses the signal.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a case where re-enumeration of contacts and import have identical effects on the residual at the same scale, with no instrument that distinguishes them;
> 2. a host or trade pathway that is hazardous for every pathogen in every receiving community, independent of what is being tracked;
> 3. a documented import that moves animals and pathogens across a boundary without adding any reachable transmission route to either community;
> 4. one continuous variable that predicts the change better than the three unbundled conditions (crossing, competent receiving habitat, detectable cascade), with no interaction and no loss of resolution.
>
> If any holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. **Before showing you any source, I would value your verdict on the shape and your answer to one question: what would you count as a failure of it?**
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Keesing et al. 2010, Nature: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09575
> - Rohr et al. 2020, Nature Ecology and Evolution: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7224049
> - Rush et al. 2021, Animals: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8233965

Write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state whether your result is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`.

## Legal and practical note

Use public institutional submission or contact venues only. No scraped personal emails, no purchased lists, no bulk send, and an opt-out line in any one-to-one message. GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules apply to unsolicited outreach in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one: a list selected for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that makes agreement worthless (Fig 2 :40).
