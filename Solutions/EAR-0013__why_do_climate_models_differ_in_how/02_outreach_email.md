**Subject:** One checkable claim about why climate models disagree on clouds

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded analysis of a residual in climate modeling: given the same forcing, models predict different amounts of warming, and most of that spread sits in how they represent clouds and cloud radiative effects. I am not asking you to agree with a theory, and I am not asking you to read a framework first. I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. If it fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "why models differ" is not one hard problem with a right amount of cloud physics. It is a union of at least four overlapping conditions: different closures inside each model, different instrument reach across the cloud-to-grid scale membrane, different conventions about what counts as a match, and genuine observational underdetermination of the SST pattern effect and aerosol forcing. A single lever, such as higher resolution or a better microphysics scheme, clears the regions where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
>
> The most checkable part is this: **a climate model can match the observed net top-of-atmosphere cloud radiative effect while being wrong about cloud fraction, liquid water path and droplet effective radius in ways that cancel in the flux.** So "which model matches clouds best?" cannot be answered from net CRE alone. This is a structural claim about what a flat scalar records, not a claim about any named model.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a single intervention that removes the whole intermodel spread of cloud feedback with no residual regime;
> 2. a multimodel-mean statistic that locates the responsible cloud regime as well as a per-regime decomposition;
> 3. matching net CRE across all regimes entailing a matching joint distribution of cloud fraction, optical depth and phase;
> 4. present-day mean-state cloud skill reliably selecting realistic cloud feedback.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I will retract the claim and record the retraction. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.

## Distribution rules

- One to one. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message.
- Public institutional or opt-in channels only (see `03`). No personal emails harvested from private posts.
- Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 **before** showing any framework material. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- Declare any stake in the outcome before the check (Fig 10 :59).
