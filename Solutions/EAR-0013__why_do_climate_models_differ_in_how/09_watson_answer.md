## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the spread among climate models in cloud representation and cloud radiative effect. Option A: it is one hard problem with a right amount of cloud physics, so better models converge on one scheme and one tuning target. Option B: it is a union of overlapping conditions, different closures, different instrument reach across the cloud-to-grid scale membrane, different conventions about what counts as a match, and observational underdetermination. What each leads to: A invites a single scalar verdict and a search inside one closure, and it lets compensating errors pass as correctness; B requires per-domain verdicts, a non-updating anchor, a fixed objective, and a declared gap where no figure selects a scheme.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The spread across model frames of cloud radiative effect and its change per K, indexed per model and per cloud-regime slot. Arity: many models crossed with many regimes, so per-slot, never a global mean (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: shortwave cooling and longwave warming are opposite-signed and partly cancel; the question "effect on warming" is a comparison (⋛), not a symmetric sum. Scale: microns to grid boxes to global decadal warming, crossing a lossy scale membrane (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, the model frame.** Actors: dynamics, radiation, convection, microphysics (liquid/ice fraction, effective radius), boundary layer, aerosols, land surface. Currency: W m^-2, cloud fraction, optical depth, water paths. Anchor: none internal; a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :27). Union condition (i), different closures.
- **Domain 2, the instrument frame.** Actors: CERES, CloudSat, CALIPSO, MODIS, EarthCARE, field campaigns, reanalyses, kernels. Currency: measured TOA flux and cloud profile. Anchor: the measured radiative imbalance and CRE, which do not move when a model changes. Reanalyses are frames, not anchors. Union condition (ii), instrument reach.
- **Domain 3, the verification and tuning frame.** Actors: model developers, tuning targets, emergent constraints, CMIP/CFMIP, satellite simulators (COSP). Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground only, never agreement. Union condition (iii), conventions about what counts as a match, plus condition (iv) underdetermination when the evaluation pool is shared.
- **Domain 4, the cloud-process habitat.** Actors: SST patterns, aerosols, inversion strength, updrafts, phase, circulation regime. Currency: physical contact. Anchor: process measurement. Union condition (iv). Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- **Partition or union:** union. The four conditions overlap, so one lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54). One verdict per domain, never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception (can we measure flux and profile), vocabulary (a common cloud definition via simulators), objective (present-day flux or dCRE/dT), planning (scheme and resolution), learn and carry (CMIP). The chain breaks at the objective in much of the literature.

Strain test: the apparent conflict between Fig 11 valence (actors flip with the residual) and Fig 2 anchor (the ground does not move) dissolves on re-census: one governs an actor's effect on the residual, the other governs what settles a verdict. The apparent conflict between Fig 8 surplus and Fig 2 agreement-worthlessness dissolves because independence is one quantity read twice. No strain was smoothed over.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures decide for Option B, in `specified` mode. The spread is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard problem. It is indexed per model and per cloud regime, so a multimodel mean is the wrong object. Cloud radiative effect is anchored by a measurement that does not update, and reanalyses are frames. Scheme differences live in the typed bond, so schemes over a fixed variable set are isomers and schemes sharing only a few variables are a functional group. Net CRE matching cannot establish correctness because compensating errors cancel in the flux. The broken link is the objective, and resolution is an inward instrument extension with a measured round trip gap. Actor valence flips with regime. No figure selects a winning parameterization, so that part is a `gap`, unreached at this budget. The strongest checkable claim is the compensating-errors finding; the widest claim, that this is a union, is structural and `general` in its shape.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the union verdict is wrong and the spread really is one lever, the cost is a redundant per-domain audit and the loss of a tuning opportunity, not a physical error in anyone's model. The larger avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending them a framework summary before they have given a verdict on the residual. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Leading with C1 and its refuter avoids that cost.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive has to be withdrawn. If a single intervention is shown to remove the spread, the union is narrowed to a partition and the affected findings are retracted in `01` §6 with the retraction kept visible. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument (co-located cloud fraction, LWP, effective radius and phase with radiative closure) showing that matching net CRE entails a matching arrangement would reverse C1. A deployed mean-state metric that predicts dCRE/dT would move the chain break. A tuning target independent of model output and model-built reanalyses, against which models range separately, would change the anchor verdict. A measured lossless cross-scale round trip would set R_T to zero. A figure line that selects a cloud scheme would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
