## What is being decided, in effect terms

A family of climate models, given the same forcing, predicts different amounts of warming. The disagreement is not spread evenly across the models or across physical processes: it concentrates in one slot, the representation of clouds and of their radiative effect and its change with warming. The question asks for the shape of the explanation of that spread. Two answers are compared. Option A: the spread is one hard problem with a right amount of cloud physics that better models will converge on. Option B: the spread is a union of overlapping conditions, different model closures, different instrument reach across the cloud-to-grid scale membrane, different conventions about what counts as a match, and genuine observational underdetermination. What each leads to: A invites a single tuning target and a search inside one closure; B requires per-domain verdicts, independent anchors, and a refusal to average the conditions into one number. The figures decide for B in `specified` mode, and return a shape, not a number or a winning model (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settles which cloud parameterization is correct. That would be a verdict in `general` mode with no ground reading, the output-layer failure named at Seat Map :281.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for people likely to agree manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless (Seat Map :163).
2. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The outside frame must hold the residual, not our interiors.
3. **Prestige cannot settle a physical question.** The ground settles it: the measured top-of-atmosphere flux, the satellite profile, the experiment. Agreement among modelers is a convention until it ranges against a non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29, :33).
