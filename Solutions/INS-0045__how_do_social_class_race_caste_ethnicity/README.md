## What an answer would look like (Direction bound as an ordinary term)

An answer would (1) name the slot over which the residual is indexed, (2) name an anchor that the institution cannot move, (3) separate the institutional procedure from the social habitat and the measurement channel, (4) state which of those three conditions is failing for a low-access group, and (5) say what instrument would discriminate among them. Anything that names a single cause before doing steps 1 to 3 is a candidate, not an answer (Fig 9 :27–28, Fig 8 :53).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

The reasons are figure-grounded: a room does not add an anchor (Fig 7 :13, Fig 2 :35); selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40); and a recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42, :52). Public opt-in venues also avoid the legal failure of unsolicited bulk outreach.
