## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Aggregation can reverse a per-group reading.** Sprenger and Weinberger (SEP 2026) is the mathematical corroboration of Fig 1 :22. A headline that looks neutral can be a weighted average of group readings with opposite sign. This is the strongest low-overlap witness for the per-slot rule.
2. **Multiple impartial criteria conflict.** Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan (2017) proves that three plausible fairness conditions cannot generally be satisfied at once. This corroborates the union reading in Fig 9 :57: adding a second condition does not make the first harder, it makes a compound.
3. **Discrimination is not a single-level phenomenon.** Barocas, Hardt and Narayanan (2023) separate structural, organizational and interpersonal levels and show that formal equality chiefly addresses direct discrimination. This corroborates the domain census in `01`, sections 2.2 and 2.3.
4. **Measurement itself is a venue of failure.** The same book's testing chapter documents selective labels, infra-marginality and the absence of a single necessary-and-sufficient fairness test. This corroborates F6 and F8. Use the audit-study literature as a *lower bound* on a disparity, never as a complete reading of the ground.
5. **Habitat can move a reading without moving the person.** Steele and Aronson (1995) gives the individual-stratum case: making identity salient changes performance. Corroborates Fig 11 :40 at a different scale, with the anchor re-named per Fig 4 :20.

All world sources are secondary. The figures decide. A conflict sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the verdict (see `09_watson_answer.md`, WORLD).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape and its refuter first. The framework interior is offered only after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27–28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before naming any field or category as the cause.
- **Fig 2 :40**, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* No list ranked by receptiveness. No pooling of later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted, not an answer.

**Order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then offer the links, noting that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a reusable method with the instruction to write a fresh refuter and state a mode.
