# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the frame

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 claim below must survive the checks in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` before this is sent. If it fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about group differences in access to authority and protection

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of how social class, race, caste, ethnicity and gender affect access to public authority and legal protection. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the effect is not a single inequality. It is a gap that has to be read per group, not as an average. The figures say *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"* (Fig 1 :22). So an overall fairness statistic can be near zero while one group's access is failing. The claim is that three things have to hold together for a group to receive protection: a channel that still records what happens to that group, a procedure that can be ranged against an outcome the institution cannot move, and a habitat whose relevant actors are read from the actual dispute. When one fails, the gap grows. When two fail, the result is a different compound, not a worse case of either (Fig 9 :57).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a named category is always the cause; that a single reform clears the gap; that a formally impartial rule is enough; or that this is a measured result. The figures return a shape, not a name. I have deliberately left the specific lever open.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. Aggregate outcome data loses no information about any group's access.
> 2. An institution's own self-assessment reliably predicts independently recorded group outcomes across a change of office.
> 3. A formally impartial rule produces a group-invariant outcome distribution at every aggregation.
> 4. A fixed ranking of which category matters survives a change of dispute and institution.
> 5. A single fairness criterion is sufficient for both authority access and legal protection across institutions.
>
> If any of those holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question.
