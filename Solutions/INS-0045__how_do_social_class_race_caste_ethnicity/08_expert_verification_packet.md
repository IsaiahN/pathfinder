# Expert verification packet: INS-0045

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in inequality and authority research can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` for the application, `general` for the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No outcome dataset was read. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Group differences in access to public authority and legal protection are a per-slot outcome residual, not an aggregate inequality. The effect is settled by the recorded distribution of outcomes for people, not by an institution's own account of its fairness. Three conditions hold together as a union: a channel that still records what happens to each group, a procedure that can be ranged against an outcome the institution cannot move, and a habitat whose relevant actors are enumerated from the specific dispute. Formal impartiality is a convention until its application distributes protection, at which point it is graded as a verdict. The framework does not name which category binds or which reform closes a gap; that is a declared gap awaiting a source-discriminating instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** effects must be indexed per group slot; aggregate fairness statistics are filters, not witnesses; the anchor is the recorded outcome distribution.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :36, *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **Refuter:** an aggregate statistic that loses no information about any group's access, or an institution self-assessment that predicts independently recorded group outcomes across a change of office.

### C2: field-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own outcome records

- **Statement:** the conditions form a union, not a list. A person failing two conditions is in a categorically different compound. Relevant actors are enumerated from the world; their valence changes with the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 11 :43, *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** in a domain you know, do the recorded disparities split into disjoint single-cause sets, or do they overlap such that a single lever leaves a residue? Does the same institution help one group and harm another when the question changes?
- **Refuter:** a complete disjoint partition of disparities with no interaction, or a fixed category ranking that survives a documented change of residual.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a field consequence

- **Statement:** formal equal treatment is a declared meaning, not a grounded one; once its application decides outcomes it must itself be graded against the anchor. The missing discriminator is a gap, not a hidden answer.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :47, *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **Refuter:** a formally impartial rule whose application produces a group-invariant outcome distribution at every aggregation and under a different office.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No ranking of class above race above caste above ethnicity above gender, or any other ordering.
- No single cause and no single reform.
- No claim that a formally impartial rule is sufficient, or that it is irrelevant.
- No claim that this is a measurement, a `grounded` result, or a settled answer.
- No claim that a fairness metric is useless. It is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict.
- No claim about which specific institution fails which condition. That needs the outcome record.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument on real outcome records, not a further argument. Three readings would close the gap:

1. Hold procedure fixed, change habitat, read the outcome. Does the group gap move?
2. Hold habitat fixed, repair the channel, read the outcome. Does the stored gap shrink or was it in the record all along?
3. Hold the formal rule fixed, move its application to a different office, read the outcome. Does impartiality survive the move?

If all three readings leave the same per-slot residual, the union claim is refuted.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29 and :36, Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 11 :43 and :51, Fig 10 :18 and :47. Ten short passages.
2. Check C2 against one domain you know: are the conditions a union in your data, or a partition?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish procedure failure from habitat failure and channel failure, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
