## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the outcome

The figures README gives the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For CMP-0080 the shape is the three-part separator: structural conditions that constrain the response, contact conditions that keep a non-updating ground reachable, and a written refuter. It does not include a number, a feature list, or a yes/no. `SPACE(???)TIME` is a shape in the exemplar; `per-slot structure with habitat contact and a non-updating crisis ground` is the analogous shape here. A universal capacity index is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring terms that overlap or correlate, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the first contact carries the residual, not the model.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This question is such a bundle: structure, habitat, and ground (Fig 9 :55).

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A pre-crisis structural reading is a gate, not the ground.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the limit: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* The declared set is a visible choice, not nature's list.

## 4. A source-discriminating instrument is owed, not a further argument

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A color instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it. *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

Applied here: a structural reading that says "high redundancy, modular, diverse" is a green reading. Several very different habitat-and-ground configurations could produce the same reading. Naming "adaptive capacity" from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates the source: a held-out, non-updating crisis outcome that separates the candidate mechanisms (Fig 11 :70: a harness proves wiring, never capability).

## 6. Correction to my own earlier use of "shape, not a name"

I initially applied the rule to every structural feature and wrote that the framework could not name any feature that predicts adaptation. That was an overstatement. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name).*" It governs a primitive not held in `Γ`. Redundancy, modularity, bonds and networks are already available bindings from the complex-systems frame onto held atoms; naming them is provenance, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is (a) inventing a seventh kind, (b) claiming such a binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*), and (c) presenting a named feature as a standing predictor across residuals (Fig 11 :51). Both readings are kept visible here so neither is quietly lost. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1.

## 7. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A panel of well-known complexity scientists agreeing that a system "looks adaptable" is the named collapse, not corroboration (Fig 2 :35, :40). The legitimate ask is a budget to try the claim against a ground, not an endorsement.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
