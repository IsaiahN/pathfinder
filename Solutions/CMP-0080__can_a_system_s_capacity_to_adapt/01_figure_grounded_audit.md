## 0. Residual, in effect terms

The residual is not "how much capacity does this system have." It is the mismatch between what a structure-only reading can predict and what would actually happen when the question moves (a crisis). Stated as an effect: a model built from the system's structure before the shock fails to range against the outcome the shock produces, and the failing signal is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), not averaged into one resilience score.

- **Arity.** Two terms plus a third: a structural description `S`, a realized adaptive response `C`, and the crisis ground `g` that settles `C`. The question asks whether `S` alone predicts `C` before `g` acts. That is a binary prediction relation with a hidden third term, so it is a **union**, not a two-option partition (Fig 9 :55): structure is one condition, contact is a second, and the ground is a third.
- **Symmetry.** The relation is ordered. `S` comes before `g`; `C` is read after. Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (sequence, `→`). It is not a symmetric join: a correlation between a structural feature and an outcome cannot choose the arrow (Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*).
- **Scale.** One scale for the structural reading (a system at a moment), another for the response (a cycle through crisis and recovery). Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The transform between the scales is lossy, so a round-trip gap `R_T` is owed (Fig 4 :30).

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (formula "Direction").** I write down what an answer would look like before deriving: *a separator that names, per domain and per slot, which structural facts constrain adaptive capacity and which part of the capacity cannot be read until the ground settles it, with a written refuter and a stated mode.* That shape is now a term that can accrue a residual like anything else. It is not a preference smuggled in after the run.

**Proposal header.** See `README.md` (DECIDES / HOURS / PRIOR CASE / ANALOGUE / LIMIT). DECIDES is non-empty; A and B are not the same act.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line was chosen

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the structural frame** (the library `Γ` of the system at time t) | atoms, bonds, agents, network | description length in bits, plus actions to test the arrangement (Fig 12 :125-126) | none internal; it ranges against the ground only through D2/D3 | partition boundary: which compositions are legal across the two kinds (Fig 13 :74) |
| **D2, the habitat / contact field** | actors, conditions, relations (Fig 11 :45-47) | contact and its cascade | the ground, which settles; the habitat itself updates (Fig 11 :73) | union: structure ∩ contact is the compound that decides capacity |
| **D3, the prediction / verification frame** | the predictor, the checker, the crisis | prediction residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot | the realized crisis outcome; an anchor only if it does not update on what the predictor thinks (Fig 2 :29-30) | verdict: is the predicted capacity in the closure at all (Fig 9 :6) |

**Partition or union.** The question as posed is a **union with an unassignable shared fraction**, not a partition. `S` determines what is composable; `C` is a property of agent-and-habitat; `g` settles it. A single lever that improves the structural reading leaves the contact and ground intersections standing (Fig 9 :55, :57). So the honest object is the compound `S ∩ D2 ∩ g`, with its own loop, not one hard question with a yes/no answer.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) perceive the structure per slot (Fig 3 :4-5); (2) have vocabulary for what the structure affords (Fig 3 :6-8, the middle link with the fewest instruments, Fig 3 :27); (3) say what would count as progress, i.e. bind the shape above (Fig 3 :9-11); (4) plan the probe; (5) name what worked, verify, carry (Fig 3 :14-16). A capacity reading taken below the break in link 2 is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). Order: describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle the first-order structure before judging the second-order response (Fig 12 :104); refuse a false term before minting (formula step 2).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Capability is not a property of the structure alone (decisive)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The structural frame D1 holds the agent side. Capacity lives in the compound `D1 ∩ D2`. A prediction made from `S` alone omits the habitat and the contact that settle whether the structure pays. The question's premise ("from its structure") is therefore the exact isolation error the figure names. Mode `specified`, **triangulated**: Fig 11 :39-40 (habitat) plus Fig 2 :29-30 (anchor defined by what settles, not by what any frame thinks) are low-overlap lines. **Refuter:** a held-out demonstration that a structure-only description predicts realized adaptation across systems and crises, with a non-updating outcome, at accuracy above a habitat-aware baseline. That would refute the capability claim. The framework essay *Arrival of the Fittest* lists this claim under "A note on what is not cited," so I do not pretend a world source proves it.

### F2 - A synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability

Fig 11 :70:

> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Fig 11 :66:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Simulating adaptation on a model of the structure substitutes a habitat. It tests whether the wiring is correct, not whether the system will meet its ground in the real crisis. This is the most common false positive in the literature: an agent-based model that reproduces a pattern is a harness result (see `04_links_and_distribution.md`, CoMSES Net as the venue that discusses this distinction). Mode `specified`, **triangulated** with Fig 6 :34 (*"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*). **Refuter:** a harness result that transfers to a real, non-updating crisis outcome without re-substituting the habitat; or evidence that the model's habitat reproduces every actor and condition the real residual contacts (Fig 11 :44).

### F3 - The predicted capacity is per slot, and averaging kills the signal

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single "adaptive capacity" score across a system's slots, or across a population of systems, is the named error. One slot can be live (positive mass) while the global residual is near zero (Fig 1 :13, :20). The separable claim is per system, per slot, per residual. Mode `general` structural, **triangulated** with Fig 9 :4-5 (split disagreements that do not shrink). **Refuter:** show that the residual relevant to a crisis is genuinely global and not slot-indexed, by an instrument that resolves the crisis without slot decomposition.

### F4 - No figure line returns a number from structure; the literal question is a `gap`

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

After the census, no figure line maps `S` to a scalar capacity. The six-term set is *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* (Fig 13 :115). Searching the arrangements of the structure cannot add the atom that the crisis would supply (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*). This is **unreachable at this time**, not underivable in principle: the missing term is the ground's realized demand, which arrives with the crisis. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Mode `gap`, recorded as a verdict. **Refuter:** exhibit the figure line that returns the capacity, or a measurement that settles it before the crisis.

### F5 - The channel decays, the ground does not

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26:

> **"stale percepts, dropped transients,"**

Fig 10 :27:

> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

A pre-crisis structural reading is a channel reading. Its failure mode is not that the ground changed; it is that the channel to the ground saturated, pooled, or dropped the transient that mattered. So "we predicted capacity from structure and were wrong" usually means "we lost contact," which Fig 10 :28 names as *"you have lost touch"* rather than a verdict. Mode `specified`, **triangulated** with Fig 2 :33-34 (coherent drift when the reference updates). **Refuter:** a case where the prediction failed while the channel demonstrably stayed reachable and the ground demonstrably changed.

### F6 - The broken link is the affordance/objective link, not the structure inventory

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :18:

> **"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."**

Fig 3 :29:

> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

Most effort in adaptive-capacity work lands on structure inventories (perception, components) because those have instruments. The link that decides capacity is having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress (Fig 3 :25-26), which has neither. A structural inventory cannot rescue a broken objective link (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). Mode `specified`, **single-source** on the chain figure; corroborated by the world literature on early-warning signals (which measures a trajectory, not an inventory; see `09`). **Refuter:** an instrument that measures the affordance link directly and shows it is intact where the prediction fails.

### F7 - Same atoms, different bond, different capacity (isomers)

Fig 12 :117:

> **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Two systems with the same components but different typed bonds can have different adaptive capacity. A component count is a flat formula; the bond is "the half nothing was recording" (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*). Fig 12 :119 names a *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* and :121 a *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."* Both change the response across uses, and neither appears in an ingredient list. Mode `specified`, **triangulated** with the world source on weak linkage and compartmentation (Kirschner and Gerhart 1998, see `10_source_ledger.csv`). **Refuter:** two systems with identical atom sets and identical bonds (including sign of every loop) that adapt differently under the same residual, with no habitat difference.

### F8 - Two currencies, and a capacity index can only read one

Fig 12 :125:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126:

> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A structural description is priced in bits; whether the system can actually pay for the response is priced in actions. A structural predictor holds one accounting and "will not notice the shortfall" (Fig 12 :127). This is why a system can be describable as adaptable and still be unable to afford the test. Mode `general` structural, **single-source** on Fig 12. **Refuter:** a single measure that prices both in the same units without loss, demonstrated on a real case.

### F9 - The valence of a structural feature is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :51:

> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A feature that helps in one crisis harms in another. There is no standing structural list of adaptability features; the list is read off the residual (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*). This is the structural reason a universal predictor is refused. Mode `specified`, **triangulated** with Fig 10 :71-72 (room must change with the question). **Refuter:** a feature with positive valence across a census of residuals drawn from low-overlap domains, measured against a non-updating anchor.

### F10 - A capacity judgment must be triangulated, not negotiated

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

A panel of structural experts who all read the same model is one frame wearing many names (Fig 8 :48). Their agreement about "this system looks adaptable" is efficient for a convention and worthless as a verdict about the crisis. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Mode `general`, **triangulated** with Fig 7 :35 (*"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*). **Refuter:** an independent frame whose closure differs, ranging separately against the same non-updating crisis, that predicts the same shape for residual-relative reasons.

### F11 - The verifier of a capacity claim must not hold its interiors

Fig 10 :42:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Fig 10 :52:

> **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

This governs the outreach order: send the residual, the shape, and the refuter first; record the verdict before showing the framework or the model details. Mode `general`, **triangulated** with Fig 9 :27-28 (*"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*). **Refuter:** a verifier who reconstructs the model from the claim and still returns an independent ground reading, with the channel provenance shown.

### F12 - The question is a union, so verdicts stay separate and are never averaged

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A capacity index is a filter: it decides which systems get tried first. It is not a witness for the verdict about any one system. Per-domain verdicts (D1 structural shape, D2 habitat contact, D3 ground) are kept separate in `07` and `09` and never averaged into one confidence number. Mode `general`, **triangulated** with Fig 1 :19-22. **Refuter:** a single scalar that is shown to be a witness for every slot, not merely a budget filter.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat-map rule *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The B verdict (structure alone cannot deliver the capacity; only a shape plus a refuter) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Capability-is-agent refutation.** A held-out demonstration that a structure-only description predicts realized adaptation across low-overlap systems and crises at accuracy above a habitat-aware baseline, against a non-updating outcome (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. **Harness refutation.** A synthetic solve that transfers to a real crisis without substituting a habitat, or a harness whose habitat reproduces every contacting actor and condition (breaks F2, Fig 11 :70).
3. **Figure-line refutation.** A figure line is exhibited that returns the capacity from structure before the ground settles it (breaks F4, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). This is the direct refuter of the `gap`.
4. **Standing-list refutation.** A structural feature is shown to have positive valence across a census of residuals drawn from different fields, not merely in the crisis that selected it (breaks F9, Fig 11 :51).
5. **Isomer refutation.** Two systems with identical atoms and identical typed bonds (including loop signs), with no habitat difference, adapt differently under the same residual (breaks F7, Fig 12 :117).
6. **Channel refutation.** A prediction failed while the channel stayed reachable and the ground demonstrably moved (breaks F5, Fig 10 :24).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is recorded here, not silently edited. Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse the premise "from its structure" as a complete predictor. Restate the deliverable as a per-slot separator: structural conditions that constrain capacity, contact conditions that settle it, and the ground that decides (F1, F3, F12).
2. Report the literal pre-crisis capacity as `gap` / unreachable-at-this-time, not silence and not a fabricated number (F4).
3. Treat a synthetic solve or an agent-based reproduction as wiring evidence only (F2). Require a non-updating, held-out crisis to call it capability.
4. Price the description in bits and the test in actions separately (F8). A cheap structural description is not an affordable response.
5. Lead outreach with the residual, the shape, and the written refuter; offer the framework only after the verdict is recorded (F10, F11). Never ask for trust or agreement.
6. Use filters (a capacity index, a tier) for the budget only; witnesses are independent frames ranging against the ground (F12).

## 7. Strains and re-census summary

- **S1, the question's premise versus F1.** Re-census: "from its structure" is the agent-only isolation the figure refuses. The premise is narrowed to "from the structure-and-habitat compound, read per slot." Strain dissolved, reading kept visible.
- **S2, early-warning signals versus the `gap`.** Re-census as above: a precursor signal is a channel reading, not a capacity-from-structure reading. Dissolved; the world source is kept as corroboration for F5 and F6.
- **S3, applying the physical six-term set to complex systems.** Re-census: Fig 4 :20 says only actors, currency and anchor change with size; the domain-agnostic rules (closure, bonds, habitat, ground) govern, not a literal claim that every system has space/time/energy. Recorded as a narrowing, not a hidden mapping.
- **S4, live type question.** The Operators table defines comparison `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* A pre-crisis capacity judgment is a comparison of a system against a threshold on the realized outcome. Whether a structure-only description counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not smoothed.
