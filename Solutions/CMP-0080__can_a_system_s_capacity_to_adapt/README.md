## The question as an effect

Decide what a structure-only reading can predict about a system's capacity to adapt when the reading is taken before any crisis. Option A: assert that structure pre-commits capacity, so a yes/no prediction is in reach. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape (which structural conditions keep the ground reachable and the channel open, plus a pre-registered refuter) and record the realized capacity as unreachable until the ground settles it. A leads to an unmeasured verdict stated as settled and to readers being handed a recording instead of a generator. B leads to a checkable shape, a written refuter, and an open slot.

An answer would look like: a separator that tells, per domain and per slot, which structural facts constrain adaptive capacity and which part of the capacity cannot be read until the question moves. That shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0 and allowed to accrue a residual, per the formula's "Direction" rule.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that adaptation is predictable from structure.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting readers for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that voids their agreement as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
3. **Prestige is not action.** *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*.) A famous name does not settle whether a system will adapt.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
