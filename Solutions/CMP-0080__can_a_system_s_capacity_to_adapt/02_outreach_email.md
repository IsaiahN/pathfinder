**Subject:** Check one claim about predicting adaptation before you decide about the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about complex systems that I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the per-domain parts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal yes/no).** A system's capacity to adapt is not a property of its structure alone. The figure-grounded derivation says capability is a property of agent-and-habitat: the structure supplies what can compose, the habitat supplies the contact, and the crisis is the ground that settles the verdict. So a pre-crisis reading can deliver a shape, not a capacity number. The shape is a separator with three parts: which structural conditions constrain the response (per slot, not averaged), which contact conditions keep a non-updating ground reachable, and what would refute the reading. A synthetic solve on an agent-based model proves the wiring, not the capability, because it substitutes a habitat.
>
> **What is explicitly not claimed:** that there is a standing list of structural features that predict adaptation across crises. The framework's rule is that an actor's valence is relative to the residual, so the same feature helps in one crisis and harms in another. I also do not claim a universal capacity index. Such an index is a filter for which systems to try first, not a witness for any system's verdict.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written before this run):**
> 1. A held-out demonstration that structure alone predicts realized adaptation across low-overlap systems and crises, above a habitat-aware baseline, against a non-updating outcome.
> 2. A synthetic solve that transfers to a real crisis without re-substituting the habitat.
> 3. A measurement of a direct capacity reading from structure with no contact term, or a figure line that returns the number.
> 4. A structural feature with positive valence across a census of residuals drawn from different fields.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture and the six-term substrate: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Open venue for the resilience / adaptability framing: https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/submissions/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own warning is that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
