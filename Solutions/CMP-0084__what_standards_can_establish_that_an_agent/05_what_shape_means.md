## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit. `FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands," and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For CMP-0084 the shape is: *grounding is a relation to a non-updating anchor, reported per slot, corroborated by low-overlap evidence, discriminated where alternatives are observationally equivalent, pre-refutable, and recorded with a lineage.* The shape does **not** name ODD, TRACE, FAIR, or CoMSES as the answer. Those are bindings from the world, provenanced as such.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Stopping condition: "the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the operational rule for stage 1 of the outreach: describe the discriminating observation, not the framework.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- Applied here: describe the residual in effect terms (a model scores itself; a global fit hides a live slot; the same data calibrates and validates) before choosing any standard. Otherwise any standard will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical rule: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space. Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all, and only the second reaches structure the current primitives cannot represent." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six. Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For this question, a hold-out dataset from a different instrument is closer to an import of an axis than a new line of code is. Adding rules to M only extends what M can say (Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves").

## 4. Why the illustrative-story failure is the named error here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or an undiscovered scheme can all produce the reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." It was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Windrum et al. (2007), read this session, give the same structure in the ABM field: "different models can be consistent with the data that is used for empirical validation," known as "the under-determination of theory with respect to data," and Haavelmo's "it is impossible for statistical inference to decide between hypotheses that are observationally equivalent." A paper that reports only a reproduced stylised fact has read the projection and named the source. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a better narrative.

*Arrival of the Fittest* states the positive rule in one line: this framework "does not attempt to validate itself, and lets an anchor score the artefacts it produced." That is the whole of F1 and F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` in the author's own words.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a standard?

**No, and the distinction matters.** The canonical phrase governs a *missing* thing: what a primitive not held in Γ would have to look like. ODD, TRACE, FAIR metadata, and the CoMSES checklist are not missing primitives of the framework; they are existing artifacts of a world frame. Naming them is a **binding** from that frame, not a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" - these add none).

What *is* forbidden is naming a standard as *the* answer to "what establishes grounding." The framework returns a shape. A binding must be provenanced, and its claim to be a ground-contact criterion must be tested, not asserted. The CoMSES review criteria are the clearest case: they are real, public, checkable, and explicitly do not assess output validity. That is a binding we can state with provenance, and it survives or fails on the text of the criteria, not on our preference.

**I overstated this rule in an earlier reading** (retracted here, preserved as evidence). My first draft said "the framework cannot name any standard, so the answer must remain abstract." That is wrong: the limit applies to missing primitives, not to naming held artifacts from another frame with a source and a date. The corrected form is in the paragraph above. Both readings are recorded so the correction is visible.

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof. Someone let reputation substitute for result. Someone let the trusted name validate the untested claim. The ground is never consulted." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A well-cited modelling paper and a peer-reviewed badge can get a model tried. They cannot establish that it is grounded.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Every substantive claim in `01` through `09` is therefore labelled `specified` or `general`, never `grounded`. The verdict stays `candidate` until a ground pays.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* supplies the closing caution: "Figure 11 says 'capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone.'" A standard is a property of the checking arrangement, not of the model alone. A badge attached to a model in isolation is the agent-only improvement Fig 11 :40 rules out.
