## What the wider literature contributes

1. **The field already separates gates from grounding, if you read its own criteria.** CoMSES peer review checks execution, documentation and code quality and explicitly declines output validity. That is the strongest real-world datum in this solution, because it is checkable in five minutes and it is the field's own standard, not our reading of it.
2. **Underdetermination is the technical name for the illustrative-story risk.** Windrum et al. (2007) put it plainly: different models can be consistent with the same data, and statistical inference cannot decide between observationally equivalent hypotheses. This is why "it reproduced the pattern" cannot settle grounding.
3. **Empirical adequacy can rise without mechanistic understanding.** Frigg and Hartmann (SEP 2026) say more adjustable parameters can increase empirical adequacy "but not necessarily... a better understanding of the underlying mechanisms." Low-overlap corroboration, philosophy of science.
4. **A global statistic can hide the effect that matters.** Pulicka et al. (2016) found an interference effect hidden inside standard deviation. Concrete, same-field corroboration of the per-slot requirement.
5. **A better instrument, not a better argument, separates sources.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the metamerism case. High overlap with the framework author, so weak corroboration; it does not decide anything.
6. **Reproducibility is a live concern but a different one.** Baker (2016) is cited only to point at the metascience literature; its full text was not read.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the full framework before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the interiors before giving a verdict has joined the frame.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."** A mass send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A journal or repository route decides where the claim gets tried, never whether it is true.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the shape claim, the CoMSES criterion question, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask: what would you count as a failure of this distinction? Record the verdict before any theory is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links in `02_outreach_email.md`: Windrum et al., CoMSES peer review, SEP. Note that the figures, not our summary, are the authority.

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode, and names their own ground. A reported failure is more useful than a reported success.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Every recipient in this campaign starts from the same packet, so their agreement would be one pool talking.
