## What is being decided, in effect terms

Two readings of the question are bundled together. Option A: `intelligent` names a capability that can run without any representation of the goal, so capable systems may have opaque objectives. Option B: a system counts as intelligent only when it understands the goal, so any capable system must represent its objective. What each leads to: A licenses deployment and evaluation by outward behaviour alone; B requires an objective link before planning is credited. The figures unbundle the pair (Fig 9 :54), refuse the term `intelligence` as the wrong question (Fig 11 :34-35), and replace it with a question about capability in a habitat (Fig 11 :39).

The answer is not a single yes or no. Per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged: the after-the-fact label reading is `yes but empty`; the capability reading is `yes for lower links, no for the full chain`; the self-check reading is `the system cannot decide this about itself`; the verification reading is `only the ground maintainer can settle it`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, no purchased or scraped contact list, no bulk-send plan.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree. Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- No claim that this simulated derivation is a measurement, and no claim that a behaviour reading identifies an inner source.

Three reasons, from the figures: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; and Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Legally, unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, which is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
