# Union, order and analogues for the intelligence question

**Mode: `specified` on the analogue, `general` on the union and order claims, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a measurement (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles two conditions: a system being capable, and a system stating what counts as progress toward its objective. Two derivations are owed: (1) is the bundle a union or a partition, and in what dependency order do its parts run; (2) do the after-the-fact label, habitual control and goal-directed control stand to one another as isomers or as members of a functional group?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: six terms in two kinds (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network), seven bonds including `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison and `−` subtraction, plus negation. No seventh primitive is available without import. The objective link (Fig 3 :10) and the bond requirements (Fig 12 :112) are bindings onto held terms, recorded as such.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition? (`general`)

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- A capable system need not state an objective (the unmaintained lineage, Fig 11 :36). A system that states an objective need not be capable in a new habitat (the substituted harness, Fig 11 :70). Each condition holds without the other, so the overlap is a compound with its own loop.
- ⇒ **Union.** Unbundle into D1 label, D2 capability with objective link, D3 self-check, D4 verification. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. A single lever applied to "intelligence" clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

### Q2 — What is the dependency order? (`general`)

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

The five links are perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The objective is link 3 (Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*; Fig 3 :11: *"what the search is for"*). So:

```
perception -> vocabulary -> objective link -> planning -> learn and carry
                              ^
                              link 3 is the prerequisite for link 4
```

- If link 3 is absent, effort put into planning arrives at a step with no input. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The instrument for link 3, from the animal source, is outcome devaluation with contingency degradation. No analogous instrument is claimed here for an arbitrary machine.
- ⇒ **Order:** D1 (label) before D2 (capability/link) before D3 (self-check) before D4 (verification). Optimising a system's internal machinery before checking link 3 is effort above the break.

### Q3 — What does it take to write the objective link? (`specified`)

- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- Fig 12 :112-113: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond. A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."*
- Operators :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* ; Operators :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*
- ⇒ A library with only atoms and `→` can name states and sequences. It cannot state a goal or measure progress. The objective link needs `⋛` and `−`. This is the figure's operational analogue of goal-understanding, labelled a binding onto held bonds, not a definition and not a new primitive.
- Fig 12 :114: *"Which is the bound that would bite at the middle of Figure 3's chain. Whether it is what breaks there is not yet measured, because a link below a break cannot be read."* Mode stays `specified`: the figure says the bound exists, not that it is where any real system breaks.

### Q4 — Are the three modes isomers or a functional group? (`specified`)

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

| mode | atom set | bond that carries the objective | figure status |
|---|---|---|---|
| after-the-fact label | lineage plus ground, no seat | none; `intelligent` is applied afterwards (Fig 11 :36) | not a molecule of the agent loop |
| habitual control | agent, habitat, stimulus, response | `→` sequence only, no outcome comparison | a member of the same family, no `⋛` |
| goal-directed control | agent, habitat, outcome, value | `⋛` comparison plus `−` progress | the full objective link |

The animal source gives the empirical separation: Balleine and O'Doherty report that *"actions under goal-directed control are performed with regard to their consequences, whereas those under habitual control are more reflexive in nature, by virtue of their control by antecedent stimuli rather than their consequences."* The outcome devaluation and contingency degradation tests discriminate them.

- As written, the three modes do **not** share one atom set: the label case has no seat, the habitual case has no outcome comparison. By Fig 12's definition they are therefore **not isomers**.
- They do share a substructure, the agent-and-habitat loop that ranges against a ground, with the objective link present or absent. That is a **functional group**: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"* (Fig 12 :123). The qualifier, `⋛` present or absent, decides whether a goal can be stated at all.
- The stronger isomer claim would require all three to be written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}` with only the bond arrangement differing. That is not established here and is not asserted.

**Falsifier, written before the conclusion:** if habitual control (stimulus-response) is shown sensitive to outcome devaluation in extinction with no outcome representation, or if a system with no `⋛`-like comparison plans toward a novel objective and the plan transfers to a new habitat, the bond derivation fails and the three modes collapse into one. The result would be sent back to the census, not smoothed into a softer reading.

### Q5 — What order is the union of domains? (`general`)

From the residual alone, the order is D1 → D2 → D3 → D4:

1. D1 decides whether `intelligent` names anything before a ground settles it (Fig 11 :36).
2. D2 decides what the objective link needs and whether planning can consume it (Fig 3 :20-23, Fig 12 :112).
3. D3 decides whether the system can certify its own direction (Fig 5 :62-68, Fig 8 :37).
4. D4 decides who may settle the claim (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :43).

Reversing this order is the failure mode: asking D4 (experts) before D1 and D2 manufactures agreement about an undefined label. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

## CONCLUSION

The bundle is a union, not a partition. Its dependency order puts the objective link before planning and the verification seat after self-check. The operational analogue of goal-understanding is a comparison plus subtraction written into link 3, and a vocabulary without those bonds cannot state a goal. The three modes (after-the-fact label, habit, goal-directed control) are not isomers because they do not share an atom set; they are members of a functional group distinguished by the presence or absence of the objective link. Each claim carries a falsifier. No new primitive and no import that moves the wall is needed for any of it.
