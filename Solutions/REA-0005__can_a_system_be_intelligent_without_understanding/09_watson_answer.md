## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that a system can be intelligent without understanding the goals it pursues. Option A: yes, capability runs without a represented objective, so behaviour alone is enough. Option B: no, intelligence requires goal understanding, so a system must represent its objective before capability is credited. What each leads to: A makes a specification into a goal and lets a convention decide an outcome; B demands a self-report the system cannot soundly give. The figures refuse both framings: Fig 11 :34 *"What is intelligence?"* and Fig 11 :35 *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* The operative question becomes what makes a system capable (Fig 11 :39).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed is refused and replaced. The four domain verdicts stay separate: D1 `yes but empty`, the label is applied afterwards and selection ran; D2 `yes for lower links, no for the full chain`, a system can close local residuals without the objective link but cannot then plan toward a stated goal; D3 `undecidable from inside`, no system certifies its own direction; D4 `only the ground settles it`, agreement is a convention. "Understanding the goal" has an operational analogue, link 3 written with comparison and subtraction, and is labelled a provenanced binding onto held bonds rather than a definition or a seventh primitive. This is `general` on the shape, `specified` on the analogue, ungrounded, candidate. The specific name of a real system's objective link is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not an underivable-in-principle claim.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and five refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is treating a specification as a goal and letting a convention decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47), which is the failure the DeepMind examples make concrete. That cost is avoidable by asking what would refute the objective-link claim before showing any theory.
- **undoable:** yes. The operational analogue is removable without touching the re-census; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already hold it as a binding. No term is deleted: a refusal adds a term and the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :41-43). A superseded claim can be corrected without withdrawing the shape.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A test that distinguishes outcome-tracking from stimulus-driven behaviour in a real system, a demonstration that a system derives its own direction as a bound predicting term that the ground pays for, an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, or a seventh primitive derived from the six would each fire one of the five pre-registered refuters in `01` §3. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
