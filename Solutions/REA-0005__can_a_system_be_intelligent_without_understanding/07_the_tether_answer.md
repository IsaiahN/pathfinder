# The Tether's answer: can a system be intelligent without understanding its goals?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the operational analogue, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two conditions: being capable, and stating what would count as progress toward an objective. The figures refuse the label question outright. Fig 11 :34: *"What is intelligence?"* Fig 11 :35: *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* The replacement is Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

## 2. The answer, per domain, never averaged

**D1, the unmaintained case: `yes, but the yes is empty`.** Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* A lineage that met its ground can be called intelligent after the fact without any goal being understood. Selection ran. Calling that a case of intelligence without understanding is not a discovery about systems; it is a remark about a label. Mode `general`, single-source for the figure line.

**D2, the maintained case: `yes for lower links, no for the full chain`.** Capability is agent-and-habitat. Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* "Understanding the goal" has an operational analogue: link 3 of the dependency chain, which *"can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"* (Fig 3 :10). The chain is ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*). A system can perceive and name what a situation affords without link 3, but planning then receives no input (Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*). So a system can be capable at lower links without goal-understanding, but it cannot be credited with planning toward a goal it does not state. Writing that link needs bonds: Fig 12 :112-113: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond. A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."* Mode `specified`, triangulated across Fig 3 and Fig 12.

**D3, self-check: `undecidable from inside`.** Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."* Fig 5 :63-64: *"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"* A second, low-overlap figure agrees: Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."* So whether a system understands its own goal is not something the system can settle about itself. The remedy is to bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term with a residual (Fig 5 :65-66) and let the ground decide. Mode `general`, triangulated.

**D4, verification: `only the ground settles it`.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* Agreement that a system "seems to understand" is a convention, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47). Mode `general`, single-source for the figure rule, with world corroboration declared in `09`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a yes or no to the original question. The question is refused and replaced (F1).
- Not that any named system does or does not understand its goals. No system was measured.
- Not that `understanding` is a primitive or a seventh term. The analogue is comparison plus subtraction at link 3, a provenanced binding onto held bonds (F11).
- Not that a behaviour reading or a fluent self-report identifies a source. That is the metamerism error (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that this work is `grounded`. It is a simulated derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating test, not more argument. The closest real instrument found was opened this session: Balleine and O'Doherty's outcome devaluation and contingency degradation tests, which separate goal-directed control from habitual control. Their claim: *"actions under goal-directed control are performed with regard to their consequences, whereas those under habitual control are more reflexive in nature, by virtue of their control by antecedent stimuli rather than their consequences."* Applied here, an objective link exists when behaviour tracks the current value of the outcome and the action-outcome contingency, not merely an antecedent stimulus.

The standing refuters, written before the conclusion in `01` §3:

1. a system certifies its own direction from inside and the ground pays for it;
2. a system plans toward an objective it cannot state, with no comparison or subtraction, and the plan transfers to a novel habitat;
3. a frame scores itself with the quantity it produces and the ground pays for it;
4. an agent-only improvement moves the ground;
5. a seventh primitive, including one named `understanding`, is derived from the six.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* What is unreached at this budget is the name of a particular system's objective link and a measurement of whether any real system has it. That is a verdict of `gap`, not silence. The canonical README adds *"Indiscernibility is not identity"*, so a system whose behaviour is indiscernible from one that understands has not thereby been shown to understand. Record the lineage and stop where the instruments stop.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to "can a system be intelligent without understanding its goals?" because it refuses the label question and asks what makes a system capable. In the unmaintained case, "intelligent" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground; selection ran, and the word does no work there, so the yes is empty. In a maintained system, capability belongs to the agent together with its habitat, not to the agent alone. "Understanding the goal" has an operational analogue: the objective link in a dependency chain, the link that says what would count as progress in the system's own terms, and writing that link needs comparison and subtraction, not just an ingredient list. A system can therefore be capable at lower links without that link, but then planning gets no input and its reading is of nothing. And no system can certify its own direction, because what it would have to check is held as a preference, not as a bound term. The shape the framework returns is those four constraints with five refuters written in advance. It stops where the instruments stop and records the lineage, rather than naming a system that understands.
