## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question is: *how much oxygen, if any, was present before oxygen-producing life evolved?*

Stated as a residual R between what a frame predicts and what the rock record returns:

- **Option A.** The pre-biological atmosphere held essentially no free O2. Everything in the record that looks like oxygen before the Great Oxidation Event is either contamination, a local artefact, or a misread proxy. This leads to a clean story in which oxygen is a product of life and arrives with the producer.
- **Option B.** The pre-biological atmosphere held small but real amounts of O2, from abiotic sources (photodissociation of water with hydrogen escape, sulfur dioxide photolysis, radiolysis, tectonic and impact oxidants), possibly with transient local "whiffs." This leads to a story in which oxygen predates the producer and life enters an already occupied slot.

What is being decided, and what each option leads to:

1. **Magnitude.** A number in pressure or mixing ratio. "How much" is a comparison, an ordering of more and less (Fig 13 :20, *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*; Operators table on ⋛, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*).
2. **Existence.** "If any" is a negation as much as an assertion: the frame must be able to refuse the claim that there was none, or refuse the claim that there was some (Operators table :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*).
3. **Ordering.** "Before oxygen-producing life" is a sequence relation. The producer and the product must be ordered, and the bond that orders them is not the bond that produces them (Fig 12 :71, sequence, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, versus Fig 12 :77, production, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*).

**The residual is not one question.** Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The three asks above have different anchors and must be split before any figure line is chosen.

**Bound term (formula step "Direction").** The sought-for shape is made an ordinary term before the run: an answer would look like (i) a per-slot statement, (ii) a source attributed through a discriminator with provenance, (iii) a threshold comparison of sources against sinks, and (iv) an explicit refusal of the universal number. That shape now accrues a residual like anything else and can be refuted by the conditions in section 3.

## 1. Domain census, done before any figure line was chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least three: magnitude, existence, ordering. Not a single scalar. Each is a different bond type (comparison, negation, sequence). One lever cannot clear all three; Fig 9 :56, *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- **Symmetry.** *"How much"* is ordered (more or less), not symmetric. *"If any"* is a unary negation, not a binary bond; Fig 12 :134, *"a bond is a binary operator, and this figure defines the bonds. It does not define the unary ones."* *"Before"* is ordered. There is no symmetric join here that would let us treat two readings as one term by swapping operands.
- **Scale.** The residual is indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :13, *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The slots here are distinct: the global atmosphere, the shallow ocean, a restricted basin such as the Mount McRae Shale, a weathering profile, a local microbial mat. The S-MIF record constrains the first; Mo and U enrichments constrain the third; chromium isotopes constrain the fourth. They are not interchangeable readings of one quantity.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1, the reservoir and magnitude domain.**
- Actors: O2 molecules, the atmosphere, the ocean, sinks (reduced volcanic gases, dissolved ferrous iron, hydrogen sulfide, methane), sources (abiotic photochemistry, oxygenic photosynthesis once it exists).
- Currency: concentration and flux, a comparison in the ⋛ sense (Operators table :48-51; Fig 12 :87, *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*).
- Anchor: a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). The candidate anchors are the rock proxies. They are readings, not the ground; the ground is the actual ancient atmosphere, which is reachable only through instruments. Verdict for this domain: `gap` on the number.

**D2, the provenance and source domain.**
- Actors: oxygenic photosynthesis, anoxygenic photosynthesis, abiotic water photolysis with hydrogen escape, SO2 photolysis, radiolysis, tectonic and impact oxidants, and the geological processes that deliver reductants.
- Currency: provenance, that is, which process produced a given O2 reading, plus the flux attributable to each.
- Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument (the governing essay quote is *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"*). Fig 8 :52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*; Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- Verdict for this domain: `specified` on the structural requirement (a discriminator is owed), `gap` on the actual source of any pre-GOE oxygen.

**D3, the timeline and dependency domain.**
- Actors: the origin of oxygenic photosynthesis, transient oxygenation events, the Great Oxidation Event, the depletion of surface reductants.
- Currency: temporal order (Fig 12 :71, sequence), and dependency (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).
- Anchor: radiometric dating of the rock record.
- Verdict for this domain: `general` on the shape of the dependency (sources must exceed sinks before oxygen accumulates), `gap` on the specific dates. No figure line returns a chronology.

### 1.3 Partition or union

These three domains form a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. A single Mo enrichment reading touches all three: it is a magnitude (how much Mo), a provenance question (oxidative weathering or something else), and a time marker. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* It becomes a specific compound. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Verdicts are therefore kept separate per domain and never averaged into one "how much oxygen" statement.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** Is there an instrument that registers O2 at all in this slot? Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 6 :34, *"It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for source versus sink, and for the bond between them? A vocabulary with only ingredients can name a state and cannot name a route to one (Fig 12 :111, *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*).
3. **Objective.** What would count as progress? Here: a per-slot statement with an attributed source and a stated refusal condition.
4. **Refusal before mint.** Formula step 2: a term held to be false owes a refusal, not an explanation. The claim "there was no oxygen" and the claim "the whiff was biological" are both terms that may need refusal.
5. **Describe before frame.** Fig 8 :53, *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The residual was described in section 0 before any domain was mapped to a figure line.

**Strain test applied before conclusion:** the Fig 1 per-slot rule governs the residual's indexing; the Fig 13 spectrum line governs the meaning of "how much"; the Operators negation governs "if any"; the Fig 12 sequence and production bonds govern "before." Each line governs a different object, so none is strained. Where a line later seemed to strain (F6, the independence of proxies), a re-census was done and is recorded in section 7.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 — The question is a union of three conditions, not one magnitude (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

Magnitude, existence and ordering do not shrink together. The literature confirms the split empirically: estimates of the onset of atmospheric oxygenation published between 2016 and 2022 "differed ... by approximately 500 million years" across at least seven values (Wikipedia, *Geological history of oxygen*, citing the primary literature). That is not one hard number, it is several well-formed questions with different anchors.

- **Refuter:** a single measurement that returns magnitude, existence and chronological order at once, with one anchor, would collapse the union into a partition.
- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 13 :20 (spectrum is ordering, not quantity) with low overlap between the split rule and the substrate line.

### F2 — The answer is indexed per slot; a global average is a filter, not a witness (specified, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :13, *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The world returns exactly this shape. The atmospheric consensus is "practically no O2" globally, at roughly 0.001 percent of present atmospheric level, yet the same record allows local "oxygen oases" (Wikipedia, *Great Oxidation Event*), transient surface ocean oxygenation in the 2.66 Ga Jeerinah Formation (Koehler et al. 2018, via PubMed search index), and Mo and U enrichments in a restricted basin (Anbar et al. 2007, via PubMed search index). A global near-zero is a legal state with one live local slot. Collapsing these into one "how much oxygen" number throws away the only signal that says where to look.

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A global percentage is a budget filter for choosing which basin to sample; it is not a witness for the verdict.

- **Refuter:** demonstration that the per-slot signals are identical, so aggregation loses nothing, would remove the per-slot requirement.
- **Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 2 :31 (*"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"*) with low overlap between the agent-loop indexing rule and the independence rule.

### F3 — "How much" is a shape-not-a-name gap, not a number (gap, triangulated)

Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :115, *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* Fig 13 :134, *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."*

No figure line, operators entry or symbols entry returns a partial pressure, a mixing ratio or a ppm value. The canonical usage rule is explicit: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Here the missing thing is not a primitive but an instrument, so the honest output is the shape of the instrument and the shape of the answer: per slot, source-attributed, threshold structured. The number is a `gap` of two kinds at once: unreachable at this budget for a direct reading, and shape-not-a-name for the universal quantity. Fig 13 :134-135: *"narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."* "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

- **Refuter:** a figure line that returns a numerical concentration, or a direct paleo-oxybarometer validated against a non-updating anchor, would close this gap.
- **Mode:** `general` shape, `gap` for the number, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 13.

### F4 — Failure to detect is not proof of absence (specified, triangulated)

Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 8 :44, *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* Fig 6 :34, *"It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*

The "if any" ask cannot be answered by zero detections. The rock record is incomplete (subduction and metamorphism), depositional ages are uncertain, and proxy interpretations are contested. The honest form of Option A is therefore not "there was no oxygen" but "no atmospheric O2 above trace levels has been resolved by the available instruments." The framework's own limit applies: a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution, is not something a description can manufacture. Here something has registered, at very low levels, so the edge exists and INWARD sharpening is legitimate. But sharpening must not be confused with a proof of zero.

- **Refuter:** a sealed-room argument, that is a demonstration that one instrument exhausts the possible space at every resolution, would make zero a verdict rather than an unreached state.
- **Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 6 with low overlap between the idempotence theorem and the instrument rule.

### F5 — A redox reading cannot name its source (specified on structure, gap on fact; one witness)

Fig 8 :52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*; Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

This is the governing point. Molybdenum, rhenium, uranium, chromium and sulfur isotopes read the redox state of a environment. They do not, by themselves, identify which process oxidised it. The framework's canonical essay states the mechanism directly: the instrument *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"* and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The real case supplies the illustration: Archean steranes in the Pilbara were once read as biomarkers for cyanobacteria and eukaryotes, then *"shown to have been contaminated, and so the results are no longer accepted"* (Wikipedia, *Great Oxidation Event*). That is a source attributed from a reading without a discriminator, later refused.

Import of a source claim into the framework's library is an import, not a mint. Fig 6 :14 names IMPORT as *"the only operator that moves the wall"*, but it carries a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap). A claim that a given whiff was biological must carry its provenance and must explain the gap it closes, or it is an unpriced atom.

- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating measurement that maps one reading to one process with a stated provenance and shadow test would close this gap.
- **Mode:** `specified` on the requirement for a discriminator, `gap` on which source actually operated. Triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 6 and the essay, but the essay shares the framework author's evidence pool, so this is **one witness**, not three. Counted as `single-source` for corroboration purposes.

### F6 — Proxy agreement is one evidence pool until independence is bought (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :44: *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."*

Multiple redox proxies are calibrated against shared depositional and diagenetic models. Their agreement is often the pool talking. The world case is explicit: chromium isotope ratios in banded iron formations were read as evidence for oxygen before the GOE, but *"the chromium data may conflict with the sulfur isotope data, which calls the reliability of the chromium data into question"* (Wikipedia, *Great Oxidation Event*). The 2.5 Ga "whiff" was reexamined and argued to be an anoxic ocean (Slotznick et al. 2022, via PubMed search index), then defended by the original authors (Anbar et al. 2023, via PubMed search index). A disagreement that does not shrink because each reading works on a different subset is Fig 9 :4's signal to split, not to average.

- **Refuter:** a demonstration that two proxies are independent at the evidence level (different minerals, different depositional models, different analytical methods, no shared calibration) would upgrade agreement from one witness to two.
- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7. The statistics corroborator (design effect / effective sample size, Kish 1965) is a genuinely low-overlap witness for the arithmetic, but it corroborates the figure, not the geological fact.

### F7 — The chain breaks at the instrument link (general, single-source on the figure side)

Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

For most of the pre-GOE interval there is no direct instrument for atmospheric pO2. The geological literature calls its techniques *"paleo-oxybarometers"* and notes that *"an ideal technique would rely on trapped gas or fluid in a well-dated rock layer, but such examples are scarce. Most measurements are indirect analysis of oxidation in sedimentary rocks"* (Wikipedia, *Geological history of oxygen*, citing Kendall 2021). More source and sink modelling cannot rescue a missing measurement link. This is a structural claim about the chain, not a claim that nobody has looked; it is a claim that the link has no direct instrument.

- **Refuter:** a direct, validated trapped-gas paleo-oxybarometer for the Archean would move the break.
- **Mode:** `general`, `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 3), corroborated at low overlap by the geological literature on proxy limitations.

### F8 — Source and sink are relative to the residual, and the net is a threshold (general, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :87, *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*

Whether a process counts as a source or a sink of atmospheric O2 depends on the atmospheric state, which is the residual. Reduced volcanic gas is a sink while free oxygen is scarce and a different object once oxygen is abundant. There is no standing list of oxygen sources. The quantity that matters is a comparison: flux from sources versus capacity of sinks. The geological literature states the same balance in effect terms: *"The rate of change of oxygen can be calculated from the difference between global sources and sinks"* (Wikipedia, *Great Oxidation Event*).

- **Refuter:** a process whose valence as source or sink is invariant across all atmospheric states would break the relativity claim.
- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 12 with low overlap between valence and bond type.

### F9 — Importing the geological vocabulary adds no atom and must be gated (general, triangulated)

Fig 6 :25, *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 6 :12-13 name the source gate (provenance) and shadow test (explains a gap). Fig 8 :50, *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

Terms such as "Great Oxidation Event", "whiff" and "oxygen oasis" belong to the geological frame. Bringing them into the derivation does not add a primitive to the world; it gives our frame access to atoms another frame already holds. That is legitimate, but it is an import and must carry provenance. A "whiff" is not a new kind of oxygen; it is a name for a pattern of redox readings in a particular basin at a particular time. Confusing the name with a new atom is the flat-formula error Fig 12 :5 names: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the imported term cannot be derived from, or mapped onto, the atoms already held would make it a genuine new primitive rather than an import.
- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, and per the standing instruction. The shape claim is the bound term of section 0: per slot, source-attributed with provenance, threshold structured, and not a universal number. It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1 (instrument).** A direct paleo-oxybarometer returns atmospheric pO2 at multiple slots for the pre-biological interval, validated against an anchor that does not update on the frame's own model. This closes the F3 and F7 gaps.
2. **R2 (discriminator).** A measurement maps a single redox reading to one production process with a stated provenance and a shadow test that explains the gap it closes. This closes F5.
3. **R3 (aggregation).** The per-slot O2 signals are demonstrated to be identical across slots, so aggregation loses no live signal. This removes the F2 requirement.
4. **R4 (figure line).** A figure line, operators entry or symbols definition is found that returns a numerical oxygen concentration. This breaks the shape-not-a-name verdict in F3.
5. **R5 (sealed room).** A demonstration that an instrument exhausts the possible O2 space at every resolution, making "zero" a settled verdict rather than an unreached state. This breaks F4.

If any fires, the shape claim as stated is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Split the question before answering it. Give one verdict per domain and never average them into a single percentage (F1).
2. State the answer per slot: global atmosphere, shallow ocean, restricted basin, weathering profile, microbial mat (F2).
3. Say "no resolved atmospheric O2 above trace levels," not "zero" (F4). Non-detection is a limit of the channel, not a fact about the ground (Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).
4. Refuse to name the source of any pre-GOE oxygen until a discriminator with provenance exists (F5). This is the point to lead with in outreach.
5. Treat proxy agreement as one witness unless independence is bought (F6). Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the method and the refuter, not a recording of one reading.
6. Keep the chronology and the number as declared gaps, with the instruments that would close them named (F3, F7).
