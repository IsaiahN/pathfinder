# Two oxygen sources as isomers, and the order of "before"

**Mode: `specified` on the structural comparison, `gap` on the chronology.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks about oxygen "before oxygen-producing life evolved". Two supporting questions are owed:

1. Are the abiotic and biological oxygen pathways different kinds of thing, or the same atoms arranged differently (Fig 12's isomer question)?
2. What bond does "before" name, and does the figure set supply an ordering of these particular events?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: photons, water, reductants (Fe2+, H2S, CH4, H2), an energy gradient, a reaction network, and the O2 product. Bonds held: the seven, including → sequence, ⇒ production, ⋛ comparison, and ¬ negation as the unary operator. No new primitive is available without import. No figure line returns a date.

Order by dependency: settle the type of the two pathways before asking for their order (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*). Refuse a false term before minting a new one (formula step 2).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the two pathways isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Over the fixed atom set {photon, water, reductant, O2}, the abiotic path and the biological path share every ingredient.
  - Abiotic: photon + H2O (photodissociation) → H escape + O2. The bond is a direct physical production driven by a gradient; no part of the mechanism survives its own use.
  - Biological: photon + H2O (oxygenase complex) → O2 + stored energy, with the organism surviving the reaction. The bond runs through a reproducing network.
- They therefore satisfy Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a formula that lists only "H2O, photon, O2" and decisive in behaviour, because only one pathway is coupled to reproduction.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either pathway can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, abiotic O2 without photons, or biological O2 without water), the isomer claim fails and the two are different classes rather than isomers.
- **Mode:** `specified`, `single-source` on the figure definition (Fig 12), with a world corroborator that photodissociation plus hydrogen escape and oxygenic photosynthesis are both discussed as oxygen sources in the literature. The corroborator shares the early Earth evidence pool with the rest of the world sources.

### Q2 — Catalyst versus spent part

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- The biological pathway is catalyst-like: the photosystem and the organism survive the reaction and are regenerated, so the same arrangement can fire again at scale.
- The abiotic pathway is spent-part-like: a photon is consumed; nothing in the photolysis event is regenerated by the event itself.
- This matters because a library that records only ingredients cannot tell which a term is. An "oxygen source" list that names both pathways without recording which survives its own use has recorded half the information.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the biological system does not regenerate the relevant part would collapse the catalyst reading into the spent-part reading.
- **Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 :119 and Fig 11 :49 (*"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"*): which pathway matters depends on the atmospheric residual.

### Q3 — What bond does "before" name?

- Fig 12 :71: sequence, **"A then B; B needs A to have happened."** Ordered: swapping the operands makes a different thing.
- Fig 12 :77: production, **"A produces B; B did not exist before."** Ordered: the second operand is absent until the first fires.
- The question's phrase "before oxygen-producing life evolved" is about **temporal order between two producers**. It is a sequence claim about the abiotic pathway relative to the biological one. The relation between the biological pathway and O2 is a production claim (⇒), but that is a different bond and a different question.
- Fig 12 :92-93 warns that under a symmetric bond k operands have k! spellings and one term. Here the bond is ordered, so swapping gives a different claim: "oxygen before life" is not "life before oxygen". The two cannot be treated as variants of one statement.
- **Type strain, named:** the Operators table calls ⋛ *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* If we try to read "before" as ⋛ we get a type error, because "before" orders events while ⋛ compares quantities. This is not smoothed over; the order claim uses →, not ⋛.
- **Mode:** `general` on the bond type, `single-source` (Fig 12).

### Q4 — Do the figures supply the chronology?

- No. Fig 13 :134: **"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."**
- Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**
- The world supplies dates, and they do not agree: onset estimates published between 2016 and 2022 differ by about 500 million years (Wikipedia, *Geological history of oxygen*). The geological literature also reports that oxygen persisted in small quantities about 50 million years before the start of the GOE (Anbar et al. 2007, via PubMed search index), while cyanobacteria are inferred to have appeared at least 2.7 Ga and perhaps well before that.
- So the chronology is a `gap` in the framework and a contested matter in the world. The two statements are kept separate. A figure cannot settle a date, and a date cannot settle the bond type.
- **Falsifier:** a figure line that returns a date would break the gap verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set the two oxygen pathways are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different bond, different substance. One is catalyst-like and the other spent-part-like (Fig 12 :119), which is why an ingredient-only list of "oxygen sources" loses the part that matters. "Before" is a **sequence** bond (Fig 12 :71), not a production bond and not a comparison, and swapping the order gives a different claim. The figures supply no chronology; the world supplies several conflicting ones. The isomer and catalyst readings are `specified` and checkable; the chronology is a declared `gap`.

This rescues the structural content of the question without inventing a primitive, importing a new atom or naming a date. What is refused is any claim that the framework returns the timing or the source name.
