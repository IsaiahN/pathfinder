# Isomers and order: the three pressures, the union, and the route to durability

**Mode: `specified` on the firm-level mapping, `general` on the structural rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names three remedies (regulation, consumer pressure, investor incentives) and asks which is required. Before choosing, two questions are owed: (1) are the three different kinds of thing, or one kind under different names, and (2) what is the order in which the conditions must be satisfied for a change to be durable?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: a differing closure (Fig 6 :23), a harm made reachable inside the firm's loop (Fig 5 :21), a condition that can be violated with a consequence (Fig 10 :19), and a ground reading that can settle the result (Fig 2 :29). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the three pressures isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Write each pressure as a triple at the figure level:

| pressure | differing closure | violable condition with consequence | ground reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| regulation | the state and its legal field | a mandate with a penalty for violation | compliance and measured harm |
| consumer pressure | customers and their field of substitutes | a purchase that can be withheld | revealed choice and measured harm |
| investor pressure | capital providers and their peer field | a cost of capital or exclusion that can change | disclosed harm data and returns |

All three draw on the same three atom types and differ in what connects them: command, purchase, allocation. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers** if the connecting relation is counted as a bond, same atoms arranged differently, different substance. Their emissions curves can be identical (Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*), which is exactly the isomer signature: invisible in the formula, decisive in the behaviour.

**Strain, named.** The figure's seven bonds are +, ∥, ≡, →, ⇒, −, ⋛. None of them is "command", "purchase" or "allocation". So the isomer claim is `specified` and strained-for-now at the level of the figure's own bond vocabulary. What survives without strain is the weaker **functional-group** claim: all three share the substructure *differing closure → violable condition → ground reading*, and the qualifier (state, customer, capital) says which member. Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one of the three cannot be written with the same three atom types, for example if consumer pressure supplies no violable condition with a consequence, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. If none can be written with those atoms, the whole one-operator reading of `01` F5 fails.

### Q2: What is the route to durability?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
- Fig 12 :72: `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*.
- Operators table :51: a goal is *"a comparison"*, and Fig 12 :87 defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.

An ESG disclosure can name a state. It cannot name a route. The route required by the shape is a sequence with four links:

1. **Reachability** (`→`): the harm enters the firm's metric, because a closing is possible (Fig 5 :21, Fig 6 :23).
2. **Objective** (`⋛`): the firm can say what counts as progress, a comparison between the current harm level and a threshold (Fig 12 :87, Operators table :51).
3. **Action and pressure** (`→`): the violable condition is installed and kept live (Fig 10 :19, Fig 7 :39).
4. **Settlement** (`−` on the residual): the ground pays, and progress is a subtraction of the harm (Fig 12 :112, Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*).

A disclosure performs step 2's vocabulary without steps 1, 3 or 4. That is why Fig 3 :27 applies: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20):** perception of the harm → terms for the harm → objective for the harm → plan → carry. Steps cannot be swapped. Buying a rating (step 2 language) before an instrument for the harm (step 1) lands effort above the break, where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29).

### Q3: Union, not partition

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The conditions C1 (reachability) and C2 (pressure) overlap. A regulation that makes the harm reachable but is weakly enforced clears C1 only. A well-enforced rule for a harm that is three steps out in the habitat (Fig 11 :52) clears C2 only. The intersection needs both and is a specific compound, not a harder case of either. A single lever, "pass a law" or "publish a pledge", leaves the intersection standing. This is the structural reason the question's either/or framing cannot work.

### Q4: Can a self-organised arrangement compose at the same level?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
- Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Ostrom's long-enduring common-pool institutions supply the same triple as the three named pressures: a closure (the community of appropriators), a violable condition (monitoring and graduated sanctions), and a ground reading (the resource condition, publicly audited). Having survived long periods, they are settled molecules and can serve as atoms at the same level as regulation. So the honest atom set for `???` is four-membered: state, customer, capital, community. The seat can introduce one of these into a firm's habitat; it cannot subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61), and the result is permanent work rather than a solved state (Fig 11 :63). This is the non-state instance that prevents "regulation" from being a name the framework returns.

**Falsifier for Q4:** a self-organised arrangement that endures without monitoring or graduated sanctions would break the figure-derived condition and widen the shape; conversely, a case where no closure can be introduced without creating a new atom would break the import reading in `01` F5.

## CONCLUSION

The three named remedies are not three mechanisms at the figure level. They are at minimum a **functional group** sharing one substructure, and plausibly **isomers** if the connecting relation is counted as a bond; the isomer claim is `specified` and strained on the bond type, and is recorded as such. Durability is a **route**, a four-link sequence that a disclosure cannot replace. The two conditions form a **union**, so a single lever leaves the intersection standing. A fourth, non-state instance (self-organised community governance) composes at the same level because it is settled against the ground. The shape stays open: the framework returns the conditions, not the name of the institution.
