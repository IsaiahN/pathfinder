**Subject:** Check one claim about corporate harm reduction before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific, checkable output I would like you to test against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural rule, `gap` on the frequency):** "Can firms voluntarily address harms that reduce short-term profit?" is really two conditions bundled into one question.
>
> 1. **Reachability.** A firm's own loop ranges against one metric: what the market returns. If a harm is not priced into that return, there is no live signal for it inside the firm's own frame, so no voluntary action is initiated. This is not a defect in the firm's decision machinery; it is what a system with no registered gap produces. The harm's material effects are often second or third order anyway, outside the firm's first-order view of suppliers and customers.
> 2. **Pressure.** Even when the harm is visible, a change is durable only if something keeps pressure on the decision. Agreement among firms is not pressure. An industry pledge with no violated condition makes the harm statable in words but not in the firm's objective, so it tends to decay into reporting.
>
> So "voluntary" and "pressured" are not two rival mechanisms. Regulation, consumer pressure and investor incentives are three ways of doing the same thing: making the harm reachable in the firm's own loop and keeping a ground reading attached to it. A firm can act voluntarily, but only where the harm already reaches its own metric; and it stays acted on only while something keeps that reading live. What we cannot say from here is how often this happens across firms. That is an open measurement, not a finding.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that regulation is always required (self-organised community governance can supply the same two conditions without a state); not that firms never act from conviction; not that an ESG disclosure is a harm reduction; not that the harmed parties can be read off a single rating; and not a frequency verdict. Two firms can show the identical emissions curve, one from conviction and one from a mandate. The curve cannot tell them apart; only the record can. I report the record, not the adjective.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a documented, durable harm reduction where the harm was invisible to the firm's market anchor and no outside contact or pressure was present;
> 2. a durable, ground-measured reduction sustained after all external pressure and internal agreement activity stopped;
> 3. evidence that a self-organised arrangement stayed durable without monitoring or graduated sanctions;
> 4. a measurement that tells a voluntary action from a pressured one from the harm trajectory alone, with no provenance.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or amend the claim and record the amendment. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
