## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what the market will pay for, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what the firm does: price, product, sourcing, process, investment, disclosure.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library of routines predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what the market actually returns: demand, price, share, cost, survival.
- **s** is the firm and the harm it imposes, indexed per firm and per harm, never averaged into an industry ESG score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is not whether a harm gets *talked about* but whether the harm-reducing action **persists**: call it `D_h`, the duration for which a harm slot stays reduced after the action. Observed: many voluntary initiatives decay or are relabeled; durable reductions often follow a pressure event. A frame that predicts all harm-reducing pledges are equally durable, or that a pledge simply persists once made, has a residual here.

State the residual as: *what makes the gap between a firm's profit-maximizing prediction and the harm's actual trajectory stay small over time, and which conditions have to be present for that to happen?* This names no theory, no "stakeholder capitalism", no "enlightened self-interest". Those are candidate names, withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a conjunction of conditions, each with an instrument that discriminates its failure from the failure of the others, plus a mapping from each named pressure (regulation, consumer, investor) to the condition it supplies. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names the candidate: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The market is explicitly a named anchor, and a harm counter is the other obvious candidate. Management opinion, an ESG rating, and an industry pledge move when pushed; they are instruments at best. Viable anchors here: a measured harm trajectory (emissions, injuries, discharges) and a market return, each read per firm and per harm. *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* (Fig 2 :30)

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation: firm Γ, action a, harm trajectory h, indexed by slot (firm, harm) and ordered by time. It carries an ordered bond (`→`, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) because durability is a sequence, and a comparison bond (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87) because the short-term-profit test is a threshold against the current profit level. It is not symmetric: firm and harm are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** "Voluntary" versus "pressured" is a claim about the *source* of the action, not about its content. Two actions with identical harm trajectories can differ only in provenance (Fig 8 :50-51). So the distinguishing mark is ordered (cause before effect), not symmetric.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single firm's loop, the population of firms in an industry field, and the wider habitat (communities, ecosystems, future generations) that carries the harm. The membrane between them is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The firm loop** | decision makers, routines, the firm's Γ | actions, profit, residual reduction | yes: realized market return on the action | admitted |
| **D2 The harm's ground** | the physical and social trajectory: emissions, water, workers, communities | harm level, exposure, health | yes: a measurement or counter, a patient outcome (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D3 The pressure instruments** | regulators, consumers, investors, courts, auditors | price signals, mandates, capital access, liability | yes: a rule that can be violated, a purchase decision, a cost of capital (Fig 10 :19, Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D4 The verification / outreach frame** | expert readers | agreement and residual | yes: the ground, never agreement | admitted |
| **X1 Pure corporate benevolence as a free-floating property** | the firm's "will" | intent | **no** anchor independent of what the ground returns | not a domain yet |
| **X2 "Shareholder value" as sole cause** | owners | profit | measurable, but already inside D1/D3; it is the firm's objective link, not a separate habitat | absorbed |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that managers never act from conviction. It says only that "intent" cannot be the thing that settles a verdict about durability, because it moves when pushed.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: the harm is produced inside the firm loop and registered (or not) on the harm's ground, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D3 and D4 overlap: the pressure instrument is itself read by the verification frame, so that overlap is a second compound. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)
> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1 Reachability of the harm in the firm's loop.** Can the harm register as a live residual on a slot the firm actually ranges against? If not, no voluntary mint can start.
- **C2 Ground pressure on the room.** Is there a pressure that keeps the change from decaying into agreement and relabeling?

A firm failing only C1 and a firm failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure. They are different compounds and need different instruments. The political question "voluntary or regulated?" is a bundled way of asking about C2, and it hides C1 entirely.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the firm read the harm trajectory?) → 2 vocabulary (does its Γ have terms for what the harm affords?) → 3 objective (can it say what progress on the harm would count as, in its own terms?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link is the one that breaks: firms have precise terms for profit and coarse or absent terms for an unpriced harm. *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* (Fig 12 :115). And: *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."* (Fig 3 :29). Effort goes into more ESG reporting (above the break) rather than into an instrument for the harm (the missing middle link).

## 3. Findings

### F1: The firm's own anchor may not register the harm (decisive for C1)

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Fig 2 :29: **"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

A firm's loop ranges against the market return. If a harm is an externality, it is absent from that return, so there is no live positive residual on the harm slot inside the firm's frame. The mint pipeline's first guard is a product, not a checklist:

> **"the gap has real mass somewhere"** (Fig 5 :21)
> **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."** (Fig 5 :41)

So an absent voluntary response is *not* evidence of a broken firm. It is the expected output of a loop whose only metric cannot see the harm. **Mode:** `specified` for firms, `general` for the guard structure. **Triangulated:** Fig 1 :23 (only metric) and Fig 5 :21, :41 (support guard), low overlap. **Refuter:** a firm whose market return fully prices the harm (through liability, customer loss, or input cost) still showing zero response on the harm slot.

### F2: The harm sits three steps out, so first-order enumeration misses it

> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** (Fig 11 :52)
> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)

The firm enumerates customers and suppliers (first order). The harmed parties, the ecosystem, and future generations are second and third order. Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** The same supplier is a cost input for one residual and a conduit of harm for another. This is why a voluntary initiative scoped to direct suppliers misses the cascade. **Mode:** `general` for the cascade rule, `specified` for firms. **Triangulated:** Fig 11 :44 and :52 (habitat) with Fig 3 :24-27 (uninstrumented middle link). **Refuter:** a documented harm whose material effects are all first-order to the firm and still go unaddressed while profit falls.

### F3: Durability needs ground pressure, not agreement

> **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"** (Fig 7 :37)
> **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"** (Fig 7 :38)
> **"the ground's pressure on the room"** (Fig 7 :39)
> **"one long room: motion, no progress"** (Fig 7 :32)

A voluntary pledge circulated among firms is a handoff with high within-room agreement and zero ground pressure. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** And Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** with Fig 7 :12 **"same closure, handed along;"**. An industry that congratulates itself is one long room. Fig 7 :42: **"booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."** **Mode:** `general` for the two-factor rule, `specified` for the pledge case. **Triangulated:** Fig 7 :37-39 (pressure) and Fig 2 :35 (**"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**). **Refuter:** a durable, ground-measured harm reduction sustained after all external pressure and internal agreement activity is withdrawn.

### F4: Regulation is a convention that makes the bug statable; a pledge is only a declaration

Fig 10 :16-19: **"the convention does not fix the bug"**, **"it makes the bug statable"**, **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**, **"→ install what can be violated"**.

A mandate that can be violated gives the firm a live residual on the harm slot: violating it has a consequence, so the harm reaches the firm's ground. A voluntary declaration that cannot be violated does not. Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**; a convention used as a verdict is corrupted. Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**, with **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26) and **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :27). A firm can publish a perfect ESG report and have lost touch with the harm. **Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated:** Fig 10 :16-19 and Fig 10 :24-28 (convention vs channel), distinct panels. **Refuter:** a voluntary commitment with a violated-condition clause and a ground-measured penalty that produces the same durable reduction as a mandate, with identical measurement.

### F5: The named pressures are one kind of thing: an import from a differing closure

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"An import adds no atom to the world."** (Fig 6 :25)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)
> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** (Fig 8 :45)

Regulation, consumer pressure, and investor incentives are not three different mechanisms at the figure level. Each is a contact with a frame whose closure already contains the harm, and each makes the harm reachable inside the firm's loop without creating a new atom. This dissolves the question's "or": the three listed alternatives are three instances of one operator (import), differing in which closure supplies the contact. Fig 8 :29: **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"** is the condition under which that contact has surplus. **Mode:** `general` for import, `specified` for the three instances. **Triangulated:** Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :45 (wall moves on contact), low overlap between the reachability and the union figures. **Refuter:** a demonstrated change in the firm's harm slot produced purely by internal composition, with no new atom and no contact with a differing closure.

### F6: Voluntary and pressured actions are indistinguishable in content

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"** (Fig 8 :50)

Two firms can show the same emissions curve. In one case the firm reached it from its own resources; in the other the curve was imported under pressure. The harm trajectory cannot tell them apart. Only the provenance can. This is why a public claim of "we did it voluntarily" is not a finding about causation; it is a claim about a record. Fig 10 :62: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."** The honest package supplies the record, not the adjective. **Mode:** `general`. **Single-source** for the exact sentence (Fig 8), corroborated in effect by Fig 10's provenance rule. **Refuter:** a measurement that discriminates voluntary from pressured origin from the harm trajectory alone, with no record.

### F7: A "shape, not a name" limit governs the frequency claim

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** and Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

The figures give the *shape* of the condition (reachability plus pressure) but no cross-firm frequency. The question's word "generally" asks how often this happens in the world. No figure line governs a frequency, and no count was taken here. After a genuine re-census this is declared **`gap`**, not a hidden answer. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument per harm and market, not more framework text. **Mode:** `general` for the limit, `gap` for the frequency. **Triangulated:** Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134. **Refuter:** a figure line that directly predicts a cross-firm frequency, produced by re-census, would move this out of `gap`.

### F8: The world sources: one pressure pool, one low-overlap counterpoint

The business and organizational-sociology sources (Porter and van der Linde 1995; DiMaggio and Powell 1983; Zhang et al. 2024; Powell retrospective 2024) are one broad pressure/institution pool. Their agreement is one witness, not four. Their strongest common observation is that profitable harm reduction is often not taken up without a push: in the EPA Green Lights audit, *"nearly 80% of the projects offered paybacks within two years or less, and yet the companies considering them had not taken action"* (Porter and van der Linde, HBR 1995). That corroborates F1 and F3 at the ground level.

Ostrom 1990 is the low-overlap counterpoint. From self-organized common-pool resource cases: *"none of the success cases involved direct regulation by a centralized authority"* and, with equal force, *"No strong external pressures drive individuals toward positive solutions to such problems."* Those two sentences look contradictory only if "external pressure" means "government regulation". The framework resolves it: what the successful cases supply is exactly C1 and C2 in another form, namely enumerated boundaries, monitoring, graduated sanctions, collective choice, and shared norms (Ostrom's design principles). That is ground pressure plus reachability delivered by a community rather than a state. The conflict sends us back to census, and the census refines F5: the necessary condition is **contact with a differing closure that makes the harm reachable and keeps pressure on the room**, of which regulation, consumer pressure, investor incentives, and self-organized community governance are four instances. The figure verdict survives, sharpened. A conflict did not flip it; it removed a name ("regulation") from the shape and kept the function.

**Mode:** `specified` for the mapping, `gap` for any claim about which institution dominates in which sector. **Status:** corroboration (pressure pool) and one genuine low-overlap correction (Ostrom), recorded, not hidden.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented case in which a firm reduced a harm that its own market anchor did not register, with no import from a differing closure and no ground pressure, and the reduction was durable against a ground measurement. (Breaks C1 and C2.)
2. A documented case in which a voluntary pledge produced a durable, ground-measured reduction after internal agreement activity and all external pressure had ceased. (Breaks F3.)
3. A low-overlap source showing that self-organized governance of a shared harm is durable without monitoring or graduated sanctions. (Breaks the Fig 11 condition imported via Ostrom.)
4. Evidence that a harm trajectory alone, with no provenance record, discriminates a voluntary action from a pressured one. (Breaks F6.)
5. A figure re-census that yields a line governing cross-firm frequency. (Moves F7 out of `gap`.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or amended and the amendment is recorded here, not silently substituted. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape, not with a verdict about "voluntary versus regulated". The question is a union (Fig 9 :55), and the two listed alternatives are one operator (Fig 6 :23).
2. Ask, for any claimed success, two questions: was the harm reachable in the firm's own loop, and what ground pressure kept the room moving? (Fig 7 :37-39.)
3. Treat every "we chose to do this voluntarily" as a provenance claim requiring a record, not as a causal finding (Fig 8 :50, Fig 10 :62).
4. Refuse the word "generally" as a verdict. Report it as `gap` and name the instrument that would close it (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
5. Send the generator, not the recording: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52)

### Recursive derivation summary

1. **Does the harm register in the firm's loop?** Only if priced. Fig 1 :23, Fig 5 :21. `specified`.
2. **Is it first order to the firm?** Usually not; the cascade runs three steps out. Fig 11 :52. `general`.
3. **What makes the action durable?** Bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Fig 7 :37-39. `general`.
4. **What are regulation, consumers, investors?** One operator: import from a differing closure. Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45. `general`.
5. **Can we tell voluntary from pressured from the outcome?** No, only from the record. Fig 8 :50. `general`.
6. **Is regulation necessary?** Not as a name. The invariant is reachability plus pressure; Ostrom gives a non-state instance. `specified`, refined.
7. **Does it generally happen?** No figure governs a frequency. `gap`. Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and is sharper than the question's framing: a firm can voluntarily reduce a harm only when that harm is already reachable in its own loop, and the reduction is durable only when something keeps ground pressure on the room. Regulation, consumer pressure, and investor incentives are three names for the import that supplies both. They are not proven to be the only possible suppliers (Ostrom), and the word "generally" is a `gap`. Strains after re-census: **one named**, the naming of the pressure institution (F8), resolved by re-census into a function rather than a name; **none hidden**.
