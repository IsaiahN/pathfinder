# Solution BUS-0091: can firms voluntarily address profit-reducing harms?

**Question:** Can firms voluntarily address harms that reduce short-term profit, or do durable changes generally require regulation, consumer pressure, or investor incentives?

**Restated as an effect.** A firm currently imposes a harm H that is not fully priced in the return its own loop observes. One option is a unilateral, voluntary action that lowers H and lowers short-term profit; the other is a change driven by an external pressure (regulation, consumer pressure, investor incentives). What is being decided is what makes such a harm-reducing action *durable* rather than a one-off or a relabeling. The answer would look like a set of conditions, each with an instrument that could show its failure, and a statement of which condition each of the three external pressures supplies. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing.

**Source:** business.txt line 122

**Mode:** `specified` on the mechanism shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural figures, `gap` on the empirical frequency ("generally"). No measurement settles the frequency, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that external pressure is required.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* (Seat Map :163) Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. A public institutional submission route avoids both failures.
