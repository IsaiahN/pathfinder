# Expert verification packet: the Tether on voluntary corporate harm reduction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a business-ethics or CSR researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the frequency.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can firms voluntarily address harms that reduce short-term profit?" is a union of two conditions, not one question. First, a firm cannot initiate a reduction of a harm that does not register in the metric it ranges against; an unpriced externality produces no live residual, so no voluntary mint starts, and this is not a defect in the firm's decision machinery. Second, a reduction is durable only while something keeps ground pressure on the decision; agreement among firms is not pressure, and a pledge with no violable condition is a declaration rather than a ground. Regulation, consumer pressure and investor incentives are three names for one operation, contact with a frame whose closure already holds the harm, and self-organised community governance is a fourth. "Voluntary" is a claim about provenance, which a harm curve cannot show. Whether such change *generally* requires pressure is a `gap` that a census with a harm instrument would have to close.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5 and 11

- **Statement:** no live residual on the harm slot means no voluntary action, and the harm's material effects are usually second or third order to the firm.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :23, *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 5 :21, *"the gap has real mass somewhere"*; Fig 5 :41, *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."* Fig 11 :52, *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **Refuter:** a firm whose market return fully prices the harm still showing zero response on the harm slot; or a documented harm whose material effects are all first order to the firm and still go unaddressed while profit falls.

### C2: business-facing; checkable against case evidence

- **Statement:** a harm reduction decays once ground pressure is removed; a violable mandate and a non-violable pledge are different in kind, not in degree.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :37-39, *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"*, *"the ground's pressure on the room"*; Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 10 :16-19, *"the convention does not fix the bug"*, *"it makes the bug statable"*, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*, *"→ install what can be violated"*.
- **What to check:** find a firm whose harm trajectory was tracked before, during and after a pressure event (a mandate, a campaign, a divestment). Does the residual rise again when the pressure goes? Then compare a pledge with a violated-condition clause against one without.
- **Refuter:** a durable, ground-measured reduction sustained after all external pressure and internal agreement activity have ceased; or a non-violable pledge that produces the same durable reduction as a mandate under identical measurement.

### C3: structural; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the three named pressures are one operator (import from a differing closure), and they are a functional group at minimum; "voluntary" versus "pressured" is a provenance distinction, not an outcome distinction. No figure line governs a cross-firm frequency.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :50, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*; Fig 8 :45, *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated change in the firm's harm slot produced purely by internal composition with no contact with a differing closure; or a measurement that discriminates voluntary from pressured origin from the harm trajectory alone; or a figure re-census that yields a frequency line.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that firms never act from conviction. The claim is about when the harm can register, not who initiates.
- Not that regulation is always required. Self-organised community governance supplies the same two conditions without a state; see the Ostrom source in §4.
- Not that ESG disclosure, a pledge, or a rating is a harm reduction. *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* (Fig 10 :18)
- Not that the three pressures are literally figure isomers. They are a functional group; the isomer reading is `specified` and strained on the bond type (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).
- Not `grounded`, and not a verdict on "generally". That is declared `gap`.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement.

1. A harm counter with a public measurement protocol per firm and per harm, so reachability is observed rather than asserted.
2. A pressure-removal event study tracking whether the harm residual rises again when the pressure ends.
3. A provenance record per claimed voluntary action: who decided, under what constraint, with what violated condition attached.
4. For "generally": a census of firms and harms using those instruments. Until then, `gap`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :23, Fig 5 :21 and :41, Fig 7 :37-39, Fig 10 :16-19 and Fig 8 :50. Six short passages.
2. Name one harm case where the reduction survived the removal of pressure, or say why none is known.
3. Decide whether the three pressures share the functional group in §2 C3, and whether the Ostrom case belongs in the same set.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not send a statement of agreement as corroboration; the framework disqualifies it (Seat Map :163, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*).
