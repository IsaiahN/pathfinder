# The Tether's answer on voluntary harm reduction

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural figures, `gap` on the empirical frequency.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm imposes a harm that reduces the profit it would otherwise report. The question asks whether it can stop on its own, or whether durable change generally needs regulation, consumer pressure, or investor incentives. The contact is a **union compound** of two conditions, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). It has its own loop and its own ground. R is indexed per firm and per harm, never averaged into an industry score (Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape

1. **A firm cannot voluntarily address a harm its own anchor does not register.** The market is a named anchor (Fig 2 :29: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*) and *"The ground enters as the only metric."* (Fig 1 :23). An unpriced externality produces no live residual on the harm slot, so the mint never starts. Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."* The harm is usually second or third order to the firm anyway (Fig 11 :52).
2. **Durability needs ground pressure as well as a faithful reading.** Fig 7 :37-39: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"*, *"the ground's pressure on the room"*. Without the second factor an initiative is *"one long room: motion, no progress"* (Fig 7 :32) and *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13). An industry pledge is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35).
3. **A mandate is a violable convention; a pledge is a declaration.** Fig 10 :16-19: *"the convention does not fix the bug"*, *"it makes the bug statable"*, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*, *"→ install what can be violated"*. A rule that can be violated puts the harm on the firm's objective; a declaration that cannot be violated does not. A disclosure is frame-internal: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27)
4. **The three named pressures are one operator under three names.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Regulation, consumer pressure and investor incentives are each a contact with a closure that already holds the harm. *"An import adds no atom to the world."* (Fig 6 :25) They do not create the harm, they make it reachable. Their union has surplus because the closures differ (Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*).
5. **"Voluntary" is a provenance claim, not an observable.** Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Two firms can show the same curve for different reasons. The curve cannot decide the cause; only the record can.
6. **The institution is not named by the framework.** Self-organised community governance supplies the same two conditions without a state, so "regulation" is not a name the framework returns. The four-membered atom set is state, customer, capital, community. Which member applies is a provenanced binding from the institutional frame, recorded as such.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that firms never act from conviction. C1 says the harm must be reachable; it does not say who initiates the action.
- Not that regulation is always required. Ostrom's cases supply reachability and pressure through monitoring and sanctions without central authority; the invariant is the function, not the institution.
- Not that the word "generally" has been answered. That is a frequency over firms and harms; no figure governs it and no count was taken. It is declared `gap`.
- Not that ESG disclosure, a pledge, or a rating is a harm reduction. A declared meaning is not a grounded one.
- Not that the three pressures are literally figure isomers. They are a functional group at minimum; the isomer reading is `specified` and strained on the bond type (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would refute it

**The ground, not agreement.** The instruments that would close the shape:

- A harm counter with a public measurement protocol per firm and per harm, so reachability can be observed rather than asserted (Fig 2 :29).
- An event study around the removal of a pressure (a mandate expiring, a contract ending, a campaign fading) that tracks whether the harm residual rises again. This measures C2 directly.
- A provenance record for each claimed "voluntary" action, stating who decided and under what constraint, so Fig 8 :50 can be applied.
- For the frequency question: a census of firms and harms with those instruments applied. Until it exists, "generally" remains `gap`.

**Pre-registered falsifiers** (written before this conclusion, see `01` §5):

1. A durable, ground-measured reduction where the harm was invisible to the firm's market anchor and no outside contact or pressure was present.
2. A durable reduction sustained after all external pressure and internal agreement activity stopped.
3. Evidence that a self-organised arrangement stayed durable without monitoring or graduated sanctions.
4. A measurement that tells a voluntary action from a pressured one from the harm trajectory alone, with no provenance.
5. A figure re-census yielding a line that governs cross-firm frequency.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, frequency unreached.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The framework can say what any durable answer must look like and what would refute it. It cannot say how often the world supplies those conditions. The stop is recorded so a later reader can locate the limit rather than feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "voluntary or regulated?" as a choice between two mechanisms. It splits the question into two conditions. A firm cannot reduce a harm it cannot register: if the harm is not in the metric the firm ranges against, there is no live signal to act on. And a reduction does not persist unless something keeps pressure on the decision, because agreement among firms is not pressure. Regulation, consumer pressure and investor incentives are three names for the same operation, contact with a frame that already holds the harm and makes it reachable; self-organised community governance can do it too, so no single institution is the answer. "Voluntary" describes where the action came from, which the harm curve cannot show, so it needs a provenance record. What the framework returns is that shape, its five written refuters, and the instruments that would close it. Whether durable change *generally* requires pressure is an open measurement, not a finding.
