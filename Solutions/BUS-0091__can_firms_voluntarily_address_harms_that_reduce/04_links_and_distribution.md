## Wider literature actually opened (secondary corroboration only)

These are not contact points. They are the sources read for the WATSON world section, with what each contributes.

1. **Porter and van der Linde, "Green and Competitive: Ending the Stalemate", Harvard Business Review, 1995.** Full text retrieved via http://www.kentlundgren.se/artiklar/vetenskapliga/Porter_Linde_1995.pdf this session; publisher record at https://hbr.org/1995/09/green-and-competitive-ending-the-stalemate. Key line: *"Our research on competitiveness highlights the role that outside pressure plays in motivating companies to innovate."* Also the EPA Green Lights audit: *"nearly 80% of the projects offered paybacks within two years or less, and yet the companies considering them had not taken action."* This is the cleanest world-level statement of the reachability and pressure conditions. It explicitly asks why regulation is needed when innovation can be profitable, and answers with incomplete information and organizational inertia. **Corroborates F1 and F3.**
2. **Porter and van der Linde, "Toward a New Conception of the Environment-Competitiveness Relationship", Journal of Economic Perspectives 9(4), 1995.** Abstract opened at https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fjep.9.4.97. *"properly crafted environmental standards can trigger innovation offsets, allowing companies to improve their resource productivity."* Same evidence pool as item 1. **Corroborates F5 at the level of function, not name.**
3. **DiMaggio and Powell, "The Iron Cage Revisited: Institutional Isomorphism and Collective Rationality in Organizational Fields", American Sociological Review 48(2), 1983.** Full text retrieved via https://woodypowell.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DiMaggioPowellThe-Iron-Cage-Revisited1983.pdf. *"manufacturers adopt new pollution control technologies to conform to environmental regulations"*; and *"a threshold is reached beyond which adoption provides legitimacy rather than improves performance"*. **Corroborates F3 and F4**, especially the declaration-versus-ground distinction. Same broad pressure/institution pool as items 1 and 2, so one witness.
4. **Powell and DiMaggio retrospective, Stanford Graduate School of Business, 2024.** Opened at https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/unlocking-iron-cage-corporate-conformity. Describes coercive, mimetic and normative isomorphism in firms. Practical restatement of item 3, not an independent witness.
5. **Zhang, Zhu, Li and Yan, "Revisiting the Porter hypothesis: a multi-country meta-analysis...", Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 11:232, 2024.** Opened at https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-02671-9. *"command and control regulation had shown its highest consistency and effectiveness in driving green innovation, whereas voluntary regulation had the highest level of flexibility among all regulatory measures."* A 58-paper synthesis; **corroborates F3** (pressure works) while refusing a simple "one institution wins" claim.
6. **Ostrom, "Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action", Cambridge University Press, 1990.** Excerpts opened at http://publiccommons.ca/documents/398. Two lines in tension: *"none of the success cases involved direct regulation by a centralized authority"* and *"No strong external pressures drive individuals toward positive solutions to such problems."* With her design principles (boundaries, congruence, collective choice, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution, minimal recognition of rights, nested enterprises), this resolves into the figure invariant: reachability plus pressure, delivered by a community. **Low-overlap correction to F5**, recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F8.

Overlap note: items 1 through 5 are one broad institutional/economic evidence pool. Items 1 and 2 share authors. Only item 6 comes from a genuinely different field (common-pool resource institutional analysis), and its agreement with the figures is about function, not about the framework's wording.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the two conditions and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the articles.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Validate the output against a ground, **(2)** only if curious, read the theory that produced it, **(3)** use the formula recursively on other problems.

- Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- Stage 2 is opt-in by construction. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown and never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted: Fig 4 :22. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and state the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1-3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The shape in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is stated with its refuter so that it can fail on contact. Nothing here is a claim that has been settled by a measurement.
