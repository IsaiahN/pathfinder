# The Tether's answer on inequality of opportunity versus inequality of outcomes

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the grounded ranking.** Not `grounded`. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two problems that must be answered separately (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). One is a ranking, "matters more". The other is a separability claim, "can one be reduced without the other". A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it is a specific compound (Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."). Each half has its own status.

## 2. The answer, per half

### Half A: "matters more" is `gap`

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

No such anchor for priority appears in the figures or in the world sources read. A welfare function or a majority preference is a convention authored by a seat, and Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". The framework's limit is explicit: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself", and Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". So the honest report is `gap`, not "opportunity wins" and not "outcomes win". The Gatsby correlation is a filter for where to look, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

### Half B: "can one be reduced without the other?" is a union with asymmetric overlap

1. **Opportunity is upstream of outcome.** Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." The same figure: "a generator crosses, an instance does not". Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." In structural terms the habitat governs what the readout can become. This is a dependency order, not a moral ranking.
2. **The two fields overlap.** Circumstances partly produce outcomes, and the same persons sit in both distributions. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A lever on one clears only the region where that condition holds alone; the intersection stands.
3. **The overlap is asymmetric.** Opportunity inequality is a component of outcome inequality under the standard decomposition, so reducing the former reduces the latter; reducing the latter through the effort component can leave the former unchanged. This is world-sourced (Roemer) and `specified` as a causal lever claim.
4. **Aggregation hides the signal.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single outcome-inequality index can be near zero while one slot carries a large opportunity residual. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".
5. **The comparison is well-typed but ungrounded.** Two measured distributions can be compared in type (Fig 12 comparison), but the anchor a verdict requires is missing. Naming "matters more" exposes it as a goal, not a measurement.

**Answer in one sentence:** opportunity is the generator and sits upstream of the outcome readout, the two overlap as a union so that a single lever clears only the non-overlap region, and the requested ranking cannot be settled without an anchor that does not yet exist.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that opportunity "matters more" morally. The dependency order is structural, not normative.
- Not that outcome inequality is irrelevant. The readout is what a ground can measure, and an outcome index is a legitimate reporting summary; it is just disqualified as a guide to where to look (Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.").
- Not that the Gatsby curve settles anything. It is evidence of coupling, and it has a live heterogeneity objection in the literature.
- Not that the decomposition is causally identified. The asymmetry is a prediction of the standard construction, not a measured lever effect here.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument at this budget returns the anchor or the lever result.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

1. **An anchor for priority:** a quantity that ranks interventions on the two distributions, does not move when either side pushes on it, and is not authored by the ranker. That closes F1 and moves "matters more" out of `gap`.
2. **A lever experiment:** move circumstances while holding the outcome distribution fixed and read downstream mobility; or move the effort component and read opportunity inequality. This decides F2 and F5.
3. **A partition measurement:** opportunity and outcome conditions with zero intersection in every slot. That kills the union reading (F4).
4. **A source-discriminating instrument:** from *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; a spectrometer resolved the case, not an argument. Here the analogous instrument would distinguish an equal-outcomes distribution produced by a fair process from one produced by compensation. Until it exists, the source is not nameable from the reading.

## 5. The honest stop

Unreached, not unreachable in principle. The figures return a shape: dependency order, union overlap, aggregation warning. They do not return a ranking. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The choice of which inequality to prioritize is an interest in the outcome; it is declared here rather than presented as neutral. The record stops at the limit of the available instruments and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. Standing falsifiers

The pre-registered set in `01` §5 applies in full. The three shortest forms:

1. an invariant, non-authored anchor for "matters more" is produced;
2. a habitat substitution restructures opportunity with outcomes held fixed and no downstream change;
3. the two conditions are measured to have zero intersection, with a one-lever result leaving the other untouched.

Any one fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the derivation is re-censused.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank inequality of opportunity above inequality of outcomes, and it does not claim to. It says that opportunity is the generator and outcomes are the readout, so the generator sits upstream in dependency order; that the two are not independent levers but a union with an intersection, so reducing one clears only the region where the other does not also bind; and that a single outcome-inequality index hides the slot-level signal where the live residual sits, because a global near-zero can coexist with one live slot. The ranking "matters more" is a comparison with no anchor that would settle it, so it is reported as a gap rather than filled with a welfare function or a cross-country correlation. What the framework offers is that shape, the asymmetry of the overlap, and falsifiers written in advance. The stop is honest and the lineage is kept until a ground, not an agreement, moves it.
