## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The Gatsby curve couples the two inequalities but does not rank them.** The Wikipedia synthesis states that "The GGC suggests that a society can simultaneously pursue equality of opportunity (measured by mobility) and equality of outcomes (measured by cross-section differences)." That is evidence for the union reading in F4, not for a priority verdict. The same page records Mankiw's objection that the correlation may be an artefact of heterogeneity, which is a live conflict. Conflicts go back to the census, not into the verdict column.
2. **Goodhart's law disqualifies the outcome index as a target.** "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" and "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." This is the world-side parallel to Fig 1 :27, "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces", and to Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Both findings are secondary corroboration. They share no author with the figures, which makes their agreement weakly independent, but neither supplies the missing anchor. They cannot close F1.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** (exemplar quotation): "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what would count as a failure before giving them the essays.
- **Fig 2 :35**: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Do not select recipients for likely agreement.
- **Fig 10 :59**: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." State the stake in choosing a priority; do not present it as neutral.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the structural claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure and whether they know of an anchor that settles the ranking; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) record their verdict on stage 1 before any theory is shown.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, output only.** Send the claim, the mode and the pre-registered refuter. The reader checks it without holding the framework's interiors. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** Only if the reader is curious, offer the figure text, the Tether answer and the expert packet. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, method reuse.** Offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each reader writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a method; the framework's own Direction rule says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 claim survives. The three refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are the test. If one fires, send the retraction instead of the packet.
