**Subject:** Check one claim about inequality of opportunity before reading any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing with one specific claim, not a theory. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the framework behind it first. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "inequality of opportunity matters more than inequality of outcomes" cannot be answered as a measurement with the vocabulary currently in the question. What can be answered is structural. Opportunity is the generator, outcomes are the readout. A change that alters only the readout, leaving the opportunity habitat untouched, is a change that does not touch what produces the distribution. The two inequalities are also not independent levers: they overlap, because the same people are in both and circumstances partly produce outcomes. A lever that reduces one clears only the region where that condition holds alone. The intersection stands.
>
> **The refuter, written before this email:** I would withdraw the claim if any of the following holds:
> 1. an anchor is produced that ranks interventions on opportunity and on outcomes, does not change its answer when either side pushes on it, and is not chosen by the person doing the ranking;
> 2. a habitat substitution that restructures opportunity while holding outcomes fixed leaves all downstream mobility dynamics unchanged;
> 3. the opportunity condition and the outcome condition are measured to have zero intersection in every slot, and a one-lever reduction leaves the other measure unchanged.
>
> The first item is why I do not claim opportunity "matters more" in a grounded sense. I do not have that anchor. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" is the figure's rule, so I report the gap rather than filling it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the cross-country correlation between income inequality and mobility (the Great Gatsby curve) settles the ranking. It is evidence that the two are coupled, not evidence that either should be prioritized. The literature also contains a serious objection that the pattern is an artefact of group heterogeneity. A correlation cannot be the verdict.
>
> If a one-line "this fails because..." comes back, that is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the derivation is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - The Tether answer for this question: `07_the_tether_answer.md` in the same folder as this draft
