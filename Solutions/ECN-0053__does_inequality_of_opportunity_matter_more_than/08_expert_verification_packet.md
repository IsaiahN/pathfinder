# Expert verification packet — the Tether on inequality of opportunity versus outcomes

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in equality of opportunity can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the grounded ranking.** No measurement at this budget settles the ranking. Not `grounded`.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Opportunity is the generator and outcomes are the readout, so opportunity sits upstream of outcome in dependency order. The two are not independent levers. They form a union with an intersection, because the same people sit in both and circumstances partly produce outcomes; a lever on one clears only the region where the other condition does not also bind. Under the standard decomposition the overlap is asymmetric: the opportunity component is inside the outcome total, so reducing it reduces total inequality, while reducing the outcome total through the effort component can leave opportunity inequality unchanged. A single aggregate outcome index hides slot-level signal, because a global near-zero is compatible with one live slot residual. The ranking "matters more" is a comparison with no invariant anchor, so it is reported as `gap`, not answered by a welfare function or a cross-country correlation. Falsifiers were written before this packet.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9, 10 and 11

- **Statement:** opportunity is upstream of outcome; the two form a union with intersection; an outcome-only intervention does not change contact.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." and "a generator crosses, an instance does not"
  - Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
  - Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
  - Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
- **Refuter:** a habitat substitution that restructures opportunity while holding the outcome distribution fixed, with all downstream mobility dynamics unchanged; or a measured zero intersection between the two conditions in every slot.

### C2 — economics-facing; checkable against the inequality-of-opportunity literature

- **Statement:** under the Roemer decomposition, the opportunity component is a component of total outcome inequality, so the two levers are asymmetric. Reducing the opportunity component reduces the total; reducing the total through the effort component need not reduce the opportunity component.
- **What to check:** is that the right reading of the decomposition? Does the literature treat circumstances and effort as separately variable, or are interaction terms dominant enough that no such decomposition is identified?
- **Refuter:** a decomposition with dominant non-separable circumstance/effort interactions, so neither component can be reduced independently. That kills the asymmetry claim and leaves the weaker union claim standing.

### C3 — framework-internal type check, with an honest gap

- **Statement:** "matters more" is a comparison (`⋛`) over two measured indices, so it is well-typed, but it is ungrounded because no invariant anchor exists.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
  - Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
  - Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
  - Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
  - Operators table: "a threshold relation between two quantities"
- **Refuter:** produce an anchor that returns a determinate ordering of the two levers, does not change its answer when either side pushes on it, and is not authored by the person doing the ranking. If it fires, `gap` closes. If it is a declared welfare function, it is a convention and does not fire.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that opportunity matters more morally. The dependency order is structural.
- Not that outcome inequality is irrelevant. It is the measurable readout and a legitimate reporting summary; it is disqualified only as a self-scored ground (Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.").
- Not that the Gatsby curve settles the ranking. It is a filter for where to look and has a live heterogeneity objection in the literature.
- Not that the decomposition is causally identified here. The asymmetry is a prediction of the standard construction.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this packet is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the framework names which generator is at work in a particular country. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground, not an agreement. Three items, in increasing order of cost:

1. An invariant anchor for "matters more" (closes C3 and the F1 gap).
2. A lever experiment on circumstances or effort with the other measure read after (decides C1 and C2).
3. A source-discriminating instrument, in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and a spectrometer, not an argument, resolved that case. Here the analogue would distinguish equal outcomes produced by a fair process from equal outcomes produced by compensation.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :13, Fig 9 :55, Fig 10 :68 and Fig 11 :40, four short passages. Decide whether C1's structural reading is faithful to the figures.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of the inequality-of-opportunity decomposition: is the asymmetry real, or do interactions make the components non-separable?
3. Ask yourself one question: what would you count as an anchor that settles "matters more" and does not move when either side pushes on it? If you have one, C3 is refuted and the gap closes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
