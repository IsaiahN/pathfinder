## Restating the question as an effect

Two distributions sit over the same population of slots: realized outcomes (O) and the opportunity or circumstance field that precedes action (C). The question asks (a) for an ordered comparison between them, "matters more", which is a comparison bond over a common currency, and (b) whether manipulating one leaves the other unchanged, a separability question. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a verdict on the comparison plus a separability result, each with a named anchor that does not move when either distribution changes. That binding lets the sought-for shape accrue a residual like anything else (Formula step "Direction"). The honest result below is that (a) has no nameable anchor and is therefore `gap`, while (b) resolves into a union compound with an asymmetric overlap.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with a position on inequality.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
2. **Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163 (quoted in the exemplar): "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A list of recipients chosen because they already favour one inequality over the other is a convention, not corroboration.
3. **A stake in the ranking must be declared, not hidden.** Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Choosing which inequality to prioritize is an interest in the outcome. It is declared in `09_watson_answer.md`, not presented as neutral.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
