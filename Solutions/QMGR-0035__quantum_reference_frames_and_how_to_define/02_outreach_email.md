# Outreach draft: check the shape before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about quantum reference frames, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** in a spacetime with no fixed background, an observable cannot be a field value at a coordinate point, because that value changes under the symmetry. What survives is a shape with three parts. (1) An anchor that settles the reading and does not change its answer when the frame changes. (2) A quantity indexed per slot and defined as a relation between at least two physical systems, so it is invariant under the symmetry. (3) A transform between frame descriptions that names the pose of the reference system. The smooth background is not one of the primitives; it is a derivative, like smoothness over space and time. The choice of clock, group or relational coordinate is left open and recorded as a choice, not presented as a result.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have the unique observable for quantum gravity, that the framework derives general relativity, that gauge fixing settles a physical value, or that it supplies the transformation law for a frame in a superposition of poses. That last item is a declared `gap`. The figure set gives the frame transform a precondition (a pose) and no instrument that reads a quantum pose, so the honest answer is "unreachable" rather than a recipe.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a local coordinate-dependent field value that settles a measurement with no reference subsystem;
> 2. a derivation of the continuum from the other substrate terms that makes it a seventh primitive;
> 3. a gauge convention that changes the value of a properly constructed relational observable while the physical situation stays fixed;
> 4. a ground measurement in which a frame in superposed poses returns a value that cannot be written as any superposition of the held transforms.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, canonical local extract: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md` in the Pathfinder repository.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Giacomini, Castro-Ruiz and Brukner, "Quantum mechanics and the covariance of physical laws in quantum reference frames", Nature Communications 10, 494 (2019): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-08155-0
> - Tambornino, "Relational Observables in Gravity: a Review" (2012): https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0740
> - Vanrietvelde, Höhn, Giacomini and Castro-Ruiz, perspective-neutral QRF framework (2019): https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00556
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
