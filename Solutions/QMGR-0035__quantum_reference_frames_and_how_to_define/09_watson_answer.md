## WORLD (secondary, each source opened)

World sources are corroboration only. Most are in one QRF evidence pool; overlap is declared.

- **Quantum foundations / QRF formalism.** Giacomini, Castro-Ruiz and Brukner, Nature Communications 10, 494 (2019). Quote: "we introduce a general method to quantise reference frame transformations, which generalises the usual reference frame transformation to a 'superposition of coordinate transformations'". Predicts states and observables defined relative to a chosen frame, with no external absolute frame; predicts that superposition is frame dependent. Corroborates the relational shape and the fact that the quantum pose case is nontrivial. Overlap: core QRF pool.
- **Gravity / relational observables.** Tambornino, "Relational Observables in Gravity: a Review", SIGMA / arXiv 1109.0740 (2012). Quote: "no preferred notion of external time". Predicts that local observables are relations between dynamical fields and that de-parameterisable clock models reintroduce a preferred frame when the clock is classical. Corroborates the anchor and convention distinction. Overlap: gravity pool, low with the quantum-information sources.
- **Quantum gravity / quantum information.** Vanrietvelde, Höhn, Giacomini and Castro-Ruiz, arXiv 1809.00556 (2019). Quote: "physics is purely relational". Predicts that taking a frame perspective is a gauge fixing and that global perspectives generally fail. Corroborates Fig 10 :47 and the pose precondition. Overlap: same core QRF pool as Giacomini et al., so one witness, not two.
- **Mathematical physics / symmetry groups.** de la Hamette and Galley, arXiv 2004.14292 (2020). Quote: "states are defined to be relative to one of the systems". Predicts a coherent change-of-frame operator and notes that diffeomorphism groups are generally not locally compact. Corroborates the bond shape and the `gap` at the diffeomorphism boundary. Overlap: same QRF pool.
- **Mathematical physics / measurement theory.** Loveridge, Miyadera and Busch, Foundations of Physics 48, 135 (2018). Quote: "certain quantities require two systems for their definition". Predicts that absolute quantities approximate relative ones only when the reference is suitably localised. Corroborates the two-system arity and supplies the localisation instrument of F6. Overlap: partly independent of the transformation-focused QRF papers.
- **Philosophy of physics / observation.** Adlam, Journal for General Philosophy of Science 57, 285 (2026, online 2025). Quote: "the way in which quantum reference frames are standardly described does not give us adequate resources to model agency". Predicts that relational observables alone do not connect to observation without a schematised observer. Corroborates the need for a ground in D3; not a conflict with the figure verdict. Overlap: philosophy pool, low with physics sources.
- **Animal behaviour / spatial cognition, low overlap.** Review on dissociating spatial strategies, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews (2021), and Nature Communications (2025) on egocentric and allocentric representations in rodent medial entorhinal cortex. Quote: "a reciprocal transformation between self-centered ('egocentric') and allocentric coordinate systems is essential for spatial navigation". Predicts that a frame transform presupposes an oriented, located observer. Corroborates the shape of the Symbols table `F` only; carries no weight on quantum gravity. Overlap: genuinely low, different field and evidence.
- **Conflict, partial read.** Lake and Miller, "Quantum reference frames, revisited", arXiv 2312.03811 (2023), abstract obtained via search integration after a direct PDF fetch returned an unsupported content type; marked partial with reduced weight. Quote: "our QRF formalism pre-supposes the existence of a classical spacetime background". This conflicts with a naive reading of "no background at all". ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the unique recipe does not. A fixed background fails the anchor test and is a convention that must not decide readings. An observable is a per-slot bond between at least two physical systems, invariant under the symmetry and stated relative to a named reference reading. The smooth continuum is a derivative, not a primitive. The frame transform requires a pose, and the transform between quantum frames in superposed poses is a declared `gap` that no figure line closes. Clock, group and relational coordinate are provenanced bindings. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result, with `general` structural shape and `gap` at the pose transform.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode labelled, states four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage of bindings, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a universal recipe instead of a shape with a refuter. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no physics is overwritten. The claimed bindings are removable without touching the shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that reads the pose of a quantum reference system, or a measurement in superposed-pose frames returning a relational value not writable as any superposition of held transforms, would close or break the `gap`. A derivation of the continuum as a seventh primitive, a local coordinate-dependent quantity settling a measurement with no reference, a gauge convention changing a relational value, or an anchor that moves when frames push on it would break the shape. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
