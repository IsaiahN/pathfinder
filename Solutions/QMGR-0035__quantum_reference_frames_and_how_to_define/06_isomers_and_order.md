### Q1 Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a clock or reference system?

### Q2 What is the structure of a legitimate observable?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- A field value at a coordinate is an ingredient. A relational observable is a typed comparison bond between a system quantity and a reference quantity, evaluated where the reference reads a chosen value. The bond carries the invariance; the two ingredients alone do not.
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two observables over the same system and clock but with different relational coordinates are isomers in this sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Over the full shared atom set the isomer reading is real; as written with different clocks they share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124 *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*) rather than the full atom set.
- **Falsifier written before the run:** if a relational observable is fully determined by its ingredient list with no bond information, or if two choices of relational coordinate over the same atoms produce the same substance, the bond claim fails.

### Q3 In what order must the parts be built?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** The ordered links for this question:

1. **Perceive / describe R in effect terms.** Two symmetry-related descriptions disagree on a reading. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
2. **Vocabulary / name what the situation affords.** A reference subsystem affords a pose and a reading; the system affords a quantity. Without terms for these affordances there is no objective to state (Fig 3 :6-11).
3. **Objective / say what counts as progress.** Progress is a residual that stops registering against the ground, not a term count (Formula step 8: *"IT IS THE REACH THAT DRIVES THE LOOP, NOT THE LIBRARY."*).
4. **Anchor test.** Does the chosen reference change its answer when frames push on it? Fig 2 :29-30. If yes, it is a frame, not the anchor.
5. **Construct the relational bond** (Q2), then **name the pose** required by `F` (Symbols table), then **attempt the transform**.
6. **Settle.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*

The link most likely to break is 2 to 3, where a physicist can name the ingredients (system, clock, group) but has no instrument saying what would count as a solved observable. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** That is exactly the declared `gap` F5/F6 of `01`.

### Q4 Is the egocentric/allocentric transform a usable analogue?

- The Symbols table defines `F` as *"The map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale"* and says it *"Cannot be run without a pose."*
- Low-overlap world source: a 2021 animal-navigation review reports that allocentric (observer-independent) and egocentric (observer-dependent) frames are both available to animals and often go unaccounted for, and that a coordinate transformation is needed to relate them (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763421001378).
- A 2025 Nature Communications paper reports both egocentric and allocentric spatial representations in rodent medial entorhinal cortex and states that *"a reciprocal transformation between self-centered ('egocentric') and allocentric coordinate systems is essential for spatial navigation"* (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54699-9).
- These corroborate the **shape** of `F`: a transform between frames is needed, and it presupposes an oriented, located observer (a pose). They do not corroborate any quantum result and carry no weight on the physics claim. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* This is a genuinely low-overlap witness for the shape only.
- **Falsifier:** an animal-navigation result showing reliable allocentric behaviour with no transformation from the egocentric sensory input and no pose-like anchor would weaken the analogue. It would not refute the figure claim, because the figure claim is primary.

### Q5 Is the smooth background an isomer of the relational description?

No. Fig 13 :43: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."** A smooth background is a derivative left by a composition, not an arrangement of the same atoms at the same level as a relational observable. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* The relation between background language and relational language is a **binding between levels**, to be handled by `T_A` and `T_E` with a measured round-trip gap `R_T`, not by the within-scale `F` and not by an isomer claim. Misreading this is the strain recorded and dissolved in `01` section 6.

## CONCLUSION

A legitimate observable is a priced bond between at least two physical systems plus a reference reading, indexed per slot; choices over the same atoms differing only in relational coordinate stand as isomers or functional-group members. The answer must be built in dependency order: residual, affordances, objective, anchor test, relational bond, pose, transform, settlement. The egocentric/allocentric literature is a genuinely low-overlap analogue for the pose precondition of `F`, not evidence for quantum gravity. All of this is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
