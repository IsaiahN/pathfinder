# Settings as isomers: structure, order and convergence

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names four settings plus an open slot. Are they four instances of one thing (so a single lever could decide), or different arrangements that may reach the same outcome (so one lever clears only the region where a single condition holds)? Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's definition, are the settings **isomers**; (2) is the union a partition or a compound requiring unbundling?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the settings isomers under Fig 12?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Write the candidate bundles over the shared condition set {water, CHNOPS, energy, mineral surface, concentration, cycling, duration, UV shielding}:

- Submarine alkaline vent: water plus redox/pH gradient plus Fe(Ni)S surface plus mineral pores. Cycling is continuous flow, not wet-dry. UV shielded by water column.
- Terrestrial hot spring or pond: water plus wet-dry cycling plus silica/metal-sulfide surface plus evaporation/concentration. UV exposure is a hazard that mineral layers mitigate (Mulkidjanian et al. 2012; Omran and Pasek 2020).
- Shallow alkaline vent: water plus vent minerals *and* wet-dry cycling plus salt/freshwater influx (Barge and Price 2022). It is a compound of the first two, not a third independent kind.
- Ice / eutectic brine: water plus freeze-thaw concentration plus low temperature. Westall et al. (2023) list ice among aqueous microenvironments.

Under Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers**: same condition atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour and different refuters. As written in the question they share most atoms and differ in the bond pattern (which condition is doing the concentration, which is supplying the gradient). The stronger claim, that they are functional-group variants (Fig 12 :124) with the group "gradient + network + cycle" and different qualifiers, is the weaker and safer reading.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one setting can be written without a condition that another requires, and that condition turns out to be necessary for crossing the threshold, the isomer claim fails and the settings are a partition by that condition instead.

### Q2 - Is the union a partition, a union, or a compound?

Fig 9 :54-58:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Applied here: shallow vents are a compound (vent plus wet-dry cycling), not a harder vent and not a harder pond. Ice-brine settings may couple freeze concentration to mineral catalysis, another compound. So the candidate list is a union with at least two internal compounds. Unbundling means asking, for each candidate, which of the three necessary conditions (gradient, network, cycle) each component supplies, instead of treating "vent" or "pond" as an indivisible lever.

Fig 9 :56 says a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. That is exactly the failure mode of a campaign that argues only "wet-dry cycling is essential" or only "proton gradients are essential."

**Falsifier:** exhibit a single necessary-and-sufficient condition present in exactly one candidate and absent in all others. Then the union collapses to a partition and the unbundling step is unnecessary.

### Q3 - What order, if any, is licensed?

Fig 12 :49 and the Operators table define sequence as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, an ordered bond. Does the derivation license an order among settings?

No. Fig 13 :121 says *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Convergence is not sequence. Imposing "vents first, then ponds" would import an order the figures disclaim, the same error the exemplar found in its own chain (its F3). The only order licensed is the **dependency** order of the investigation, not of the events: describe residual (Fig 9 :27-28), enumerate habitat (Fig 11 :43), test conditions (Fig 13 :22, :24; Fig 12 :121), then let the ground decide (Fig 9 :42).

Geologically, there is a proposed order *within* a setting: wetting and drying repeatedly, or flow and precipitation in a vent chimney. That is habitat chemistry, not a figure-derived composition, and it is recorded as an import (Mulkidjanian et al. 2012; Barge and Price 2022).

### Q4 - Convergence and the one-site inference

Fig 8 :50:

> **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

Fig 13 :121:

> **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

Omran and Pasek (2020), opened 2026-11-23: *"If life originated only once, then we must choose between these two environments; however, there is no reason to assume life emerged only once."* Their section 2.2 notes that extensive horizontal gene transfer in the LUCA epoch undermines the naive idea that LUCA originated once. The chemical precedent is in Fig 12 :97-98 itself: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."*

Consequence: agreement among the early biota on a vent-like metabolism is a reading (metamerism), not a source. Several settings can converge on the same metabolic core. This is triangulated across Fig 13, Fig 8 and an independent world source (low overlap), so the convergence claim is stronger than a single-source figure reading, but it still does not name a setting.

### Q5 - Cycle and closure

Fig 12 :121 again: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

A prebiotic pathway that ends in a stable molecule is a chain, not a cycle. It cannot close by construction. Autocatalysis, where a product catalyzes its own production, is the cycle condition, and it is the third necessary condition in F4 of the audit. Each candidate setting must be tested for whether it permits such closure; wet-dry cycling and mineral surfaces have been argued to support it (Omran and Pasek 2020; Mulkidjanian et al. 2012), while continuous dilution in an open vent is a standing objection that the same source raises (*"the immense diluting potential of the surrounding environment"*).

**Falsifier:** show a claimed setting whose chemistry is strictly a chain yet which yields a self-maintaining evolving system. That breaks the cycle condition.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate settings are **isomers** over a shared condition set (same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour), with at least two **compounds** inside the union (shallow vents; ice plus mineral surfaces). No order among them is licensed; only the investigation order is. Different settings can converge on the same metabolic outcome, so a single LUCA does not force a single site. Each candidate must be tested separately for gradient, network and cycle, and the absence of evidence in one is not evidence against it.

This rescues the defensible part of the "where did life begin" debate without naming a winner: the isomer/union/compound reading needs no new primitive and no import that moves the wall. The name remains ground-owned and is reported as `gap`.
