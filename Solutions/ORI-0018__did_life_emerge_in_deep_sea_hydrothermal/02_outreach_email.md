# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the setting

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 to F4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated accurately and the stage-1 verdict is recorded before any theory is shown. If the shape does not survive validation, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide which origin-of-life setting won

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the "where did life begin" question that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` union finding, not `grounded`):** the question is not one hard multiple-choice problem. Deep-sea vents, shallow ponds, volcanic land and ice are different bundles of conditions, and some overlap (a shallow alkaline vent is both a vent and a wet-dry cycling setting). The framework's rule is to enumerate the habitat from the world and keep the candidates as separate slots rather than average them. From that rule it follows that any candidate setting must supply three separate things: a gradient that can be spent, a network in which products feed further reactions, and a cycle whose output re-enters as input. These are necessary conditions, not a score.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any one of those settings *is* the site. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. The name of the setting is owed to the ground, meaning dated rocks, isotope fractionation, microfossils and reproducible prebiotic syntheses. I am also not claiming that life arose only once; different routes can reach the same substance, and a single LUCA is a bottleneck in descent, not proof of a single site.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a verbatim figure line that selects one setting and excludes the others by its own predicates;
> 2. a setting that lacks a spendable gradient, a reaction network or a closing cycle yet produces a self-maintaining evolving system;
> 3. a demonstration that the candidates reduce to one partition under a single necessary condition;
> 4. a sealed, complete record for one setting in which absence of evidence is evidence of absence.
>
> I wrote these down before running anything. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The question I would actually like answered:** which instrument, applied to which Hadean-relevant material, would discriminate between at least two of these settings? That is the missing link, and I would value your judgement on it more than your verdict on the framework.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I can send the figure text and the derivation on request.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
