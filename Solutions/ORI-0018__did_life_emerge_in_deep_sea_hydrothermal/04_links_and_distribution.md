## Wider literature that bears on the setting question

The four scientific sources above are genuinely independent of the Tether framework, so their agreement with the figure-derived shape is corroboration from a different evidence pool, not an author quoting himself. They disagree with each other about the *name*, which is exactly what the figure verdict predicts: the shape is settled structurally, the name is ground-decided and currently contested.

1. **Vents.** Omran and Pasek (2020) and Barge and Price (2022) supply the gradient/compartment/concentration case, including the overlooked shallow-vent intermediate.
2. **Volcanic land.** Mulkidjanian et al. (2012) supply the ion-composition case against marine settings.
3. **Geological constraint.** Westall et al. (2023) supply the anchor audit: stability, longevity, and the contested status of the oldest traces.
4. **Convergence.** Omran and Pasek (2020) argue that one LUCA does not entail one site and recommend a "yes and" paradigm, the world-level twin of Fig 13 :121.

A conflict rule was triggered by Mulkidjanian et al. (2012), which conflicts with the marine-vent hypothesis. Per the WATSON instruction, a conflict sends the analysis back to the domain census, never flips the verdict. Re-census: the disagreement is between two frames inside D1 (evidence and ground), both of which accept the same three necessary conditions (gradient, network, cycle) and differ on which setting satisfies them. The figure verdict (name owed to ground, shape triangulated) is unchanged. The conflict is recorded rather than smoothed.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure and which instrument would discriminate between settings. Record the verdict before showing any source.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text and the four scientific sources above. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* warns that an LLM can *"simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* but that *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* That is why each user must supply their own ground and refuter.
