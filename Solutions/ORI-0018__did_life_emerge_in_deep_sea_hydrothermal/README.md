## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

A satisfactory answer has two parts that must not be confused. (1) A **shape**: the necessary conditions any setting must meet to be a candidate, and the rule for keeping several candidates separate rather than averaging them. (2) A **name**: the particular setting, which is a claim about the Hadean ground and can be settled only by geological or experimental evidence. The shape is derivable here. The name is not: the figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32), and the anchor that settles a name is a measurement or experiment (Fig 2 :29). Binding the shape as an ordinary term lets the pre-registered refuter test it like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of anyone by likelihood of agreeing with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words: a room cannot supply an anchor (Fig 2 :29, :35); selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40); and a verifier who has already absorbed the interior cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42). An outside reader whose verdict is requested must see the residual and the refuter first, the theory only later and only by opt-in.

## Key verdict in one line

The figures do not name a setting, and they are not silent about why: the name is owed to the ground, while the shape (enumerate habitats from the world, keep candidate slots separate, require a spendable gradient, a reaction network and a closing cycle, accept that different routes can reach the same substance) is triangulated across several figures. Tether status for the specific setting: `gap`, reported plainly.
