# The Tether's answer: when to build internally rather than acquire

**Mode: `specified` on the decision rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading and not an answer about any named company.

## 1. The residual it answers

An established firm has a gap between what its present closure can compose and what a new venture needs. "Build or acquire?" presents that as one choice. It is three overlapping conditions with different failure modes, so the honest answer is a conditional rule with per-domain verdicts, never an average. The residual must stay indexed by capability slot (Fig 1 :19), because a global aggregate of the form: we need innovation, is where the signal disappears.

## 2. The shape: a three-domain decision rule

**Domain 1, the in-here test (build region).** Ask first: Fig 9 :6 "the verdict: is φ* in here at all?" If the needed capability is already in the firm's closure and merely unfound, the remedy is more search at the same reach, with searchers kept independent. If it is a new *arrangement* of atoms the firm already holds, build: Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." The set of kinds is closed but the arrangements are not, so new combinations are reachable. Build is also gated by the mint bargain: does stating the venture plus what remains unexplained cost less than the confusion it removes?

**Domain 2, the somewhere-else test (acquire region).** If the capability requires a primitive the firm does not hold, internal effort cannot produce it: Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom". Fig 6 :14 names import as "the only operator that moves the wall". Acquiring a startup is the import operator: the target is a frame whose closure differs, and the deal gives the firm access to an atom it could not derive. Describe the residual in effect terms before naming a target: Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Acquisition is not a shortcut to be tried first; it is the answer to a specific verdict.

**Domain 3, the survives-the-move test (governs both).** The imported capability still has to settle at the ground and then compose over in the acquirer. Fig 5 :72 "a library that grows is not a reach that grows." The atom metaphor alone under-describes this: Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" and Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." So an acquisition must reproduce the habitat (team, routines, incentives, contacts) or it buys a capability that fails silently. What was not reproduced shows up when the goal fails; what was unintentionally introduced shows up when it acts.

**Across all three domains.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Keep the verdicts separate. Hold two prices apart: Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Keep provenance: Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". After the deal, record which capabilities were derived and which were adopted, because the artifact will not tell you.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "always build" and not "always buy". The rule assigns levers to residual types; it does not rank modes by fashion or by the reputation of firms that used them. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not a named target, a named capability slot, or a purchase price. Those are bindings from the business frame, not outputs of the derivation.
- Not a numerical threshold for "how large a gap". No figure line quantifies it. That is a declared gap, recorded as `unreachable`, not a silence (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
- Not that a settled acquisition is a success. Settling is necessary and not sufficient; the imported atom must also compose over.
- Not a claim about any specific company or deal. No ground reading was taken.
- Not `grounded`. The whole rule is `specified` and a candidate.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A paying market that does not move when either firm changes its account of the deal (Fig 2 :29, :45). The checks that would fire:

1. **In-here check:** does the firm actually hold the atoms claimed, or is the "build" plan relying on an atom that is absent? A source-discriminating internal audit, not an argument.
2. **Verdict check:** does a build program reach a capability that is provably outside the firm's closure, with no import, and does the market pay for it? If yes, the build boundary is wrong.
3. **Habitat check:** does the imported capability compose over without reproducing the target's habitat? If yes, the habitat requirement is wrong.
4. **Cost check:** is a wrong acquisition measurably no more expensive than starting from primitives? If yes, the import debit is wrong.
5. **Union check:** does one lever govern every venture decision at a non-updating ground? If yes, the three-domain union collapses to a partition.
6. **Provenance check:** after the deal, does the distinction between derived and adopted change nothing in any later diagnosis? If yes, the provenance requirement is wrong.

## 5. The honest stop

The firm's capability set is a visible guess, not nature's own list. Another frame's minimum set need not be these atoms. The answer stops at what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: a built capability and an imported one may be indistinguishable in the artifact and still carry different warrants. Where no discriminator exists, keep the record rather than reconstructing the source from the reading. The round trip from the condensed rule back to a specific deal is lossy; Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "build or buy" with a universal preference. It says the question is three overlapping conditions. First ask whether the capability is already inside the firm's closure and merely unfound; if so, search there, and if it is a new arrangement of things the firm already holds, build it, because internal composition can rearrange what it holds. If the capability needs a primitive the firm does not hold, no amount of internal search creates it; acquire the frame that holds it, but describe the gap before naming a target. Third, an acquisition only works if the imported capability can settle and then compose over in the new habitat, because capability belongs to an agent together with its habitat; what you failed to reproduce appears only when the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced appears only when it acts. Keep the two prices, description and actions, apart, and keep a record of which capability was built and which was bought, because the finished artifact will not tell you. What is offered is that shape, five written refuters, and the instruments that would close it. No target, no threshold, and no claim about any particular firm.
