## 0. The residual, in effect terms

An established company faces a gap between what its present library can compose and what a new venture requires. The question asks which lever closes that gap.

- **Build internally** means mint a new term from inside the firm's closure: compose existing atoms into a new venture, paying in design effort and in actions to test it.
- **Acquire a startup** means import an atom from a frame whose closure differs: take in a capability the firm does not hold and cannot derive from what it holds.

What each leads to. Build keeps control and strategic fit and pays in time, but it cannot manufacture a primitive the firm's closure lacks. Acquire reaches a capability not derivable inside and pays in independence, integration, and the risk of importing the wrong atom.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"): a **conditional decision rule** that assigns the build lever to residuals that are new arrangements of held atoms, and the acquire lever to residuals that require a primitive held only by another closure, with a third domain governing whether an imported atom survives the move. The rule must state what would refute it before it is offered.

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** more than one slot. The residual is indexed by capability slot, not aggregated: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A single global "we need innovation" number throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22).
- **Symmetry:** build and acquire are not inverses. One composes inside a wall; the other moves it (Fig 6 :21, Fig 6 :14). Direction is load bearing, so this is an ordered, not a symmetric, relation between the two levers.
- **Scale:** the firm is the population scale, the venture team is the one-agent scale, and the boundary between them is a filter with direction (Fig 4 :21). The same loop runs at both sizes; only actors, currency and anchor change.

## 1. Domain census

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain. Then partition or union, then dependency order.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. Firm internal closure (build)** | existing capabilities as atoms, the firm's library and routines, venture team, budget | composition reach; two prices in bits and actions (Fig 12 :125) | the paying market and its non-updating reading of the venture (Fig 2 :29, Fig 2 :45) | is the needed capability in here and merely unfound, or not composable at all? |
| **D2. Startup external frame (acquire)** | the startup's atoms, its team, its closure, its own habitat | the primitive it holds that we lack; provenance | the startup's own ground, its customers and measurements | does another closure predict the shape of R? |
| **D3. **Partition or union?** Union, not partition. The three conditions overlap. A venture can need a new arrangement of held atoms *and* a different habitat; a startup can hold the needed atom *and* require internal absorptive capacity before it can be recognized. The figures forbid treating a union as a list: Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." and Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds alone and leaves the intersections standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Three questions, in this order, from Fig 9 :6-8 and :43:
1. **Verdict:** is the needed capability in our closure at all?
2. **Lookup:** if not, which frame holds it?
3. **Narrowing and integration:** once imported, does it settle and compose over here?

Refuse before mint (Formula step 2), and describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53, Fig 9 :27-28). Effort spent on a lower link cannot rescue a higher one that is broken (Fig 3 :19).

**Strain test applied during census.** The first draft read "build" as the mirror image of "acquire" and tried to treat them as a symmetric pair. That strained Fig 6 :21 and Fig 6 :14 at once. Re-census showed the two levers act on different quantities (reach inside a closure versus the wall), so the symmetry reading was dropped. Recorded in the retraction log below.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Build is mint, acquire is import (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"
Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."
Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

A startup is a frame whose closure differs. Acquiring it is the import operator: the acquirer gains access to a primitive it could not derive, and its own closure expands. Building internally is the mint operator: it rearranges the atoms already held. This is not an analogy imposed on the question. It is the same distinction the figures make between composing inside a closure and moving its wall.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` as applied to firms. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 (wall operator), Fig 8 (union surplus) and Fig 13 (closed set), three arguments with low overlap. **Refuter:** show a firm composing a capability that is provably not in the closure of its held atoms, with no import, and the paying market settles it. That breaks Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8's idempotence claim.

### F2 — No amount of internal search adds a missing primitive (triangulated)

Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom"
Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
Fig 13 :116 "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."

This is the hard boundary on build. If the venture requires a new *kind* of capability, not a new arrangement of existing kinds, internal effort cannot produce it. This is the figure-grounded content behind the world observation that firms which tried to redesign themselves internally into a new business model wasted years before looking outward (Capron, 2015, opened). It also explains why "build harder" is not a remedy for a gap of the wrong type: Fig 6 :29 warns the inward instrument "Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it."

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 13. **Refuter:** a single counterexample in which a firm derives a genuinely new kind from existing atoms with no import, settled at the ground.

### F3 — The verdict must precede the lookup (single figure for the rule, corroborated by world)

Fig 9 :6 "the verdict: is φ* in here at all?"
Fig 9 :7 "the lookup: which frame holds it?"
Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."

The order is part of the answer. Naming an attractive startup before describing the residual is the inversion the figures forbid: any target will then seem to fit. The world literature independently warns against the same inversion, describing firms that "overestimate how competitive their internal resources are and underestimate what it takes to get where they want to go" and default to a familiar mode (Capron, 2015). Agreement here is weak corroboration from a different field, not a second witness for the figure rule.

**Mode:** `specified` decision procedure. **Status:** single-source for the ordered rule (Fig 9), with low-overlap business corroboration. **Refuter:** a documented case where target-first selection outperforms residual-first selection across many ventures with the ground settling the result.

### F4 — Acquiring grows the library, not necessarily the reach (triangulated)

Fig 5 :69 "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."
Fig 5 :72 "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."
Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

An acquisition that closes adds an atom to Γ. It does not follow that the firm can compose over it. Two extra prices are named: the target's independence is spent by the import, and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. The world literature prices the same thing in different units: "70% of acquisitions fail. Post-purchase integration is a nightmare" (Capron, 2015), and the warning to consider buying "only after ruling out the other two options, and only if you feel you have a viable integration plan" (Mitchell, 2013). Asset specificity and search, negotiation and monitoring costs name the same debit in transaction-cost terms (Jimmink, 2025, on Coase and Williamson).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6 and Fig 12's two-currency rule. **Refuter:** a case where an imported atom composes over and pays on the ground with no debit to independence and no integration cost.

### F5 — Capability is agent-and-habitat, so the acquisition must reproduce the habitat (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

A startup's capability lives in its team, incentives, routines and surrounding contacts. Buying the legal entity and inserting it into a different habitat is isolation, which substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. The two failure modes are invisible on different clocks: what was not reproduced shows up only when the goal fails; what was unintentionally introduced shows up only when it acts. This is the figure-grounded reason an acquisition can be structurally correct (the atom was imported) and still return nothing (the atom cannot compose over in the new habitat).

The world source with the lowest overlap and the closest fit is Cohen and Levinthal (1990), opened this session: a requisite part of absorptive capacity "is often firm-specific and therefore cannot be bought and quickly integrated into the firm." That is the habitat law stated in organizational-learning terms. The figure decides; the source corroborates.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11, Fig 4 :34-35 (a seed carries conditions, not a small tree), and Fig 6 :24. **Refuter:** an acquisition where the imported capability composes over and pays on the ground with no reproduction of the target's habitat and no internal absorptive capacity.

### F6 — Introduction is available, exclusion is not (single-source)

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :64 "the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author."

The integrating firm can add an actor, a process or an incentive; it cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. A managed acquisition is therefore permanent work rather than a solved state. This is a figure-imposed rule, not a finding from the business literature, and it is marked `single-source`.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source (Fig 11). **Refuter:** a demonstration that the firm can subtract an unwanted habitat relation by decree, with the ground confirming the subtraction.

### F7 — What crosses the membrane is a method or a head start, never a recording (single-source)

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

After an acquisition the tempting artifact is the target's last success: its playbook, its deck, its growth curve. That is a recording of one occasion. The only thing that travels between scales is a reapplicable method, and what travels down is a head start that makes the next search cheaper. The round trip is lossy, so its gap must be measured rather than assumed zero: Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source (Fig 4), corroborated in effect by the world literature on integration but not derived from it. **Refuter:** a case where replaying the target's recorded success in the parent produces a new success on the ground.

### F8 — Provenance is the whole post-deal record (triangulated)

Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"
Fig 10 :62 "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
Fig 10 :65 "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."

After the deal, a capability built in-house and one imported can be indistinguishable in the artifact. Only the record of origin separates them. That record is not documentation of the decision, it is part of the decision: a later reader needs to know whether the capability was derived here or adopted from a frame whose closure differs, because the two have different failure modes and different follow-up actions. A clean record does not make the decision right. The ground does that.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 10. **Refuter:** a case where provenance makes no difference to any later decision or diagnosis, and the ground settles both routes identically.

### F9 — The three questions form a union, not a partition (decisive, single-source)

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The question as posed ("build or acquire?") is a forced partition over a union. There are at least three conditions: the needed capability is a new arrangement of held atoms (build region), it requires a primitive held elsewhere (acquire region), and it requires a habitat the firm cannot reproduce (a compound that neither lever alone clears). The second-order value of unbundling is not a smaller pile; it is that the residue becomes describable (Fig 9 :59). Keeping verdicts per domain separate and never averaging them is the discipline the figures require.

**Mode:** `general` structural. **Status:** single-source for the union reading (Fig 9), with business corroboration that firms run a portfolio of modes rather than one mode (Capron, 2015; Mitchell, 2013). **Refuter:** a well-measured sample in which one lever governs every venture with a non-updating ground, so the union collapses to a partition.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A firm builds, with no import, a capability provably not composable from the atoms it held, and the paying ground settles it (breaks F1 and F2).
2. An imported capability composes over and pays on the ground with no reproduction of the target's habitat and no internal absorptive capacity (breaks F5).
3. A wrong atom costs the acquirer less than starting from primitives, measured at the ground (breaks Fig 6 :24).
4. A single lever is shown to govern all venture decisions with a non-updating ground, so the union is false (breaks F9).
5. After the deal, artifact-identical built and imported capabilities produce different later diagnoses with no difference in recorded provenance (breaks F8).

If any fires, the decision rule is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failure reported by a reader is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask the verdict question first: is the needed capability in the firm's closure and merely unfound? If yes, run more search inside, with independent searchers (Fig 6 :49-50), before contemplating a purchase.
2. If the capability is a new arrangement of held atoms, build. Check the mint bargain: does stating the venture plus what remains unexplained cost less than the confusion it removes (Fig 5 :26, :42)?
3. If the capability requires a primitive the firm does not hold, describe R in effect terms, then look for the frame whose closure predicts its shape (Fig 9 :26-28). Acquire that frame if and only if a habitat and integration plan exists (Fig 11 :39, Fig 6 :24).
4. Treat the two prices separately: bits to describe the arrangement, actions to find out whether it holds (Fig 12 :125-127). Summing them into one score is the error, not the fix.
5. Record provenance. After the deal, keep the record of which capability was derived and which was adopted, because the artifact will not tell you (Fig 8 :50).
6. Send the generator (the decision rule, its residual and its refuter), not the conclusion (Fig 4 :25).

### Strain log

- **Live strain, named:** acquisition is treated as import of an atom (Fig 6), but the habitat law (Fig 11) prices something the atom metaphor does not carry. Re-census: two currencies, bits and actions, which "do not add" (Fig 12 :125). Both are read; neither is summed.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a conditional rule over three domains, with build assigned to reachable new arrangements, acquire assigned to primitives held in another closure, and a third domain governing whether the import survives the move. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. There is no figure line selecting a numerical gap threshold, and that gap is recorded as `unreachable`, which is a verdict and not a silence. Nothing is `grounded`: no firm, deal or market was measured in this run.
