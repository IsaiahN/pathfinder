# Expert verification packet — the Tether on build internally versus acquire a startup

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate venturing or acquisitions researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the decision rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No firm or deal was measured in producing it. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Build or acquire?" is not one choice with two answers. It is three overlapping conditions with different failure modes. When the needed capability is a new arrangement of atoms the firm already holds, internal building is the reachable lever, because minting composes inside a closure. When the capability requires a primitive the firm does not hold, internal search cannot produce it and acquisition is the import of that primitive from a frame whose closure differs. A third condition governs both: an imported capability has to settle at the ground and then compose over in the acquirer, which requires reproducing the target's habitat, because capability is a property of agent and habitat, never of the agent alone. The rule keeps two prices apart, description and actions, and keeps a provenance record of which capability was derived and which was adopted, because the finished artifact does not reveal the difference. What is offered is the shape, written refuters, and the instruments that would close it. No target, no threshold, no claim about a named company.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** build is the mint operator, acquire is the import operator, and only import adds a primitive the firm did not hold.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom". Fig 13 :116 "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."
- **Refuter:** show a firm composing, with no import, a capability provably outside the closure of the atoms it held, with the paying ground settling it. Or show a single lever governing all venture decisions at a non-updating ground, collapsing the union to a partition.

### C2 — business-facing; checkable against a portfolio the reader knows

- **Statement:** acquisition is governed by a habitat condition, not only by the acquisition of the legal entity. The two failure modes are invisible on different clocks.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
- **What to check:** in cases the reader knows well, did the acquired capability survive because the habitat was reproduced, or did it fail in one of the two named ways? Did the deal's cost include a debit that a plain "buy the atom" account omits?
- **Refuter:** an acquisition where the imported capability composes over and pays at the ground with no reproduction of the target's habitat and no internal absorptive capacity; or a measured case where a wrong acquisition costs less than starting from primitives.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a business consequence

- **Statement:** after the deal, a built capability and an imported one can be artifact-identical, so the record of origin is part of the decision, not documentation of it. Import can grow the library without growing reach.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" Fig 5 :72 "a library that grows is not a reach that grows." Fig 5 :69 "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **Refuter:** a case where provenance makes no difference to any later diagnosis and the ground settles both routes identically; or a case where replaying the target's recorded playbook produces a new success in the parent, so the recording behaves as a generator.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "always build" and not "always buy". The rule assigns levers to residual types.
- Not a named target, a named capability slot, a purchase price, or a numerical threshold for "how large a gap". No figure line supplies any of these.
- Not that a closed acquisition is a success. Settling is necessary and not sufficient; the atom must also compose over.
- Not a derivation of one mode from the other, and no new primitive.
- Not a claim about any specific company or deal.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not a claim that the target's independence is worth preserving for its own sake. Fig 6 :24 prices its loss; the price is the point.

## 5. What would settle it

A non-updating ground, not agreement among readers. The checks that can fire:

1. **In-here audit:** does the firm hold the atoms the build plan assumes? Absence changes the lever without an argument.
2. **Boundary test:** does a build program reach a provably outside capability and get paid for it? If yes, the mint boundary in C1 is wrong.
3. **Habitat test:** does an import compose over with no reproduced habitat? If yes, C2 is wrong.
4. **Cost test:** is a wrong acquisition measurably no more expensive than starting from primitives? If yes, the Fig 6 :24 debit is wrong.
5. **Provenance test:** after the deal, does the origin record change no later diagnosis? If yes, C3 is wrong.
6. **Union test:** does one lever govern every case at a non-updating ground? If yes, the three-domain structure is wrong.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read the four short passages in C1 (Fig 6 :21, :14, Fig 8 :32, Fig 13 :116). Decide whether the build/acquire mapping is faithful to the figures.
2. Read the C2 passages on habitat and the import debit. Name one deal you know where the habitat either was or was not reproduced, and say which clock the failure followed.
3. Check C3 against a real post-deal record: did anyone distinguish the derived capability from the adopted one, and did it matter later?
4. State what you would count as a failure of the rule. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would like the sources, ask; the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
