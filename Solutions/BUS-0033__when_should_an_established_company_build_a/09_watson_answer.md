## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, when an established firm should build a new venture internally and when it should acquire a startup. Option A: treat it as one choice with two opposite answers and pick by preference, cost score or fashion. Option B: treat it as three overlapping conditions and assign a lever per condition, with a separate test for whether an imported capability survives the move. What each leads to: A hides the union, lets a budget filter hand the verdict, and buys a capability that may never compose over; B returns a shape, written refuters, and the instruments that would close the residual, while refusing to name a target or a numerical threshold.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, then order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A gap between what the firm's present closure can compose and what a new venture requires. Arity: several capability slots, not one aggregate (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: build and acquire are not inverses, since one composes inside a closure and the other moves its wall. Scale: the firm is the population scale, the venture team is the one-agent scale, the membrane is Fig 4. Fig 9 names the structure a union, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, firm internal closure (build).** Actors: held capabilities as atoms, the firm's library and routines, the venture team, the budget. Currency: composition reach, priced in bits and actions. Anchor: the paying market, which does not move when the firm changes its account of the venture. Question: is the capability in here and unfound, or is it a new arrangement of held atoms?
- **Domain 2, startup external frame (acquire).** Actors: the target's atoms, team, closure and habitat. Currency: the primitive the firm lacks, plus provenance. Anchor: the target's own market reading. Question: does another closure predict the shape of R?
- **Domain 3, membrane and integration.** Actors: the imported atom, the acquired habitat, the incumbent habitat, the maintainers. Currency: whether the atom settles and then composes over, and the round trip gap R_T. Anchor: the same non-updating market. Question: does the capability survive the move?
- **Partition or union:** union of three overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54-57), not a partition. Single levers clear single-condition regions only.
- **Order by dependency:** verdict first (is it in here?), lookup second (which frame holds it?), settle and compose third. Refuse the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before minting a candidate. Describe the residual before choosing a frame.

Strain test applied: build versus acquire as mint versus import is not strained. The apparent conflict between "mint cannot add an atom" and everyday capability growth is resolved by re-census: new capabilities are new molecules, not new atoms. The apparent conflict between "import moves the wall" and high acquisition failure rates is resolved by re-census: moving the wall is necessary, not sufficient (Fig 5 :69-72). One live strain remains, named in `01` §6: the habitat price is carried by Fig 11 but not by the atom metaphor in Fig 6, so the two prices are read apart and never summed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; no numerical threshold or named target does. Build when the venture is a new arrangement of atoms the firm already holds, because minting composes inside a closure. Acquire when the capability requires a primitive the firm does not hold, because import is the only operator that moves the wall, but describe the residual before naming a target and buy only with a habitat and integration plan. In both cases the imported or minted capability must settle at a non-updating ground and then compose over; a closed deal grows the library without necessarily growing reach. Keep the two prices apart and keep provenance, because built and adopted capabilities become artifact-identical. The residual is a union of three overlapping conditions, not a two-way partition, and verdicts per domain are never averaged. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate rule. The strongest single figure-grounded point is the boundary on internal search: no search, however long, adds an atom.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording (a target list or a success story) instead of a generator (the rule and its refuter). Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The three-domain rule can be narrowed or withdrawn without discarding the derivation. No primitive has to be removed, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. Refusals add to the record; nothing is erased.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A build program that reaches a provably outside capability and gets paid for it would break the mint boundary; an import that composes over with no reproduced habitat would break the habitat verdict; a measured case where a wrong acquisition costs less than starting from primitives would break the import debit; a single lever governing every venture at a non-updating ground would break the union; a case where provenance changes no later diagnosis would break the provenance requirement; an instrument distinguishing derived from adopted capabilities at the artifact level would make the record unnecessary. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. The numerical gap is `unreachable` at this budget, which is a verdict and not a silence.
