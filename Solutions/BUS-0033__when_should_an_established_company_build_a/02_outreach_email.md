# Outreach draft — check the decision rule before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader has recorded a verdict on stage 1. The whole offer depends on that order being kept. Once someone has the framework's interior, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42).

---

**Subject:** Would this refute a three-part rule for build versus buy?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know about corporate venturing and acquisitions. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An established firm has a gap between what its current set of capabilities can compose and what a new venture needs. The question "build or acquire?" treats that as one choice. The output says it is three overlapping conditions:
>
> 1. The needed capability is a **new arrangement of things the firm already holds**. Internal building can reach it. Internal search on an axis that cannot carry it cannot.
> 2. The needed capability requires a **primitive the firm does not hold**, held by another frame. No amount of internal composition produces it; that capability has to be imported, which in business terms means acquiring the startup that holds it.
> 3. The imported capability has to **survive the move**. A capability belongs to an agent together with its habitat (its team, routines, incentives and contacts). Moving the agent without reproducing the habitat fails in two silent ways: what you failed to reproduce shows up only when the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced shows up only when it acts.
>
> **The decision rule (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Ask the verdict question first: is the capability in here and merely unfound? If yes, search inside with independent searchers before buying anything. If it is a new arrangement of held atoms, build. If it needs a primitive held elsewhere, describe the gap in effect terms before naming a target, then acquire only if there is a real habitat and integration plan. Keep two prices apart: the cost of describing the arrangement and the cost of finding out whether it holds. Record afterwards which capabilities were built and which were adopted, because the finished artifact will not tell you.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that acquisition is always faster, not that internal ventures always fail, not a numerical threshold for "how large a gap", and not a prediction about any named company. Those are bindings from the business frame, not outputs of the derivation. The rule returns a shape and a route, not a target list.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this email):**
> 1. A firm builds, with no import, a capability provably not composable from the atoms it held, and the paying market settles it.
> 2. An imported capability composes over and pays on the ground with no reproduction of the target's habitat.
> 3. A wrong acquisition costs less than starting from primitives, measured at the ground.
> 4. One lever is shown to govern every venture decision with a non-updating ground, so the three conditions are a partition after all.
>
> If any of these holds, I will narrow or withdraw the rule. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** run it on a venture decision you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order in one place

1. **Stage 1:** residual, decision rule, refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record that answer.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, the three local framework links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it.

Later agreement from the same reader is never pooled as corroboration. A correction on shape, a named failure class, or the question "could this have been anything else?" is what the outside frame is for.
