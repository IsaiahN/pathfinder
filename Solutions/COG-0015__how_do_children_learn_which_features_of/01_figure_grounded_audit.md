## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A child faces an environment with a very large number of potentially attendable features. Attention is a limited budget. The residual is the gap, per feature-slot, between what the child's current library predicts will happen at that slot and what actually happens. Symbols table: "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it." Fig 1 :11 names it "residual R indexed by object slot".

The phrase "worth attending to" is not a property carried by a feature. It is a three-place relation between a feature, the child's current residual, and a habitat whose ground settles whether the bet paid. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

**Bound answer-shape (formula step "Direction"), written before the run.** An answer will be a *separator* with four conditions, not a list:

1. attention is allocated per slot by a boundary diff that sorts residual into held, new, mis-attached and broken;
2. the valence of a slot is relative to the child's current residual and habitat, so no standing list survives;
3. the child inherits a head start (priors, affective biases) and imports pointers from differing closures, both with provenance;
4. a non-updating ground settles which attentional commitments pay, and the research instrument must keep that ground reachable.

This shape is now an ordinary term. It can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else. It is not assumed to be true because it was written down first.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale** (Fig 9 :22: "arity · symmetry · scale"):

- **Arity:** three or four conditions, so a **union** with overlapping regions, not a partition and not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54-58). A single lever, for example "novelty alone", clears the region where only novelty holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry:** "worth attending to" is **ordered**, not co-present. A correlation between a feature and an outcome is symmetric; attending *to* a feature *for* a goal is a sequence or a comparison. Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Operators table :33: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." An averaged effect size cannot choose the arrow.
- **Scale:** first order within one developmental stage (one slot, one bet, one observation); second order when a settled attentional habit becomes an atom for the next stage (Fig 12 :104). The membrane between scales is a filter with a direction, not a wall (Fig 4 :21).

## 3. Findings

### F1. The literal question asks for a standing list, and a standing list is structurally refused (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

A feature that is worth attending to for a hungry infant (a breast, a bottle) is not worth attending to for the same infant satiated, or for an infant whose current residual is social rather than nutritional. The list changes when the residual changes. This is not an empirical generalization awaiting a table; it is the valence rule applied to the question's own object.

**Refuter:** exhibit a feature that remains worth attending to across every change of residual and habitat, with the ground paying for it in each case. If one exists, the standing-list reading survives and F1 fails.

**World corroboration (secondary):** Gibson's affordance, via the Wikipedia synthesis read this session: "the key to understanding affordance is that it is relational and characterizes the suitability of the environment to the observer, and so, depends on their current intentions and their capabilities." And Gibson's own line quoted there: "Needs control the perception of affordances (selective attention) and also initiate acts." Low overlap with the figure census (ecological psychology versus the six-term habitat), so triangulated.

### F2. Attention is per-slot residual, not a global salience score (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look." Symbols table :43: "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."

A child can be globally well predicted and still have one live slot. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Averaging looking times across objects destroys exactly the signal the question asks for. This governs D1 and the instrument design in D4.

**Refuter:** show a global attention statistic that predicts where a child will next look better than the per-slot residual does, across habitats, without importing the slot structure in disguise.

**World corroboration:** the predictive-coding synthesis describes precision weighting as follows: "It has also been proposed that such weighting of prediction errors in proportion to their estimated precision is, in essence, attention". That is a per-signal gain, not a global sum. Friston 2010, opened directly: "Perception optimizes predictions by minimizing free energy with respect to synaptic activity (perceptual inference), efficacy (learning and memory) and gain (attention and salience)." Different field and vocabulary from Fig 1, so triangulated.

### F3. The orienting response is the boundary diff: habituation and dishabituation are the mechanism (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :15: "where to look: broken vs novel". Fig 5 :10: "sort the gap into four kinds". Fig 5 :11: "held: the model was right, nothing owed"; Fig 5 :12: "new: extend perception, aim the probe here"; Fig 5 :13: "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"; Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed".

The habituation synthesis gives the world-side mechanism: "when a stimulus is experienced several times, the nervous system creates a model of the expected stimulus (a stimulus model). With additional presentations of the stimulus, the experienced stimulus is compared with the stimulus model. If the experienced stimulus matches the stimulus model, responding is inhibited." And: "if the stimulus is changed so that it no longer matches the stimulus model, the orienting response is no longer inhibited." This is the prediction-versus-observation comparison of Symbols table :38 ("The gap between prediction and observation"), realized in a classical infant paradigm. The same synthesis states the functional reason: habituation is thought to free "cognitive resources for other stimuli that are associated with biologically important events by diminishing the response to inconsequential stimuli." That is the budget reallocation the question asks about.

**Refuter:** show that dishabituation occurs with no change in the child's prediction model and no change in residual, that is, that the orienting response is a pure fatigue or sensory-adaptation artifact. The habituation literature already has its own refuters for this (dishabituation, spontaneous recovery, stimulus specificity), which strengthens rather than weakens the point.

**Caution, kept visible:** the same Wikipedia page says dishabituation "is not perceived as a direct measure for mental processes" and notes a novelty/familiarity confound. This is an instrument limitation in D4, not a refutation of D1. Recorded in F8.

### F4. "Worth" is the large and compressible gap, not the maximum gap and not zero (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 5 :15 to :18: "the curiosity drive", "fires when nothing is scoring", "aimed at the new bin", "seeks the gap that is large and compressible". Fig 5 :47: "So a system that starts with nothing can still be shaped, by seeking the gap that is large and compressible, which is the same quantity the acceptance test scores." Fig 5 :26: "and then: does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"

Pure noise is large but not compressible; the fully familiar is compressible but has no gap. "Worth attending to" is the conjunction. Fig 5 :45: "This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978". The bargain is a comparison, not a threshold on novelty alone.

**Refuter:** show children systematically preferring either uncompressed noise or fully predicted stimuli over the intermediate, compressible discrepancy, with the ground paying for the preference. Also refuted if an attentional preference can be shown to be driven by a quantity that is neither residual mass nor compressibility while still paying at the ground.

**World corroboration:** the curiosity synthesis reports Berlyne's collative variables, "novelty", "complexity", "uncertainty", and "conflict", plus "change, surprisingness, and incongruity", and optimal-arousal theory: arousal above the optimum from complexity or novelty drives exploration to reduce it, while a boring environment drives exploration to increase input. This is exactly an intermediate target with two failure directions. It adds: "Their reactions to not having their expectations met may fuel their curiosity more than the introduction of a novel or complex object would." That favours prediction error over novelty per se, matching Fig 5's "new" bin being one of four, not the whole. Low overlap (motivation research), so triangulated. Friston 2010 adds that "value is inversely proportional to surprise (and implicitly free energy), and rewards correspond to innate priors that constrain policies", which is the same two-sided objective in a different calculus.

### F5. The child inherits a head start, not a recording (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the developmental claim, triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

What is transmitted to a child is a prior that makes the next search cheaper, not a list of the features their ancestors attended to. A recorded feature list replayed in a changed habitat is Fig 4 :25's failure: a description of one occasion presented as knowledge.

**Refuter:** show an organism with no inherited prior, no affective bias and no head start that still allocates attention productively from a standing start, with the ground paying. Or show that an inherited prior is a replay of a particular past success rather than a reusable method.

**World corroboration:** Friston 2010: "Priors can be acquired (as empirical priors during hierarchical inference) or they can be innate (epigenetic) and therefore subject to selective pressure." The affordance synthesis notes that "During childhood development, a child learns to perceive not only the affordances for the self, but also how those same objects furnish similar affordances to another." Two low-overlap fields, so triangulated for the shape. The specific developmental timetable is not corroborated by figures or world sources and is a `gap` (F9).

### F6. Capability is agent-and-habitat; "which features" lives in the contact, not in the child or the feature (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

Fig 11 :46-47 gives the census categories directly: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain" and "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency". For a child these read as the sensory conditions and the contingent, dependent relations the habitat supplies. Fig 11 :52-53 warns that contact cascades: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A feature three steps out can matter more than the one in front of the child.

**Refuter:** show an improvement to the child's attention that moves the ground while leaving contact unchanged. The figures name this as the one observation that would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :72).

**World corroboration:** Gibson's definition quoted in the affordance synthesis: "The affordances of the environment are what it offers the animal, what it provides or furnishes, either for good or ill. ... It implies the complementarity of the animal and the environment." Triangulated across ecology and the figures.

### F7. Social attention is an import from a differing closure with a shadow test, not a copy (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Joint attention is one individual alerting another to an object "by means of eye-gazing, pointing or other verbal or non-verbal indications". The joint-attention synthesis adds: "Gaze following reflects an expectation-based type of orienting in which an individual's attention is cued by another's head turn or eye turn. Individuals are motivated to follow another's gaze and engage in joint attention because gaze is a cue for which rewarding events occur." The pointer is a cue, not a command; the child ranges it against its own residual and the ground.

The source gate and shadow test are visible in the same synthesis: "up to 78% of maternal speech is matched to the object the child is focusing on." The import arrives attached to a gap the child is already attending to. That is a shadow test: it explains a live residual rather than arriving as an unattached atom. Fig 8 :27: "describe R, then find the frame that already predicts its shape".

**Refuter:** show that following another's gaze or pointing gives the child no predictive feature it could not have reached from its own closure alone, and that the surplus is zero even where the adult's closure demonstrably differs. Also refuted if the child adopts the pointer without ranging it against its own residual, that is, if the import decides an outcome and has become a verdict (Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one").

### F8. The measurement link is the broken middle link, and the channel can decay while the ground does not (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 3 :25: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 12 :115: "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 2 :37-38: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

Looking time is an instrument for a person's attention, which is a question about people, so human judgment can be the right anchor there; but it is only an instrument for questions about, say, the child's underlying model, and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. The habituation synthesis records exactly this: researchers use dishabituation to infer what infants perceive, yet "dishabituation also is not perceived as a direct measure for mental processes", and a "dichotomy of novelty vs familiar stimuli" complicates interpretation. The predictive-coding synthesis records a second limit: "most research studies testing predictive coding through neural measures (e.g., event-related brain potentials) require responses from the participant, which is not possible for infants."

**Refuter:** produce an instrument that reads the child's per-slot residual without a channel that updates on the researcher's own report, and show it agrees with the ground rather than with the experimenter's expectation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A feature list stays worth attending to across every change of residual and habitat, with the ground paying in each case (breaks Fig 11 :51).
2. A global attention statistic outperforms per-slot residual in predicting where the child looks, across habitats, without smuggling the slot structure back in (breaks Fig 1 :19, :22, :28).
3. Children systematically prefer uncompressed noise or fully predicted input over the intermediate compressible discrepancy, with the ground paying for the preference (breaks Fig 5 :18, :47).
4. An improvement to the child in isolation changes contact-free capability at the ground (breaks Fig 11 :40).
5. Following another's gaze or pointing yields zero surplus even where the adult's closure differs, or the pointer is adopted without being ranged against the child's residual (breaks Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45, Fig 10 :47).
6. An attention instrument reads the residual without a non-updating ground and still agrees with the ground rather than with the experimenter's report (breaks Fig 2 :29, :35, Fig 10 :42).

Any one firing sends the derivation back to the census. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not the list. The four conditions are checkable; a list is not available (F1, F9).
2. Measure per slot. Report residual by feature and by child, never a pooled looking-time average that hides the live slot (F2).
3. Treat novelty as one of four gap kinds, and score the large-and-compressible quantity with an explicit description-length budget (F4).
4. Enumerate the habitat outward before naming actors, and record valence as relative to the residual at that moment (F6).
5. Source-gate every social pointer: where did it come from, which live gap did it explain, and what would make the child refuse it (F7).
6. Repair the measurement link before making claims: name the ground, keep the anchor from updating, and state the instrument's blind axes (F8).
7. Offer the formula as a method with each user's own refuter and mode, not as an answer (Fig 4 :22).

## 7. Conclusion

The residual is a union of four conditions, and the verb is "learn", not "list". What a child learns is a per-slot allocation rule whose valence is relative to the current residual and habitat, seeded by an inherited head start and corrected by imports from differing closures, with a ground that settles which commitments pay. The literal feature list is a `gap`. The derivation is `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, and not `grounded`. Its strongest single line is Fig 11 :51: no standing list survives a change of residual. Its most useful instrument is the boundary diff of Fig 5 :10-14, and its most honest stop is Fig 9 :32: a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
