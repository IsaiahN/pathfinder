**Subject:** One checkable output on how attention gets allocated in development

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know, before showing you where it came from. I am not asking you to agree with it and I am not asking you to read the framework first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A child faces many potentially attendable features and a limited attentional budget. The claim is that "worth attending to" is not a property carried by a feature. It is a relation between the feature, the child's current residual (the per-slot gap between prediction and observation), and the habitat. Four conditions follow: attention is allocated per slot by a boundary diff that sorts the gap into held, new, mis-attached and broken; the valence of a slot flips when the residual changes; the child inherits a head start and imports pointers from other people's differing frames; a ground that does not update on the child's report settles which commitments pay.
>
> The framework's own line is that no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. So I am **not** offering a list of features or an age schedule. It returns a shape, not a name. The literal list is recorded as a `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that novelty alone drives attention, that a pooled looking-time average identifies the live feature, that social pointing is a command rather than a cue that must be ranged against the child's own residual, or that this is settled by experiment. The selected gap is large **and** compressible. Pure noise and the fully familiar both fail. An instrument can be wrong on the axes it does not measure.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a feature that stays worth attending to across every change of residual and habitat, with the ground paying in each case;
> 2. a global attention statistic that predicts where a child looks better than the per-slot residual, without importing the slot structure in disguise;
> 3. a systematic preference for uncompressed noise or for fully predicted input over the intermediate compressible discrepancy, with the ground paying for the preference;
> 4. an improvement to the child in isolation that changes capability while contact is unchanged;
> 5. a case where following another's gaze or pointing yields no surplus even though the other person's frame demonstrably differs.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. My own stake is that I wrote the derivation, so by the framework's rule I declare it and ask you to check the output without holding the machinery.
