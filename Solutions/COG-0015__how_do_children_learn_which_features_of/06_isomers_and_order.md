# Union, functional group, isomer and order in the attention question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural separations, `general` on the ordering rule, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). Has its own falsifier at the end.

## RESIDUAL

The question "which features are worth attending to" arrives bundled. Before searching for an answer we must decide whether it is a partition (separate features we can list) or a union (overlapping conditions that must be unbundled), and whether "worth attending to" is a state we can name or a route we must compose. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Atoms and relations already in play: the held library Γ and its priors; object slots; the prediction and observation pair; the four gap kinds; the habitat actors, conditions and relations; imported pointers; a non-updating ground. ## DERIVATION

### Q1. Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".

The four conditions of `01` section 0 overlap: the same caregiver is a habitat actor (D2) and an import source (D3); the same slot is a residual in D1 and a measurement in D4; the same head start is a prior in D1 and an inheritance in D3. So this is a union. One lever, for example boosting novelty, clears only the region where novelty alone binds and leaves the intersections standing, because the other conditions still bind. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged into a single "attention weight".

**Falsifier for Q1:** if the four conditions turn out to be separable with an assignable share of the variance in children's allocation, the union claim fails and a partition search is warranted.

### Q2. Is "worth attending to" a state or a route?

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

A standing list of features names states. The question asks for a route: how a slot comes to be attended to. That route needs ordered bonds. Operators table :33: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." "Feature attended to for goal G" is not the same term as "goal G reached by attending to feature", so a symmetric co-presence bond will not carry the answer. Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence."

**Falsifier for Q2:** if a symmetric co-occurrence term predicts the child's next look as well as an ordered term, the route reading is unnecessary and the state reading stands.

### Q3. Functional group or isomer?

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Two attentional regimes that share the same feature set, for example the same objects, the same caregiver and the same prior, but differ in which bond mediates which slot, are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different arrangement, different outcome. The difference is invisible in a feature list and decisive in behaviour. This is the strongest structural claim available: not "which features" but "which arrangement of the same features".

If the two regimes share only the budget and no atoms, they are not isomers; they are two members of a **functional group**, the group being "a per-slot boundary diff with a ground". The weaker claim survives even when the stronger one fails, just as the exemplar's weaker functional-group reading survived when only Time was shared.

**Falsifier for Q3:** if two habitats with identical feature sets and identical bond arrangements yield identical attentional valence at the ground, the isomer claim fails. If they share no atoms at all, the functional-group claim is the only one left. Both outcomes are recorded, not smoothed.

### Q4. What order does the derivation run in?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Order: perception (Fig 3 :5), then terms for affordances (Fig 3 :7), then the objective in the child's own terms (Fig 3 :10), then the boundary diff, then learn and carry. Fig 3 :25: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link, terms for what a situation affords plus what counts as progress, is where the research chain usually breaks, and that is where the instrument is owed.

The "learn and carry" link is cross-scale. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A settled attentional habit becomes an atom at the next developmental stage, so the membrane carries a method and a head start, never a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22).

### Q5. The analogue that carries the mechanism

Habituation with dishabituation is the closest world analogue of the boundary diff: a model of the expected stimulus is built, the experienced stimulus is compared with it, and a match inhibits while a change releases the orienting response. The analogy is retained as an analogue, not promoted to a figure line. Its limits are recorded: the encyclopedia synthesis notes that dishabituation "is not perceived as a direct measure for mental processes", and the curiosity synthesis notes that surprise can fuel exploration more than novelty alone. So the analogue supports the *shape* of a comparator, not the identity of the underlying mechanism. Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a feature partition (Q1). "Worth attending to" is a route requiring ordered bonds, not a state name (Q2). Two regimes over a shared atom set are isomers; over disjoint sets they are members of one functional group (Q3). The derivation runs perception, affordances, objective, boundary diff, learn and carry, and the instrument is owed at the middle link (Q4). Habituation is an analogue of the comparator, with its own measurement caveat (Q5). No new primitive and no wall-moving import is required for any of this. The feature list itself remains a `gap`.
