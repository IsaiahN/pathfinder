# Order, bonds and isomers in the activity set

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground
reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question gives three activity names and two outcome families. Two questions are owed: (1) what
orders the three activities, if anything, and (2) are they a partition, a union, or isomers over a
shared set of activity atoms? No new primitive is available without import.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms read off the world: child, material or affordance, other people, an objective, a standard, a
representation. Bonds held: the seven, including sequence (→), production (⇒), comparison (⋛) and
the symmetric conjunction (+) and identity (≡). The habitat figures (Fig 11), not the physical

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What order, if any, holds among play, work and storytelling?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The chain is perceive the
  affordance, have terms for what it affords, say what counts as progress, plan, carry what worked.
- Fig 13 :4-5 says of its own set that *"the two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role
  rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* Applied only as an
  analogy to activity forms, this rules out ranking play below work below storytelling or any other
  ladder. **The activities are not ordered by seniority.**
- Fig 3 :24-27 governs the only meaningful ordering: the objective link depends on the vocabulary
  link, which depends on perception. So if the activities differ, the difference is ordered by the
  dependency chain, not by a maturity sequence.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a study showing a fixed developmental sequence in which one
  activity necessarily precedes another, with the middle link held constant, would break the
  no-seniority reading and reinstate a ladder.

### Q2 : Partition, union or isomers?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of
  separate ones."* Play and work overlap in the world (*"Many communities use play to emulate work"*),
  and storytelling is itself listed as a play pattern by the National Institute for Play. So the
  three are **not a partition** (F1 of `01`).
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the
  formula and decisive in the behaviour."** If the three activities are written over the shared atom
  set `{child, material, other people, objective, standard, representation}`, they are **isomers**:
  same atoms, different arrangement, different developmental substance.
- Specifically: work typically binds the objective to an externally supplied standard; play binds
  the objective to a child-generated one; storytelling binds the representation to other people via
  a method rather than to the material alone. These are three different bond arrangements over the
  same atoms, not three different ingredient lists.
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."** All three share the functional group
  `child - objective - material`, so a measurement that records only the group will find no
  difference between them and will conclude, wrongly, that activity form does not matter.
- **Falsifier:** if two activities can be written without an atom the others require, for example
  storytelling with no other people and no representation, the isomer claim fails and only the
  functional-group claim survives.

### Q3 : Which bonds, and with what mode?

- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Work's
  external standard makes progress a subtraction from a target state. Play's rule-changing makes the
  plan a sequence the child can rewrite. Storytelling's possible-world comparison makes the goal a
  comparison.
- Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* fits the moment a child first represents
  another mind in a narrative. **But this is our binding, not a figure line.** The figures define
  production; they do not assign it to storytelling. F3 of `01` records the retraction of the earlier
  overstatement and marks the whole assignment `specified`, ungrounded.
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* This is the strongest figure-grounded reason to
  keep the three verdicts separate rather than pooling them.
- **Falsifier:** two activities shown to have the same bond arrangement over the shared atom set
  collapse the isomer claim, even if their surface vocabulary differs.

### Q4 : What is the low-overlap analogue?

Activity theory (opened 2026-11-22) gives a genuinely different frame from the figure text. Kuutti:
*"An activity is a form of doing directed to an object, and activities are distinguished from each
other according to their objects. Transforming the object into an outcome motivates the existence of
an activity."* The page also describes an activity as an *"object-oriented, collective and culturally
mediated human activity, or activity system"* with object, subject, mediating artifacts, rules,
community and division of labour.

This corroborates two things without overruling either: that the activities are distinguished by
their objects (the objective link of Fig 3), and that the unit of analysis is a system, not an
isolated child (Fig 11 :39). Its independence is limited: activity theory shares the Vygotskian pool
with the Zone of proximal development page, so these two together are one witness, not two. Fig 2
:31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

### Q5 : What crosses between a child and a culture?

- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A story retold as a method for
  modelling another mind crosses the membrane. A verbatim script is a recording and does not.
- Vygotsky's *"internal reconstruction of an external operation"*, as reported on the Activity theory
  page, names the same direction in the field's own vocabulary: an external operation becomes an
  internal one. This is corroboration from a different host, not a figure derivation.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A skill for
  taking another's perspective, once settled, becomes an operand for the next interaction. This is the recursion,
  not an import that moves a wall.

## CONCLUSION

The three activities are not seniority-ordered. They are a union with overlap and, read over a shared
atom set, **isomers**: same atoms, different bond arrangement, different substance. Their common
functional group explains why coarse measurement finds no difference between them and why a per-slot
instrument is owed. The specific bond assigned to each activity is a `specified` binding, not a
figure derivation. The low-overlap analogue (activity theory) corroborates the objective link and the
system-level unit of analysis, but shares a Vygotskian pool with the ZPD source, so it is one witness.
