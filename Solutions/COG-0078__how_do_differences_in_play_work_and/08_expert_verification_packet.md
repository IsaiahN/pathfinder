# Expert verification packet: the Tether on play, work, storytelling, learning and social imagination

**What this is:** a claim in a form a developmental or cultural psychologist can check. Mode,
derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what
would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structure, `gap` on slot assignment and
magnitudes. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle this packet.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Play, work and storytelling are three overlapping habitat substitutions, not three doses of one
enrichment variable and not a developmental ladder. Their effect lives in the child-and-habitat
system: an activity changes what the child is in contact with and which per-slot residuals are live,
and a single global score hides exactly the signal that says where to look. The three differ most at
the objective and vocabulary link of the dependency chain, the link that currently has no instrument.
Over a shared atom set they are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, and their common functional
group explains why coarse measures find no difference among them. Storytelling transmits a reusable
method for modelling other minds when it transmits a method, and a single occasion when it transmits
a script. The framework does not name which activity fills which slot or give any effect size. What it
offers is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would
close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal and measurement-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11
- **Statement:** effects are per slot, and an activity acts by changing contact, not by improving the
  child in isolation. No activity has a fixed valence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22
  *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :40
  *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11
  :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :49
  *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** is there any measurement in which a global development score preserves all
  per-slot residuals and predicts where to look next? Is there any case of improvement with no
  contact change?
- **Refuter:** a global score that preserves all live slots (breaks R1), or a within-child improvement
  with no contact change (breaks R2).

### C2 : method-facing; checkable by a researcher against the dependency-chain literature
- **Statement:** the three activities differ at the objective/vocabulary link, not at perception or
  planning, and that link has no instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :24 *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."* Fig 3 :26
  *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious
  tests."* Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has
  looked at."* Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a
  subtraction."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- **What to check:** can you name an instrument that measures, from the child's own terms, what counts
  as progress in free play, in a work task with an external standard, and in a storytelling episode?
  If the answer is yes for perception or planning but not here, the claim stands. Cankaya et al. 2023
  independently state the absence for loose parts play.
- **Refuter:** two activities shown to share the same objective/vocabulary structure and to differ
  only at perception or planning (breaks R3).

### C3 : framework-internal with a world-facing half; checkable against narrative and ToM work
- **Statement:** storytelling transmits a method, not a recording, and a shared story is a convention
  until it decides an outcome. Over the shared activity atoms the three forms are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25
  *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 2 :41
  *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 10 :47
  *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different
  bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the
  behaviour."*
- **What to check:** does a fixed script produce the same reusable perspective-taking effect as a
  method for generating stories? Do two activities require different atoms, which would collapse the
  isomer claim to the functional-group claim?
- **Refuter:** a verbatim recording producing the same effect as a method (breaks R4); two activities
  written without an atom the others require (breaks the isomer clause).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of play, work and storytelling, and not a developmental ladder.
- Not which activity fills which slot, and not any effect size, sample size or age norm. Those are a
  declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that the figures derive the bond assignment. The figures define bonds; the assignment is our
  `specified` binding and the earlier overstatement is retracted in `01` F3.
- Not that the six-term physical substrate governs activity forms. That strain was re-censused to the
  habitat and dependency figures (`01` S1).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that agreement from the eight essays is independent corroboration. They share an author and an
  evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried):
an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A contact discriminator separating a change in what the child is in contact with from a change
   internal to the child.
2. An instrument for the objective/vocabulary link, measuring what counts as progress from the
   child's own terms in each activity.
3. A method-versus-recording test for a story handed to a child: reuse of a model versus replay of one
   occasion.
4. Two genuinely low-overlap frames, sharing no data, ranged against the same ground (Fig 2 :31).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 3 :24-27; Fig 11 :39, :40, :49; Fig 12 :94, :117; Fig 4 :22, :25. Nine
   short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: can you name an instrument for the objective link, or is the
   absence real?
3. Name one design that would distinguish a habitat change from a within-child change, or say why
   none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
