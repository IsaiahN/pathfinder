# The Tether's answer: stories, collective memory, moral identity, political belonging

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7). No person, group or institution was measured. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).

## 1. The residual it answers

A person holds a library of terms about what is right and who is "us". A story or collective memory is an action from another frame. The residual is the gap between what that story predicts about subsequent judgment and alignment, and what the ground actually returns. The question is a **union compound** with two conditions, not one hard question: Fig 9 :4-5 *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, narrative transmission. Triangulated (Fig 4 + Fig 6/Fig 8).** A story crosses a directional membrane from the population scale to the person scale. Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What arrives as a reapplicable method makes the next search cheaper; what arrives as a fixed recording produces repetition: *"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."* The surplus exists because closures differ (Fig 8 :45-46 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone. Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."*), and an import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13).

**D2, moral identity. Triangulated for the shape (Fig 12 + Fig 9 + Fig 11); gap on any specific content.** Identity is not a theme list. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112-113: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* A narrative without a refusal can only accumulate obligations (Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*). And a change is real only if it changes contact: Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Which particular story is required is not given: Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

**D3, political belonging. Triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 10 + Fig 7); gap on any specific boundary.** Belonging is the convention half. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* and Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Those two rules govern different halves and do not conflict after re-census. Guard: Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* A retold chain is a mirror chain unless each room differs and matches the residual the last one surfaced: Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* and :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Valence is relational: Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

**Across all three:** collective memory is the lineage that makes the identity term checkable, not the current statement of it. Fig 13 :128-129 *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* Fig 13 :130-131 *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."* Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 3. The one-line answer

Stories and collective memories shape identity and belonging by importing bonds across a directional filter, not by replacing beliefs; their agreement is the product for the convention of "us" and disqualifying for any verdict about the world; they change something only when contact changes; and the identity they produce is checkable only through the lineage that produced it.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular myth, commemoration or national narrative is required. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that shared memory is bad for belonging. Agreement about shared meaning is the product in the convention half.
- Not that a community's self-account is false because it is shared. It is unverified as a world-verdict until ranged against a ground.
- Not that a person's identity can be read off their self-description. The route is not recoverable from the artefact (Fig 13 :128-129).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles `left(R, φ)` here.
- Not a diagnosis of any real group. Any such claim would be an import and needs provenance.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

Fig 2 :36-37 makes human judgment the legitimate anchor when the question is constitutively about people, and :37 adds *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* So the ground is structured judgment of conduct, not consensus. The discriminating tests are:

1. **Contact test.** Find a case where a narrative changes self-description only and another where it changes behaviour and alliance. If both are reported as "changed identity" by the same instrument, the instrument is measuring vocabulary, not contact, and Fig 11 :40 is being ignored.
2. **Bond test.** Find a story that changes conduct by vocabulary alone, with no ordering, comparison, progress or refusal. If it works reliably, the bond claim (Fig 12 :111-113) fails.
3. **Valence test.** Find a shared founding narrative whose agreement raises the truth of a world-verdict while the agreeing frames share one evidence pool. If it does, Fig 2 :40 fails.
4. **Lineage test.** Find a person whose identity is fully checkable from their present self-report without any route record. If so, Fig 13 :128-130 fails.
5. **Convention test.** Find a belonging convention used as the final authority on an outcome that remains revisable and non-decisive. If so, Fig 2 :43 fails.

A search that finds none of these is not a proof they cannot occur: Fig 8 :39 *"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* It is an honest stop at the available instruments.

## 6. The honest stop

The shape stops where the instrument stops. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the framework cannot name the missing primitive without already holding it. What remains unmeasured here is large: the actual contact changes produced by actual narratives in actual communities. Those require the long, multi-generational, multi-disciplinary work the Cordonnier paper calls for, and they require frames that differ from one another. This derivation does not supply them.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which founding story matters. It says that the question is two loops, not one: a verdict about what is true, where agreement among people who share evidence is disqualifying, and a convention about who is "us", where agreement about shared meaning is the product. It says a story reaches a person through a directional filter as an import carrying bonds, not just themes, and that a recording passed down produces repetition while a method passed down produces new reach. It says a narrative without a way to refuse can only accumulate, and that nothing has changed unless what people do and who they relate to has changed. It says a present self-story cannot be checked from itself, only from the lineage that produced it. What it offers is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific story stays open until a ground settles it.
