**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used or supplied here.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about collective memory and belonging, before any theory

> Hello [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: how stories and collective memories change a person's moral identity and political belonging. I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything.
>
> **The output (stated as `specified`, not `grounded`):** the question splits into two loops that obey opposite rules about agreement.
>
> 1. In the verdict half (what happened, what is right), agreement among people who draw on the same evidence cannot raise the truth of the claim. It is a convention acting like a ground.
> 2. In the convention half (who counts as "us"), agreement about shared meaning is the product. A founding narrative does its work by producing that shared meaning. But the moment the narrative is used to decide an outcome, for example who may belong or who may decide, it stops being a convention and becomes an authority that cannot be corrected.
>
> The predicted mechanism is that a story crosses into a person as an import that carries structure, not just themes: an ordered account, a comparison, a sense of progress, and a way to refuse or revise. What gets passed down as a fixed recording produces repetition; what gets passed down as a reapplicable method produces new reach. And a change counts as real only if it changes what people actually do and who they actually relate to. A more vivid self-description with no change in conduct is, on this account, no change at all.
>
> **What this does not claim:** that a particular founding myth is required, that any specific community is being diagnosed, that shared memory is bad for belonging, or that this has been measured. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before contacting anyone:**
> 1. A shared narrative's agreement raises the truth of a world-verdict while the agreeing people draw on one evidence pool.
> 2. A story changes observable behaviour, alliances and boundaries while leaving contact with the ground unchanged.
> 3. A story changes moral and political conduct by vocabulary alone, with no change in ordering, comparison, progress or the ability to refuse.
> 4. A person's identity is fully checkable from their current self-description with no account of the route that produced it.
> 5. A belonging convention used as the final authority on an outcome stays revisable and non-decisive.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 2, only if you are curious and only after you have recorded a verdict on the above:** I can send the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **Stage 3, only if you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. If you run it on a problem you can check, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]

## Sending rules

1. Send to one person at a time through the public, opt-in channels in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Do not scrape personal addresses.
2. Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 before sending any source link or framework description.
3. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: agreement that shares an evidence pool is the pool talking.
4. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
