# Are the two halves isomers? Bond structure, order and two analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` established a union of two loops over the same incoming material: a story and a collective memory. Two questions are owed. (1) If both halves are built from the same atoms, are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and which bond distinguishes them? (2) What is the dependency order, and which operation must come first?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this derivation: a frame (the person), a story (a bonded arrangement of events), an import (material crossing a membrane), a ground (what settles), a convention (shared meaning), a refusal (¬). Bonds held: the seven, including → sequence, ⋛ comparison, ≡ identity and + conjunction, plus negation as the unary eighth operator. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Do the two halves share an atom set?

They do not share a story in the sense of a single content. They share a **role structure**: something crosses a membrane, is admitted into a frame, and is ranged against something. Written minimally:

- Verdict half: `import → frame ⋛ ground` (the comparison bond tests the story against what settles).
- Convention half: `import → frame ≡ "we"` (identity bond: two names, one referent, the group and its story).

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Both halves have the same ingredients (import, frame, bond, ground reference). They differ in which bond holds. Under Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* the two are not variants of one another. They are different substances sharing an ingredient list.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either half can be written without an atom the other requires, for example a convention with no ground reference at all, or a verdict with no import, the isomer claim fails and the two are merely analogous.

### Q2 — Which bonds, exactly?

Fig 12 :55 and :67 give the symmetric bonds: `+` *"both hold at once"*, `≡` *"two names, one referent"*. Fig 12 :85-89 gives ⋛ comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, identified by *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*

- **Convention half → `≡`.** A master narrative and the group are two names for one referent in the eyes of members. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* describes exactly what a symmetric identity bond produces: agreement is the product.
- **Verdict half → `⋛`.** The story is ranged against the ground; the anchor is not a third party to the disagreement (Fig 2 :46 *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."*). Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- **Both halves need `→`.** Fig 12 :71 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Without sequence, a story cannot carry a route, only a state (Fig 12 :111).
- **Both halves need `¬` if they are to be corrigible.** Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."*

**Strain, named:** ⋛ is defined as a threshold relation between two *quantities*. In the verdict half the operands are a story and a ground reading. Whether a ground reading counts as a quantity at this level is a type question. It is recorded as a strain, not smoothed. The exemplar had the same open type question at second order; here it is at first order because the operands are heterogeneous.

### Q3 — What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The order is forced by three separate figure rules, which must not be read against one another:

1. **Describe the residual before naming a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*
2. **Refuse before mint.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 sorts a wrong term before offering a new one; the fifth bin is REFUTED, *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*. A positive account of identity cannot be minted on top of an unrefused false claim about the past.
3. **Settle before composing over.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."* A story that has not been ranged against the ground cannot become an atom for the next link.

So the loop order for this question is: describe the effect → refuse the false term → admit the import with provenance → test contact → only then compose the identity and belonging claims. Putting the identity claim first is the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 names.

**Falsifier for the order:** exhibit a case where a group's belonging claim is settled against the ground before its historical narrative is described and before any false term is refused. If that works and survives contact, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 — The two analogues the question needs

**(a) Recording versus method (membrane analogue).** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Garland et al. (2021), opened this session, found that whale song changes in complexity and is punctuated by rapid replacement from a neighbouring population, and that *"it is the ability of the singer to adopt novelty and change their songs that is important"* while the artefact may not be *"particularly meaningful"*. The engineering analogue is SLSA v1.2, opened this session: *"provenance doesn't do anything unless somebody inspects it."* A supplied attestation and an inspected attestation are the same artefact with different contact. A remembered story and a checked story are the same artefact with different contact. **Falsifier:** show a case where a fixed recording and a reapplicable method produce identical reach under identical contact.

**(b) Shared pretext, verdict versus convention (valence analogue).** *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened this session: *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."* This is Fig 2 :40-41 applied directly to stories. It is written by the framework's author and shares the framework's evidence pool, so it corroborates internal consistency, not truth. The independent analogue is the social-psychological finding that master narratives synchronize members' predictions and facilitate belonging (Bouizegarene et al. 2024, opened), which predicts the convention half without importing the framework's vocabulary. **Falsifier:** show a shared narrative whose agreement improves a world-verdict while the frames share an evidence pool.

### Q5 — Why the isomer reading matters for the answer

It prevents the most common failure in this literature: treating a well-functioning belonging convention as if it were evidence for a moral verdict, or treating a failed verdict as if it were a failed convention. Under Fig 12, those are different substances. A correction to one is not a correction to the other. Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The isomer claim is what makes that guard visible: the moment the `≡` bond is replaced by a deciding `⋛` bond, the substance changes.

## CONCLUSION

The two halves are **isomers over a shared role structure**: same atoms (import, frame, ground reference, bond), different bond (`≡` for the convention half, `⋛` for the verdict half), different behaviour. Both need `→` to carry a route and `¬` to be corrigible. The dependency order is describe → refuse → import with provenance → test contact → compose. Two analogues, one from animal behaviour and software engineering and one from cognitive science, corroborate the predictions but share no evidence pool with the figures. One strain remains open: the operand type of ⋛ when one operand is a ground reading. It is named, not hidden.
