## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about how stories and collective memories shape moral identity and political belonging. Option A: treat this as one question about belief change, where a shared narrative reshapes what people believe and agreement among tellers is evidence that the reshaping worked. Option B: unbundle it into a union of two loops with opposite rules about agreement, where the story is an import carrying bonds across a directional filter, change is real only if contact changes, and the identity term is checkable only through its lineage. What each leads to: A produces a single averaged story in which a convention is silently promoted to a verdict, and it makes agreement do the work of a ground. B produces two separate verdicts, five stated refuters, and an open slot where a specific story would have to be imported with provenance.

- **R (the residual).** The gap, per slot, between what an incoming story predicts about a person's judgments and alignments and what the ground returns. Scale: crosses the membrane from population to person (Fig 4 :21).
- **Domain 1, narrative transmission.** Actors: teller, listener, events and agents named, bonds, medium. Currency: bits to state and actions to test (Fig 12 :125-126). Anchor: the ground for whether anything registered, a recall or behavioural change; if nothing ever registered, this domain has no contact yet. Question type: partition (what crosses the filter and in which direction).
- **Domain 2, moral identity.** Actors: the person as frame, the library, the belief, the weighting, candidate terms, refusals. Currency: per-slot residuals and the bargain between term cost and confusion removed. Anchor: structured human judgment of conduct, which is legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-37), never agreement among tellers. Question type: union of verdict and convention.
- **Domain 3, political belonging.** Actors: the room, the habitat, prospective members, shared narratives, boundary-makers. Currency: agreement and ground pressure. Anchor: shared meaning for the convention half, the ground for any claim the convention makes about the world. Question type: union of convention and verdict.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse a false term before minting a new one (Formula step 2); admit the import with provenance before composing over it; test contact before declaring a change; only then compose the identity and belonging claims.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any specific story does not. The question is a union. Stories and collective memories cross a directional membrane as imports that carry bonds, not merely vocabulary; a recording passed down produces repetition, a method passed down produces new reach. In the verdict half, agreement among frames sharing evidence is disqualifying; in the convention half, agreement about shared meaning is the product, and the moment the convention decides an outcome it stops being a convention. A narrative with no refusal can only accumulate. Nothing has changed unless contact changed. Identity is checkable only through the lineage. Which story, which myth and which boundary are required is a gap: the framework returns a shape, not a name, and this run has no ground. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** if the valence split is wrong, there are two symmetric costs. Treating a convention as a verdict demands impossible independence from people whose shared meaning is the thing being built, and delegitimizes ordinary belonging. Treating a verdict as a convention lets a narrative decide an outcome it cannot settle, which Fig 2 :43 names and which is borne by whoever is excluded or by whatever ground claim goes uncorrected. Fig 2 :42 'Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.' The avoidable part is spending the independence of outside readers by sending them a recording instead of a generator.
- **undoable:** yes. The claim is local to this derivation. A refuter removes one sub-claim without withdrawing any framework primitive, and the packet can be superseded. No world source would have to be retracted; only its interpretation. A sent claim can be corrected because the record of the route is kept.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A structured comparison of narrative-only change against contact change (Fig 11 :40), a vocabulary-only conduct change (Fig 12 :111-113), a shared-narrative truth gain at maximum independence (Fig 2 :40), a self-report checkable with no route record (Fig 13 :128-130), or a deciding convention that stays revisable (Fig 2 :43) would each reverse the relevant sub-claim. A figure re-census that collapses the union into one residual would reverse the whole shape. Prestige, citation count and institutional uptake are not such instruments (Fig 9 :60).
