# Expert verification packet — the Tether on stories, collective memory and belonging

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in collective memory, narrative identity, political psychology or cognitive science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of people or groups is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question splits into a union of two loops with opposite rules about agreement. In the verdict half (what happened, what is right), agreement among frames that share an evidence pool cannot raise the truth of a claim; it is a convention acting like a ground. In the convention half (who counts as "us"), agreement about shared meaning is the product, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. The mechanism is an import across a directional membrane: a story carries bonds (sequence, comparison, subtraction, refusal), not just vocabulary, and what crosses as a reapplicable method increases reach while what crosses as a fixed recording produces repetition. Nothing has changed unless contact with the ground changed. The identity term is checkable only through its lineage, never from its current statement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** agreement has opposite valence in the two halves. This is not a general claim that agreement is good or bad; it is a claim about which quantity agreement bears on.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **What to check:** does the rule actually distinguish a verdict from a convention, or is it being used to protect a preferred conclusion? Is the claimed guard at the point where a shared story starts deciding who belongs or who may decide?
- **Refuter:** produce a case where agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool raises the truth of a world-verdict, or a convention that remains revisable and non-decisive while serving as the final authority on an outcome.

### C2 — Cognitive and social facing; checkable against the narrative and memory literatures

- **Statement:** the structure of a story, not its theme list, is what can change a route through the world. A narrative with no refusal operator can only accumulate; a passed-down recording produces repetition, a passed-down method produces new reach.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112-113 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25-26 *"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."*
- **What to check:** is this a fair reading of what narrative structure does in your field? Bouizegarene et al. (2024) report that master narratives synchronize members' predictions, facilitate belonging and are rigid; Garland et al. (2021) found that in humpback whale song the artefact may be arbitrary while the singer's ability to adopt novelty is what matters. Do those findings support, or merely resemble, the claim?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a story that changes observable moral conduct and alliance by vocabulary alone, with no change in ordering, comparison, progress or the possibility of refusal; or show a fixed recording and a reapplicable method with identical reach under identical contact.

### C3 — Framework-internal derivation with an empirical edge; checkable by anyone who studies transmission

- **Statement:** a chain of agreeing retellings adds vocabulary and not ground-bearing evidence; the identity term is checkable only through the lineage that produced it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :12-13 *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"* Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 13 :128-129 *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** Cordonnier et al. (2022) found that by the third generation detail-rich family memories are lost or reduced to a gist while feelings of victimisation persist. Loftus, quoted in Shaw (2016), requires *"independent corroboration"* to distinguish an authentic memory from one produced by another process. Do these support the claimed lossy transform and lineage requirement, or do they show something else?
- **Refuter:** show a chain of retellings in which each step's agreement adds new ground-bearing evidence without any step differing in closure or matching a surfaced residual; or show a person's present identity fully checkable without the route that produced it; or show an adopted import distinguishable from a convergent derivation by content alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a particular founding myth, commemoration or national narrative is required. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that shared memory harms belonging. Agreement is the product in the convention half.
- Not that a community's self-account is false because it is shared. It is only unverified as a world-verdict until ranged against a ground.
- Not that the framework can name the missing thing. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`, and not a diagnosis of any real group. Any such claim would be an import requiring provenance.
- Not independent corroboration from this packet's own author: the derivation is simulated and checked for internal consistency against the figures, against essays sharing the framework's evidence pool, and against six world sources opened in a different pool. The second seat in `11_audit.md` also shares the primary seat's pool, so its agreement is not a witness.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement. The discriminating observations are:

1. **Contact:** a narrative that changes only self-description versus one that changes conduct and alliance, measured with the same instrument. If the instrument cannot tell them apart, it measures vocabulary, not contact (Fig 11 :40).
2. **Bond:** a story that changes conduct with vocabulary alone, no sequence, comparison, subtraction or refusal (Fig 12 :111-113).
3. **Valence:** a shared narrative whose agreement raises the truth of a world-verdict while its frames share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
4. **Lineage:** a self-report fully checkable with no route record (Fig 13 :128-130).
5. **Convention:** a deciding convention that remains revisable (Fig 2 :43).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :39-43, Fig 4 :21-26, Fig 7 :12-13 and :36, Fig 12 :111-113, and Fig 13 :128-131. Six short passages.
2. Decide whether C1's valence split is a real distinction in your field or an artifact of the framework's vocabulary. Name one case from your own work that would falsify it.
3. Check C2 against one concrete narrative you know: can it refuse anything? What method, if any, crossed with it?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a change in self-description from a change in contact, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
