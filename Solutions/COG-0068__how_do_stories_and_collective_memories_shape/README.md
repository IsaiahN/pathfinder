## What an answer would look like (Direction term, bound before the run)

An answer would have to say, in effect terms: which quantity in a person changes when a story is told or remembered; what crosses from the group to the person and in which direction; why agreement among tellers is evidence in one half of the question and disqualifying in the other; and what observation would show that the change was real rather than only a new self-description. Written down and bound as an ordinary term, that shape is refusable by the same test as anything else (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66).

The working answer is: **a union of two loops, not one**. Stories and collective memories cross a directional membrane into a person's frame as imports that carry bonds, not just vocabulary. In the verdict half (what is right and what happened), agreement is disqualifying and the import needs a source gate; in the convention half (who is "us"), agreement about shared meaning is the product, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. A change is real only if it changes contact with the ground. The framework returns these shapes, not the name of any particular story.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the derivation.
- No bulk-send plan, and no claim that agreement from a list of receptive readers would corroborate anything.

Reason, in the figures' own words: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40) and *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless as a verdict, while confusing the convention half of this question with the verdict half. The outreach artifacts ask for a refutation, not an endorsement.
