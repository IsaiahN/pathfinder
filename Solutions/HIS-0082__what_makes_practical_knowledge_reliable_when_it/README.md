# Solution HIS-0082

**Question:** What makes practical knowledge reliable when it is transmitted through apprenticeship rather than explicit theory?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 101

**Section:** 9. Knowledge outside formal science

**Field for public contact points:** apprenticeship and practical knowledge research

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separator and the reliability conditions, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the concrete practice list and any numerical reliability coefficient, not `grounded`. Every substantive file labels this a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending (Registry ingest is outside this folder)

## What this folder answers

Restated as an effect: R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| stays small across learners and across a lineage when the transmission channel carries a generator (a method plus its arrangement) and keeps a non-updating ground reachable at each slot. R grows when the channel carries a recording of one occasion, when the arrangement is not preserved, or when the reference moves from the ground to agreement with a master's report. The question asks which conditions hold. It is a union of conditions, not one dial with a right setting, and the verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged.
