# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 are checked against the verbatim figure text by the sender. A stage-1 claim that cannot survive its own refuter collapses the two-stage offer on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would you refute this shape of interaction for learning a person's expectations?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: what kinds of interaction let an AI system learn the perspectives and expectations of *different* people, rather than an average person? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** four necessary conditions on the interaction, kept separate and never averaged into one score.
>
> 1. **Contact with a differing closure.** The interaction must put the system in touch with a frame whose library differs from its own, because a person's expectation is underivable from the system's own closed set. A corpus is a weaker version of this; a live person is a peer frame.
> 2. **Per-person attribution.** The prediction about each person must be scored as that person's residual. A single pooled satisfaction or accuracy score throws away the only signal that says where to look.
> 3. **An anchor the system cannot move.** The person's response must be a reading the system did not author. If the person only sees the system's own summaries, the two converge coherently and neither notices the drift. Because the question is about people, the person's own judgment in a novel situation is the legitimate anchor, not a proxy metric.
> 4. **History and provenance, with room for refusal.** What has to travel is the person's history and how they weigh it, carried in effect terms and marked as coming from that person. The interaction must be able to revoke the system's generic prediction about "someone like this," not just append more detail to it.
>
> Two further conditions are attached in the packet: the interaction should draw on people with genuinely separate evidence (independence is the parameter, not headcount), and it should be situated in the real habitat rather than a substituted test harness. I do **not** claim that any one named protocol, dialogue, demonstration, correction, imitation, role-play, satisfies these best. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which protocol wins is an open `gap` for me, not a hidden answer.
>
> **The six refuters, written before this email:** (1) per-person residual drops with no new contact with a differing frame; (2) one pooled signal does as well as per-person attribution; (3) people who only see the system's own outputs give the same held-out accuracy as people reached through an unupdating reading; (4) removing a person's history and situated corrections changes nothing; (5) the model never refuses a held generic prediction after repeated attributed contradictions; (6) a substituted synthetic habitat transfers to the real one with no loss. If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the condition. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a result about any deployed system; that I have measured which interaction protocol works best; that text alone cannot produce correct answers on theory-of-mind tasks (there is good evidence that it can, and I treat that as a source-discrimination question, not a refutation of the shape); or that a larger model in isolation would help. No measurement of an AI system was taken here. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the three sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Two low-overlap external checks: Rendell et al. 2010, "Why Copy Others?", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2989663/ ; Lorenz et al. 2011, "How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** run the eight-step loop on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
