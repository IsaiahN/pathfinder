# Expert verification packet - the Tether on executive pay

**What this is:** a claim in a form a compensation researcher or governance scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a single percentage. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of a named executive was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is executive pay driven mainly by productivity and scarce talent, or by bargaining power and weak governance?" is not one well-formed question. It bundles at least three overlapping conditions: a price set in an external talent market, a pay decision made inside a boardroom, and the institutions (tax, accounting, disclosure, say-on-pay) that shape both. A case where two conditions bind is categorically different from either alone. Pay is indexed per executive slot, never averaged. The anchor for any verdict must be an outcome the executive did not move, and the missing object is a source-discriminating instrument that separates marginal product, costly signalling, and rent at the level of the named slot. Governance has valence relative to the residual, so no arrangement is a standing villain. The framework returns this shape and its refuters; it does not return a percentage split.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - union and per-slot indexing; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** the either/or is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition with one dominant cause, and R is indexed per executive slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Also Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* And Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **World cross-check:** Edmans, Gabaix and Jenter, NBER WP 23596 (2017), describe *"three non-exclusive explanations"* and conclude *"no single explanation can account for all facts and historical trends"*.
- **Refuter:** a single intervention that fixes both the price condition and the room condition in every failure type and leaves no categorically different residue; or an averaged industry statistic that prospectively predicts which named executive slot carries a rent component.

### C2 - the anchor and the mirror chain; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 7

- **Statement:** the anchor is an outcome the executive did not move; a room whose reference updates on peer pay converges on influence rather than on the anchor. Reported pay and an uncontested share price are instruments, not anchors.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 - *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :14-15 - *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **World cross-check:** the peer-benchmarking practice of pegging pay to the 50th, 75th or 90th percentile of peer firms, and the confounding literature's definition of a common cause (firm size and industry predict both pay and governance).
- **Refuter:** an outcome the executive cannot move, read by two frames with genuinely different evidence, that orders the two conditions and shows one contributes zero across all slots.

### C3 - the gap and the missing instrument; checkable by reading Figures 9, 13 and 8

- **Statement:** no figure line returns a percentage split between productivity and power. What is missing is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name for a cause.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **World cross-check:** *The Hard Problem is About Source*, metamerism: one reading, several possible sources, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Spence signalling shows that even the talent camp contains a source-discrimination problem.
- **Refuter:** two instruments that discriminate product, signal and rent at slot level from different evidence pools and satisfy the independence condition of Fig 2 :31.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a named executive is overpaid, and not that any firm's pay is wrong.
- Not that one camp wins, and not a percentage split.
- Not that "weak governance" is a standing villain. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51).
- Not that a pay-performance correlation settles the price condition. Settlement is necessary and not sufficient (Fig 5 :69).
- Not that any dataset or natural experiment has been shown to name the source. An import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only account of executive pay.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The discrimination required is: (1) does pay track an outcome the executive did not move; (2) does the committee's reference update on the executive's influence; (3) can two instruments from different evidence pools separate marginal product, costly signal, and rent at the named slot? A ruling that ignores (3) and chooses a camp is a convention that has decided an outcome (Fig 10 :47).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29-30 and :14-15, Fig 7 :36, Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against the NBER survey abstract: does it really list three non-exclusive explanations and deny a single winner?
3. Name one instrument that would separate a costly signal of talent from a rent at the level of a named executive, or say why none is possible.
4. Identify one governance arrangement whose valence flips with the residual, or refute Fig 11 :51 with an invariant one.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
