# The Tether's answer on executive pay differences

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the request for a single percentage. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which of two causes dominates. Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` stay small in some executive slots and grow in others, where `b` is the pay frame's inherited benchmark, `a` is the contract, and `o′` is an outcome the executive did not set? R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22). The answer sought is a conjunction of conditions plus an anchor and a discriminator, written down before the run.

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57). A case where talent and capture both operate is categorically different from either alone and needs its own loop.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

**Loop D1, the executive labour market. Verdict `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, habitat versus membrane).**

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A slot's pay cannot be priced from the executive considered alone. The contribution that would justify a premium is a relation to a habitat and to an outcome that does not update on the executive's own report. World corroboration from information economics: Spence signalling shows that even a high salary interpreted as talent may be a costly signal rather than a marginal product payment. Mode stays `specified`; no firm-level measurement pays for it here. Refuter: a priced contribution invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment for what the executive did.

**Loop D2, the governance and contracting room. Verdict `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, anchor rule versus channel decay).**

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :14-15: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.

Peer benchmarking is a mirror chain. The committee, the consultant and the executive each update on the same peer pay, so the room converges on whoever has the most influence rather than on an independently read outcome. Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* The room is repaired not by more benchmarking but by a chair with a different closure and a maintained channel to an anchor that does not move. Refuter: an outcome the executive cannot move, read by two frames with different evidence, that shows the room's contribution is zero across all slots.

**Loop D3, the measurement frame. Verdict `general` structural with a `specified` consequence, triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9).**

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A variance share is a filter for budget, not a witness for a cause. "Productivity" and "scarce talent" are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49), and "power" and "weak governance" are one frame wearing two names. Counting all four as witnesses double counts (Fig 2 :31). Refuter: an averaged statistic that prospectively predicts which named slot has a rent component.

**Loop D4, institutions and identification. Verdict `general`, single-source for the figure rule with field corroboration.**

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

The only route from the shape to a name is an import from a differing closure, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13). In this field that means an exogenous rule change, a tax or accounting shift, or an independently read instrument. The NBER survey's third explanation, institutional factors such as regulation, taxation, and accounting policy, is exactly this domain, and the survey says no single explanation accounts for all facts. Refuter: two instruments from different evidence pools that discriminate the sources at slot level and satisfy Fig 2 :31.

**Union verdict, kept separate from the loops.** Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersection. Verdicts are not averaged into a percentage.

**The percentage, `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. What is missing is a source-discriminating instrument, not a cause. Salary is a reading that cannot comment on its source.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named executive is overpaid or underpaid. No slot measurement was taken.
- Not that one camp is right and the other wrong. The question is a union, so per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
- Not that "weak governance" is always bad. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51); the same committee can help one residual and hurt another.
- Not that a pay-performance correlation settles the price condition. Settlement is necessary and not sufficient (Fig 5 :69).
- Not that a particular dataset or natural experiment names the source. That would be an import with no source gate or shadow test.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument settled `left(R,φ)`.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely:

- **The outcome test:** does the slot's pay track an outcome the executive demonstrably did not move, rather than the room's own benchmark?
- **The room test:** does the committee's reference update on the executive's influence, so that the chain converges on whoever has most influence (Fig 2 :14-15, Fig 7 :36)?
- **The source test:** can two instruments, drawing on different evidence pools, separate marginal product, costly signal, and rent at the level of the named slot?
- **Standing falsifiers:** the seven conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, written before the run.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The atom list in this derivation is a candidate library, not nature's own list. Naming atoms makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer distinguish the sources, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between talent and power. It says the question is a union of at least three overlapping conditions, a price set in an external talent market, a pay decision made inside a boardroom, and the institutions that shape both, and that a case where two bind is categorically different from either alone. Pay is indexed per executive slot (Fig 1 :19), and the anchor must be an outcome the executive did not move (Fig 2 :29, :30). Peer benchmarking is a mirror chain that converges on whoever has the most influence (Fig 2 :14-15, Fig 7 :36), and the productivity and talent labels are one witness, as the power and weak governance labels are one witness (Fig 8 :48-49). Governance has valence relative to the residual, so no arrangement is a standing villain (Fig 11 :51). What it offers is the shape any answer must have, seven falsifiers written in advance, and the description of the source-discriminating instrument that would close it. It does not return a percentage. That is a declared gap, not silence (Fig 13 :134).
