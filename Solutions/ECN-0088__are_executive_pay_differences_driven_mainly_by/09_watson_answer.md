## QUESTION

Are executive pay differences driven mainly by productivity and scarce talent, or by bargaining power and weak governance? Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` stay small in some executive slots and grow in others, where `b` is the pay frame's inherited benchmark, `a` is the contract, and `o′` is an outcome the executive did not move? R is indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Option A leads to the prediction that the gap is priced by an external talent market and shrinks when measurement improves. Option B leads to the prediction that the gap is set inside a room whose reference updates on the executive's influence and does not shrink when measurement improves. Option C, not offered by the question, is that the two are overlapping conditions with a third compound.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** At least three-place: executive slot, contract, independently read outcome, indexed by slot and ordered by time. Carries an ordered bond (`→`, contract set before outcome read) and a comparison bond (`⋛`, "above peer median"). Not symmetric: pay and outcome are not interchangeable. Scale runs from one slot, to the boardroom, to the population, to the time channel across a career.
- **D1 Executive labour market.** Actors: executives, rival firms, recruiters, private equity. Currency: pay as price of scarce talent. Anchor: an exogenous measure of contribution the executive did not move, nameable in principle but not held here. Admitted.
- **D2 Governance and contracting room.** Actors: board, committee, CEO, consultants, shareholders, proxy advisors. Currency: pay as output of a bargaining process. Anchor: a CEO-recused decision or an imposed rule. Admitted.
- **D3 Measurement frame.** Actors: researchers, datasets, regressions. Currency: estimated pay-outcome sensitivity. Anchor: the data, but all studies share one evidence pool. Admitted with a low-independence flag.
- **D4 Institutions and identification.** Actors: regulators, tax code, accounting standards, exchange rules. Currency: exogenous rule changes. Anchor: an enacted change the executive did not choose. Admitted.
- **X1 Talent as a free-floating property.** No anchor independent of the firm outcome. Not a domain yet, excluded structurally.
- **X2 Firm size or industry as sole cause.** Already a habitat condition inside D1 and D2. Absorbed, with a common-cause warning.

Order by dependency: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The whole is a **union**, not a partition, because D1 and D2 overlap in the same executive, D2 and D4 overlap through rule making, and D3 reads all three.

Strain test applied: an early attempt to map Fig 13's physical six-term substrate onto pay components only fit by renaming pay as energy and governance as a gradient. Re-census sends Fig 13 to the honest-stop role. A second strain, treating the market as both anchor and executive-movable price, is resolved by re-census into an outcome anchor plus an instrument (Fig 2 :37-38).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict.** The question as posed is a union of at least three conditions, not a partition with one dominant cause. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged: D1 (labour market) `specified`, triangulated; D2 (governance room) `specified`, triangulated; D3 (measurement) `general` structural with a `specified` methodological consequence; D4 (institutions) `general`, single-source on the figure rule with field corroboration. A slot's gap stays small when pay is priced by an outcome the executive did not move and the room's reference does not update on the executive's influence; it grows when either fails, and an arrangement that once helped can change valence when the residual changes. "Productivity plus talent" and "power plus weak governance" are each one witness, not four. The request for a single "mainly" is a declared `gap` of the instrument kind: the missing object is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name. Mode `specified` on substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected reform. A room failing the anchor test would be sold more incentive intensity, or a talent-priced slot would be told to add governance seats, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs three. The cost is real but bounded and locatable, because the shape, its four domain verdicts and seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can find the error (Fig 10 :62-64). The avoidable extra cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices.
- **undoable:** yes. No atom is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The union can be narrowed to a partition if one falsifier fires, and the provenance trail keeps the earlier reading visible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Specifically: (1) an anchor the executive cannot move, read by two frames with different evidence, showing one condition contributes zero across all slots; (2) one lever restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue; (3) an averaged statistic prospectively predicting which named slot carries a rent component; (4) an invariant governance valence across a change of residual and across binding versus advisory forms; (5) two instruments separating product, signal and rent at slot level from different evidence pools; (6) idempotence failing inside a closed set. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
