# Isomers, functional groups and order in the diversity-stability question

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The literature reports two communities with the same species count and different stability, and two communities with the same species list and different stability. Figure 12 provides the vocabulary to say what differs. Two questions are owed: (1) are such communities **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) does the influential-species side correspond to a **functional group** or to a particular member? A third question, order, is needed because the question is phrased as a sequence of alternatives.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the ecological frame: species identities, traits, population time series, environmental drivers. Bonds held: temporal covariance or asynchrony, competition, predation, facilitation, response similarity. No new primitive is available without import. The framework-internal atoms are the six terms and seven bonds; none is minted here.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a species list a flat formula? YES

Fig 12 :6, :14:

> **"collapse 6: the flat formula"**
> **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

A species list is exactly the flat formula. It records the ingredients and nothing downstream can tell which arrangement held. Richness is even flatter: it records the count, not even the identities. This is why a richness-versus-stability regression can be simultaneously robust and uninformative about mechanism. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

**Falsifier:** if two communities with identical species lists and identical species-level dynamics always have identical community stability, the bond carries no information and the flat-formula objection is wrong.

### Q2 — Are two same-list communities isomers? YES, as a checkable structural claim

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two plots with the same species list but different temporal covariance matrices are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the list and decisive in the stability behaviour. This is not an analogy imported from chemistry; Fig 12 :97-98 says *"This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."*

The ecological literature already measures the bond: Zhao et al. 2022 decompose asynchrony into a compensatory effect and a statistical-averaging effect; Hsieh et al. 2026 estimate time-varying response profiles as partial derivatives. Both are instruments for the bond, not new atom lists.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two communities with the same species list but different measured covariance or response structure have the same community stability, across the habitats sampled, the isomer claim fails and the flat-formula reading stands instead.

### Q3 — Is the influential species a functional group or a member? A GROUP, with the member left open

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A dominant competitor, a nitrogen-fixing legume, a foundation species or a keystone predator is a substructure whose *presence* fixes an outcome. Fig 12 licenses the kind. It does not license the claim that this particular member is irreplaceable in every habitat. That conclusion is independently supported by the keystone-species case history: in wave-exposed sites the ochre seastar was a powerful keystone, in sheltered sites far less important, and without its mussel prey it was *"just another sea star"* (Wikipedia contributors 2026, quoting Paine and Menge). Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

**Falsifier:** a substitution series in which functionally similar species replace the putative influential actor and stability is unchanged in every habitat would kill the member-level claim. A series in which no substitute works while the kind remains necessary would strengthen it. This is the cleanest ground test available for F4 of `01`.

### Q4 — What is the order of the two carriers? UNION, not sequence

Fig 12 :48: *"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."* Writing the question as "differences, then influential species" or the reverse imposes a single sequence on a union. Wagg et al. 2022 report that the mechanisms *"are not mutually exclusive"* and change with community age; Zhao et al. 2022 and Hsieh et al. 2026 disagree about the internal sub-mechanism. Neither result licenses a chain.

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The correct structure is one loop per condition, then an examination of the intersection. The intersection is the specific compound: a plot with high asynchrony and a dominant species. Fig 9 :58: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

**Falsifier:** evidence that one carrier's effect is identically zero whenever the other is present, across habitats, would turn the union into a partition and make a sequence meaningful.

### Q5 — Level relativity: can a species-level result become a community atom? YES

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

A species' own temporal variability, measurable in monoculture, is a settled molecule at the species level. It can become an atom in the community composition. Meng et al. 2025 report that *"species-specific dynamics observable in monocultures ... rather than interspecific interactions observable only in mixtures, explain much of the stabilizing effects of biodiversity"*. That is precisely the settled-molecule rule applied to grassland data: the monoculture reading settles the atom, and the mixture composes over it. Hsieh et al. 2026 find interactions primary in a lake plankton system. Both can be true because *which level was settled first* differs between the systems.

**Falsifier:** if monoculture dynamics cannot be made stable enough to serve as an atom, or if mixture stability cannot be derived from settled species-level inputs plus bonds, the level-relativity claim fails for that system. Record it as a system-specific failure, not a global refutation.

### Q6 — Ecosystem engineering is introduction, not composition

Fig 11 :61 and :65:

> **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

The influential-species channel is an introduction channel. A seat can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat. Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* This governs the intervention implication: if a particular actor is doing the work, management is permanent maintenance of that actor and its cascade, not a one-off fix.

**Falsifier:** a managed ecosystem in which removal of the influential actor leaves stability unchanged after one intervention would break this reading.

## CONCLUSION

The two carriers are not rivals in a flat formula. A species list with a stability reading is a flat formula (Q1); two communities sharing that list but differing in covariance or response structure are isomers (Q2); the influential-species channel is a functional group, with the particular member left open by the figure (Q3); the carriers form a union rather than a sequence (Q4); settled species-level dynamics can become atoms for community-level composition (Q5); and the manipulation channel is asymmetric introduction with permanent work (Q6). Each claim has a falsifier. None is `grounded`.
