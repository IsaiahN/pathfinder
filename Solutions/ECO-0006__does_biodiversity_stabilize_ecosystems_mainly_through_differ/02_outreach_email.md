# Outreach draft — check the residual first, then decide about the shape

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 has been checked by someone outside this frame. The first contact must survive a refutation attempt or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about biodiversity and stability, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "does biodiversity stabilize ecosystems mainly through differences among species, or through the presence of particular influential species?" is posed as an either/or. The derivation says the `or` is the defect. Three things follow:
>
> 1. **The two conditions overlap.** A plot can contain asynchronous species and a dominant species at once. Under the figure rule that governs this, a bundled problem is *"a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*, and when a split lands on a union rather than a partition you unbundle before you search. A single lever, adding species, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.
> 2. **A species list is only half the record.** Two communities with the same species list but different temporal covariance are different systems. The arrangement, not the ingredient list, carries the information about stability.
> 3. **"Influential species" is a role, not a name.** Whether a species is influential depends on which function, which perturbation and which habitat is at issue. A functional kind whose presence fixes an outcome is supportable; a permanent list of irreplaceable names is not.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures tell us which mechanism dominates in nature. They do not. That fraction is a `gap` from the figures and is owed to an experiment or a monitoring record. I am also not claiming that statistical averaging, compensatory dynamics, species-specific dynamics or biotic interactions is the winner inside the differences side; the published results disagree across systems, and I record that disagreement rather than resolve it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written down before the run):
> 1. one mechanism accounts for the whole diversity-stability effect across habitats with the other at zero;
> 2. stability is a property of one species in isolation, invariant to habitat and to the other species;
> 3. a fixed, context-independent species list predicts stability across residuals;
> 4. a species list alone predicts stability as well as a list plus its covariance structure;
> 5. averaging across species slots preserves the signal that per-slot analysis finds.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The world result that first made me unbundle the question: Wagg et al. 2022, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35189-2
> - The decomposition that sharpens the instrument: Zhao et al. 2022, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35514-9
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Contact discipline

Stage 1: send the residual, the shape and the refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material. Stage 2, opt-in only: if they are curious, offer the links. Stage 3: offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
