## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about representing ecosystem-climate-land-use feedbacks without losing useful detail. Option A: treat the question as a demand for lossless, maximally resolved representation and name a model that achieves it. Option B: treat "without losing useful detail" as a request to make the loss measurable against the ground, keep the per-slot signal, close the feedback cycles, and refuse agreement as a substitute for the anchor. What each leads to: A sends a `general`-mode verdict with a named architecture the framework says it cannot name, hides the loss in a global score, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B sends a checkable shape, five written refuters, and an open slot until a ground reading settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between what a coupled model predicts and what observations return, at the scale of a land-use decision. Arity: three coupled subsystems plus a decision maker, so a **union compound** with overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58). Symmetry: the couplings are directional and cyclic, not symmetric conjunctions. Scale: patch to globe, season to century, with multiple membranes.
- **Domain 1, climate physics frame.** Actors: atmosphere, ocean, ice, radiation, greenhouse gases. Currency: energy and water fluxes. Anchor: reanalysis and radiative observations. Union member.
- **Domain 2, ecosystem frame.** Actors: vegetation, soils, microbes, species, carbon and nutrient pools. Currency: carbon, water and nutrient fluxes. Anchor: eddy-covariance towers and inventories. Union member; overlaps domains 1 and 3.
- **Domain 3, land-use and decision frame.** Actors: managers, policies, land cover, agriculture. Currency: decisions, land cover, emissions. Anchor: land-use records and decision outcomes. Union member; overlaps domain 2.
- **Domain 4, modeling and compression frame.** Actors: state variables, resolution, parameters, emulators. Currency: bits to describe and actions to evaluate. Anchor: the round-trip gap measured against observations. Cross-cutting, not a peer subsystem.
- **Domain 5, verification and decision frame.** Actors: modelers, reviewers, decision-makers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Cross-cutting. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so domain 5 independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Observation, then vocabulary (what the model can represent), then objective (what counts as progress for the decision), then plan (which detail to keep), then carry forward (Fig 3 :20-21). The break is usually at vocabulary to objective, where a dictionary is mistaken for a grammar.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a named model does not. Representation across scales and frames is necessarily lossy, so "without losing useful detail" is honored by measuring the round-trip gap against the ground rather than promising losslessness, keeping residuals per slot, wiring feedbacks as cycles with a sign, enumerating each habitat to third-order contact, triangulating observationally independent frames, and treating resolution and cost as filters. The three subsystem frames are a functional group as written and a genuine isomer claim only over a shared atom set. The framework returns the shape, not an architecture; the slot for a particular model stays open. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a named architecture or a mass agreement instead of a generator and a refuter. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its five refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no existing Earth-system result is overturned. The five refuters each retire one part of the shape without touching the others, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already withhold the naming that would be hardest to undo.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measured round-trip gap of zero on every decision-relevant slot would break the loss finding; aggregation preserving the per-slot signal would break the slot finding; a faithful one-way chain with unchanged decision outcome would break the cycle finding; independent corroboration from a shared evidence pool would break the independence finding; proof that third-order effects are irrelevant would break the habitat finding. Any of these is a measurement or a demonstrated case, not an opinion. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
