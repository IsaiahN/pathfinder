# Are the three subsystem representations isomers, and what order is the coupling?

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The three frames in the domain census (ecosystems, climate, land use) are usually written with disjoint variable sets, so they look like three different problems. Two questions are owed: (1) can they be written over one shared atom set so that the isomer test of Fig 12 applies, and (2) what is the order of the composition that joins them into a feedback rather than a list?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13, namely Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12, in particular the network relation "that entities relate, and influence travels," the cycle "The output re-enters as input," and comparison. No new primitive is available without import. The three subsystem frames are a union with overlapping boundaries, not a partition (see `01` section 1).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Isomer or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- As the frames are normally written (climate with radiative and flux variables, ecosystems with carbon pools and species, land use with decisions and land cover), they share almost no atoms. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are three members of a **functional group** whose common substructure is "a coupled state-structure-state composition that returns a prediction to the ground." The group says what kind (a cross-kind coupled model); the qualifier says which member.
- The stronger reading: if all three are written over the **full shared atom set** `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they **are** isomers, same atoms and different arrangement. This is the claim worth testing, because it would make the coupling a matter of arrangement rather than a new primitive.
- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Each one-frame representation is a cross-kind composition; the union is a composition of those settled compositions.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the three frames can be represented only with an atom the other two do not require (for example, a representation of land use that needs a decision primitive not reducible to Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient or Network), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. That outcome is a `gap` for the isomer claim, not a defeat of the whole shape.

### Q2 - What order joins them into a feedback?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The coupled system is a cycle: land use changes forcing, forcing changes climate, climate changes ecosystems, ecosystems change the sink and surface that constrain land use. The sign at each return decides whether the loop amplifies or damps. A chain of one-way modules "cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :103-104: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." and "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom." Each subsystem model, once settled against observations, becomes an atom at the next level; the coupled feedback model is a second-order composition of settled first-order models. It needs no new primitive, which is why the shape claim imports nothing from the figures.
- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The dependency order is observation, then vocabulary, then objective, then plan, then carry. Adding resolution at the plan stage cannot rescue a missing vocabulary at the earlier link.

**Falsifier:** if the three frames can be joined into a decision-relevant feedback by a one-way chain with no return path and no change in the decision outcome, the cycle claim in `01` F3 is unnecessary for that case. Record it; do not smooth it over.

### Q3 - What does the two-currency rule say about the arrangement?

Fig 12 :125-126: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A finer coupled model costs more bits to describe and more actions to run and validate. The two readings come apart: a high-resolution representation can be cheap to state and expensive to settle, or the reverse. Lu and Ricciuto 2019 (opened; world source, not a figure) reduce a surrogate from 42,660 outputs using SVD and report consistency 0.93, which is a measured round trip in the actions currency. The figure rule says both currencies must be read or the choice is blind. This is the concrete content of "useful detail": detail whose bit cost is paid by a corresponding reduction in decision-relevant residual, with the action cost actually affordable.

## CONCLUSION

The three subsystem representations are a **functional group** as usually written, and a real **isomer claim only under the stronger shared-atom reading** with an explicit falsifier. Their union is a **second-order cycle**, not a sequence: each settled frame becomes an atom, and the return path is what makes it a feedback. The dependency order of the modeling chain (Fig 3) is separate from the topological order of the loop (Fig 12 cycle); conflating them is a category error. Both falsifiers are stated above and neither has been run here.
