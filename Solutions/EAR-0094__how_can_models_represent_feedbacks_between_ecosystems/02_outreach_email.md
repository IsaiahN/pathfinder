# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives the reader's own check. If it does not, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about coupled models before you decide whether to read the framing

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question about ecosystem-climate-land-use feedback models: how can they represent the feedbacks without losing useful detail? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "without losing useful detail" cannot mean "without any loss." Every representation that passes from the coupled system to a coarser model is lossy. The framework's own figure puts it plainly: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." So the useful question is not how to avoid the loss but how to report it honestly. The shape claim has seven parts:
>
> 1. The model ranges against an observational anchor, not against other models' agreement.
> 2. The loss in coarsening is measured as a round-trip gap against the ground, not assumed to be zero.
> 3. Residuals are kept per slot, region, and season, not averaged into one global score.
> 4. Feedbacks are wired as cycles with a sign, not one-way forcing chains.
> 5. Each feedback's habitat is enumerated out to where the cascade stops mattering, including third-order effects.
> 6. Observationally independent frames are triangulated; shared bias is one witness, not two.
> 7. Resolution and computational cost are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular model, resolution, or coupling scheme is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming a lossless coupled model exists, not claiming the shape is grounded in a measurement, and not claiming that models which agree are therefore right.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written down before this message was sent):
> 1. a coupling of the three subsystems with zero round-trip gap on every decision-relevant slot against the ground;
> 2. evidence that averaging residuals across slots preserves the decision-relevant signal as well as a per-slot reading;
> 3. a faithful representation of one of the feedbacks as a one-way chain with no return path and no change in the decision outcome;
> 4. two frames sharing the same evidence shown to give independent corroboration;
> 5. proof that third-order contact effects are irrelevant to the decision, so first-order scoping loses nothing.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Check Fig 4 :27-32, Fig 1 :19-28, Fig 12 :121, Fig 11 :43-53.
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - A worked open-access case on emulator error sources: https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/17/107/2026/esd-17-107-2026.html
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a coupled-model problem you know and can check against observations. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1.** Send the seven-part shape and its five refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they have seen the source (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The framework's "Direction" section says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the user must bind their sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running.
