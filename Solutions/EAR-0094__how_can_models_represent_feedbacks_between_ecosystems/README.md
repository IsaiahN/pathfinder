## What an answer would look like (Direction, bound as an ordinary term)

An answer is a **shape** any coupled model must have, not a named architecture. Bind it now so it can accrue a residual like anything else: (1) the model ranges against an observational anchor, (2) the loss in going from the coupled system to the model is measured as a round-trip gap rather than assumed zero, (3) residuals are kept per slot rather than averaged away, (4) feedbacks are wired as cycles, (5) each feedback's habitat is enumerated out to where the cascade stops mattering, (6) low-overlap observational frames are triangulated, and (7) resolution and cost are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. A candidate model fails if it violates any of these seven.

## Verdict in one line

`specified` and ungrounded: "without losing useful detail" cannot mean "without any loss"; every cross-scale representation is lossy (Fig 4 :27), so the honest move is to measure what was given up against the ground, keep the per-slot signal, close the loops, and refuse agreement as a substitute for the anchor.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Why, in the framework's own words: "agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :13); "high correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention" (Seat Map :163); and "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it" (Fig 10 :42). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless, and sending the full theory before asking for a verdict spends the one thing an outside reader contributes, their independence from our frame.
