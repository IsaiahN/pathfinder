## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Loss is a property of the transform, not a bug to be argued away.** Womack et al. 2026 name four error sources (memory, hidden variables, noise, nonlinearities) that a reduced model can drop. That is the world-facing counterpart of Fig 4 :27 and Fig 13 :32. Cite it when explaining why "without losing any detail" is not a reachable specification.
2. **Agreement is not independence.** Cox 2019: "Where the models share similar systematic biases, or neglect similar processes, even statistically significant emergent relationships may mislead about the likely changes in the real system." That is Fig 2 :40 in the vocabulary of carbon-cycle feedbacks. It is the reason the shape claim asks for observationally independent frames and records overlap in the provenance.
3. **Reduce, then measure the reduction.** Lu and Ricciuto 2019 report a compressed surrogate that retains 42,660 outputs with 20 simulations, but the number that matters is the reported consistency 0.93 and mean squared error 0.02, that is, the round trip was measured. Van Katwyk et al. 2026 warn that emulators "typically fail to generalize beyond their training distributions," which is a filter failure, not a verdict.

None of these sources decides the answer. They are instances and warnings from adjacent closures; the figures decide (Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks").

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the whole framework before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the seven-part shape and its five refuters; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reader who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :13:** "agreement compounds, evidence does not." A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration. There is no bulk-send plan in this folder.
- **Seat Map :163:** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then, the local figure text and the two open-access papers, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
