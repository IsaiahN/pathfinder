## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down without extra decisions that the figures do not supply:

- **Which slots and which decision.** The shorthand says "per slot," but the figures do not name the slot or the decision; the modeler must, from the world (Fig 11 :48). This is a legitimate free parameter, not a flaw, but the round trip does not determine it.
- **Which frames are independent.** Fig 2 :40 rules out shared-pool agreement but does not certify any particular pair of observations as independent; that needs provenance (Cox 2019).
- **The sign of each loop.** Fig 12 :121 says the sign decides whether the loop closes; the figures do not give the sign for a specific carbon-climate or albedo-productivity feedback. That is a ground reading.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a cross-scale representation is lossy and the loss must be measured against the ground, and a chain cannot close a feedback. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists in this derivation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped decisions. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
