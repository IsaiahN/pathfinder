# Expert verification packet - the Tether on public information reliability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an information-systems researcher, fact-checker, platform-governance scholar or science-studies scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No institutional measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Institutions can make public information more reliable without suppressing legitimate disagreement or minority evidence only if they treat the two goals as a union of conditions rather than one dial. R_truth (the gap between what is presented and what the ground returns) falls when per-claim frames range separately against a non-moving ground. R_silence (the gap between reachable claims and claims that would pay against the ground) stays near zero when the institution keeps reach to closures that differ from its own, keeps its conventions from deciding outcomes, and uses introduction rather than exclusion. Verification must come from low-overlap frames chosen by an unauthored mechanism, corrections must be refusals added to the record, and the two residuals must be priced in separate accounts. "Legitimate" is a contact question per claim, not a standing list of speakers. The framework does not name the label, threshold or source list that pays; that remains a `gap` until a measurement closes it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 2, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** consensus cannot be the anchor for truth, and a convention that decides an outcome is no longer a convention. Therefore an averaged trust or accuracy score cannot be the verdict, and a fixed source list cannot decide a claim's status.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :43 - *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** a fixed convention that decides outcomes still improves per-claim truth contact after a habitat substitution removes the ground's payment for its own source list; or a single averaged metric predicts prospectively, per claim and per community, which minority slot will lose reach next with no loss of discrimination in that slot.

### C2 - framework-internal plus world-facing, checkable by an information researcher

- **Statement:** differing closures supply the atoms an institution does not hold, so suppressing minority evidence shrinks reach and therefore harms reliability itself. Independent low-overlap frames, not a single shared evidence pool, are what make agreement mean anything.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **What to check against the world:** Condorcet's jury theorem requires p > 1/2 and independence for majority accuracy to improve with group size. Hong and Page's diversity result, as reported by UMich News, finds a diverse randomly selected pool outperforming the best solvers. Pennycook and Rand 2019 find that crowd reliability depends on per-slot familiarity. Do these three actually support the claim that independence and differing closures are load-bearing, or only the weaker claim that they are useful? The honest answer may be the weaker one.
- **Refuter:** an institution that cuts contact with every differing closure still improves truth contact across a habitat change with no new atom entering; or a verification regime drawing on one shared evidence pool produces new ground-verified readings across a habitat change without importing a differing frame.

### C3 - framework-internal plus institutional, checkable by a governance scholar

- **Statement:** the available lever is introduction, not exclusion. Removal substitutes a habitat and hides two failures: what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced. A correction is a negation added to the record, not a deletion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61-62 - *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 5 :55 - *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."*
- **What to check against the world:** Peng and Grimmelmann 2024 argue that bridging-based counterspeech *"adds context to speech rather than removing it entirely"* and report that Community Notes can be too slow to reach consensus early. The EU Digital Services Act requires *"Transparency in content removals"* and appeal options. Do these support asymmetric power as a design rule, or only as a caution? The caution alone is enough to corroborate the shape's mechanism, not its uniqueness.
- **Refuter:** a removal-based intervention that reduces R_truth with R_silence measurably near zero in the affected community and no two-step cascade in the habitat; or a documented correction that removes the old claim from the record with no negation added and whose effect survives habitat substitution.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that institutions should never remove content. The rule about the lever is not a rule about zero outcomes (Fig 10 :53).
- Not that every minority claim is true, or that majority agreement is evidence.
- Not that any particular label, threshold, source list, appeal window or platform rule is correct. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not that crowd ratings are an anchor. They are a budget filter whose reliability is per-slot (Pennycook and Rand 2019).
- Not that this derivation is `grounded`, and not that the framework is the only account of public information.
- Not that the world sources independently prove the figure verdict. They are low-overlap corroboration; one shared pool is one witness.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot instrument that discriminates a true minority claim from a false one in the same community, instead of reading "reliable" for both.
2. A method that distinguishes truth contact gained by import from truth contact gained by re-ranking the institution's existing library.
3. A habitat substitution that removes the ground's payment for an institution's own source list, with a measured record of whether the list was revised.
4. A two-step cascade check after any removal.
5. The seven pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31, :35, :43; Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :32; Fig 11 :61-62; Fig 12 :126; Fig 9 :60. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against one source you know well. Is independence load-bearing for the reliability claim, or merely helpful? Say which.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a true minority claim from a false one in a single community, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
