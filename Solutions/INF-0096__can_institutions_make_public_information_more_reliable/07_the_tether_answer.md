# The Tether's answer: can institutions make public information more reliable without suppressing legitimate disagreement or minority evidence?

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An institution changes what an audience sees. Its own library predicts that R_truth will fall (fewer false claims believed). The world also returns a second quantity: whether R_silence grew (true minority claims became unreachable). The question is the conjunction: R_truth down while R_silence stays near zero. R is indexed per claim and per community, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The two quantities are a union with an intersection that is its own compound (Fig 9 :54-57), so they must be reported in separate columns, each with its own anchor and its own falsifier.

## 2. The shape

1. **The ground, not agreement, settles truth.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* A trust score, a consensus count, an engagement metric and the institution's own reputation all move when pushed, so they are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. **Differing closures are the only source of new reach.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Minority evidence is exactly the atom the institution does not hold. Cutting it off does not just risk a fairness problem; it removes the only operator that can move the wall (Fig 6 :14), so the institution becomes less able to find what is true in its own terms.
3. **Conventions must not decide outcomes.** Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* A source list, a label definition and a standard of evidence are conventions. They are useful for shared meaning (Fig 2 :41). They become a hidden verdict the moment they are used as the reason a claim is false.
4. **The lever is introduction, not subtraction.** Fig 11 :61-62: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* An institution can add context, add provenance, add a divergent review, add a refusal (Fig 5 :53). It cannot subtract the habitat; a removal substitutes one habitat for another, with invisible failures at two steps out (Fig 11 :66, :68, :69).
5. **"Legitimate" is a contact question, not a standing list.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* What is preserved is contact with claims that register against the ground now, not an identity category granted permanent standing. Seat Map :171: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*
6. **Witnesses must be independent and unauthored.** Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 10 :76-78 names the mechanisms: *"a lottery"*, *"everyone who qualifies"*, *"a set assembled before the question existed"*.
7. **Keep two currencies.** Fig 12 :126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* The cost of stating a rule and the cost of checking whether it holds are in different units. One integrity score prices both wrong (Fig 12 :130).
8. **Corrections are refusals, not deletions.** Fig 5 :55: *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* A vocabulary that can only add labels and cannot say an arrangement is wrong can accumulate without correcting (Operators table :58).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that institutions should never remove anything. Fig 10 :53: *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* The claim is about which lever is available and what must be recorded when it is used, not about zero removal.
- Not that every minority claim is true, and not that every majority claim is false. Both are ranged against the ground separately.
- Not that a crowd, a panel or a trust score is an anchor. They are instruments.
- Not which label, threshold, source list or appeal window is correct. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here pays for the shape.
- Not that the framework is the only account of public information. It cannot certify its own limit (Fig 8 :37).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* states: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*. Here the discriminations owed are:

- **Per-slot discrimination:** does a proposed metric distinguish a true minority claim from a false one in the same community? If it reads "reliable" for both, it is a metamerism, not a verdict.
- **Import discrimination:** when truth contact improves, did a new atom enter from a differing closure, or did the institution only re-rank what it already held? Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- **Convention discrimination:** under a habitat substitution that removes the ground's payment for its own source list, does the institution revise the list? If not, the convention has become a verdict (Fig 2 :43).
- **Cascade discrimination:** after a removal, is there a two-step effect in the habitat? Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.
- Standing falsifiers from the audit: one scalar predicting both residuals prospectively; a fixed outcome-deciding convention that survives habitat substitution; truth contact improving with no differing closure; a removal with no R_silence cost and no cascade; a standing source list whose valence never changes.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The conditions above are a shape that any working intervention must satisfy under the figures. They do not name an intervention. Which policies pay, in which communities, at which cost, is a `gap` closed only by measurement. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The world sources read for this question corroborate the shape from low-overlap fields (Condorcet's independence condition, diversity beating ability, bridging-based ranking, the continued influence effect, epistemic injustice), but the verdict comes from the figures. Pennycook and Rand's crowd reliability result is a budget filter, not an anchor; Peng and Grimmelmann's bridging proposal is an engineering instance of introduction rather than exclusion; Hong and Page is a mathematical instance of surplus from differing closures. Agreement among these sources and the figures is expected where they share a concern, and is one witness where they share an evidence pool.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The short answer is yes, but only if institutions stop treating reliability and disagreement as two ends of one dial. They are a union of conditions with different anchors. Truth contact improves when each claim is ranged separately against a ground that does not move when people push on it, and when the institution keeps reach to sources whose evidence differs from its own, because those are the only source of an atom its library does not hold. Disagreement is preserved not by granting a permanent protected list but by keeping conventions from deciding outcomes and by using introduction rather than subtraction: context, provenance, divergent review and refusals added to the record. Verification has to come from low-overlap frames chosen by an unauthored mechanism, and the two residuals must stay in separate columns because they are priced in different currencies. What the framework cannot give is the label, the threshold or the source list that pays in a particular community. That is a gap, not a silence, and it closes only when a measurement pays for it.
