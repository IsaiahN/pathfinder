# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reviewer has checked the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and found at least one of them wrong or right on the merits. A stage-1 failure ends the sequence.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about reliability and disagreement, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to trust a framework. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an institution that wants public information more reliable without suppressing legitimate disagreement is not facing one dial with two outputs. It is facing a union of at least three conditions. First, each claim has to be ranged separately against a ground that does not move when people push on it, rather than against a trust score that does. Second, the institution has to keep reach to sources and communities whose evidence differs from its own, because those are the only source of an atom its own library does not hold. Third, its conventions (what counts as evidence, what a label means) have to stay conventions. The moment a convention decides an outcome, it has stopped being a convention and has become an unexamined verdict.
>
> The consequence that surprises people is the second one. Cutting minority evidence does not only risk a fairness problem. It removes the only operator that can expand the institution's reach, so it makes the institution less able to find what is true, including in the majority's own terms.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular label, threshold, source list or moderation rule is the right one. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that false claims should never be removed, because a rule that no real institution can satisfy is a rule stated wrong. I am claiming that the available lever is introduction (context, provenance, divergent review, a refusal added to the record), not subtraction of a claim from the habitat.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message was sent:**
> 1. One averaged accuracy or trust metric predicts prospectively, per claim and per community, which minority slot will lose reach next, with no loss of discrimination in that slot.
> 2. A fixed source list that decides outcomes still improves per-claim truth contact after the habitat changes so that the list's own ground no longer pays.
> 3. An institution that cuts contact with every differing closure still improves truth contact across a change of habitat, with no new atom entering.
> 4. A removal-based intervention reduces the error with no two-step cascade in the habitat and no measurable loss of reachable evidence in the affected community.
> 5. One scalar predicts both truth contact and preserved reach prospectively, with nothing left over in either column.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the figure text, the formula and the essays. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. The relevant verbatim lines are in the checkable packet.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.

## Sending rules

1. Send to one person or one public opt-in venue at a time. No list, no CC chain, no automation.
2. Record the stage-1 verdict before any links are shown. Later agreement about the framework is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. Label the package as a simulated derivation. Do not present it as a completed empirical result. Several venues have explicit policies on AI-generated submissions; check the current policy of the venue before submitting anything under a human author's name.
4. A failure is a successful outcome of stage 1. Quit when the shape fails, not when it is agreed with.
