## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, one witness.** Pennycook and Rand 2019, Pennycook et al. 2021 and the accuracy-prompt line of work all come from the same social-computing and misinformation pool. They agree that attention, familiarity and independence govern how well a crowd tracks reliability. That agreement is one witness, not three (Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"*). Within that pool the most useful single result for this question is the familiarity finding: the crowd looked reliable in aggregate, and the reliability fell apart when unfamiliar ratings were excluded. That is exactly the per-slot aggregation failure named in Fig 1 :22, and it is also a warning against treating a trust score as an anchor.

**Low-overlap witnesses**, each from a different field and each read directly:

1. **Mathematics and collective decision.** Condorcet's jury theorem states that adding voters improves majority accuracy only when each voter is more likely than not to be right and votes independently. Below p = 1/2 the optimal jury is one voter. This corroborates two figure rules at once: independence is load-bearing (Fig 2 :31, :42), and adding people to an evidence pool adds agreement, not evidence (Fig 7 :13). It also refuses the naive "more voices, more truth" reading of the question.
2. **Complex systems and organizational design.** Hong and Page's result, as reported by the University of Michigan, is that a diverse randomly selected pool outperforms the best individual solvers because a person's contribution is contextual. This corroborates Fig 8 :45 - *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone"* - from a field with no shared evidence pool with either platform engineering or philosophy. It is a news summary of the paper, so it is recorded as one witness with that limitation stated.
3. **Engineering and law of platform governance.** Peng and Grimmelmann argue for promoting counterspeech approved across differing viewpoints and note that the approach adds context instead of removing speech. They also report that Community Notes can be too slow to reach consensus early. That is an honest limit on one implementation of Fig 11 :61 (introduction rather than exclusion), and it is a corroboration of the mechanism shape, not of any particular platform's rule.
4. **Cognitive psychology.** Susmann and Wegener show that a retraction can increase discomfort and that the discomfort predicts continued belief. This corroborates Fig 5 :55 - *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach"* - at the audience stratum: adding a correction does not subtract the old claim from inference. The anchor must be re-named at the new stratum (Fig 4 :20-23), so this is mechanism corroboration, not an institutional measurement.
5. **Philosophy of knowledge.** The IEP entry on epistemic injustice names credibility deficit and hermeneutical marginalization as structural wrongs against someone in their capacity as a knower. This corroborates that R_silence is a real residual with its own anchor, not a rhetorical addition to R_truth. It cannot tell the framework which particular deflated judgment is unjust; that needs a ground reading.
6. **Institutional practice.** The IFCN Code of Principles and the EU Digital Services Act both build the shape into public rules: transparent procedures, nonpartisan standards, appeal routes, and duties to consider pluralism. They are evidence about what institutions currently do, not evidence that the shape is true. They are `silent` on the derivation and are used as venue and corroboration only.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so their agreement carries no independent weight. Overlap is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the two residuals and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Seat Map :171:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.
- **Fig 10 :53:** *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Do not ask a venue to endorse a no-removal rule that no real institution can meet. Ask them to check the lever claim instead.

**A hard practical constraint.** This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. Many scholarly and professional venues have policies on AI-generated or AI-assisted submissions; the specific IFCN and ICA policies were not opened in this session, so this folder makes no claim about their current text. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the two residuals and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the applicable policy.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
