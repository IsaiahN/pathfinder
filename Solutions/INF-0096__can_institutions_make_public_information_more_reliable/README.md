## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether institutions should intervene, but which lever keeps two different residuals small at the same time. In effect terms, an audience or a community holds a belief b about a claim, an institution takes an action a (label, rank, remove, add context, set a source standard), its library Γ predicts what will happen, and the world returns o′. The residual is R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| measured per claim-slot and per community-slot, never as one global accuracy score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- **Option A, reliability is a single dial.** Push the accuracy metric up and accept some loss of disagreement as the price. This leads to the prediction that removal and consensus are legitimate instruments, and that a single averaged trust score can be the verdict.
- **Option B, reliability and preserved disagreement are two conditions of a union.** Each has its own anchor and its own instrument, and they must be kept separate (Fig 9 :54-57). This leads to the prediction that the institution works only when it keeps contact with a non-moving ground and keeps reach to closures that differ from its own.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape naming the conditions that must hold simultaneously for R_truth to fall while R_silencing stays near zero, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of the other. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is bound in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0, so the pre-registered refuter can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. In one line: yes, but only if the institution behaves as a ground maintainer and habitat cultivator, not as an arbiter of consensus.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of public information.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* (Seat Map :283). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"* (Seat Map :163). This question is exactly about the difference between a verdict (is this claim true) and a convention (what counts as evidence). Selecting recipients for receptiveness would corrupt the first while pretending to serve the second.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
