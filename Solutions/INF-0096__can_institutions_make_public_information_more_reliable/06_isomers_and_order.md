## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

R_truth and R_silence overlap: a false claim that is removed reduces the apparent R_truth while growing R_silence if the removal cuts a differing closure. A true minority claim that is amplified reduces R_silence but may grow R_truth if it was not checked. The conditions are therefore a union with an intersection that is its own compound. The third condition, keeping conventions as conventions, overlaps both: a source list that decides outcomes affects both truth contact and reach.

**Consequence:** a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. This is the figure's second-order value of a big lever: *"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable"* (Fig 9 :59). The institutional analogue is that the intervention becomes reportable as a shape rather than as a score.

**Falsifier:** an intervention that restores all three conditions and leaves no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### Q2 - Are the two residuals isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

R_truth and R_silence as written share the atom "claim" and the atom "ground", but they are not molecules over the same full atom set: R_truth requires the ground-return slot, R_silence requires the reach slot. So they are not isomers as written. They are better read as two members of a **functional group**: both are comparison relations over a claim, with different qualifier atoms (Fig 12 :124).

The stronger form is available. If both are written over the full shared atom set `{claim, ground, reach, closure, convention, witness, correction, habitat}`, differing only in which atom occupies the anchor slot and which bond carries the relation, then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that the same library can produce either residual by a different bond, and that changing the bond changes the substance without changing the ingredients.

**Falsifier, written before this run:** if either residual can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, R_silence with no closure, or R_truth with no ground), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3 - What is the structural form of a correction?

- Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
- Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A correction is a negation added to the record. It joins nothing, so the symmetry question does not apply to it, and it does not enter the bond price (Operators table :77-78). What it removes is a term's standing to be composed over, not the term itself (Fig 5 :55). That is why an institution with only additive labels cannot carry a correction: it can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say the arrangement is wrong.

A verification routine is a **catalyst** if it survives its own use (Fig 12 :119): the same method pays again on the next claim. It becomes a **spent part** when it is a recording of one occasion carried upward as if it were a method (Fig 4 :25). Nothing in the routine's description records which it is, which is why the institution must record provenance and re-run the check against the ground each time.

An appeal or review path is a **cycle** (Fig 12 :121): the output of a ruling re-enters as input to a later ruling. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes around the ground or around the institution's own prior verdicts. A chain of decisions with no returns cannot close by construction, so a system with no appeal path cannot correct itself even though it can accumulate rulings.

**Falsifier:** a correction that removes the old term from the record with no negation added, and whose effect is stable across a habitat substitution. Under Fig 5 :55 that should be impossible.

### Q4 - What order do the moves come in?

- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: *"REFUTED              a term held is false; what is owed"* and *"is a refusal, not an explanation"*.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Order: describe the two residuals in effect terms (done in `01`, section 0) before naming any frame; refuse the framing "one dial with two outputs" before minting an intervention; settle each per-claim check against the ground before composing a convention over it; only then promote the method upward (Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

This order is not cosmetic. An institution that mints a convention first and then ranges claims against it has reversed Fig 12 :104: it is composing over a term that was never settled. That is the structural definition of F3 in the primary audit, where a convention becomes a verdict.

**Falsifier:** a working institution that composes a stable convention over unsettled per-claim checks and still tracks a changing ground without revision. If one is documented, the dependency order is wrong for this domain.

### Q5 - Two currencies, not one score

- Fig 12 :126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :127: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**
- Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

For institutions: one currency is the description cost of a ruling (how many standards, labels and exception clauses must be stated); the other is the action cost of checking whether it holds (how many independent instruments must actually be run against a claim). A single "integrity score" prices both in one unit and will not notice when a cheap-to-state rule is unaffordable to settle. The two readings come apart in both directions (Fig 12 :128-129), so both must be kept.

**Falsifier:** a single scalar that predicts both description cost and action cost prospectively, in one unit, with no residual left over. Fig 12 :130 says this is the error rather than the fix.

## CONCLUSION

The reliability-versus-disagreement question is a union of three conditions with an intersection that is its own compound, not a harder version of one condition (Q1). The two residuals are a functional group as written and a genuine isomer claim over the full shared atom set (Q2). A correction is a negation added to the record, a verification routine is a catalyst only while it keeps paying, and an appeal path is a cycle whose sign decides whether the loop closes around the ground (Q3). The moves must be ordered describe, refuse, settle, compose, promote (Q4), and the two costs must be held in separate accounts (Q5).

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without inventing a threshold: it gives a checkable shape with four falsifiers and no new primitive. Which particular intervention pays is still open.
