# WATSON answer, INF-0096

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Can institutions make public information more reliable without suppressing legitimate disagreement or minority evidence?

Restated as an effect: can R_truth = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| fall while R_silence stays near zero, where R is indexed per claim and per community and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22)? Option A treats reliability and disagreement as ends of one dial and accepts some silencing as the price. Option B treats them as a union of conditions with separate anchors and instruments. What each leads to: A produces a single trust score, a fixed source list that decides outcomes, and removal as the main lever, which makes the institution less able to find atoms it does not hold. B produces two columns, imports from differing closures, kept conventions, introduction rather than exclusion, and an open slot where the discriminating instrument is missing.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: R_truth is a three-place relation (claim, institutional action, ground return), indexed per claim and community, ordered in time, carrying an ordered bond and a comparison bond. R_silence is a reach relation over the habitat of claims. Symmetry: "reliable" is a threshold relative to a ground, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :87). Suppression is asymmetric in time. Scale: single claim check, population of claims with a ranking system, and the trust channel across time; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :21-30).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** the question is a union, not a dial. Yes, institutions can make public information more reliable without suppressing legitimate disagreement or minority evidence, but only under three kept-apart conditions: per-claim ranging against a non-moving ground, preserved reach to closures that differ, and conventions that never decide outcomes. The lever is introduction rather than subtraction, corrections are refusals added to the record, witnesses come from low-overlap unauthored pools, and the two residuals are priced in separate accounts. "Legitimate" is a contact question per claim, not a standing list. Which label, threshold or source list pays is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. The world sources corroborate the mechanism at the level of shape, but the verdict comes from the figures and does not depend on any of them.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected intervention and invisible loss. An institution failing reach would be sold more accuracy tooling, and an institution failing ground contact would be told to import more voices. A single score hides both. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent checking a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible, and it is avoidable by leading with the two residuals and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. A refutation is *"a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* (Operators table :58) and is added; the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the seven pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) one scalar predicting both residuals prospectively per slot with no leftover, (2) a fixed outcome-deciding convention surviving habitat substitution, (3) truth contact improving with no differing closure, (4) a removal with no R_silence cost and no two-step cascade, (5) a standing source list whose valence never changes. A source-discriminating instrument in a community would close the `gap` on which policy pays. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
