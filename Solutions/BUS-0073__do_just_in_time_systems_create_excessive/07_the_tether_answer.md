## 1. The residual it answers

For supply slot s, R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o′|: the prediction that an input arrives just in time versus what actually arrived. The question offers two options, "excessive vulnerability" or "resilient when managed well". They hold different things fixed, so they form a **union compound**, not one hard question. ## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that JIT is always fragile. That would be a `general` verdict about a `specified` habitat, and the anchor would be "managed well", which moves when pushed (Fig 2 :30).
- Not that a managed JIT system is safe. "Managed well" is permanent work, not a terminal state.
- Not which lever wins, not a buffer size, not a supplier count. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the ecology or safety-science sources measure supply chains. They are low-overlap corroboration of the mechanism shape, and their concepts are imports that must be recorded as such.
- Not `grounded`. No disruption experiment or firm dataset was consulted.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* makes the point with the spectrometer: the reading "green" cannot name the source until an instrument discriminates among the causes. Here the corresponding instruments are:

- a **per-slot continuity counter** that fires when one component goes live, rather than a firm-wide on-time-delivery average (Fig 1 :19, :22);
- a **tier-3 trace** that enumerates the cascade before the disruption rather than after (Fig 11 :52);
- a **controlled comparison** holding the schedule fixed and varying the habitat, and holding the habitat fixed and varying the channel (F2 and F4 refuters);
- a **threshold test** on time-to-survive versus time-to-recover that can actually fire (Fig 9 :32 and the supply-chain-resilience source);
- the standing refuters from `01` §4, any one of which withdraws the corresponding finding.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that just-in-time is always fragile, and it does not say that good management makes it safe. It says the question's "or" is a false split: a schedule and its supply habitat and its visibility channel are one compound, and resilience is a property of that compound measured per slot, not of the schedule in isolation. A firm-wide "green" metric can hide one live sole-source part, and the supplier that matters can be three tiers out, so the habitat has to be enumerated rather than assumed and the channel has to resolve the single slot. "Managed well" is not a state you reach: management can add alternatives but cannot subtract contact, so it is permanent work. What the framework will not do is name the winning lever, whether buffers, dual sourcing, nearshoring or flexibility; that is a gap owed to a disruption instrument, not a best-practice list. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
