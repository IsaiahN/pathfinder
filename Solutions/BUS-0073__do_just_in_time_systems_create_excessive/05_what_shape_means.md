## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before a particular intervention is named. It constrains; it does not fill. Examples:

- a shape: "resilience is a property of the JIT-and-habitat-and-channel compound, measured per slot, with a named class of interventions";
- a name: "add a 15 percent buffer at the tier-2 supplier in Osaka";
- a shape: "the habitat must be enumerated past direct contact because the actor that matters can be three tiers out";
- a name: "dual-source part X from vendor Y".

The JIT question's own wording invites a name, because "managed well" sounds like a recipe. The framework refuses the recipe and keeps the slot open.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on proximity: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the bootstrapping vocabulary is pulled from operations, ecology, safety science and network science, then narrowed against the residual.
- *Library of Babel* on the malformed question: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is exactly F1 in `01`: the question's "or" bundles two conditions that hold different things fixed.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :31: *"rather than the cause"*. So describe the residual as a continuity failure per slot, not as a failure of "lean culture".
- Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Pick the frame after the effect is described, or any frame will seem to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: the ground settles the term; verified only by what pays. In this field that means a disruption event, a per-slot continuity counter, a controlled comparison with habitat and channel varied. Not a literature consensus and not a CSF-style success story.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The ecology and safety-science frames qualify as low-overlap frames; their concepts (panarchy, graceful extensibility) travel as shapes, and must be recorded as imports, not presented as supply-chain measurements.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the import rule: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Importing "buffer" into a lean vocabulary adds a term inside the same space. Importing "per-slot continuity under a tier-3 surprise" adds a dimension, because it changes what can be perceived at all.

The same essay warns about the substrate: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible"*. The six-term set is a visible choice, not nature's own list. Counting a supply network as an instance of the network structure is a mapping onto a held atom, not a discovery of a seventh primitive.

## 4. Why the metamerism point matters here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. The essay's line: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

Applied here: a high service level is a reading. It can be produced by low inventory plus a superb habitat, by high inventory plus a poor habitat, by stable demand, or by a supplier absorbing a disruption upstream. The manager who names the source from the service-level reading commits the metamerism error. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates among sources: a per-slot continuity counter, a tier-3 trace, a controlled disruption. This is why F7 in `01` is a `gap` rather than a hidden answer.

## 5. What licenses a particular lever for a particular firm?

Nothing in the framework. Fig 9 :32 gives a kind of thing, never the answer itself. A specific lever is licensed only by the ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until then, the lever is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. This is recorded as `gap`, not papered over with a best-practice list.

## 6. Two consequences for this outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A campaign that gathers respected operations scholars to agree that JIT is fragile, or that lean is obviously resilient, is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* And: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of whether JIT actually creates excessive vulnerability in any industry, and they cannot settle the question they report on.
