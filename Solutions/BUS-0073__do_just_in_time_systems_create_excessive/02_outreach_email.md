# Outreach draft : check the output first, then decide about the frame

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuters in `01` have been checked against the figures. If the stage-1 output does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about JIT resilience, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about lean operations and supply chain resilience. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does JIT create excessive vulnerability, or can it be resilient when managed well?" is a false partition. The two sides hold different things fixed. A firm-average on-time-delivery metric can be near zero overall while one sole-source part carries a live residual: per the figures, the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated, and a global reading near zero with one live slot is a legal state. The shape that survives is this: resilience is a property of the scheduling rule together with its supply habitat and its visibility channel, measured per slot. The habitat has to be enumerated past direct suppliers because the actor that matters can be three tiers out. The channel has to resolve a single live slot, because aggregated metrics pool readings until the line stops.
>
> **What this does not say:** it does not say JIT is always fragile, and it does not say a well managed JIT system is safe. It does not name dual sourcing, buffers, nearshoring or flexibility as the winner, and it gives no buffer size. The figures return a shape, not a name. Which lever fits which residual is left open and marked as a gap, not smuggled in as an answer.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this message was sent):**
> 1. the same schedule, with suppliers and logistics held fixed, gives materially different resilience across repeated disruptions;
> 2. a disruption reaches the focal firm from three or more tiers out with no live residual anywhere in the chain;
> 3. an aggregated metric with a stated firing threshold stays diagnostic when one component slot goes live;
> 4. an arrangement stays resilient after managers stop acting, across several habitat changes and no re-enumeration;
> 5. one intervention clears both the contact condition and the channel condition with no different residue left over.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public source links behind it. The figures, not my summary, are the authority. I am also happy to send the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that you write your own refuter and state your own mode before running it. I am not asking you to trust the frame, to endorse it, or to say whether you already believed something like this.
>
> Thank you for reading.
