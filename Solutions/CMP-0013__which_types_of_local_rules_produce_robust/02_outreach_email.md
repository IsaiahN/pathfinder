# Outreach draft : check the shape before you decide about the rule list

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 are stated exactly as written. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about robust collective behavior, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question is which local rules give collective behavior that is robust across different initial conditions. Three things come out of a figure-grounded derivation, in effect terms:
>
> 1. Robustness is not a property of a rule alone. It is a property of the rule together with its habitat, its coupling, and the family of initial conditions. Change the contact and the same rule can stop being robust.
> 2. The question bundles two different conditions: does local interaction produce order at all, and is that order invariant to the starting state? A rule can satisfy one without the other, so the question should be split before it is searched.
> 3. The shape of a rule that is invariant to initial conditions is a closing feedback loop that damps its own per-slot remainder instead of amplifying it, and a regulator that survives its own use. The framework does not name a specific rule type. Naming "negative feedback", "majority rule", or "topological alignment" is a binding from your field onto the shape, and it needs a measurement, not an argument.
>
> I am deliberately not sending a list of favorite rules. The framework returns a shape, not a name. What would license a name is a measurement in which a per-slot invariance metric shows one class surviving across initial conditions while another does not.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a local rule with no closing or damping loop, in a system with a genuinely amplifying remainder, whose macro pattern is still invariant to initial conditions;
> 2. a rule type shown to be robust in every habitat tested, independent of contact;
> 3. a standing list of robust rules that survives a change of the task;
> 4. invariance established by agreement among people reading the same literature, with no measurement behind it.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract, cited as Fig N :line): see `04_links_and_distribution.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **What one check looks like:** pick a rule you know well, state the habitat and the family of initial conditions, and say what you would expect to see if the closing-loop shape were true. Then tell me what observation would count as a failure. I would rather have the failure.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground, and write your own refuter before you run it. The write-up warns that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula", so a reported failure is more useful than a reported success.
