# The Tether's answer: which local rules produce robust collective behavior

**Mode: `specified` on the structural shape, `general` on the reframe. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a list of winners. Simulated derivation checked against the verbatim figure text (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Running a fixed local rule set from different initial conditions gives either one macro pattern or many. We want the class of rules for which the outcome is invariant. But the question as posed names a level (the local rule) and omits the habitat, and it asks for types (names) where the framework returns shapes. The residual is restated in effect terms before answering: what structural property of a rule, in an enumerated habitat, gives macro invariance to a stated family of initial conditions, and what measurement would license naming a specific rule type.

## 2. The shape

1. **Robustness is a property of rule and habitat together.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*; Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The same loop holds at one agent or a thousand (Fig 4 :19 to :20). A rule-only answer silently changes the habitat, which is the choice the figures require to be made visible.
2. **The question is a union, not one question.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Separate "does order form" from "is it invariant to the starting state". A rule can satisfy one without the other, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds.
3. **The robust shape is a closing loop with a maintained regulator and a per-slot damped remainder.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 13 :31 to :32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Amplification of an undiscriminated remainder produces sensitivity; a loop whose sign opposes the deviation closes and stabilizes. Amplification of a ground-discriminated difference, as in ant trail reinforcement, is a different case and is not a contradiction (`06` Q3).
4. **Measure invariance per slot, not as an average.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :28: *"A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."* Averaging over initial conditions hides the subset on which a rule fails, which is exactly the subset the union split needs.
5. **No rule type is named by the framework.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* "Negative feedback", "majority rule", and "topological neighbourhood" are bindings from the CAS frame onto the shape, licensed only by a ground measurement or a provenanced import with a shadow test. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
6. **Ingredients are not arrangements.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Two rule sets with the same local rules under different bonds are different substances. "Which rules" is incomplete without "which arrangement" (`06` Q1 to Q4).
7. **The selector is the ground, not the community.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of winning rule types. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that negative feedback always wins. The sign that closes depends on what the loop feeds on; a maintained regulator and a per-slot damped remainder are part of the shape.
- Not that the rule level is the right level. The habitat is co-equal (Fig 11 :39).
- Not that the two bundled conditions always agree. They are a union until shown otherwise.
- Not grounded. No per-slot invariance measurement was taken and no rule class was settled.
- Not that amplification is always destabilizing. Amplifying a ground-discriminated difference is how a collective decision forms; amplifying an undiscriminated remainder is chaos.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Per-slot invariance metric.** A measurement that, for a stated family of initial conditions, returns for each slot the residual under each candidate rule, without pooling. If the distribution collapses to the same macro pattern across the family for one class and not for another, a name is licensed for that habitat.
2. **Loop-sign test.** Hold the ingredients fixed and vary only the bond sign and the regulator's maintenance. Does the closing, self-regenerating version give invariance while the open or spent version does not?
3. **Bundle test.** For each rule class, measure order formation and initial-condition invariance separately. If they dissociate across the class, the union split is confirmed.
4. **Standing refuters from `01` §5:** an invariant rule with no damping and an amplifying remainder; a rule robust in every habitat; a standing list of robust rules; invariance by agreement without measurement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework has returned the shape any robust-rule candidate must have, refuters written in advance, and the frames that already hold parts of the answer (attractor, SOC, canalisation, swarm, resilience engineering, no-free-lunch). It has not measured a rule class. *"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* (Fig 8 :39), so this is `candidate`, not `gap`. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap between the shorthand and the concrete rule list is recorded in `11_audit.md`. Indiscernibility is not identity, and the lineage is the proof.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of winning local rules. It says that robustness is a property of a rule together with its habitat, so a rule-only answer hides the choice that does the work; that the question bundles two conditions, does order form and is it invariant to the starting state, which must be separated before searching; and that the shape of a rule that is invariant is a closing feedback loop with a maintained regulator that damps its per-slot remainder rather than amplifying it, with amplification allowed only when it feeds on a difference the ground has already discriminated. It refuses to name a specific rule type, because the framework returns a shape, not a name: negative feedback, majority rule, and topological neighbourhood are bindings from complex adaptive systems research onto the shape, and they need a measurement. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the per-slot instrument that would close the question. The name stays open until the ground settles it.
