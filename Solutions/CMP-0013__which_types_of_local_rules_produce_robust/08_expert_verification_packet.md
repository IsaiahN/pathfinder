# Expert verification packet : the Tether on robust collective behavior

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural shape, `general` on the reframe. Not `grounded`.** No rule class was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a list of winners.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Robustness across initial conditions is not a property of a local rule alone. It is a property of the rule together with its habitat, its coupling, and the family of starting states. The question bundles two conditions, does local interaction produce order at all and is that order invariant to the starting state, and it should be split before searching. The shape of a rule whose macro outcome is invariant is a closing feedback loop with a maintained regulator that damps its per-slot remainder rather than amplifying it, with amplification permitted only when the loop feeds on a difference the ground has already discriminated. The framework does not name a specific rule type; it returns a shape. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the per-slot instrument that would close the question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal and structural; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 4
- **Statement:** robustness is a property of rule and habitat, not of the rule alone. A rule-only answer changes the habitat silently.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 : *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :40 : *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 4 :19 to :20 : *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- **What to check:** is the habitat variable actually doing work in the systems you know, or is the same rule robust regardless of coupling and starting set?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a local rule whose macro invariance holds in every habitat and coupling tested, independent of contact. That breaks Fig 11 :39 to :40.

### C2 : the structural shape; checkable against Fig 12 and Fig 13
- **Statement:** the robust shape is a closing loop with a maintained regulator and a damped per-slot remainder. Amplifying an undiscriminated remainder gives chaos; amplifying a ground-discriminated difference gives a collective decision.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 : *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 : *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 13 :31 to :32 : *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Fig 1 :19 : *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** in a flocking, foraging, or segregation model you know, does the closing, self-regenerating version give invariance while the open or spent version does not? Does amplification operate on a discriminated difference or on raw remainder?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a rule with no closing loop and no per-slot damping, in a system with a genuinely amplifying remainder, whose macro pattern is still invariant to initial conditions. Or show that an amplifying loop feeding on an undiscriminated remainder still yields invariance.

### C3 : framework-internal limit and verification rule
- **Statement:** no rule type is named by the framework; the selector is the ground, not agreement among modellers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 : *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 11 :51 : *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 7 :35 : *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35 : *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **What to check:** can a single rule class be named from the figures alone? Is there a standing list of robust rules that survives a change of task?
- **Refuter:** derive a rule type from the figures that dominates all habitats; or establish invariance by agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool with no measurement. Either breaks the limit.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of winning local rules, and not a ranking of rule types.
- Not that negative feedback always wins. The sign that closes depends on the input to the loop; a maintained regulator and a per-slot damped remainder are part of the shape.
- Not that the rule level is the right level. Habitat and coupling are co-equal.
- Not that the two bundled conditions always agree.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that a specific bond or rule is figure-derived. Naming "negative feedback", "majority rule" or "topological neighbourhood" is a binding from the CAS frame, provenanced as such, and still unproven.
- Not that amplification is always destabilizing. Ground-discriminated amplification builds collective decisions.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot, source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*. Here:

1. **Per-slot invariance metric.** For a stated family of initial conditions, return the residual for each slot under each candidate rule, without pooling. Which class collapses to one macro pattern, in which habitat?
2. **Loop-sign test.** Hold ingredients fixed and vary only bond sign and regulator maintenance. Does the closing, self-regenerating version give invariance?
3. **Bundle test.** Measure order formation and initial-condition invariance separately for each rule class. Do they dissociate?
4. **Standing refuters from `01` §5.** An invariant rule with no damping and an amplifying remainder; a rule robust in every habitat; a standing robust list; invariance by agreement without measurement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39 to :40, Fig 12 :119, :121, Fig 13 :31 to :32, and Fig 9 :32 (five short passages).
2. Check C1 and C2 against one system you know: name the habitat, the family of initial conditions, and the loop sign.
3. Say which measurement would distinguish two rule sets that give the same macro pattern.
4. Name one case where amplification feeds on a ground-discriminated difference rather than an undiscriminated remainder, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
