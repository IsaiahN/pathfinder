## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Atom or molecule?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 13 :74-75: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** and **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- A horizon is a null boundary: containment (space, inside and outside), a future direction of no return (time), a spendable difference (surface gravity, gradient-like), and an area that can be ordered (spectrum-like). That is a molecule over held atoms, not a seventh atom.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** A horizon that is settled against the ground could become an atom at the next level. In this derivation it is not settled at the quantum level, so it stays a molecule.
- **Falsifier:** derive the horizon as a seventh primitive from the other six, or show the framework admits a direct state-state bond at the horizon that it cannot refuse. Then Q1 fails.

### Q2 - What order is the contact?

- First order: a single crossing `state - structure - state`. The horizon as a classical object reads `Space - (boundary structure) - Time`, with surface gravity as a gradient over that boundary. Legal under Fig 13 :75.
- Semiclassical: QFT on a fixed background is an imported frame (Fig 6 :23). It crosses from Domain 2 into the horizon composite but does not yet compose at second order, because the background is not itself a settled molecule in the quantum sense.
- Second order: the union requires both GR and QM as settled first-order molecules, each becoming an atom for the next level under Fig 12 :104. Their comparison is a threshold relation at a scale, not a merged field. This is the order claim, and it needs no new primitive.
- **Type strain, named not hidden:** the Operators table defines `⋛` as **"a threshold relation between two quantities."** At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Triangulated for the recursion (Fig 12 :104 and Fig 13 :110-112), strained on operand type.
- **Falsifier:** show a settled molecule cannot serve as an atom, or that `⋛`'s operands must be bare quantities at every level. Then the second-order claim fails and only the first-order horizon composite survives.

### Q3 - Are the classical horizon and the stretched horizon isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The classical event horizon is a global causal boundary. The stretched horizon of complementarity is a local dissipative membrane. If both are written over the same atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` and differ only in which bond does the joining, they are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their behavioral difference (global no-escape versus local thermalization) matches the figure's "decisive in the behaviour."
- If the stretched horizon requires an atom the classical horizon does not, they are not isomers; they are two members of a **functional group** (`state - structure - state`) with different atoms in the slots. This is the weaker reading and is the one to fall back on.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit an atom required by one horizon concept and not the other. The isomer claim then fails and the functional-group reading stands. Mark `specified`, single-source for the figure definition, physics-facing check unmeasured.

### Q4 - Where does the firewall enter the operator set?

- Formula route step: `REFUTED` is the fifth bin, and *"a term we hold is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*
- Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
- Operators table :57: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**
- Operators table :73-74: *"− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim. A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*
- The firewall result is therefore a `¬` on the term "smooth, information-free horizon with valid semiclassical exterior and unitary evaporation." It is not a new bond and not a new atom. It removes the standing of that term to be composed over; nothing is deleted from the record.
- **Falsifier:** show the three postulates are mutually consistent, in which case the route is `TRANSFERRED`, no negation is owed, and this Q4 derivation is withdrawn.

### Q5 - Smoothness, discreteness and the continuum

- Fig 13 :43-44: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"** and **"smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."**
- Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
- So "is the horizon smooth?" is a well-formed question about what a composition leaves, not a question about a primitive. It is not answered by the figures; it is answered by an instrument, which does not exist at a real horizon.
- **Falsifier:** derive smoothness or discreteness as a member of the minimum set from the other five. Then Fig 13's minimality claim fails.

## CONCLUSION

The horizon is a first-order molecule over the six, not a seventh atom. The horizon/quantum contact is second-order and licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule, with a named operand-type strain. The classical and stretched horizons are a real isomer claim if they share the full atom set, otherwise a functional-group claim. The firewall result is a negation, not a mint. Smoothness and discreteness are composition remainders. These are structural results, not measurements.
