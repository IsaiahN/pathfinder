**Subject:** Check one output about black hole horizons before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (in effect terms).** A black hole horizon is a causal boundary from general relativity: a null surface dividing events that can still signal infinity from those that cannot. Quantum field theory on that background makes it radiate thermally, with an entropy proportional to area. Semiclassical radiation is thermal and appears to carry no information about the infalling state, while quantum evolution is unitary. The question is not "which theory wins" but what a consistent account would have to look like, and where the current machinery stops.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures say four things about this:
>
> 1. The horizon is a composite over a six-term minimum set (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures). Composition runs across the two kinds, never within one, so a horizon is a molecule, not a primitive. Smoothness and discreteness are what a composition leaves behind, not extra ingredients.
> 2. "How does the horizon fit with quantum physics?" is a union of at least three questions: entropy and temperature, information and unitarity, smoothness and the interior. It must be split before it is searched. A lever that clears one condition leaves the intersections standing.
> 3. The firewall conflict is a **refutation**, not a discovery of new matter. When three held terms conflict, what is owed is a refusal of one of them, not a new field or a new primitive.
> 4. The framework's link to black-hole thermodynamics and holography is a **retrodiction**. The figures name Bekenstein and holography as a case already known that the framework fits, and say the claim has not been tested forward. I am not claiming it predicted Hawking radiation or the area law.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. deriving the horizon, smoothness or discreteness as a seventh primitive from the other five terms;
> 2. showing a forward, novel prediction about horizon physics derived from the six-term set and then confirmed at the ground;
> 3. an instrument that discriminates candidate microstate accounts at a real horizon, settling smoothness as a primitive;
> 4. showing the three firewall postulates are mutually consistent, so no refusal is owed;
> 5. showing the three sub-questions share one ground and cannot be unbundled.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework solves the information paradox, that it derives Hawking radiation or the Bekenstein-Hawking formula, that a particular microstate (fuzzball, firewall, condensate, remnant) is the answer, or that the result is grounded. It returns a shape, not a name. Agreement from people who already accept the framework is not evidence; by the framework's own rule, agreement from a shared evidence pool is the pool talking.
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
