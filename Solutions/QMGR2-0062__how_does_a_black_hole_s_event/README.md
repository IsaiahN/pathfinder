## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim to have solved the black hole information paradox.
- No claim that the framework predicts Hawking radiation or the area law. It names them as known cases it fits.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40, agreement from a shared evidence pool is worthless as corroboration).
2. Sending the full architecture before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence (Fig 10 :42).
3. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32). Presenting a named microstate as the answer would exceed what the framework claims.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional submission channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
