## 0. The residual stated in effect terms

The source section states the coupling problem: a replicating molecule alone is not yet a cell, and a simple compartment alone does not automatically reproduce information. The residual R is therefore **the coupling of replication, metabolism and boundary**. Stated as an effect, not a mechanism:

> Something must keep the products of a reaction network near their producers, while the network stays in contact with a flow of matter and energy that can be spent. Without that, products diffuse away, no slot accumulates a live residual, and no lineage can settle.

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The slot here is the compartment. A well-mixed ocean has no slot; everything is averaged, and Fig 1 :22 warns that *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* So the question is not "is a pore a nice place for chemistry" but "does a pore provide a slot with a live, positive residual that stays in contact with a gradient?"

Direction (formula step "Direction"): I write down what an answer would look like before the run so the preference can be refused. **Answer shape S:** a compartment is a directed filter, a spatial containment bonded through a structure to a gradient, and the handoff from pore to membrane is a substitution of habitats rather than a removal of one. S is an ordinary term here and accrues a residual like anything else.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A compartment is a filter with a direction, not a wall (decisive for the shape)

Fig 4 :21 *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*

**Refuter:** a boundary that retains everything, with no directed exchange of matter or energy, that still sustains a composing reaction network. That would break the filter shape claim. Note the re-census in §6 R1: this figure's membrane governs the crossing between scales, not a lipid bilayer, so it constrains the *rule* a compartment obeys rather than naming a physical object.

### F2 - Containment is a state, not a new primitive; it cannot bond directly to energy

Fig 13 :10 *"near, far, between, contained"* names containment as a relation within the state **space**. Fig 13 :74 *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 13 :75 *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*

So a mineral pore (space, contained) cannot compose directly with energy. A structure must stand between: a gradient, which Fig 13 :22 calls *"a difference that can be spent"*, or a network, Fig 13 :24 *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*. A pore that merely encloses molecules without a spendable gradient satisfies only one term of the compound and does not close R.

**Refuter:** a measurable case where a bounded region with no maintained gradient still accumulates and composes a reaction network that a well-mixed control does not. That breaks the cross-kind requirement at the ground.

### F3 - The handoff is a substitution of habitats, not a removal

Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*

A pore-to-membrane transition is therefore not "the membrane replaces the pore and the pore vanishes". It is the residual moved to a new habitat, with the goal's conditions reproduced and the new habitat's unchosen actors inherited. This is why the exhaust of a vent and a lipid boundary in a hot spring are not interchangeable settings: each brings actors the searcher did not choose. Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the same chemistry retains the same contact and the same composition after the boundary is swapped, so that the habitat made no difference to the goal. That would dissolve the substitution claim.

### F4 - The habitat must be enumerated, not composed from a story

Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

For this question the enumeration includes pore walls and their mineralogy, the thermal gradient, pH contrast, wet-dry cycling, UV, flows, the molecules retained, and the lipids or condensates that may later form. Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* Any account that begins with "compartments must have been..." and then picks a mineral has composed the habitat. Baaske et al. 2007 enumerates a concrete case: interlinked mineral pores in a thermal gradient (world source W3).

**Refuter:** name an actor in contact with the residual that the enumeration cannot place, and show the derivation has no slot for it. That is a `NOVEL` boundary diff, not a reason to force it into the six terms.

### F5 - Describe the residual before choosing a frame

Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

The public literature often starts from the setting (vent, hot spring, clay) and maps the chemistry onto it. The figures require the opposite: state the coupling residual first, then ask which frame predicts its shape. The shape returned is a directed filter with a gradient, not the name "iron sulfide pore" or "fatty acid vesicle". Naming the carrier is a binding from the world frame onto held atoms, provenanced as such (F7).

**Refuter:** a frame selected before the residual is described whose predictions fit the residual better than the shape claim. That is apophenia by the figure's own test, and the frame wins the comparison until the residual is redescribed.

### F6 - Historical priority is a `gap`, and `gap` is a verdict

Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The word "before" in the question is a historical ordering claim. No figure line governs it. The operators table defines sequence (`→`) as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, but that is Composition inside a closure, not a geological chronology. The figures can constrain what a compartment must be and what would settle it; they cannot date the first compartment. Reporting `gap` here is the honest stop, not an omission. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Refuter (of the gap verdict):** a figure line that directly governs temporal priority between two habitat kinds, quoted verbatim, with the strain test passed. Until one is shown, the priority claim stays `gap`.

### F7 - The pore is a composition of held atoms, not an import that moves the wall

Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :25 *"An import adds no atom to the world."*

Space (contained), gradient and network are already among the six, so a mineral pore adds no new primitive. Calling the pore an import that moves the wall is a category error; the import would be a new kind, not an arrangement. What can cross between frames is the binding of a world term, such as "thermodiffusive concentration in a plugged pore", onto the held term gradient. That binding needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :11-13 *"IMPORT / source gate: provenance / shadow test: explains a gap"*).

**Refuter:** show that mineral pores require a primitive not in the six, with no composition of the six that reaches it. That would reopen the import question.

### F8 - Mineral walls can be catalysts; a lineage must close a cycle

Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

A mineral pore wall that catalyzes bond formation and survives its own use is Fig 12's catalyst, not a membrane. But catalyst alone is not a lineage: the output must re-enter as input with the right sign. Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The pore and the vesicle may share the functional group "directed filter with a gradient" while being different members. This is a shape claim about kind, not a claim that one is the other.

**Refuter:** a mineral wall consumed by its own catalysis, so it cannot survive repeated use, or a pore chemistry whose output never re-enters as input. Either breaks the analogy at the specified level.

### F9 - The verification frame cannot corroborate by agreement

Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A pile of papers agreeing that "pores were probably the first compartments" is one pool talking. The first contact must carry the shape claim and its refuter, and the recipient's verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any theory is shown. Seat map line 287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

**Refuter:** a case where two independent instruments (not two reviews of the same papers) triangulate against the shape claim. That would override this caution.

## 6. Strains and re-census (kept visible)

**R1, Fig 4 read literally.** My first pass treated *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* as a claim about physical lipid membranes and therefore concluded that the figures prefer membranes over pores. That is a strain: Fig 4's membrane is the boundary between one agent and a population, the rule about what crosses between sizes. Re-census: the physical compartment is governed by Fig 13 (containment as a state) and Fig 11 (habitat substitution); Fig 4 supplies the *rule form* a boundary must have. The conclusion "a boundary filters with direction" survives; the conclusion "the figures prefer lipid membranes" is withdrawn. This is recorded, not erased.

**R2, the import reading.** My first pass called mineral pores an import from geology into the framework. Re-census: space, gradient and network are all already in the six (Fig 13), so the pore is an arrangement inside `closure(Γ)`, and Fig 6 :25 says an import adds no atom to the world. The claim is withdrawn. What crosses is the binding of the empirical description onto held atoms, which needs provenance (Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*).

**R3, the "before" operator.** The operators table defines `→` as sequence inside a composition, not as geological priority. Applying it to "pore before membrane" is a type error, not a derivation. Named, not hidden. After re-census: **no live strain; three retracted readings kept above.**
