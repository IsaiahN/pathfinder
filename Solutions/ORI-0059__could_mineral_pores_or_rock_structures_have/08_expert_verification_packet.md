# Expert verification packet - the Tether on mineral pores as compartments

**What this is:** a claim in a form a mineralogist, geochemist or origins-of-life researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the substitution claim, `general` on the structural rule, `gap` on historical priority, not `grounded`.** No measurement of `left(R,φ)` is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Any compartment must be a filter with a direction, not a wall: a spatial containment bonded through a structure to a spendable gradient, so that reaction products stay near their producers while the network remains in contact with a flow it can use. Containment is a state, so it cannot bond directly to energy; a structure must mediate. The habitat around the residual is enumerated, not composed from a story about what compartments must be. Moving a chemistry from a rock pore to a self-made membrane is a substitution of habitats, with the goal's conditions reproduced and the new habitat's actors inherited, not a replacement of one setting by the other. The framework does not name the carrier and does not date the handoff; historical priority is reported as `gap`, a verdict rather than silence. The boundary stays a directed filter, and the successor boundary inherits the contact the first one held.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 13

- **Statement:** a compartment is a directed filter, and containment (a state) cannot bond directly to energy; a structure must stand between.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :21 *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 13 :10 *"near, far, between, contained"*. Fig 13 :74 *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 13 :75 *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- **Re-census note:** Fig 4's membrane governs the crossing between scales, not a lipid bilayer. It supplies the rule form of a boundary; the physical compartment is governed by Fig 13 and Fig 11. This correction is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 R1 and is not hidden.
- **Refuter:** a bounded region that retains everything, with no directed exchange and no spendable gradient, that still sustains a composing reaction network against a well-mixed control. Or a derivation of containment from the other five terms.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the pore literature

- **Statement:** mineral pores can supply concentration and a gradient (the shape's terms), but concentration alone is not a lineage; the product must re-enter as input, and there is a live dispute about whether pore trapping prevents that.
- **What to check:** Baaske et al. 2007 report that *"interlinked mineral pores in a thermal gradient provide a compelling high-concentration starting point for the molecular evolution of life."* Erastova et al. 2017 report minerals that *"concentrate, align and act as adsorption templates for amino acids"*. Against these, W2 (Wikipedia *Protocell*) reports Deamer and Damer's objection that pore products *"would become trapped within these pore systems upon polymerization and would not undergo combinatorial selection"*. Does the trapping objection hold for the pore sizes and flow rates in the experiment being assessed?
- **Source, verbatim (figure side):** Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*
- **Refuter:** a pore system whose products demonstrably re-enter as input and undergo selection, which corroborates the shape; or a pore system that is a pure sink, which supports the objection. Either way the figure line C2 states is tested at the ground.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the pore to membrane transition is a habitat substitution, not a compositional sequence and not a replacement. The goal's conditions must be reproduced in the new habitat, and its unchosen actors are inherited.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*.
- **Refuter:** a boundary swap that preserves contact and composition exactly, so the habitat is irrelevant to the goal; or a figure line governing geological priority between two habitat kinds, quoted verbatim, which would move the `gap` on "before".

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that mineral pores were the first compartments, and not that membranes were.
- Not that the framework prefers a setting. The residual is described before the frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28).
- Not that the pore is an import that moves the wall. All its atoms are held (Fig 6 :25).
- Not an isomer claim between pore and vesicle at the narrow atom set; only the functional-group claim (Fig 12 :124) survives without the full shared set.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that this derivation is a ground reading. It is a simulation checked for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Filter test:** directed exchange measured against a well-mixed control.
2. **Gradient test:** accumulation with the gradient removed.
3. **Cycle test:** product re-entry and selection, which is where W2's objection bites.
4. **Sequencing test:** a geological or experimental reading that orders the two boundary kinds. Only this moves the `gap` on "before".

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :21, Fig 13 :10, :74-75 and Fig 11 :66-67. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one pore experiment you know: does it measure a directed exchange and a maintained gradient, or only a final concentration?
3. Say whether the trapping objection applies to that experiment's pore sizes and flow rates.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish concentration-by-pore from concentration-by-surface from concentration-by-wet-dry-cycle, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
