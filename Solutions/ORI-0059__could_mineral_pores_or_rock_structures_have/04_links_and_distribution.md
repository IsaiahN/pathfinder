## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Concentration is a real pore effect.** Baaske et al. show that plugged pores in a thermal gradient can accumulate nucleotides by many orders of magnitude, and state that *"complicated active membrane transport is not required for the initial steps."* This corroborates the shape's containment and gradient terms. It does not date the first compartment and does not show re-entry of products.
2. **Mineral surfaces do more than hold molecules.** Erastova et al. show layered hydroxides concentrating, aligning and templating amino acids, which fits Fig 12's catalyst analogue. This is a world report about a held atom, not an import that moves the wall.
3. **The setting is a substitution, not a single winner.** Damer and Deamer explicitly compare vent and pool scenarios and argue for wet-dry cycling. That is evidence for a handoff between habitats, which corroborates Fig 11 :66. It also means "pores first" is a contested empirical claim, not a figure result.
4. **A live conflict about cycles.** The Protocell article reports that Deamer and Damer argue pore products *"would become trapped within these pore systems upon polymerization and would not undergo combinatorial selection"*. If trapping is real, the pore is a concentrator and not a lineage; the cycle test in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is where this is settled.
5. **The reading does not determine the source.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a green reading *"cannot comment on the source"*. Several settings produce "concentrated chemistry". What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a favourite mineral.

The proposition is: **(1)** validate the output against the ground, **(2)** only if curious, read the theory that produced it, **(3)** use the formula recursively on other problems.

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* and Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
