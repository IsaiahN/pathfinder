**Question:** Could mineral pores or rock structures have acted as compartments before free-floating membranes evolved?

**Source:** `origins of life.txt` line 80 (question 59), section 6 "Membranes, compartments, and protocells".

## The question in effect terms

What is being decided is whether a boundary that is not self-generated (a pore or a rock cavity) can do the work a boundary must do, namely keep the products of a reaction network near their producers while the network remains in contact with a flow of matter and energy. Option A, yes, mineral pores were the first compartments. Option B, no, only a membrane the chemistry makes itself counts. A sends you to look for concentrated chemistry in ancient pore networks; B sends you to look for the first self-assembled lipid boundary. The source section states the coupling problem directly: a replicating molecule alone is not yet a cell, and a simple compartment alone does not automatically reproduce information.

The shape an answer would have, written down and bound before the run (formula step "Direction"): **a boundary is a directed filter, not a wall; it is a spatial containment bonded through a structure to a gradient, and moving a residual from one habitat to another substitutes a habitat rather than removing one.** That shape is refusable. The historical claim "before free-floating membranes evolved" is not a shape the figures can hold, and is reported as `gap`, not silence.
