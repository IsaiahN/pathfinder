# Supporting derivation: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the composition rule, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for an ordering: mineral pores *before* free-floating membranes. The effect that must be explained is not chronology but continuity of contact. How can a boundary that the chemistry did not make hand its residual to a boundary the chemistry does make, without losing the gradient and the retained products that the residual needs? Three sub-questions are owed: (1) what is a compartment made of; (2) what is the order of the handoff; (3) what analogues does the figure set already supply?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space (including *"near, far, between, contained"*, Fig 13 :10), Time, Energy as states; Gradient (*"a difference that can be spent"*, Fig 13 :22), Spectrum, Network as structures. Bonds held: the seven, including sequence `→`, production `⇒`, comparison ⋛, subtraction `−`, plus unary negation ¬. No new primitive is available without import. A mineral pore is an **arrangement** of these atoms, not a seventh member.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is a compartment made of?

- Fig 13 :74 *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- Fig 13 :75 *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- A compartment is Space under the relation *contained*. Containment is a state, so it cannot bond directly to Energy. A structure must stand between: a Gradient (spendable directedness) or a Network (relations through which influence travels).
- First-order form: `Space(contained) - structure - Energy/Time`, one crossing, legal under Fig 13 :74-75. A pore with no spendable gradient is a one-term fragment, not a first-order molecule; it cannot close R.
- Fig 4 :21 *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Re-census recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 R1: this figure supplies the **rule form** for the boundary, not the physical carrier.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a bounded region with no directed exchange and no spendable gradient that still sustains a composing reaction network against a well-mixed control. That breaks the first-order form.

### Q2 - What is the order of the handoff?

- The operators table defines `→` as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* inside a composition. Applying it to geological priority is a type error (strain R3 in `01`). Sequence here is compositional, not chronological.
- The figure that actually governs the handoff is Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* and Fig 11 :67 *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*
- So the order is not `pore → membrane` as replacement. It is: (a) a residual settles or partially settles in habitat H1 (pore); (b) the residual is moved to H2 (self-made boundary); (c) the goal's conditions are reproduced in H2; (d) H2's unchosen actors are inherited. Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*.
- **Falsifier:** a swap that leaves contact and composition unchanged, so the habitat made no difference. That dissolves the substitution claim.

### Q3 - Are the two carriers isomers, a functional group, or something else?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Written narrowly, a mineral pore `{Space(contained), Gradient, Network}` and a lipid vesicle `{Space(contained), Gradient, Lipid}` share only containment, so they are **not isomers**; they are two members of the functional group "directed filter with a spendable gradient".
- Written over the full shared atom set, with the carrier slots differing only in which structure mediates which containment, they can be a genuine isomer claim: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the stronger, checkable form.
- World conflict, recorded not hidden: W2 (Wikipedia *Protocell*) reports that Deamer and Damer argue biomolecules in pore systems *"would become trapped within these pore systems upon polymerization and would not undergo combinatorial selection"*. If trapping is real, the pore and the vesicle are not functionally equivalent members of the same group, and the isomer reading fails at the behavioural level. That is a ground question, not a figure verdict.

### Q4 - What analogues does the figure set supply?

- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A mineral wall that catalyzes bond formation and survives repeated use is a catalyst. It is not thereby a compartment; the filter rule of F1 still applies.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A lineage requires the product to re-enter as input. A pore that accumulates products which never re-enter is a sink, not a cycle. This is the sharpest structural difference between "concentrated chemistry" and "a compartment that did something".
- **Level relativity.** Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Once a pore reaction network settles against the ground, it can become an operand for the next level, which is how a habitat can be inherited without being composed from scratch.
- **Falsifier for the analogues:** a mineral wall consumed by its own catalysis, or a pore chemistry whose output never re-enters as input. Either breaks the analogy at the `specified` level.

## CONCLUSION

A compartment is a first-order cross-kind molecule: a spatial containment bonded through a structure to a spendable gradient, with the boundary behaving as a filter with direction. The pore-to-membrane relation is a **habitat substitution**, not a compositional `→` and not a replacement. The two carriers are, at best, members of a functional group; the stronger isomer reading needs the full shared atom set and fails if the world conflict about trapping is settled against it. Mineral walls fit the figure's catalyst slot, and only a cycle closes a lineage. All of this is `specified` shape, not a date and not a ground reading.
