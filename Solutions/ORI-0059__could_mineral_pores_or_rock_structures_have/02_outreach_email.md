**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether mineral pores were the first compartments

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** replication, metabolism and a boundary have to be coupled. A replicating molecule alone is not a cell, and a compartment alone does not reproduce information. In effect terms, something must keep reaction products near their producers while the chemistry stays in contact with a flow of matter and energy it can spend. Otherwise products diffuse away and no lineage accumulates.
>
> **The output I want checked:** any compartment must be a **filter with a direction, not a wall**. Containment is a spatial state, so it cannot bond directly to energy; a structure must stand between, and the structure has to be spendable, like a temperature or pH gradient. The habitat around the residual has to be enumerated, not composed from a story about what compartments should be. And moving a chemistry from a rock pore to a membrane is a **substitution of habitats**: the goal's conditions must be reproduced in the new one, and you inherit actors you did not choose. It is not "the pore was replaced by the membrane" as a simple swap.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that mineral pores *were* the first compartments, that membranes were, or that the framework prefers one setting. The framework returns a shape, not a name and not a date. The historical "before" question is reported as `gap`, a verdict rather than silence. No measurement here settles it, so none of this is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A boundary that retains everything, with no directed exchange and no maintained gradient, that still sustains a composing reaction network against a well-mixed control.
> 2. A bounded region with the gradient removed that still accumulates products against the control.
> 3. A swap of pore for self-made boundary that changes neither contact nor composition, so the habitat made no difference.
> 4. A pore system whose products demonstrably re-enter as input and undergo selection in a way the wall does not permit (this would be a successful correction, not a defeat).
>
> I wrote these before running the derivation. If any holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. Public sources:
> - Figures and formula, direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The worked architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the architecture essay warns that outputs could be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
