## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Tishby, Pereira and Bialek (2000)** supply the formal version of the kept/discarded cut: minimise I(X;T) while preserving I(T;Y). The relevance variable Y is not part of the code, which matches Fig 2's anchor requirement.
- **Barlow (1961, 2001)** supplies both the original redundancy-reduction hypothesis and its own later correction. The correction is important: the framework's MDL bargain is about what remains unexplained, not about saving neural impulses, so the two must not be equated.
- **McClelland, McNaughton and O'Reilly (1995)** and **O'Reilly and Norman (2002)** supply the union reading: fast separation of specific events and slow extraction of shared structure are two conditions, not one objective.
- **Croston and others (2015)** and the seasonal hippocampal plasticity review supply an animal-behaviour anchor: what is remembered is shaped by a ground (winter survival), not by an internal economy measure.
- **Cover and Thomas (2006)** supply the mathematics: a finite representation cannot be perfect, and a distortion measure defines the quality of a representation. This is the rate and budget side, not the structure side.

Agreement among these sources is not independent corroboration of the figure verdict where they share an evidence pool. They are from distinct fields, but each corroborates a different half of the union. See `10_source_ledger.csv` for overlap declarations.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full figure set before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 9 :60**, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A list selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the figures and the two background papers, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
