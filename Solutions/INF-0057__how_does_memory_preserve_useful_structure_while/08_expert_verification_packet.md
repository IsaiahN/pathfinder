# Expert verification packet: the Tether on memory and structure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a memory researcher, cognitive scientist or information theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, with one `general` structural claim. Not `grounded`.** No memory measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Memory's two demands have different anchors and form a union compound, not one optimisation problem. The discard half is a lossy cross-scale transform: throwing away detail is what makes a stored trace reapplicable, and the trip is not reversible. The preserve half is structural: what survives is the typed relation among items, not the item list, so two memories with the same ingredients under different bonds are different substances. A detail is retained when stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than what was unexplained before, which rules out both a vacuous summary and a perfect record of one occasion. "Useful" cannot be decided by the memory's own bookkeeping; it is settled by a relevance variable external to the frame, a later retrieval, inference, action or outcome. The cut is described per slot, the discarded detail returns as noise, and the round-trip gap is the honest report of what was given up. Retrieval re-enters as encoding, so memory has the topology of a cycle, not a chain. The framework returns this shape, not a neural mechanism.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 12

- **Statement:** any cross-scale memory is lossy, and loss is what buys reuse; what is kept is the bond, not the ingredients.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27, *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :28, *"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."* Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- **What to check:** is the loss really the enabling condition for reuse, not just an engineering limitation? Does a relation-level description of a memory predict more than an item-level one?
- **Refuter:** a cross-scale memory that is both perfectly reversible and genuinely reusable; or a memory that supports inference and generalisation while carrying only unordered item lists, where the list is not a compressed encoding of a bond.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 5

- **Statement:** retention follows a minimum-description-length bargain, not a fixed capacity or count threshold. The two named failure cases are the vacuous summary and the perfect record of one occasion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :42, *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* Fig 5 :43, *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* Fig 5 :45, *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."*
- **What to check:** does retention in the system you know track what remains unexplained, or just storage pressure? Is the vacuous-summary failure actually observed?
- **Refuter:** a memory system whose retention is a fixed capacity or count threshold, independent of what remains unexplained, and which still generalises to novel cases.

### C3: framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by reading Figures 2, 9 and 13 and by inspection of a memory task

- **Statement:** the two halves are a union compound with different anchors; usefulness is settled outside the frame; the cut is per slot; discarded detail returns as noise and is reported by `R_T`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 13 :32, *"because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- **What to check:** in your task battery, do the detail-discard and structure-preservation measures move independently? Is the reported error per item or per relation, or only a global average? Is there an external outcome that decides usefulness?
- **Refuter:** a single scalar objective with no relevance variable and no bond structure that simultaneously maximises detail discard and structure preservation on a battery where both are independently measured; or a memory system that improves its later performance while its contact with every task, outcome and relevance variable is unchanged.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a neural mechanism. No brain region, synapse, sleep stage or algorithm is named as the answer.
- Not that compression alone explains memory. That covers only one condition of the union.
- Not that the structure is a particular schema or concept. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the trace can recover its own source by inspection. Outputs are not generators, so the lineage must be recorded.
- Not that more storage is better. A recording of one occasion is not a reusable method.
- Not grounded. Nothing here was settled by a memory measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Discriminating tests:

1. **Isomer test:** two memories with the same item set but different bond type must differ on a later inference task. Same predictions means the bond is free.
2. **Bargain test:** vary what remains unexplained at fixed description length; retention should track the leftover, not the raw count.
3. **Anchor test:** change internal bookkeeping while holding task contact fixed; performance must not improve.
4. **Round-trip test:** measure `R_T` between a full episode and its compact structural description; it should be nonzero and should match the noise reported above the crossing.
5. **Cycle test:** block retrieval from modifying the stored trace; correct updating under novel conditions would break the cycle claim.
