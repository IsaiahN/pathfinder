# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been checked by the reader in the order below. The claim must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about memory and compression that you can refute in ten minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** how does memory preserve useful structure while discarding irrelevant detail?
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the two halves of that question have different anchors and must be kept separate. The discarding half is a lossy cross-scale transform. Throwing away detail is what makes a stored trace reapplicable, and the loss is not reversible. The preserving half is structural: what survives is the typed relation among items, not the list of items. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself. Retention is a bargain rather than a threshold: a detail is worth keeping when stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than what was unexplained before. A memory that explains one occasion perfectly fails that bargain. Finally, "useful" cannot be decided by the memory's own bookkeeping. It is settled by something the memory does not control, a later retrieval, an inference, an action or a task outcome.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the brain implements this in any particular region or synapse; that this is a new theory of memory; that compression alone explains the phenomenon; or that anything here is settled by measurement. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The two-system picture in the literature is corroboration from outside, not a figure-derived binding.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before writing this message:
>
> 1. A cross-scale memory that is both perfectly reversible and genuinely reusable, with no loss on the upward trip.
> 2. A memory that supports inference and generalisation while carrying only unordered item lists and no typed relations, where the list is not just a compressed encoding of a bond.
> 3. A memory system whose retention is a fixed capacity or count threshold, independent of what remains unexplained, and which still generalises to novel cases.
> 4. A memory system that improves its later performance while its contact with every task, outcome and relevance variable is unchanged.
>
> If any holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Information bottleneck paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057
> - Complementary learning systems paper: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7624455
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures are public. Run the method on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer (the rule in one place)

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because shared evidence is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust, agreement or endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
