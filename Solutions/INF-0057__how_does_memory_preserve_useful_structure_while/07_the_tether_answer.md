# The Tether's answer: how memory preserves useful structure while discarding irrelevant detail

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two conditions that have different anchors. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

- **Discard condition:** a finite trace cannot keep everything. Some loss is forced. The anchor is a distortion measure and a resource budget.
- **Preserve condition:** what survives must still support later inference, retrieval and action. The anchor is a relevance variable external to the memory's own account of itself.

A single lever, for example minimising reconstruction error, clears the region where only the discard condition holds and leaves the preserve condition standing. The Tether's answer is the shape of the cut that closes both, with the two anchors kept separate.

## 2. The shape

1. **Loss is the condition of reuse, not damage.** Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :28: *"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."* A memory that kept every occasion would be a recording, and a recording is not portable.

2. **What is kept is the bond, not the ingredient list.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Two memories with the same items under different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117), and they support different later inferences. This is what "useful structure" means in the framework.

3. **Retention is a bargain, not a threshold.** Fig 5 :43: *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* The two failure modes named here are the two failure modes of memory: a vacuous summary and an unreplayable recording.

4. **"Useful" is settled outside the memory's frame.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A memory cannot decide usefulness by counting what it holds. It is settled by a relevance variable: a later retrieval, inference, action or outcome.

5. **The cut is described per slot, not averaged.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global compression ratio cannot say which relation is still live.

6. **Kept structure becomes the vocabulary for the next episode.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom."* But settling alone is not enough: Fig 5 :70, *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."* A library that grows is not a reach that grows.

7. **The gap is the honest report of what was given up.** Fig 4 :31: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."* Fig 13 :32: *"the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* So report `R_T`, not only what was recalled.

8. **Memory is a cycle, not a store.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Retrieval re-enters as encoding, and the sign of that loop decides whether the structure stabilises or drifts. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a neural mechanism. No figure line names a hippocampus, a synapse, a sleep spindle or a compression algorithm. Those are world-source bindings with provenance, not figure-derived names.
- Not "compression explains memory." That answers only the discard condition and leaves the preserve condition standing.
- Not that the retained structure is a specific schema, concept or representation. The framework returns a shape (the bond, the bargain, the anchor), not the name of what fills it.
- Not that more storage is better. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
- Not that the trace can recover its own source by inspection. *Outputs are not generators* (*They're Scanning Books*); the lineage has to be recorded (see `05` §4).
- Not grounded. No memory measurement was taken. Every claim above is `specified` or `general`, and `candidate` until a ground pays for it.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A ground reading, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. Here the discriminating measurements are:

- **Isomer test.** Two memories with the same item set but different bond types must make different predictions on a later inference task. If they do not, the bond carries nothing (Fig 12 :101) and the structure claim fails.
- **Bargain test.** Vary what remains unexplained while holding description length fixed. Retention should track the leftover, not the raw count. If a fixed capacity threshold explains the data as well, Fig 5 :43 is refuted.
- **Anchor test.** Change the internal bookkeeping of a memory system while holding its contact with tasks and outcomes fixed. If performance improves, Fig 11 :40 (*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*) is refuted.
- **Round-trip test.** Measure `R_T` for a compact trace: send a full episode up, bring the structural description back down, and compare. The gap should be nonzero and should correspond to what is reported as noise. A zero gap for every input would break Fig 4 :28.
- **Cycle test.** Prevent retrieval from modifying the stored trace. If the system still updates correctly under novel conditions, the cycle claim in Fig 12 :121 is refuted.

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 apply in full.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a neural theory of memory and does not claim to settle the question. It says that losing detail is the price of making a trace reusable, so the loss is not a bug to repair; that what is worth keeping is the typed relation among items, not the item list, so two memories with the same ingredients can be different substances; that a detail is retained when stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than what was unexplained before, which rules out both a vacuous summary and a perfect record of one occasion; that usefulness is decided by something outside the memory's own bookkeeping, a later retrieval, inference or outcome; and that the gap between the full episode and the compact trace is the honest report of what was given up. Memory is a cycle because retrieval re-enters as encoding. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the measurements that would close it. The ground has not been consulted here, so the answer stops at a shape and records the route.
