## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Boden's three kinds of creativity** (SEP; Wikipedia Computational creativity). Combinatorial creativity combines old ideas; exploratory creativity searches an established conceptual space; transformational creativity alters the space's constitutive rules. This is the world-side analogue of the figure distinction between a mint inside `closure(Γ)` and an import that moves the wall. SEP records the transformational case as *"downright impossible"* under the previous space.
2. **P-creative versus H-creative** (Boden, as reported in both surveys). P-creative is new to the agent; H-creative is new to society. This is the same split as Fig 6 :22's *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world"*, arrived at in a different field.
3. **Csikszentmihalyi's DIFI framework** (Wikipedia Computational creativity). Novelty and value are assessed by a field, which then admits the work to a domain. Anchor-like, and independent of the framework author.
4. **Generation and evaluation as two phases** (Wikipedia Computational creativity; Wikipedia Creativity's Geneplore model). Systems generate P-creative candidates and then filter for value. Structurally the mint-then-settle loop, corroboration from cognitive science.
5. **Newell / Shaw / Simon criterion 2** (Wikipedia Computational creativity): *"The answer demands that we reject ideas we had previously accepted."* A refusal operator, which the figures place outside the bonds (Operators table amendment). Corroboration, not a figure quote.
6. **Animal culture** (low overlap): imitation is necessary but not sufficient for culture, and the zone of latent solutions hypothesis says the same behaviour can be reinvented independently without the form being transmitted. This is convergent derivation versus adopted import in a biological frame, with no contact with the framework author.
7. **Framework-author essays** (high overlap, weak corroboration): *How Intelligence Actually Works* on provenance loss and indistinguishability of artifacts; *The Hard Problem is About Source* on metamerism; *They're Scanning Books* on outputs not being generators and import as a new axis. These agree with the figures because they share an author. Recorded as one evidence pool, not as independent witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* and **Fig 4 :25** *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them any source.
- **Fig 2 :40** agreement from a shared evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict. No list selected for receptiveness, no bulk send, no personal emails.
- **Fig 7 :35** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record it; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
