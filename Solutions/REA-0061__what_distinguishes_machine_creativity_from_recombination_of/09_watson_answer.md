## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what distinguishes machine creativity from recombination of patterns in training data. Option A: collapse them, so "creativity" is an observer's label for recombination that surprises it. Option B: assert a difference in kind, so a special ingredient must be named. What each leads to: A leaves evaluation with no object, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces (Fig 1 :27); B must name an unheld primitive, which the framework forbids (Fig 13 :135). A third description is owed, and the derivation returns a union of conditions rather than either option.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the demand for a distinction and two failing answers. Arity: three bundled conditions, not one binary: mechanism (did an atom cross a closure), provenance (can the route be read off the artifact), anchor (what settles "creative" and does it update on the frame's own output). Symmetry: the requested distinction is charged and ordered, not a symmetric difference between two equal things. Scale: recombination is first-order composition inside a closure; crossing and settlement are second-order relations. Fig 9 names the bundling: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the generative frame.** Actors: `atoms(Γ)`, `closure(Γ)`, the mint operator, the curiosity drive, object slots. Currency: per-slot residual and description length. Anchor: none inside the frame, which is why it cannot self-certify. Not a domain with a nameable internal anchor; answerable only from outside.
- **Domain 2, the crossing frame.** Actors: a peer frame, a corpus, nature, the import operator, source gate and shadow test. Currency: one atom and a moved wall. Anchor: provenance plus the gap the import explains. Yes, nameable.
- **Domain 3, the evaluation field.** Actors: expert readers, a field, users, a culture. Currency: settlement, agreement, residual. Anchor: a ground that does not update; for a question about value, human judgment is the right anchor. Yes, nameable.
- **Partition or union: union.** The three conditions overlap and one lever does not clear all of them. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse or mint before settle; settle each first-order composition before applying the second-order label; publish the lineage last (Fig 3 :20-21).

Strain test applied: import changes frame access while adding no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25); re-census shows the two statements range over different objects, the frame and the world, so there is no contradiction. Nothing was smoothed over.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The two tempting answers fail. Collapse into "it is all recombination" is true at D1 but drops the provenance and anchor conditions, and would leave no non-circular evaluator (Fig 1 :27). A difference in kind fails because it must name an unheld primitive (Fig 13 :135). The surviving shape: nothing in the artifact distinguishes the two; what distinguishes them is a documented crossing into a frame whose closure differs, plus a settlement by an anchor that does not update on the generator's own output, reported per output case rather than as a global score. Over the artifact the two descriptions are the same term with different lineages; over the agent-and-habitat they are isomers with a stated falsifier. "Mere recombination" is not a dismissal, because arrangement is the substance. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result and cannot settle the question it reports on.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, its refuters and its provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework before the shape claim and its refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices as *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A second cost is treating a rater pool as evidence when the pool shares an evidence base (Fig 2 :40). Both are avoided by the two-stage offer.
- **undoable:** yes. The three per-domain verdicts can be revised independently without touching the figure text or the domain census. A failing refuter removes one condition rather than the whole shape, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A claim already sent can be superseded by a corrected packet with the refuter that fired recorded in the lineage.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An artifact-only route discriminator with published error rates would move the distinction partly into the product; an anchor that updates and still settles would break D3; an agent-only improvement that moves the field's judgment while contact is held fixed would refute the capability claim; a composition that adds an atom inside a fixed closure would break D1. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
