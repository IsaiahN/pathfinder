# Outreach draft: check the distinction claim before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter have been checked locally against the figure text. If the stage-1 claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about machine creativity and recombination

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking
> you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "Machine creativity" and "recombination of training
> patterns" are not two kinds of output. They can be the same artifact. What separates them is the
> route that produced the artifact and the thing that settled whether it counts. Recombination is
> composition inside a fixed library; novelty inside that library is novelty relative to the
> library's own atoms, not novelty relative to the world. The only operation that expands the
> library is contact with a source whose library differs, and that operation must be documented by
> where the new item came from and by the gap it explains. The label "creative" is then applied
> afterwards to that documented route by a field whose judgment does not move when the machine's
> own output changes.
>
> So the thing I would ask you to check is a **shape**, not a definition: any adequate distinction
> must be about lineage and contact, per case, and it cannot be read off the generated text, image,
> or idea alone. I am not claiming to have measured a specific machine being creative.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that a system recombining patterns is thereby uncreative;
> that a benchmark score or a model's own confidence can certify creativity; that there is a special
> spark or non-computable ingredient; or that a single global "creativity score" means anything.
> A new relation over old elements is a new thing under the framework's own composition rule, so
> "mere recombination" is not a dismissal.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. a composition inside a fixed library that adds a new primitive without any external source;
> 2. a discriminator that reads only the artifact and reliably reports which route produced it;
> 3. an anchor for creativity that changes when the generator's own output changes and still
>    settles the verdict;
> 4. an agent-only improvement that changes the field's judgment while contact is held fixed.
>
> If any of these holds, the claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..."
> is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three public sources. The primary authority is the
> framework's figure text, not my summary of it. The two secondary sources are an independently
> authored encyclopedia entry on creativity and a survey of computational creativity.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** it is public in the figures link below. Run it on a case you
> know well and can check against a ground. State your own refuter before you run it, and label the
> mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage rule

Stage 1: send the shape claim and the four refuters, record the verdict. Stage 2, opt-in only: send the three links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a recipient who has read the framework no longer stands outside it. A Track A reply is a correction on shape, a named failure class, and the question "could this have been anything else?" It verifies nothing.
