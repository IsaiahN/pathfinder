# Expert verification packet - the Tether on plural knowledge systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on plural knowledge systems can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the case-specific binding. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Plural knowledge systems can cooperate while disagreeing about causes, evidence and success, but not by settling those disagreements first. The three disagreements form a union of overlapping conditions, so verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Cooperation is triangulation of independent frames against a ground that does not move when any frame pushes on it. A difference in causes, where it reflects genuinely different evidence, is the surplus that makes the union productive. Evidence is admissible when it bears on the residual, not on the standing of its maker. Success is a comparison, and the disagreement about it is made statable by a declared, violatable convention that carries no truth value and does not decide an outcome. What crosses between systems is a method or a head start, not a recording of one success. The framework cannot name the ground or the convention; it returns the shape they must have.

### C2 - the success convention, checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 3

- **Statement:** when knowledge systems disagree about what counts as success, that is a disagreement nothing can state. The repair is a convention that makes it statable. The convention is declared, violatable and unable to decide the outcome; agreement about it is a convention, not a verdict.

- **Refuter:** a case where a convention that demonstrably decided the outcome still left both frames independently corrected by the ground, or where the success disagreement was settled by a shared measurement with no convention and no loss of per-frame correction.

### C3 - admissibility and transport, checkable by reading Figure 9, Figure 4 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** evidence is admitted when it bears on the residual, not on the maker's standing; what crosses between systems is a method or head start, not a recording; a substituted habitat proves wiring, not capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :51 "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"; Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. - **Refuter:** a case where evidence resting only on the maker's reputation settled a residual independently for both frames with no source gate, or where a recording transported with no method generated new ground-contacted results in the receiving frame.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the systems must agree on causes, or on one evidence standard, or on one success metric before cooperating.
- Not that the framework picks the ground, the convention or the author of the convention. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that one system may validate another by its own standards. Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."
- Not that all disagreements are reducible to one cooperation score (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- Not `grounded`. No case measurement was taken. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. The checks are:

1. Does the proposed ground stay fixed when each frame pushes on it, measured by a record of outputs rather than by the frames' reports?
2. Does the joint result survive when evidence sources are separated, and fail when they are shared?
3. Can the success convention be violated without the outcome changing, and is its authorship and stake recorded?
4. Does each frame hold a refusal, so a held cause term can be corrected and not merely added?
5. Does transported material regenerate ground-contacted results in the receiving habitat?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29/:35/:43/:45 and Fig 8 :45/:47/:49. Four short passages.
2. Read Fig 10 :14/:17/:18/:46/:47 and Fig 3 :24. Decide whether "convention" and "verdict" are being kept apart.
3. Read Fig 9 :51, Fig 4 :22/:25 and Fig 11 :70. Decide whether the admissibility and transport claims are faithful to your own field's practice.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate whether a ground is moving - or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
