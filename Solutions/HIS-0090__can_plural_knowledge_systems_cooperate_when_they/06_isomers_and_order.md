# Supporting derivation: union, bond type, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the union and order claims, `general` on the structural split, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles three disagreements (causes, evidence, success). Two derivations are owed: (1) is this a partition or a union, and what is the dependency order, and (2) are two knowledge systems related as isomers, as a functional group, or as a composition plus import? ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The three disagreements overlap. A frame's causal vocabulary determines what it counts as evidence; its evidence rules determine what it will accept as a success measure; its success criterion selects which cause is worth naming. Settling causes alone leaves the other two intersections standing (Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing").

**Result: union.** Three verdicts are kept separate, never averaged.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single intervention removes all three disagreements at once with no categorically different residue, the union claim fails.

### Q2 - Which bond does each disagreement carry?

- **Causes** are minted mechanism terms. Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed". Fig 5 :55: a refusal "removes... a term's standing to be composed over". Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." So a causal disagreement is a difference in `Γ` and needs the unary refusal operator, not a new bond.
- **Evidence** is admissibility. Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it." This is a comparison against a slot residual, not a join between two frames.
- **Success** is a comparison. Operators :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison." Fig 12 :87: comparison is "a threshold relation between two quantities". so "what counts as success" is a ⋛ relation against a threshold, not an ingredient in the method.

**Result.** The three disagreements are not the same kind of object. Treating them as three instances of "difference of opinion" is the flat-form error of Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".

**Falsifier:** if all three disagreements can be resolved by one and the same operation with no residue, the bond-typing claim fails.

### Q3 - Are two knowledge systems isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two knowledge systems that draw on the same substrate, the same habitat conditions and the same kinds of instruments, but arrange their cause and evidence terms differently, are **isomers** over that shared atom set: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour at the ground. That is a checkable structural claim.

If they share no atom set (different instruments, different habitat, no common object of contact), they are not isomers. They are two compounds with no common ingredient list, and the union surplus of Fig 8 :45 cannot be assumed; it must be shown by a described residual.

The weaker, safer claim is a **functional group**: each system is a `state - structure - state` composition (Fig 13 :75: "It is not space-time but space, a structure, time"), so the group says what kind of thing it is, and the qualifier says which member.

**Falsifier:** if one system can be written with an atom the other does not require under the same residual, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands. Triangulated for the figure definition (Fig 12), single-source for the application.

### Q4 - What order does the union have?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :9-10: link 3 is "the objective": "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms".
- Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Order for this question:

1. **Perception and contact** (D1): can each frame read the object of contact, and is a ground nameable?
2. **Vocabulary** (D2): does each frame have terms for what the situation affords, including cause and evidence terms?
3. **Objective** (D3): can a convention be stated that says what would count as progress without deciding the result?
4. **Planning** (D3): the cooperation protocol, chosen under incomplete information, with each frame keeping its own residual.
5. **Learn and carry** (D4): name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry it as a method (Fig 4 :22).

Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a missing objective link (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not"). Most real cooperation work lands above the break, where instruments already exist, which is why the success disagreement is usually the unexamined one.

**Falsifier:** if a cooperation succeeds with no stated objective convention and no loss of per-frame ground correction, the order claim fails.

### Q5 - Analogues as corroboration only

World analogues were opened and are used as corroboration, never as the verdict:

- **Galison's trading zone** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_zones, accessed 2026-11-23): "the trading partners can hammer out a local coordination, despite vast global differences." This matches the convention-in-D3 reading: local coordination without global agreement. It does not settle which ground to use.
- **Star and Griesemer's boundary object** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_object, accessed 2026-11-23): objects "plastic enough to adapt to local needs... yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites", "a means of translation". This matches methods crossing (Fig 4 :22) and the warning that a convention must not decide an outcome (Fig 2 :43). The political critique on the same page warns that such objects are not neutral, so declared stake (Fig 10 :59) remains required.
- **Tengo et al. 2014 multiple evidence base** (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13280-014-0501-3, accessed 2026-11-23): "evaluation of knowledge occurs primarily within rather than across knowledge systems" and the joint picture is "for triangulation and joint assessment of knowledge". This is the closest world match to per-frame residuals plus separate ranging. It corroborates; it does not overrule.
- **Surowiecki 2004** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wisdom_of_Crowds, accessed 2026-11-23): a "diverse collection of independently deciding individuals" can outperform experts; too much communication can make the group less intelligent. Corroborates Fig 8 :47-49 from economics and psychology.
- **Fricker's hermeneutical injustice** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_injustice, accessed 2026-11-23): experiences "do not fit concepts that can be found in popular language". Corroborates Fig 10 :14, "a disagreement nothing can state".

Overlap is declared: the STS sources (trading zone, boundary object, interactional expertise) share one science-studies evidence pool; the Wikipedia syntheses are secondary to the named primary works. Agreement among them is one witness for the mechanism shape, not several verdicts about a case.

## CONCLUSION

The three disagreements are a **union** with a dependency order, and they carry three different bond types. Two knowledge systems over a shared atom set are **isomers** if they share the substrate and differ in arrangement; otherwise the weaker functional-group reading applies. The order runs contact, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry. The falsifiers above were written before the run. No figure line names the shared atom set for a given case, so that part remains a `gap`.
