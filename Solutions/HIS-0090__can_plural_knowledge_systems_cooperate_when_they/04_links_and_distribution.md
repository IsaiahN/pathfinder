## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Coordination without consensus is already a named research object.** Star and Griesemer's boundary object is "a means of translation" across social worlds, and the Wikipedia synthesis states that boundary objects "allow coordination without consensus". Galison's trading zone says the same thing for physicists and engineers: local coordination despite "vast global differences". Both are world corroboration of the figure claim that cooperation is not agreement on causes. The political critique matters too: boundary objects are not neutral, so provenance and declared stake remain required.
2. **Independence is the load-bearing quantity in two separate literatures.** Surowiecki's four conditions include independence and diversity of opinion; Kao and Couzin warn that large groups are "particularly susceptible to the negative effects of correlated information"; meta-analysis treats correlation and heterogeneity as the standing threat to a pooled estimate. This is one mechanism corroborated from economics, animal behaviour and statistics, but it is still one mechanism, not three independent verdicts about this case.
3. **A source that cannot discriminate among causes cannot settle the success disagreement.** Fricker's hermeneutical injustice names the case where an experience has no concept that can state it; the Hard Problem essay's spectrometer example names the case where a reading cannot comment on its source. Both corroborate Fig 10's "a disagreement nothing can state" and the claim that what is owed is a convention and a better instrument, not a vote.
