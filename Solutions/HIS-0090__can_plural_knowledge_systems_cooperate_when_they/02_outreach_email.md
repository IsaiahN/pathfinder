**Subject:** One checkable claim about cooperation across knowledge systems, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A project has to make a joint decision when the knowledge systems involved disagree about what caused the problem, what would count as evidence, and what would count as success. My claim is `specified`, not `grounded`: those three disagreements do not have to be settled for cooperation to work. What cooperation needs is a ground that does not move when any participating system pushes on it, enough independence between the systems that their combined view reaches something neither reaches alone, and a declared convention about success that makes the disagreement statable without deciding the result.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that the systems must first agree on causes. A difference in causes, where it comes from genuinely different evidence and instruments, is the useful part. I am not claiming that a shared success metric is required; a metric that decides the outcome stops being a shared convention and becomes another interested party. I am not claiming the framework can name which ground to install in your case. It returns constraints on the shape of a ground, not its name.
>
> **The three failures I wrote down before running this:**
> 1. A case where cooperation produced an independently checkable joint result even though the supposed ground moved when a participant pushed on it.
> 2. A case where the combined view lost its extra reach when the systems' evidence sources were made separate, or gained that reach only because they shared one pool.
> 3. A case where the agreed convention about success decided the outcome, yet each system was still independently corrected by the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Two-stage discipline

**Stage 1.** Send only the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before showing anything else.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the framework interior their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method. Each user must name their own ground, write their own refuter and state their own mode. The framework itself says a loop cannot check the direction of its own question (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"), and the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap warns that simulated outputs "would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself" to be testable.

## Contact hygiene

One message to one person. No mailing list, no automation, no misleading subject line, an opt-out in every message. Use only the institutional or opt-in routes in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. If a reply contains a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and let the auditor seat re-evaluate it as it would any other source.
