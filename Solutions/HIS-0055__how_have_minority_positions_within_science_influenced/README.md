# Solution HIS-0055 , figure-grounded package

**Question:** How have minority positions within science influenced later research, even when they were initially rejected?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 68 (question 55 in section 6)

**Section:** 6. Disagreement, controversy, and consensus

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which particular position or fraction pays off. Not `grounded`. Every substantive file is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the historical question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The residual in effect terms

A claim held by few, rejected or ignored when first advanced, later becomes a live input to research. The decision is between an agreement account (influence = accumulation of adherents and prestige) and a figure-grounded account (influence = an import from a differing closure, settled later by a ground that does not update). An answer must say what the minority frame carries, why it was rejected, what changed so uptake became possible, and what settles the uptake. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` section 0 so it can be refuted by the pre-registered tests.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that minority positions matter.
- No bulk-send plan, no purchased list, no receptiveness column.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Gathering agreeable readers produces a convention, not corroboration.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The outside frame must not hold the interiors.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Suggested next step

Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. The audience that can help is the one that has opted in to receiving claims and is positioned to refute them, not a list of people already persuaded that outsiders are usually right.
