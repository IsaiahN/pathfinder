## 0. The claim under audit

The question is: **How have minority positions within science influenced later research, even when they were initially rejected?**

Stated in effect terms, a recurring observation needs a mechanism: a claim held by few, rejected or ignored when first advanced, later becomes a live input to research. Two candidate answers are on the table.

- **Option A (agreement account):** influence is the accumulation of adherents, prestige, and eventual majority conversion. The claim matters because enough people came to believe it.
- **Option B (figure-grounded account):** influence is an import. The minority position is a frame whose closure differs from the consensus. When the consensus closure hits a residual it cannot close, that differing frame supplies an atom or a method the consensus lacked; the ground settles the import later, and later research composes over the settled term. Rejection is an agreement verdict inside one closure, not a ground verdict.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula "Direction"): a four-part mechanism with (i) what the minority frame carries, (ii) why it was rejected, (iii) what changed so uptake became possible, and (iv) what settles the uptake. The sought-for shape is: **rejection = agreement without an anchor, or a reachability failure inside one closure; uptake = import from a differing closure; influence = a surplus in the union that neither frame reached alone.** That shape is a preference until it is bound here; this section binds it so the same refuters can test it.

## 1. The residual: effect, arity, symmetry, scale

The residual is not "why were they right." It is "by what operation does a low-consensus, initially rejected claim become a settled term that later research can compose over."

- **Arity: two conditions.** The content of the claim and the community's reception of it are different objects with different anchors. Fig 9 :57 puts the rule: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A single lever, for example persuasion, clears the reception region and leaves the ground condition standing.
- **Symmetry: the transition is ordered, not symmetric.** "Initially rejected, later influential" is a sequence, not a conjunction. The bond `→` is "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72). The crossing from minority frame to majority library is also an import with a direction (Fig 6 :14).
- **Scale: the same loop recurs at person, discipline, and field.** Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." A person's dissent, a discipline's paradigm, and a field's research programme are the same shape at three strata, with different actors and different anchors.

The residual is indexed per claim and per receiving frame (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). There is no single "minority position" residual to average over.

## 2. Domain census

One line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. Then partition or union, then dependency order (Fig 3).

| domain | actors | currency | nameable anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The consensus room** (majority) | established specialists, reviewers, editors, textbook writers, frames sharing an evidence pool | agreement, credibility, influence, prestige | a non-updating reading the room cannot move, often absent in practice; when absent the room has no anchor and converges on influence (Fig 2 :13-15) | verdict versus convention |
| **D2 The divergent frame** (minority) | the outsider, the small school, the instrument builder working from a different closure | a prediction that fails to resolve in the majority closure, a refused term, a method | the observations already returned by its own instrument (fit of continents, mortality counts, inheritance ratios), which do not move when the majority disagrees | reachability and refusal |
| **D3 The membrane and later research** (receiving frame) | later researchers, replication, citation, review, new instruments | methods and head starts crossing upward or downward; recordings that fail to cross | the ground reached through a channel that can decay while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24) | import with provenance and shadow test |
| **D4 The ground** | measurement, instrument, outcome, counter, patient, rock, dataset | settlement: does the claim pay | whatever settles the question and does not change because any frame thinks otherwise (Fig 2 :29) | settle before compose |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 (reception) and D4 (settlement) overlap in real cases and have different anchors. Treating "rejected then influential" as one problem with one mechanism is the bundling error Fig 9 :54 names: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before choosing a case (Fig 9 :28, "any frame you pick will seem to fit"); refuse a held false term before minting a replacement (formula step 2); import only with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13); settle before composing over the term (Fig 12 :106, "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."). Effort spent upstream of a broken link arrives nowhere (Fig 3 :20).

**Anchor test applied.** D4 has an anchor by definition. D1 usually substitutes agreement for an anchor, which is the collapse Fig 2 names. D2 has an anchor only where its instrument already returns something (Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from"). D3's anchor is inherited, never authored. Where a domain has no nameable anchor, no verdict is claimed for it.

## 4. Findings

### F1 , The question is a union of two conditions, not one mechanism (decisive)

Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

The claim's truth and the claim's reception have different anchors. Agreement is efficient for producing a shared convention, which is what a discipline needs to function, and it is disqualified as a verdict about the world when the agreeing frames read the same pool. So "the field rejected it" and "the field was right to reject it" are different sentences. A lever that changes opinion leaves ground contact untouched; a lever that changes ground contact need not change opinion at once.

- **Triangulated:** Fig 9 :57 (union structure) and Fig 2 :39-41 (correlation valence), low overlap between the search figure and the ground figure.
- **Refuter:** show that reception and settlement share one anchor at the same scope, so that one measurement both makes the claim believed and makes it true.

### F2 , Initial rejection is an agreement verdict with no anchor, not a measurement

Fig 2 :13-15: "no anchor; each updates on the others" / "they converge, and what they converge on" / "is whoever had the most influence." Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along;" / "agreement compounds, evidence does not."

A consensus room with no external reference converges on whoever had the most influence. This is not a claim that majorities are stupid; it is the named collapse. The room's agreement is variation, and the ground votes only when a differing room is selected against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced.").

- **Triangulated:** Fig 2 and Fig 7, different quantities (anchor and handoff).
- **Refuter:** a case where the rejecting majority ranges separately against a non-updating measurement that the minority cannot move, and rejection tracks that measurement. Then rejection is a ground verdict in that case, and F2 does not govern it.

### F3 , The minority is a differing closure; numerical smallness is a filter, not a witness

Symbols :111: "A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model." So a discipline, a school, and a lone researcher are frames of the same kind. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :46: "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."

What matters is the difference in closure, not the headcount or the prestige of the minority. Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Counting adherents is a budget filter.

- **Triangulated:** Fig 6 :23 (wall moves on contact) and Fig 8 :45-48 (surplus from differing closures), plus the Symbols definition of frame.
- **Refuter:** a minority position drawn from the identical evidence pool and identical closure as the majority that nonetheless supplies a new atom with no crossing and no import. That would break Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32.

### F4 , Influence is an import, not a conversion of the majority

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall." Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

If the majority closure lacked the atom, more searching inside it cannot manufacture it. The minority frame is the external source named in Fig 6 :9-10 (nature, corpus, peer frame). Uptake is therefore a wall-moving import, and it is gated: Fig 6 :12-13 name "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap." Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The provenance is the whole issue in a historical account: did the later field derive the claim separately, or import it?

- **Triangulated:** Fig 6 (mechanism of the wall) and Fig 8 (where the atom comes from), explicitly cross-referenced in the figures.
- **Refuter:** show the majority deriving the contested atom by composition alone, with no differing frame, no instrument imported, and no provenance crossing. That breaks the import claim.

### F5 , "Initially rejected" also names a refusal failure, not only a missing term

The first four kinds of gap sort by what is missing. Fig 5 :53 adds the fifth: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." The candidates are enumerable, and Fig 5 :59: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at." Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 5 :55: "Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."

So a minority position often carries the refusal of a majority-held term that keeps being wrong. Its rejection by the majority is the majority's vocabulary lacking the unary operator that could refuse its own commitment. This is not the same as F4: import adds an atom from outside; refusal adds a term that removes a held term's standing to be composed over. Both make the reach fall or rise for different reasons, and both are needed in a full account.

- **Triangulated:** Fig 5 amendment and the Operators table (negation as unary), with Fig 6 :42-43 (record grows, reach can fall).
- **Refuter:** a vocabulary with no negation operator that nonetheless refuses a held term without adding one, or a refusal that deletes a term from the record. Either breaks the amendment.

### F6 , The ground, not later consensus, settles the uptake

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 10 :22: "the ground does not update." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

A claim becomes influential in the strong sense only when an anchor pays for it. In the Wegener case read this session, acceptance waited until palaeomagnetism and other discoveries supplied readings the original closure could not hold (world source W1). In the Semmelweis case, acceptance waited until germ theory supplied a mechanism (world source W2). In both, the instrument or the mechanism did the settling; the count of adherents was downstream. Mode: `specified` on this reading of the cases, not `grounded`, because no measurement was taken here.

- **Triangulated:** Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 10 (seat/channel) and the formula step 5 ("the ground settles it"), three low-overlap framings.
- **Refuter:** a case where the claim is settled solely by the number of believers with no non-updating reading, and later research composes over it successfully. That breaks the anchor test.

### F7 , What travels is a method or a head start, not a recording of one occasion

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom."

A minority result influences later research when it is carried as a re-applicable method or as a settled term that becomes an atom for new compositions. When it is carried as the story of one success, it produces repetition rather than new reach. The Mendel case read this session is the cleanest instance: three researchers in three countries independently produced the same law around 1900, which Fig 8 :50 calls "corroboration at maximum independence," not an import of a name (world source W4).

- **Triangulated:** Fig 4 (membrane direction) and Fig 8 :50 (convergence) and Fig 12 :104 (settled molecule as atom).
- **Refuter:** a replay of one recorded success that yields a new settled term in a different closure without a method or an import. That breaks Fig 4 :25.

### F8 , Standing and interest explain resistance; they do not decide truth

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap (essay, opened this session) gives the social mechanism: "If prestige could buy actions, the system would collapse into a monoculture," and "The highest-prestige agent would monopolize compute and repeat its past success, and the system would stop exploring." The Matthew effect source (world source W7) describes credit distributed "according to how much they already have."

Prestige and standing are contact costs and stake declarations. They can fund an action (give a minority claim a hearing) and they can block one (a monoculture repeating past success), but they are never a witness for the verdict. This is the same rule the project applies to lead selection: no ranking by receptiveness, no pooling of agreement.

- **Triangulated:** Fig 10 (seat rules) plus the essay plus a low-overlap sociology source. The sociology source is corroboration only.
- **Refuter:** a case where an established reputation is itself the instrument that settles the claim at the ground, with no independent reading. That breaks Fig 10 :59 and the anchor test.

### F9 , The channel decays while the ground does not

Fig 10 :26: "saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." McClintock's reception was "puzzlement and, in some instances, hostility," and she stopped publishing data in 1953 (world source W5). The result was not false and the ground had not moved; the channel from her frame to a receiving frame had closed.

- **Triangulated:** Fig 10 (channel decay) and Fig 1 (aggregation hides the live slot), with Fig 4 (membrane) as the third framing.
- **Refuter:** a case where a ground reading is lost permanently because the channel decayed, with the ground itself unchanged and a receiver later able to rebuild it. That would contradict the figure's asymmetry.

### F10 , No general threshold or named hero is returned: this is a `gap`

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The figures return the shape of the mechanism, not which minority position will be vindicated, not what fraction of dissent pays off, and not a numerical tipping point for uptake.

**Gap G1:** which particular position, and what fraction of initially rejected claims, later influence research. This is an empirical question requiring per-case ground readings. It is of the `unreachable-at-this-budget` kind rather than `underivable-in-principle`: the atoms are in the closure, the search has not been run exhaustively, and importing a new primitive against the budget would be the error Fig 8 :42 warns about. Reported as `unreachable`, not silence.

- **Refuter:** a figure line that selects a specific position or a universal fraction. None was found after the census in section 3 and a second pass in `11_audit.md`.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before the WATSON run, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in Option B is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A minority position becomes influential purely by accumulating adherents or prestige, with no change in any non-updating anchor and no import from a differing closure. (Breaks Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :35, Fig 6 :23.)
2. The majority closure derives the contested atom from inside itself, with no contact with a differing frame and no imported instrument or term. (Breaks Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32.)
3. The same claim is ground-settled for the minority and ground-settled against it for the majority at the same scope with the same anchor. (Breaks Fig 10 :65-66, the record holds a contradiction.)
4. The ground moves when the consensus pushes on it. (Breaks Fig 2 :33.)
5. A recording of one occasion, carrying no method, produces a new settled term in a receiver in a different closure. (Breaks Fig 4 :25.)
6. Reception and settlement turn out to be one condition with one anchor, so the union claim in F1 collapses. (Breaks Fig 9 :57.)
7. A discipline that draws on exactly the same evidence pool as the dissenting individual still generates a surplus with no crossing. (Breaks Fig 8 :48.)

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a list of famous heretics (F1, F2, F10).
2. Describe the case in effect terms before naming it, so the frame does not fit by hindsight (Fig 9 :28).
3. Separate the four domains in any write-up; do not average reception into settlement (F1).
4. When classifying a case, ask what atom crossed and with what provenance and shadow test (F4, F7).
5. Ask what would have refuted the minority claim at the time, and whether any instrument existed then (F6).
6. Declare the standing and interest of every narrator, including this one (F8).
7. Report G1 as `unreachable`, not silence, and do not invent a percentage (F10).

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Send the generator, the derivation and the refuter, not a recording of which famous person was right.

### Seven-question recursive derivation, compressed

1. Is reception a verdict? No. Fig 2 :40. It is a convention effect.
2. Is the minority's smallness evidence? No. Fig 9 :60. It is a filter.
3. Can the majority reach the atom by search? No. Fig 8 :32, unless the atom was already in the closure, in which case it is a budget question.
4. What does cross? A method or head start, Fig 4 :22; a settled molecule, Fig 12 :104; or a refusal, Fig 5 :53.
5. Who licenses the import? Provenance and shadow test, Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :51.
6. What settles it? The ground, Fig 2 :29, never the room.
7. What is owed? A per-case instrument and the lineage, not a general percentage, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134.

- **S4, live limit, refusal as a unary operator.** Operators :77-78 leave the price of a unary operator open: "What a unary operator costs is open: an atom slot, nothing, or the choice of what to negate." The historical account needs refusal, but the framework does not price it. Named, not hidden; no claim is made about its cost.
- **S5, live limit, ground in historical cases.** The ground in a historical case is often a reconstruction from documents, not a present instrument. ## 9. Verdict summary

Option B survives as a `general` structural shape and `specified` per-domain mechanism: a minority position influences later research when it is a differing closure that supplies an import, a refusal, or a method to a receiving frame whose own closure has hit a persistent residual, and when an anchor that does not update eventually settles the term so later research can compose over it. Option A is refused as a verdict: agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict about the world. Verdicts are kept separate by domain. The particular position, the fraction, and the numerical tipping point are a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind. No claim is `grounded`. The sub-claims are triangulated where two figures with low overlap govern them and marked `single-source` where only one governs.
