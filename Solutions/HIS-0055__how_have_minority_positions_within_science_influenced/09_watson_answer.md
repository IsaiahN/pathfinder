## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what accounts for a recurring observation: a claim held by few and initially rejected later becomes a live input to research. Option A says influence is the accumulation of adherents and prestige, so the claim matters because enough people came to believe it. Option B says influence is a crossing from a differing closure, with an import, a refusal or a method supplied to a consensus that could not derive it from inside itself, settled later by an anchor that does not update. What each leads to: A treats a convention as a verdict and selects recipients for agreement, manufacturing the correlation that disqualifies it; B yields a checkable shape, pre-registered refuters, and per-case ground tests, with the named case and the fraction left open.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The transition from "rejected by the few" to "composed over by later research." Arity: two conditions, reception and settlement, so a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57). Symmetry: the transition is ordered; `→` is "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72), and the crossing has a direction from the differing frame to the receiving frame. Scale: the same loop recurs at person, discipline and field, with different actors, currency and anchor (Fig 10 :68).
- **D1, the consensus room.** Actors: specialists, reviewers, editors, frames sharing an evidence pool. Currency: agreement, credibility, influence. Anchor: a non-updating reading, often substituted by agreement in practice. Question type: verdict versus convention.
- **D2, the divergent frame.** Actors: the outsider, the small school, the instrument builder. Currency: a residual the majority closure cannot close, a refused term, a method. Anchor: the observations already returned by its own instrument. Question type: reachability and refusal.
- **D3, the membrane and later research.** Actors: later researchers, replication, citation, review, new instruments. Currency: methods and head starts, or recordings that fail to cross. Anchor: the ground reached through a channel that can decay. Question type: import with provenance and shadow test.
- **D4, the ground.** Actors: measurement, instrument, outcome, counter, patient, rock, dataset. Currency: settlement, does the term pay. Anchor: whatever settles the question and does not change because a frame thinks otherwise. Question type: settle before compose.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a case (Fig 9 :28); refuse a held false term before minting (formula step 2); gate the import with provenance and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13); carry a method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22); settle before composing (Fig 12 :106).

The anchor test is applied in every domain. D4 has an anchor by definition. D1 usually substitutes agreement. D2 has one only where an instrument already returns something (Fig 6 :34). D3 inherits one. No domain without a nameable anchor receives a verdict.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Option B survives as a `general` structural shape and `specified` per-domain mechanism. Reception is an agreement effect, not a ground verdict. A dissenting frame matters when its closure differs and it supplies an atom, a refusal, or a method; convergence is the separate case where two frames reach the same atom independently. Uptake is gated by provenance and a shadow test, carries a method rather than a recording, and is settled by an anchor that does not update before later research composes over it. Option A is refused as a verdict because agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a claim about the world. The particular vindicated position, the fraction, and any numerical tipping point are a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind. Verdicts are per domain and never averaged. Nothing is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before its conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a story about famous heretics instead of a checkable shape; Fig 2 :42 prices that ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is the metamerism error: reading a later consensus and naming its source without a discriminator (The Hard Problem is About Source).
- **undoable:** yes. The case bindings are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove the progress narrative and the hero list. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet and no primitive has to be withdrawn. The `gap` stays a gap rather than being filled with a guessed fraction.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor test on a named case showing uptake tracked a non-updating reading rather than adherent counts; a source test showing a different atom crossed with provenance and a shadow test, or showing the case was convergence; a refusal test identifying the majority-held term added to the record, not deleted; a channel test showing a method rather than a recording crossed; or any of the seven pre-registered failure conditions in `01` section 5 firing. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
