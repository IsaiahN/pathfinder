**Subject:** Check one output on scientific dissent before you decide whether the account is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "initially rejected, later influential" is not one question with one mechanism. It is a union of two conditions: how a claim was received inside a discipline, and what an anchor eventually settled. Reception is agreement among people who usually read the same evidence pool, and agreement is efficient for producing a shared convention but disqualifying as a verdict about the world. Influence enters when the dissenting position is a frame whose closure differs from the consensus and supplies an atom, a refusal, or a method the consensus could not derive from inside itself. Settlement is by an anchor that does not move when the consensus pushes on it. What travels successfully is a method or a head start, not the story of one famous success.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every minority position was right, that majorities are irrational, or that there is a percentage of dissent that pays off. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It cannot tell you which position will be vindicated or when the tipping point arrives. The historical cases are evidence for the mechanism only where a non-updating reading can be named, and each case has to be checked for that.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a minority position becomes influential purely by accumulating adherents or prestige, with no change in any non-updating anchor and no import from a differing closure;
> 2. the majority closure derives the contested atom by itself, with no contact with a differing frame and no imported instrument or term;
> 3. the same claim is ground-settled for and against at the same scope with the same anchor;
> 4. the ground moves when the consensus pushes on it;
> 5. a bare recording of one occasion produces a new settled term in a receiver in a different closure.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the account is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The worked WATSON answer with the world sources: `09_watson_answer.md` and `10_source_ledger.csv` in this folder.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local figures folder. Run it on a case you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
