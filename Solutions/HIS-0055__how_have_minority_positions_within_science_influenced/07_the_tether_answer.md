# The Tether's answer: minority positions and later research

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which position or fraction pays off. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The residual it answers

A claim held by few, rejected or ignored when first advanced, later becomes a live input to research. The question is not "were they right" but "by what operation does a rejected claim become a settled term later research can compose over." It is a **union compound**: reception and settlement are two conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Verdicts are kept separate per domain below and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the consensus room. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.** Rejection is an agreement effect, not a ground verdict. With no anchor, a room "converges on whoever had the most influence" (Fig 2 :15), and "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes" (Fig 7 :35). Agreement is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41) and "disqualifying for a verdict" (Fig 2 :40) when the frames share an evidence pool. Exception kept visible: if the rejecting majority ranges separately against a non-updating reading and rejection tracks it, F2 of `01` does not govern that case.

**D2, the divergent frame. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.** The dissenting position matters because it is a frame whose closure differs, not because of its headcount. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Over a fixed atom set, the two frames are isomers (Fig 12 :117, "same atoms, different bond, different substance"); where two frames reach the same atom separately, that is convergence, "corroboration at maximum independence" (Fig 8 :50). Smallness is a budget filter, never a witness (Fig 9 :60).

**D3, the membrane and later research. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.** What crosses is an atom with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13), or a method and a head start, never a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22, :25). A held false term can be refused first; refusal is a negation and "a vocabulary without it can only accumulate" (Operators :58). Once settled, the term becomes an atom for whatever composes over it, "irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world" (Fig 12 :104). The channel can decay while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24), which explains why a result can wait for a receiver in a different closure.

**D4, the ground. Verdict: `specified` on the structure, triangulated; `single-source` on each historical case.** An anchor "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29) and "must not update" (Fig 2 :33). Settlement is by the ground, not by the room; "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it" (Fig 2 :45). The rule is triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 10 and the formula; the claim that any *particular* case was settled this way rests on that case's documents alone, read this session and listed in the ledger.

**Overall verdict on Option A versus Option B.** Option B survives as a shape and a route. Option A (influence = accumulation of adherents and prestige) is refused as a verdict: it mistakes a convention for a ground result. Nothing here says that majorities are irrational or that dissent is usually right.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that every minority position was vindicated. Most are not, and the figures return no fraction.
- Not that rejection is always irrational. Where a non-updating anchor is in play and rejection tracks it, the majority may be right.
- Not a general tipping point, a named hero, or a universal sequence of historical stages.
- Not a progress narrative. "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between" (Fig 10 :68) means the mechanism recurs at a new stratum, not that the world converges on one answer.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and the historical sources are documents read this session, not anchors rebuilt from instruments.
- Not that the six-term physical substrate of Fig 13 is being applied to committees. Fig 13 governs composition in general; the audit uses its composition rules only as metalaws, with the discipline treated as a frame (Symbols :111).

## 4. What would settle it, and the declared gap

Ground tests, not further argument:

1. **Anchor test:** for a named case, exhibit a reading that did not move when the consensus pushed on it, and show that uptake tracked that reading rather than the changing count of adherents. A document counts only where its reading is independent of the current frame (see strain S5 in `01`).
2. **Source test:** show what atom or method crossed from the differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51, "Only the record of where each came from separates them"). If no crossing exists, the import claim fails for that case.
3. **Refusal test:** identify the majority-held term that was refused, and show that the refusal is a negation added to the record rather than a deletion from it (Fig 5 :55).
4. **Channel test:** show whether the method or the recording crossed (Fig 4 :22-25), and whether the receiver's habitat could reproduce the required contact (Fig 11 :66-69).
5. **Union test:** keep reception and settlement as two verdicts. If one measurement settles both, F1 collapses.

**Gap G1:** which particular position will be vindicated, and what fraction of initially rejected claims later influence research. This is `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not `underivable-in-principle`. The atoms are in the closure, the per-case search has not been run, and importing a new primitive against the budget would be the error Fig 8 :42 names. Recorded as a verdict, not silence (Fig 13 :134).

Pre-registered refuter: `01` section 5, seven failure conditions written before the conclusion and before the WATSON run.

## 5. The honest stop

The figures return the shape any account of minority influence must have. They do not return the name of the vindicated dissenter, the fraction, or the date of uptake. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Indiscernibility is not identity. The record of the route (domains, quotes, refuters, ledger, second seat) is what makes the stop checkable. Where the instruments stop distinguishing, the answer stops and the lineage is written down.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that outsiders are usually right or that majorities are irrational. It says that "initially rejected, later influential" is a union of two conditions with different anchors: how a claim was received inside a discipline, and what an anchor eventually settled. Reception is agreement among people who read the same evidence pool, and agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying as a verdict about the world. A dissenting position matters when it is a frame whose closure differs from the consensus and supplies an atom, a refusal, or a method the consensus could not derive from inside itself; what crosses is a method or a head start, not a recording of one success. The ground, not the vote, settles the term later research composes over. The framework returns that four-part shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close each case. It does not return a hero, a percentage, or a tipping point. That part stays open until a ground reading settles it.
