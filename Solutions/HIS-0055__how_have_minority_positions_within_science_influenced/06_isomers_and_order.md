# Isomers, convergence and order in the minority-influence question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

Three supporting questions remain: (1) what is the structural relation between the consensus frame and the dissenting frame over the same evidence; (2) in what order do refusal, import, settlement and composition occur; (3) which analogue from Fig 12 governs the persistence of a method across the controversy. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 , Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The reception condition and the settlement condition overlap in real cases. A claim can be rejected socially and settled later, or accepted socially and unsettled. The residue after unbundling is categorically different from either pile: it is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :57). **Verdict: union.** Triangulated with `01` F1.

### Q2 , Are the two frames isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take the consensus frame and the dissenting frame over a fixed atom set, for example the same continental outlines, fossil distributions and rock matches. If both use the same atoms but compose them under different bonds (one under a fixed-continents assumption, one under a drift assumption), they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The disagreement is carried entirely by the bond, which is invisible in the ingredient list. This is the structural reading of "a minority position": it is not a different set of facts, it is a different arrangement of the same facts.

**Important distinction.** Mendel's case is not an isomer claim of this kind. De Vries and Correns reached the same law independently in 1900 (world source W4). Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." That is **convergence**, which Fig 8 :50 calls "corroboration at maximum independence," not two arrangements of one atom set. Isomer and convergence are different relations and the ledger records which one each case instantiates.

- **Falsifier for Q2:** if the two frames share no atom at all, or if they compose the same atoms under the same bond and still differ, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading in Q4 stands instead. Written before the run.
- **Status:** `specified`, figure-defined, not measured. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 :117 and Fig 13 :121 govern different relations, both checked).

### Q3 , What is the order of the transition?

The bond `→` is "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72). The transition is ordered:

1. **Describe the residual before selecting a case.** Fig 9 :28: "any frame you pick will seem to fit." This is the cheapest step and it comes first.
2. **Refuse before mint.** Formula step 2 sorts the gap; a held false term is refused before a replacement is offered. Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 5 :55: "Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."
3. **Import with gates.** Fig 6 :12-13: "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap." The differing closure supplies an atom or a method; the record must show where it came from.
4. **Carry a method, not a replay.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
5. **Settle before compose.** Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom."
6. **Select the next room by the residual.** Fig 7 :40: "The room is summoned by the residual rather than composed."

The order is not a chronology of any one historical case; it is the dependency order the figures require. Real histories can run steps in parallel or out of order, and a case that runs them out of order has a broken link in the Fig 3 sense: a reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing.

- **Falsifier for Q3:** a case in which later research composes over a term before the ground settles it and the composition still pays at the ground. That would break the settle-before-compose step. Written before the run.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6, Fig 12 and the formula.

### Q4 , Which analogues govern persistence?

- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A method carried across a controversy is catalyst-like: it survives its own use and stays available to later research. A famous name attached to one occasion is a spent part. The difference is invisible unless the record marks it.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A persistent residual re-enters the next turn, which is why a minority claim can be taken up repeatedly after each failed closure. The sign matters: a loop that amplifies produces a movement, a loop that damps produces a forgotten result.
- **Functional group.** Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." "A dissenting frame in a discipline" is a functional group. The qualifier, not the group, says which dissent and which discipline. No standing list of which members are right survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51).
- **Mirror chain.** Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." A sequence of reviewers who share an evidence pool is one frame wearing many names; it adds spelling, not evidence.

### Q5 , The one live type question

Operators :77-78: "What a unary operator costs is open: an atom slot, nothing, or the choice of what to negate." Refusal is needed in Q3 step 2, and the framework does not price it. This is a live limit, named rather than hidden. No claim is made about the cost of a refusal, only that it is a distinct operator and that a vocabulary without one can only accumulate (Operators :58).

## FALSIFIER SUMMARY, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

1. The two frames share no atom, or compose the same atoms under the same bond and still differ (kills Q2).
2. A case composes over an unsettled term and the composition still pays at the ground (kills Q3 step 5).
3. A replay of a recording, carrying no method, survives as a catalyst across uses (kills Q4 catalyst analogue).
4. The union of Q1 collapses to one condition with one anchor (kills the whole supporting derivation).

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn in `01` section 7 and the census is redone.

## CONCLUSION

The consensus and dissenting frames are **isomers over a shared atom set** when they compose the same observations under different bonds; they are an instance of **convergence** when two independent frames reach the same atom. The transition is **ordered**: describe, refuse, import with gates, carry a method, settle, then compose. A method that survives its own use is a catalyst; the residual that re-enters is a cycle; "a dissenting frame in a discipline" is a functional group whose members are not fixed by the group. One type question, the price of negation, stays open.
