# The Tether's answer on central banks and the money supply

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the union and closure shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which lever a central bank holds. The live residual is at the contact between the policy frame and the banking frame: the policy frame predicts an outcome at a monetary slot, the published statistics return an observation, and the gap is indexed per slot (M0, M1, M2, M3, each with a national definition). The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question with two opposite answers: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single yes or no.

## 2. The shape

1. **The either/or is a bundled union.** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The policy rate influences bank lending, lending creates deposits, and deposits are most of the money stock. Unbundle into four slots: policy instruments, bank lending, public money demand, and the statistical definition. Return a verdict at each.

2. **"Control" must be rebuilt as reachability inside a closure.** Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* From inside its own library, the central bank reaches the price of reserves and its own balance sheet (base money). It does not reach the loan and deposit atoms, which are held by the banking frame. So "control the money supply" is accurate for the policy rate and base money, and not a description of directly composing broad money.

3. **Broad money is a union surplus of two frames.** Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* The central bank supplies base money and the price of reserves; commercial banks compose deposits by lending; the public chooses the currency/deposit split. No one frame holds the whole composition. The Bank of England's Monetary Analysis Directorate states that *"the majority of money in the modern economy is created by commercial banks making loans"* while adding that the amount created *"ultimately depends on the monetary policy of the central bank"*, normally via interest rates. Both clauses are needed; neither side of the either/or stands alone.

4. **Capability is policy frame plus banking habitat.** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A central bank considered in isolation cannot be assigned "controls" or "does not control" as a standing property. The answerable question is which contacts changed and whether the goal required them.

5. **The residual is indexed per slot, not pooled.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Narrow aggregates are closer to policy actions than broad ones. Pooling them into "the money supply" throws away the signal.

6. **"Control" is ordered; the readings are symmetric.** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* An observed co-movement between the policy rate and money growth cannot choose the arrow. The arrow must be imported from an identification frame with provenance and a shadow test, or withheld.

7. **A definition is a convention, and a targeting convention can break its own regularity.** Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Goodhart 1975, opened this session: *"Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes."* This is why "control M2" was a plausible target in one regime and an unreliable one in another.

8. **No figure returns a number.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The universal quantitative version of the question is a declared `gap`, of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind. An identified, jurisdiction-specific instrument could settle it; this derivation took no measurement.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that central banks are unimportant for money. They anchor the price of reserves and the monetary base, and they condition credit.
- Not that the money multiplier is a law, or that bank lending is unaffected by policy. Both are rejected by the imported economics source.
- Not that any particular aggregate is "the" money supply, or that a pooled reading licenses a global verdict.
- Not that a committee's sense of control is evidence of control.
- Not that the framework is the only true account of monetary policy, nor a theory of everything.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement, and no coefficient, horizon, or jurisdiction is returned.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- At a named slot and horizon, does an identified change in the policy rate move the aggregate by a stable amount that survives alternative aggregate definitions? If the sign reverses under redefinition, the pooled verdict is refused (Fig 1 :22).
- Does the aggregate move when bank lending is held fixed by an independent instrument? That discriminates the policy atom from the banking atom.
- Does the relation survive sustained targeting without behavioural adaptation? If not, the convention trap fires (Fig 10 :47).
- Standing falsifiers from section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: disjoint conditions, a policy-only arbitrary target, no reversal across definitions, a surviving target relation, a self-report that beats an independent reading, or a figure line that governs a monetary magnitude.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The monetary atoms are enumerated from the world, not composed from the figures. The import from the economics frame supplies the missing atom (bank deposit creation) with a source gate and a shadow test, and is debited against independence because it is one witness in one economics pool. Fig 13 :134 *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands"*; *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The answer stops where the available instruments cannot distinguish the arrow and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in the "control money versus influence rates" dispute, because the dispute is a bundled union, not a partition. A lever reaches only what is in the closure of the frame that holds it: the central bank reaches the price of reserves and its own balance sheet, while broad money is composed by commercial bank lending and the public's currency demand in a different closure. The two frames together reach the money supply, which is why the published monetary analysis says both that banks create most money and that the amount created depends on central bank policy, normally through interest rates. The answer is therefore indexed per aggregate and tested against a published series that does not move because anyone wants a verdict, with a standing warning that a definition promoted into a target tends to stop describing the thing it targets. The framework returns that shape, its refuters, and the instrument that would close it. It returns no number, and it does not settle the question.
