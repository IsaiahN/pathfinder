# Expert verification packet - the Tether on central banks and the money supply

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist or central bank researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the union and closure shape, not `grounded`.** No jurisdiction or aggregate was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do central banks control the money supply, or mainly influence interest rates and credit conditions" is a bundled union of conditions, not a partition with one winning side. A lever reaches only what is in the closure of the frame that holds it. From inside the policy frame the central bank reaches the price of reserves and base money; it does not reach the loan and deposit atoms that compose broad money, which belong to the banking frame. The two frames together reach the money supply, so "control" is accurate for the policy rate and base money and is not a description of directly composing M2 or M3. In normal times the main lever is the price of reserves and the terms of credit. Verdicts are per aggregate and per jurisdiction, tested against a published non-updating series, and no figure returns a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 9
- **Statement:** the either/or is a union with a local lever. The policy frame reaches its own instruments and base money from inside its closure; broad money is composed jointly with the banking frame, so "control" at that slot is a reachability failure, not an importance judgement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Refuter:** show the two conditions are disjoint (a money target moves the aggregate one for one without bank lending or public demand), or exhibit a single frame whose closure alone composes broad money with the other frame provably irrelevant.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against published monetary analysis
- **Statement:** commercial bank lending creates the deposits that make up most of the money stock, while the central bank normally acts through the price of reserves, with direct quantity action available (asset purchases, reserve requirements). Therefore both halves of the either/or are true at different slots, and the common "banks multiply up central bank money" picture is a shorthand that fails.
- **What to check:** read the Bank of England Monetary Analysis Directorate's 2014 Quarterly Bulletin article, especially its account of money creation and its long-run data annex. Is the reading faithful? Does the "amount ultimately depends on monetary policy" clause survive a regime with large reserve balances?
- **Source, provenance:** Michael McLeay, Amar Radia and Ryland Thomas, *"Money creation in the modern economy"*, Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1, published 14 March 2014. This is an import from the economics frame with a source gate (published institutional research) and a shadow test (it explains why a quantity target failed to compose the intended outcome). It is not a figure result.
- **Refuter:** produce a measured case in which deposits are not created by lending, or in which the central bank drives a chosen broad aggregate to an arbitrary target using only its own instruments independently of bank lending.

### C3 - measurement-facing; checkable against the released series
- **Statement:** "control" is an ordered claim and must be ranged against a series that does not update on the claimant's theory, at each aggregate separately. A definition promoted into a target tends to stop being a reliable description, and a pooled reading can reverse the per-group result.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **World corroboration:** Goodhart 1975, *"Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes."*; Simpson's paradox, *"a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined."* Both are corroboration only, not authority.
- **Refuter:** show a monetary aggregate whose target relation survives sustained targeting without adaptation and without revision of the series in response to the target; or show the policy frame's self-report of control predicting the published series better than an independent reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that central banks are unimportant for money. They anchor the price of reserves and base money and condition credit.
- Not that the money multiplier is a law, or that bank lending is unaffected by policy.
- Not that any pooled reading or single aggregate settles the question, and not that a committee's sense of control is evidence.
- Not a theory of everything, not a claim that this framework is the only true account.
- Not `grounded`. No coefficient, horizon, jurisdiction or universal number is asserted.
- Not a claim that the framework named the banking atom. That atom is an import from the economics frame, provenanced as such.

## 5. What would settle it

An identified instrument at a named slot, not a further argument. Questions for the ground:

1. At a named aggregate and horizon, does an identified policy-rate change move the aggregate by a stable amount that survives alternative definitions?
2. Does the aggregate move when bank lending is held fixed by an independent instrument? That discriminates the policy atom from the banking atom.
3. Does the relation survive sustained targeting without behavioural adaptation, or does the regularity break as Goodhart predicts?
4. Does the policy frame's own report of control beat an independent reading of the same published series?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45, Fig 9 :55 and Fig 1 :22, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the published Bank of England article. Are the two quoted claims faithful, and does the reserve-balance regime change the second?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate the policy atom from the banking atom at a named aggregate, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
