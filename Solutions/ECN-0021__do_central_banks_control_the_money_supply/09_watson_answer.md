## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether the central bank's lever composes the money supply (Option A) or mainly sets the price of reserves and conditions credit (Option B). Restated as an effect: a policy frame predicts an outcome at each monetary slot; the published statistics return an observation; the residual R = |prediction minus observation| must be indexed per slot, never pooled. Option A predicts that the money stock is in the closure of the policy frame's own instruments. Option B predicts that the policy frame reaches its own instruments and base money, while broad money is composed with the banking frame. What each leads to: A sends a lever verdict over a quantity whose atoms the policy frame does not hold; B sends a per-slot shape, a union structure, and a refuter, with the name withheld until an anchor settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what the policy frame predicts at a monetary slot and what the released series returns. Arity: four conditions (policy instruments, bank lending, public currency demand, measurement convention), so a union compound, not a binary partition. Symmetry: the verb "control" is ordered; the available readings are commonly symmetric, so the arrow must be imported or withheld. Scale: base money at the policy frame's own scale, broad money at the composed scale, with a lossy transform between.
- **Domain 1, policy frame.** Actors: monetary policy committee and balance sheet desk. Currency: reserves, policy rate, remunerated reserves, asset purchases, reserve requirements. Anchor: the published policy rate and the central bank's own balance sheet, which do not move because a reader changes their theory.
- **Domain 2, money-creation habitat.** Actors: commercial banks, borrowers, depositors and the non-bank public. Currency: loans and the deposits they create, currency holdings. Anchor: published monetary aggregates, which do not change when a frame forms an opinion about them.
- **Domain 3, measurement frame.** Actors: statistical agencies, revision histories, researchers. Currency: readings, definitions, residuals. Anchor: the released series and its revision history, the ground for the reading and not the reader's preferred definition.
- **Domain 4, public-discourse frame.** Actors: commentators, committees, the audience for an either/or. Currency: agreement, prestige, the feeling of control. Anchor: the ground, never agreement; this domain ranges legitimately only when it consults domains 2 and 3.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception of the aggregate, then vocabulary for what the frame's instruments afford, then objective at the slot, then lever choice, then carry across a regime. The chain breaks at vocabulary: the policy frame names its own instruments precisely and has no term for deposit creation. Optimising the lever above that break cannot rescue it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or is a false dichotomy, a bundled union of conditions with different anchors. In `specified` mode the figures give the following shape. The policy frame reaches the price of reserves and base money inside its own closure; broad money is composed jointly with the banking frame, so "controls the money supply" is accurate at the base-money slot and is not a description of directly composing M2 or M3. In normal times the main lever is the price of reserves and the terms of credit. Readings are per aggregate and per jurisdiction against a published series that does not update on the claim. A definition promoted into a target tends to stop describing the thing it targets. No figure returns a number, so the universal quantitative version is a declared `gap`, unreachable at this budget rather than underivable in principle. The specific economic claim about bank deposit creation is a provenanced import from the Bank of England article, not a figure derivation. This result is `specified`, ungrounded and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a single-sided verdict when the question is a union. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Here the cost is a research framing, not a trade, so one or two careful refuters are sufficient and a campaign is not.
- **undoable:** yes. The one-sided framing and the pooled reading are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent verdict can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An identified instrument at a named aggregate and horizon that moves the stock by a stable amount surviving alternative definitions, plus a demonstration with bank lending held fixed, would move the verdict toward control at that slot. A regularity that survives sustained targeting without adaptation would reverse the convention finding. A direct demonstration that the policy frame can drive a broad aggregate to an arbitrary target using only its own instruments, or a figure re-census that governs a monetary magnitude, would reverse the central verdict. Until any of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
