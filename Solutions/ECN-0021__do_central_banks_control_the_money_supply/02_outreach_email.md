**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the question is framed correctly

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do central banks control the money supply, or mainly influence rates and credit conditions" is a bundled union of conditions, not a partition with one winning side. There are at least four slots with different anchors: the central bank's own instruments (the policy rate, reserve remuneration, its balance sheet), commercial bank lending and deposit creation, the public's currency demand, and the statistical convention that defines each monetary aggregate. "Control" is an ordered claim and has to be tested per slot against a published series that does not move because anyone wants a particular verdict.
>
> The shape I derive is this. A lever reaches only what is in the closure of the frame that holds it. From inside the policy frame you can reach the price of reserves and base money. You cannot compose broad money there, because the loan and deposit atoms are in the banking frame's closure. The two frames together reach the money supply; neither reaches it alone. So "control the money supply" is accurate for the policy rate and the monetary base, and not a description of the direct composition of M2 or M3. In normal times the main lever is the price of reserves and the terms of credit. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement, and it does not return a number.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that central banks are unimportant for money, that the money multiplier is a law, that one aggregate is "the" money supply, or that any jurisdiction has been measured here. The specific claim that commercial banks create most money by lending comes from published monetary analysis, which I treat as an import with provenance, not as a result the framework derived. I am also not claiming the framework is the only true account, or that a theory of everything is possible.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. the conditions are disjoint, so a money target moves the aggregate one for one without bank lending or public demand;
> 2. the policy frame can drive a broad aggregate to an arbitrary target using only its own instruments, independently of the banking frame, against a non-updating published series;
> 3. all aggregates move the same way at the same horizon with no reversal under alternative definitions;
> 4. an aggregate's relation to its target survives sustained targeting without behavioural adaptation;
> 5. the policy frame's own report of control predicts the published series better than an independent reading.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - The published monetary analysis the import comes from: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
