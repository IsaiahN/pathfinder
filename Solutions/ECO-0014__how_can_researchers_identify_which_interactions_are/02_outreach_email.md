**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own re-census. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Would this fail in your system? A per-interaction test for essentiality

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim about how to identify essential species interactions under partial observation. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "essential interaction" cannot be a property of a species pair by itself. It must be indexed to a slot (species pair plus interaction type), to a declared focal function (persistence, stability, biomass, a service), and to a declared intervention (removal, exclusion, addition). The identification rule is: enumerate everything in contact with the function, outward to second and third order, record empty cells as channel readings rather than as absence, sharpen any method that is already returning a weak reading, and check the candidate against the outcome of the intervention with at least two frames that do not share an evidence pool. Where nothing at any resolution is returning a reading, the honest output is "unresolved at this budget" plus a description of what a discriminating instrument would have to do.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that centrality identifies essentiality, that an empty interaction cell means no interaction, that "keystone species" is the answer, or that this package can rank the interactions in your system. The framework returns a shape and a route, not a name and not a list. Any particular binding is yours, with your ground and your provenance.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. a single network-wide aggregate that preserves the ordering of per-slot intervention effects at equal cost;
> 2. an interaction whose essentiality ranking is invariant across focal functions and interventions;
> 3. researcher agreement, with no independent intervention outcome, shown to settle an essentiality verdict;
> 4. an instrument built from a description alone, with no prior reading at any resolution, that resolves a slot no existing method registered;
> 5. two groups drawing on one evidence pool shown to corroborate as well as two independent groups.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - The verbatim figure text and the formula (local framework files in this project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Ecology and Society: https://esa.org/publications/read-our-journals/
> - The public methods discussion that prompted this check: https://communities.springernature.com/posts/unraveling-hidden-connections-predicting-links-to-illuminate-ecological-networks
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** run it on a system you can check against an intervention. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
