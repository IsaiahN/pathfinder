## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel*: "This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can settle it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "plural orders are frames ranging against an enforcement ground, joined by union seams, with independence governing whether their agreement means anything" is a shape. "State law wins, custom is dying" is a name, and no instrument in this derivation settles it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, on vocabulary by proximity: "If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."
- Same essay, on recursion: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." This is why the residual in `01` is stated as "what decides which rule governs a slot", not as "legal pluralism theory".
- Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Picking the three orders first and then finding figures to fit would be apophenia; the residual came first.
- *Library of Babel*, on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." Here that means the loop stops when no further figure line changes the shape, not when a country has been named.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The figures README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." For legal pluralism, the ground is an enforcement record, a court decision that is actually carried out, a change in behaviour.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and a shadow test.

A legal-transplant claim is exactly the second case. The provenance is the whole issue: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" (Fig 8 :50). Without the record, a rule that appears in two orders is one witness with two names.

## 4. The source problem, and why "which system wins" is a metamerism error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator argument. An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; several underlying causes can produce one reading. The essay states: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." And: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." It was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument", and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy".

Applied here: observed compliance with a rule is a reading. It can be produced by state coercion, religious obligation, customary legitimacy, or a mixture. Reading the source (which order actually governed) off the behavior is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, meaning enforcement data and case-level attribution, not a confident name. This is the mechanism behind F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming any of the three orders?

**Answer: no, with a scope condition.** The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." It governs a primitive not held in the library. Customary, religious, and state orders are not missing primitives in this derivation; they are observed frames in the habitat, enumerated from the world. Naming them is observation, not invention.

**Correction to my own first reading.** I initially read the rule as a blanket ban on naming any order, which would have made the whole question unanswerable. That was an overreach. The correct scope: the framework cannot name the empirical winner for a given jurisdiction, because that name requires a ground reading. It can name the frames present, because those are read off the case. Both readings are recorded here; the initial one is not erased.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Consequence 1: correlation is not corroboration.** Seat Map: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Several schools of pluralism theory that agree because they read the same literature are one witness. The verdict in F2 is therefore about independence as a measurable quantity, not about the number of agreeing sources.

**Consequence 2: the derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading.** `PLANS/00_MASTER_PLAN.md` section 13 records the project rule: "Simulacra. LLM output is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading." So `01`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They cannot settle which legal order governs anywhere, and they must not be presented as field research. A reply from a legal pluralism researcher that returns a refutation is a correction on shape and is worth more than an endorsement; by Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
