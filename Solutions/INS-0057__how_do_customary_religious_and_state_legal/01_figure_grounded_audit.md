## 0. The residual in effect terms

The three named orders issue rules for the same conduct and the same persons. They do not merely describe the same thing in different words; they make claims that can conflict. The residual, stated as an effect, is:

> When several normative frames range against one conduct-ground, what decides which rule governs a given slot, what happens at the seam where their claims overlap, and what would have to change for the governing rule to change?

Arity: k orders, of which three are named (customary, religious, state). The figures handle k generally, so the count is not load-bearing. Symmetry: the relation between any two orders is not fixed. It depends on how much evidence they draw on and how far their closures differ (Fig 2 :31; Fig 8 :47). Scale: the same loop runs at the scale of one dispute (one slot, Fig 1 :19) and at the scale of a whole legal field (a population, Fig 4). A global statistic over orders is not the same object as a ruling in one case.

What an answer would look like is bound in `README.md`: per slot, the governing order, the reason, the seam event, and the change condition.

**Partition or union: UNION.** Fig 9 :54 "When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A person can be married under a religious regime, hold land under a customary regime, and be tried in a state court at the same time. The three conditions overlap; the intersections are the interesting cases. A partition model (each order owns a separate slice of social life) is the wrong census and will misdirect the search.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) Settle which ground decides a dispute before ranking the orders; (2) refuse the "merge them into one law" move before minting, because the fifth route is REFUTED and owes a refusal (Formula step 2); (3) describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27). Re-census puts state law in D2 with the other two. This is a re-census, not a strain, and no figure is forced.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The three orders are frames; the anchor is the enforced outcome, not any order's self-description (decisive for D1, D2)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Parse: a customary ruling, a religious ruling, and a state ruling are each outputs of a bounded frame with its own library (Symbols table :111). None is the anchor. The anchor is what actually settles the case and does not move when an order restates its claim: the decision that gets enforced, the behaviour that changes. The Seat Map says the same thing in its anchor section: "Whatever settles the question and does not update on any frame's opinion of it. An experiment, a counter, a market, a proof checker, a patient outcome." A court judgment is an instrument for this question only if enforcement is observable; an unenforced judgment is a frame output, not a ground reading.

Consequence: "which system wins" cannot be answered by reading any system's own account of its authority. Each account is a frame describing itself. The question has to be asked at the enforcement record.

- **Refuter:** show a case where the enforced outcome changes purely because one order restates its claim, with no change in conduct, evidence, or enforcement capacity. That would make the ground update on a frame and break the anchor test.
- **Mode:** `specified`, structural. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 and the Seat Map anchor section, low overlap between the metalaw figure and the checking-arrangement doc).

### F2 - Agreement between orders is evidence only in proportion to their independence (decisive for D2)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

The two collapse panels in Fig 2 are directly instantiated here. Customary and religious norms that both derive from the same local moral premise are panel 2 ("the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"), not two witnesses. Orders that only adjust to each other are panel 1 ("no anchor; each updates on the others") and converge on whoever had the most influence. Fig 13 :38 states the set-level version: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything. Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Consequence: the common claim that plural orders "reinforce one another" is a verdict only if their independence was measured. Harmonisation that raises overlap destroys the very evidence it appears to create.

- **Refuter:** exhibit two orders with measured low overlap whose agreement still fails to predict the enforced outcome, or show that their agreement predicts it worse than one order alone. Either breaks the independence account.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2, Fig 8, Fig 13; three figures, low overlap between the ground figure, the union figure, and the substrate figure).

### F3 - It is a union, not a partition (governs the census)

Fig 9 :54: "When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The world sources agree in effect. The GSDRC topic guide (Bakrania and Haider, 2016) reports "hybrid or mixed legal environments where state, local and non-state actors are linked and the lines between them are blurred" and "overlaps of norms, rules and procedures, which are based on interaction and coexistence over time." Wikipedia's legal pluralism entry records that "individuals would choose to bring their legal claims under the system that they thought would offer them the best advantage." That is a union with overlapping conditions, not three disjoint boxes.

Consequence: the right unit of analysis is the compound case in two or three conditions. Unbundle it before searching; a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

- **Refuter:** a jurisdiction in which the three orders provably partition the field, with empty intersections and no shared norms, over a defined period. That would scope F3 to a subset rather than refute the figure, and would be recorded as a real limitation.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus two independent world sources, low overlap with the framework).

### F4 - Influence requires contact with a differing closure; a transplant is an import with a provenance gate (decisive for D3)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".

"Customary rule absorbed into state statute" and "state procedure adopted by a religious tribunal" are imports in the figure's sense only if the receiving closure differs and the rule adds something not already derivable there. A transplant that merely restates an atom the receiving order already holds adds no atom and moves no wall (Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world"). The world literature supplies the same distinction under a different name: Watson's legal transplant ("transplantation is the most fertile source of legal development") and Teubner's "legal irritation", where "a foreign rule disrupts established norms and societal arrangements" rather than integrating smoothly. Teubner's irritation is the figure's "wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives" seen from the receiving frame.

Consequence: "influence" is not diffusion as a smooth flow. It is a crossing with a source gate and a shadow test, and its cost is paid in the receiving order's independence.

- **Refuter:** show a rule migrating between two orders whose closures are identical (no surplus) yet changing enforced outcomes. That would move the wall without differing closures and break Fig 6 :23.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 6 and Fig 8 for the mechanism, two independent legal sources for the phenomenon).

### F5 - A convention stops being a convention the moment it decides an outcome (decisive for D3)

Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Customary and religious norms are conventions until a state recognises, codifies, or enforces them. At that crossing they become verdicts: they make claims about the world and are backable by sanction. The framework's ground maintainer "authors conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam" but "a convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do" (Seat Map, Seat 3 and the laws). The GSDRC guide gives the empirical shadow of this: "Prohibiting discrimination in customary law can produce a law that looks good on paper, but that is unlikely to be enforced or to change social norms." A declared prohibition that does not change contact has not grounded anything.

Consequence: the interesting question is not "does the state recognise custom" but "at what point did the recognised convention become a verdict, and can it still be refused?" Codification that makes a norm look good on paper but unenforceable is the declared-meaning failure named in Fig 10 :18.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a convention that routinely decides enforced outcomes while remaining checkable, non-authoritative, and refusing-able. That would falsify Fig 10 :47 as applied to this domain.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 10, Fig 2, and an independent institutional source; the figure line appears in both figures, which is corroboration at shared source, so the world source carries the independent weight).

### F6 - Whether an order helps or harms is relative to the residual, not a property of the order (the "compete" reading)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

State courts are the access route for a woman contesting an inheritance norm, and the obstacle for a community defending that norm. Religious arbitration is a legitimate forum for one residual and a rights failure for another. There is no standing list of good orders that survives a change of residual. The GSDRC guide reports both directions: non-state provision is preferred where state provision is "unreliable", "ineffective", "inaccessible", or distrusted, while CEDAW has "called for a unification of legal systems within countries" over women's rights. Both findings are the same actor valued against different residuals.

Consequence: "compete" is not a property of the orders; it is a relation constituted by a chosen residual. Managed coexistence is permanent work because contact cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :61).

- **Refuter:** produce a ranking of customary, religious, and state orders that is stable across at least three distinct residuals (access, legitimacy, rights) with the same valence in each. That would defeat the relativity claim.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus an independent guide that explicitly reports both valences).

### F7 - Keep verdicts per order and per slot; never average them (structural discipline)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A single national "legal pluralism index" that averages over customary, religious, and state forums discards the only signal that says which order governs which slot. It turns a set of distinct verdicts into a weighted average of influence, which is exactly the room failure.

- **Refuter:** show an aggregate measure that predicts the enforced outcome per slot at least as well as the per-slot verdicts, across two independent jurisdictions. That would downgrade F7 from a structural requirement to a preference.
- **Mode:** `general` on the discipline, `specified` on the empirical prediction. **Status:** `single-source` for the figure line (Fig 1), corroborated by Fig 7 on agreement.

### F8 - Forum shopping is frames ranging against a ground

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Wikipedia's forum shopping entry defines the practice as "litigants taking actions to have their legal case heard in the court they believe is most likely to provide a favorable judgment," and quotes Judge Sam B. Hall Jr.: "in reality, every litigant who files a lawsuit engages in forum shopping when he chooses a place to file suit."

In effect terms, the litigant ranges each available frame against the same ground (the desired outcome) and selects the one whose currency pays. This is not a defect added to pluralism from outside; it is what pluralism looks like from the actor's side when the frames are available. Fig 11 :49 explains why the same forum is a benefactor to one actor and a malfactor to another.

- **Refuter:** a jurisdiction with genuinely available multiple forums in which observed behaviour is independent of the expected ruling. That would break the ranging account.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 plus an independent legal-encyclopedia source; one pool, since the encyclopedia summarises the same literature).

### F9 - What the figures cannot give: the empirical ranking (a `gap`, stated plainly)

The figures yield the shape: frames, an enforcement anchor, union rather than partition, independence as the governing quantity, import across seams, convention turning into verdict, valence relative to residual, and per-slot verdicts. They do **not** yield which of the three orders dominates in a given country, how often transplants succeed, or whether a particular codification improves rights outcomes. Those are ground questions requiring court records, enforcement data, and ethnography. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

- **Status:** `gap` for the empirical ranking. This is a verdict, not a silence. It is not to be filled by picking a favourite figure line.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted, or scoped, if any of the following holds:

1. The enforced outcome moves when a frame merely restates its claim, with no change in conduct or enforcement (breaks F1 anchor).
2. Two orders with measured low overlap agree, yet their agreement fails to predict the enforced outcome better than one order alone (breaks F2).
3. A convention demonstrably decides enforced outcomes while remaining checkable, non-authoritative, and refusing-able (breaks F5).
4. A jurisdiction shows a genuine partition of the field with empty intersections over a defined period (scopes F3).
5. A rule moves between two orders with identical closures and still changes outcomes (breaks F4).
6. A ranking of the three orders is stable across three distinct residuals with unchanged valence (breaks F6).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn or scoped, and the log below is updated. Until one is run, no part of this audit is `grounded`.
