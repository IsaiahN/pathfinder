# Expert verification packet: the Tether on legal pluralism

**What this is:** a claim in a form a legal pluralism researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the skeleton, not `grounded`.** No court record or enforcement dataset is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a field result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Customary, religious, and state orders are frames ranging against one enforcement ground, not competing descriptions of a single legal system. The thing that settles a case is the decision actually enforced, which does not move when an order restates its claim. Agreement between orders is evidence only in proportion to their independence, so orders sharing one premise are one witness with two names. Their relation is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Influence is a rule crossing into a differing closure with a provenance record, and a convention stops being a convention the moment it decides an outcome. Whether an order helps or harms is relative to the residual being tracked. Verdicts stay per case and are never averaged into one national score. The figures return this shape; they do not name a winning order.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The anchor is the enforced outcome, not an order's self-description
- **Statement:** the governing rule for a slot is settled by what is actually enforced, not by any order's claim of authority. State law is a frame because it moves when a legislature acts.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Also Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did the outcome track an enforcement record, or only a change in what one forum said about its own authority?
- **Refuter:** the enforced outcome changes when a frame merely restates its claim, with no change in conduct, evidence, or enforcement capacity. If that happens, the anchor test fails and C1 is withdrawn.

### C2 - Agreement between orders is evidence only in proportion to independence
- **Statement:** two orders that draw on the same evidence are one frame wearing two names; their mutual agreement cannot corroborate a claim about the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :48 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check:** identify a case where customary and religious norms appear to agree. Is the agreement produced by a shared premise, or did the two orders reach it separately? If separately, that is convergence at high independence, which is genuine corroboration.
- **Refuter:** two orders with measured low overlap agree, yet their agreement predicts the enforced outcome no better than one order alone. If that holds, the independence account in C2 is wrong.

### C3 - The relation is a union, and the crossing has a gate
- **Statement:** the orders overlap in one case, so the residuum is a specific compound, not a smaller mixture. Influence is an import that moves the receiving frame only on contact with a differing closure, and a convention becomes a verdict when it decides an outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** do the orders in your field partition the social field with empty intersections, or do people sit under two or three at once? For a transplant you know, was the provenance recorded, and did the imported rule add something the receiving order could not already derive?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated partition with empty intersections over a defined period scopes the union claim; a rule that moves a wall between identical closures breaks the import claim; a convention that decides outcomes while remaining checkable and refusing-able breaks the convention claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of the three orders, and not a prediction of which one wins in any jurisdiction.
- Not convergence toward a single law, and not a claim that unification is desirable.
- Not that any order is inherently legitimate or illegitimate. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not that harmonisation works, that transplants succeed, or that codification improves outcomes.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is asserted.
- Not that the framework certifies itself. The figures are the authority; this packet is a simulated derivation, not a field reading.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Case-level attribution:** for a defined set of cases, record which order's rule was actually applied, one slot at a time. An instrument that distinguishes coercion from obligation from legitimacy would name the source of compliance.
2. **Independence measurement:** for two apparently agreeing orders, measure the overlap in their evidence.
3. **The crossing point:** date the moment a recognised norm began deciding enforced outcomes and test whether it can still be refused.
4. Any of the refuters in section 2 firing is a better outcome than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31 and Fig 10 :47 (three short passages).
2. Name one case where an order's self-description disagreed with the enforced outcome. Which governed?
3. Name one pair of orders that appear to agree. Is the agreement a shared premise or separate derivation?
4. Say whether the field in your jurisdiction is a union or a partition, with one example of a person under two orders at once.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
