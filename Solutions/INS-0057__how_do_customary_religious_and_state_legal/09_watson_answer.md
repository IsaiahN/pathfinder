## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures say when several normative orders bear on the same conduct and the same people. Option A: treat the three orders as partitions of one legal field that can be ranked and harmonised. Option B: treat them as frames ranging against an enforcement ground, joined by union seams, with independence governing whether their agreement means anything. What each leads to: A names a winner without a ground reading, averages away the per-case signal, and mistakes shared-premise agreement for corroboration; B gives a checkable shape, per-slot verdicts, refuters written in advance, and an honest `gap` where the empirical ranking belongs.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Several normative frames issue conflicting rules for one conduct-ground. Arity: k orders, of which three are named; the count is not load-bearing. Symmetry: the relation between any two orders is not fixed, it depends on evidence overlap and closure difference (Fig 2 :31; Fig 8 :47). Scale: one dispute (one slot, Fig 1 :19) up to a whole legal field (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, the dispute-ground.** Actors: parties, the conduct, the decision actually enforced. Currency: the enforced outcome and the behaviour actually changed. Anchor: what settles the case and does not move because a frame thinks otherwise (Fig 2 :29). Nameable: enforcement record.
- **Domain 2, each order as a frame.** Actors: customary community, religious authority, state legislature and courts. Currency: rulings, norms, sanctions, legitimacy. Anchor: its own closure and record. State law is here, not in Domain 1, because it moves when a legislature acts.
- **Domain 3, the seam.** Actors: transplants, forum shopping, jurisdictional conflict, overlapping membership. Currency: rules crossing (imports), seam meanings (conventions). Anchor: the Domain 1 ground that decides the case after contact.
- **Domain 4, the observer.** Actors: the legal pluralism researcher. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Partition or union: UNION.** Fig 9 :54 "When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A person can be under all three orders at once.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Establish the enforcement instrument, then terms for what each order affords, then what counts as progress, then a route, then carry. Refuse the merge-into-one move before minting (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27).

Strain test: "state law is the ground" fails Fig 2 :29 and was re-census'd into Domain 2. No figure line needed forcing after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the empirical ranking does not exist yet and is declared a `gap`. The enforced outcome is the anchor and all three orders are frames ranging against it. Agreement between orders is evidence only in proportion to measured independence. The relation is a union of overlapping conditions. Influence is a rule crossing into a differing closure with a provenance record, and a recognised convention becomes a verdict when it decides an outcome. Valence is relative to the residual. Verdicts stay per case and are never averaged. The framework returns this shape, not the name of a winning order. Status: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three sub-claims, verbatim lines and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a conclusion instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The empirical overreaches are already removed: no ranking, no named winner, no claim of successful harmonisation, no `grounded` label. A refutation scopes the affected finding without withdrawing the shape, and no primitive has to be given up. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Case-level attribution that distinguishes coercion from obligation from legitimacy would name the source of compliance; a measured low-overlap agreement that fails to predict outcomes would break C2; a demonstrated partition with empty intersections would scope the union claim; a convention that decides outcomes while remaining refusing-able would break C3; a rule that moves a wall between identical closures would break the import claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
