# The Tether's answer on customary, religious, and state legal orders

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the skeleton, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Several normative frames issue rules for the same conduct and the same people. They do not merely describe the same thing differently; their claims can conflict. The effect question is what decides which rule governs a given slot, what happens where their claims overlap, and what would have to change for the governing rule to change. The contact is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54 "When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."), not three disjoint boxes and not one hard problem.

## 2. The shape

1. **The anchor is the enforced outcome, not any order's self-description.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A statute moves when a legislature acts, so state law is a frame, not the ground. Customary and religious orders are frames too. All three range against the same conduct-ground: Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
2. **Agreement between orders is evidence only in proportion to independence.** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :48 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Orders that draw on one shared premise are one witness with two names, so their mutual agreement cannot corroborate anything (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Harmonisation that raises overlap spends the evidence it appears to create.
3. **The relation is a union, not a partition.** A person can be under all three at once. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Unbundle the compound before searching; a single lever clears the one-condition region and leaves the intersection standing.
4. **Influence is import across a seam, with a provenance gate.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". A rule that simply restates what the receiving order already holds adds no atom and moves no wall. The world literature calls the disruptive case "legal irritation" (Teubner, 1998).
5. **A convention stops being a convention when it decides an outcome.** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Recognition and codification are the crossing. Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one": a prohibition written into a code that is not enforced has changed a declaration, not contact.
6. **Whether an order helps or harms is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The state court that opens an inheritance claim for one person closes a customary forum for another. There is no standing ranking of the three orders that survives a change of residual.
7. **Verdicts stay per slot and are never averaged.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A national pluralism score discards the only signal that says which order governs which case.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a ranking of customary, religious, and state law. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that the orders are converging toward one system, and not that they must be unified. There is no last room; a terminal arrangement would be the final handoff the figures rule out.
- Not that custom, religion, or the state is inherently legitimate or illegitimate. Valence is relational (claim 6).
- Not that every transplant succeeds or that harmonisation is good. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).
- Not a derivation of one order from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No court record, enforcement dataset, or ethnography was read for this question.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Attribution:** for a defined set of cases, which order's rule was actually applied, recorded case by case rather than aggregated? An instrument that distinguishes coercion from obligation from legitimacy would name the source of compliance.
- **Independence:** for two orders that appear to agree, what is the measured overlap in their evidence? If overlap is high, the agreement is one pool talking.
- **The convention crossing:** at what point did a recognised norm start deciding enforced outcomes, and can it still be refused?
- **Standing falsifiers:** a frame that moves the ground by restating a claim; low-overlap agreement that fails to predict outcomes; a convention that decides outcomes while remaining refusing-able; a genuine partition with empty intersections; a rule that moves walls between identical closures.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The empirical ranking is a `gap` in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F9. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The habitat of legal orders is enumerated from the world, and what cannot yet be perceived or measured at the enforcement record is the residual. The derivation stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. It does not name a winning order for any jurisdiction, and it does not pretend that agreement among pluralism scholars is a measurement.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank customary, religious, and state law, and it does not claim a final arrangement. It says that the thing which settles a dispute is the decision actually enforced, not any order's account of its own authority; that each order is a frame ranging against that ground; that agreement between orders counts as evidence only in proportion to their independence, so two orders sharing one premise are one witness with two names; that their relation is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition; that influence is a rule crossing into a different closure, carrying a provenance record, with a convention ceasing to be a convention the moment it decides an outcome; and that whether an order helps or harms depends on which residual you track. It keeps verdicts per case rather than averaging them into a national score. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The empirical question of which order governs where stays open until the ground settles it.
