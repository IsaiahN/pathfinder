# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the method

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01` section 3 has been read and can be stated in your own words. The offer collapses on contact if the recipient finds a refuter you had not considered.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about early warning under an uncertain forecast

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about one output, not about a theory. I am not asking you to read the
> framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does
> not survive your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** whether an early warning system and local
> knowledge can reduce harm in a particular place while the forecast stays uncertain.
> The claim in shape form is this. Harm is an outcome at the ground: a count of deaths,
> losses and damaged assets in a specific community. A forecast is a frame's prediction,
> and forecast uncertainty is a state of that frame's channel, not a change in the
> outcome. So an uncertain forecast does not remove the thing being measured. A warning
> can still change what people do.
>
> Three conditions are claimed as necessary, not as slogans:
>
> 1. The warning chain must run past prediction into a decision and an action. A warning
>    that stops at "here is the forecast" does not, on this account, reduce harm.
> 2. Local knowledge must be an independent set of readings, not the official forecast
>    restated in local words. If both channels draw on the same evidence, their
>    agreement is one witness twice.
> 3. Harm must be read per community and hazard. A regional average can be near zero
>    while one exposed community still carries the whole live signal.
>
> **What this does not claim:** that forecast skill is irrelevant, that any particular
> local indicator works, that a bigger audience would make the claim true, or that
> technology alone is enough. No outcome study was run for this note. Nothing here is
> measured.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I wrote the claim:**
>
> 1. A case where harm fell while the chain stopped at prediction, with no decision or
>    action link.
> 2. A controlled case where the reduction is fully explained by forecast skill, with
>    no independent contribution from local observation.
> 3. A case where local knowledge and the official forecast drew on exactly the same
>    evidence pool, yet the reduction is credited to their agreement.
> 4. A case where repeated non-actionable warnings left response and harm unchanged.
>
> If any one holds in the place you know, the claim is wrong there and I will withdraw
> it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you
> would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The one question I would value:** in the setting you know, which link actually
> stops the chain, and was that measured or assumed?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary
> of them, are the authority.
> - Early warning system definition and its four components (UNDRR terminology): https://www.undrr.org/terminology/early-warning-system
> - Review of local and scientific knowledge integration in early warning (Hermans et al., Natural Hazards, 2022): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-022-05468-8
> - Indigenous weather forecasting, animal and plant indicators and outcome feedback among Afar pastoralists (Balehegn et al., Pastoralism, 2019): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13570-019-0143-y
>
> **If you want to use the method:** it is a loop that states its mode, enumerates the
> habitat of a residual and writes its refuter before a run. A frame cannot check the
> direction of its own question, so each user writes what would refute their own result
> before starting. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient ranges the claim against the ground without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A receptive audience is a mirror. |
| Pre-registered refuter | Formula step 2 and the derive-first discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." An unmeasured outcome is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict. |
| Two frames only if independent | Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 8 :48-49. A restatement is not a second witness. |
| Per-slot harm, not a global average | Fig 1 :19 and :22. Aggregation is how a live signal disappears. |
| Links after the verdict | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the architecture before the check sends a recording, not a generator. |
| No trust request and no prestige appeal | Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Prestige can fund a trial; it cannot settle one. |
| Ask which link stops the chain | Fig 3 :29-30: ask which link the system stops at and whether the answer was measured or assumed. |

## Distribution constraint

Send to one person at a time, through a public or opt-in channel, with an opt-out line. Do not send the same note to a list, and do not record later agreement as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A reply is a conversation, not a witness.
