# Union, order and arrangement in an early warning question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question "can early warning systems and local knowledge reduce harm even when forecasts remain uncertain?" arrives bundled. Three questions are owed before any verdict: (1) is it one problem or several, (2) in what order do the parts depend on one another, and (3) are the two knowledge systems different substances or two names for one? The first is a union/partition question, the second a dependency question, the third an arrangement question.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the description: a hazard, a community, a forecast, a local observation, a communication channel, a decision, an outcome. Bonds available: sequence (→), production (⇒), conjunction (+), comparison (⋛), subtraction (−), negation (¬). No new atom is available without an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — One problem or a union? CORRECTION TO MY FIRST READING

My first reading treated "uncertain forecast" as the single difficult condition. Re-census: four conditions bind, forecast skill, dissemination, local interpretability, and capacity to act.

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

So a better forecast clears only the region where forecast skill alone binds. In the intersections, the second condition still binds and harm does not fall. This is why the question cannot be answered by a single number for forecast accuracy, and why `01` F2 and F3 must be kept as separate verdicts. **Retraction of my first reading recorded here:** uncertainty is not the master condition; it is one of four, and it lives in the channel (Fig 10 :24).

**Falsifier for Q1.** A case where a single lever (forecast skill alone) clears harm in every region, including where dissemination, interpretability and capacity are held fixed. That would make it a partition and this derivation wrong.

### Q2 — What is the order of the chain?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The local chain is:

```
1 perception        hazard detected + local cues read
2 vocabulary        what this situation affords; what counts as a safer outcome
3 planning          trigger, route, who acts, by when
4 learn and carry   outcome recorded; indicator re-weighted for next event
```

The middle link is the one with no instrument. Fig 3 :25-26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." In this question that link is where local knowledge lives. UNDRR's four-component definition and Hermans et al.'s finding that all four components must work together corroborate the dependency claim from outside the framework, but the two sources share the DRR evidence pool, so they are one witness.

The order also explains an asymmetry: a warning delivered at link 3 cannot be fixed by improving link 1 after the event. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

**Falsifier for Q2.** A documented case where harm falls while link 2 is absent (no local vocabulary for what the situation affords) and the warning is applied as a uniform rule. That would break the dependency claim.

### Q3 — Are early warning and local knowledge isomers, or two names for one frame?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

Consider the atom set held by both systems: the same rain, the same wind, the same animal behaviour, the same households, the same roads. If the scientific warning and the local reading arrive at the same conclusion through separate observations, they are Fig 8 :50 corroboration at maximum independence: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* The contents look identical; the record of where each came from separates them (Fig 8 :51-52).

If instead the local message simply relays the official forecast in local vocabulary, the atoms are the same but the bond is adoption, not convergence. The union has no surplus: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names" (Fig 8 :48-49). This is the practical failure mode the outreach packet asks a checker to look for.

So the isomer question has a precise answer: **they can be isomers over a shared atom set, but only if each reading is independently produced.** Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The bond is not recoverable from the message content; it is recoverable only from the provenance record. Balehegn et al. (2019) describe the institutional arrangement that keeps the two routes separate: the *Adda* elders "triangulate information from different sources" and "no prediction is taken for granted, and uncertainty is identified and declared."

**Falsifier for Q3.** A case where local knowledge and the official forecast draw on exactly the same evidence, with no independent observation, and where the programme still produces a distinct local decision. That would collapse the isomer claim and leave only one frame with two labels.

### Q4 — What does an arrangement cost in two currencies?

Fig 12 :125-126: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A warning protocol is cheap to describe (one threshold, one message) and expensive to settle (it must be tried against a ground that does not repeat). Conversely, a rich local indicator system is expensive to describe and cheap to refute in a season that fails to follow it. The two readings come apart in both directions; summing them is the error Fig 12 names. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A description cost is a budget filter; an outcome is the witness.

**Falsifier for Q4.** A case where a warning's description cost and its settlement cost are shown to be the same quantity in the same units, so the two-currency claim has no referent. None was found in the sources read.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union, not a partition (Q1). Its parts are ordered by dependency, with the unmeasured middle link doing the work that local knowledge supplies (Q2). Early warning and local knowledge can be isomers over a shared atom set, but only when each reading is independently produced; otherwise they are one frame wearing two names (Q3). Description cost and settlement cost are two currencies and must be held apart (Q4). None of this needs a seventh primitive. What it needs is a provenance record and an outcome at the ground.
