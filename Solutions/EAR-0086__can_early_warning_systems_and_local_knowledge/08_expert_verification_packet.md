# Expert verification packet: EAR-0086, early warning and local knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a practitioner or researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No outcome study is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Uncertainty in a forecast is a state of the forecasting frame's channel; harm is an outcome at the ground and is the right anchor for a question about harm to people. Therefore an uncertain forecast does not by itself prevent harm reduction. Harm must be read per community and hazard, because a regional average can erase a live local slot. An early warning system reduces harm only if the chain runs from perception through a local vocabulary of what the situation affords to a decision and a recorded outcome. Local knowledge is a set of readings already returned by a habitat of people, indicators and institutions; adding warning technology without changing contact is an agent-only improvement. The scientific forecast and the local reading must be independent enough that their agreement is triangulation rather than one evidence pool speaking twice. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not a named technology and not a measured result.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — The premise is mislocated: uncertainty is a channel state, harm is the anchor

- **Statement:** "Even when forecasts remain uncertain" does not entail "cannot reduce harm." The forecast is a frame; the outcome is the settle.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."; Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
- **Refuter:** show a case where the measured harm outcome moves when the forecast frame is pushed, so outcomes are not independent of the prediction; or show that a global average residual always carries the same information as the per-slot reading, contradicting Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".

### C2 — A warning that stops at prediction cannot reduce harm

- **Statement:** the dependency chain must complete. Perception alone is not a warning outcome. The usually missing link is having terms for what the situation affords and what would count as progress, which is where local knowledge does its work.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."; Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."; Fig 3 :29-30 "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."
- **Check against the world:** UNDRR defines an early warning system as an integrated system that enables people to "take timely action to reduce disaster risks in advance of hazardous events" and says "Failure in one component or a lack of coordination across them could lead to the failure of the whole system." Hermans et al. (2022) write "All these four components have to work together seamlessly (end to end) and in a people-centred way to make an EWS effective."
- **Refuter:** a documented case where harm measurably fell with the chain stopping at prediction and no decision or action link.

### C3 — Two frames help only if they are independent

- **Statement:** early warning and local knowledge can be isomers over a shared atom set, same atoms arranged differently. If the local message merely relays the official forecast, the union has no surplus and there is one frame with two names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."; Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."; Fig 8 :48-49 "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
- **Check against the world:** Balehegn et al. (2019) report that Afar pastoralists "do not take the information directly and have to make a lot of comparisons with local conditions and local bio-physical observations" and that "No single indicator is taken at face value".
- **Refuter:** a case where harm reduction is fully explained by one evidence pool shared by both channels, with no independent local observation contributing, yet the reduction is credited to their agreement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and no final answer. The shape stops where outcome measurement begins.
- Not that forecast skill is irrelevant, and not that a particular indicator or communication technology is being recommended.
- Not that more agreement, a larger audience, or a prestigious reviewer would corroborate the claim. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not that local knowledge is always right, and not that any single local sign is sufficient. The Balehegn study itself reports that no single indicator is taken at face value.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

An outcome at the ground, not a further argument:

1. A registered, per-slot harm measurement in a named place and hazard, with forecast uncertainty held high.
2. An ablation: does the reduction survive removing the local-knowledge channel, or removing the decision link?
3. A provenance audit: are the local readings independently produced, or copied from the official forecast?
4. A channel check: does response and harm change after a run of non-actionable alerts?
5. The five standing refuters in `01` section 3, any one of which withdraws the corresponding finding.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :29-30 and Fig 1 :13, :19. Three short passages. Do the quotes say what C1 claims?
2. Look at the warning chain you know. Name the link it actually stops at, and say whether that was measured or assumed (Fig 3 :29-30).
3. Ask whether the local observations in your setting draw on different evidence from the official forecast. If they do not, C3 does not apply there.
4. Name one outcome measurement that would distinguish "the warning reduced harm" from "the harm would have fallen anyway".
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
