## Wider literature that bears on the claim

1. **Sendai Framework terminology and the four-component chain.** UNDRR's definition treats monitoring, forecasting, risk assessment, communication and preparedness as one system, and says that failure in one component can fail the whole. This is the closest institutional analogue to Fig 3's dependency chain and the strongest external check on F2 in `01`.
2. **Local knowledge as an observing system.** The Hermans et al. review and the Balehegn et al. field study are from different methods (systematic review versus ethnographic fieldwork) and different regions. They agree that local observations exist independently of the forecast and are triangulated with it. They share the DRR evidence pool in the broad sense, so their agreement is one pool with two methods, not two fully independent witnesses.
3. **Alarm fatigue as channel decay.** Michels et al. is from clinical human factors, a genuinely low-overlap field. It predicts that a high false-alarm rate degrades later response even though the underlying hazard has not changed. That is the Fig 10 channel-decay shape in a different substrate.
4. **Quality versus value of an imperfect forecast.** Katz and Murphy provide the mathematical economics point: forecast quality and decision value are different quantities linked by a nonlinear curve. This is why "the forecast is still uncertain" does not settle "can harm fall".
5. **Vulnerability as a slot effect.** IPCC AR6 reports a 15-fold mortality difference between highly vulnerable and very low vulnerability regions for the same classes of hazard. That is the per-slot claim at population scale. It corroborates Fig 1 without relying on the framework.

No source opened for this solution conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The two DRR institutional sources are recorded as sharing a pool; the human-factors and economics sources are low overlap.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A note selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :60** "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." An audience size is a budget filter, never a witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask which link stops the chain in their setting and what would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask for a failure mode, not an endorsement. Stage 1 follows Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three public links. Their agreement after seeing the theory is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interior.

**Stage 3.** Offer the loop as a method to reuse. Each user enumerates the habitat of their own residual, states their own mode and writes their own refuter before running it. Fig 4 :22 permits methods to cross up; Fig 10 :43-46 forbids the seat from authoring verdicts or atoms.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. `01` records five ways it could fail. Ask for one of them first.
