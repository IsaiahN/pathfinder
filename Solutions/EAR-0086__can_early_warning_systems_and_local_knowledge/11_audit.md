## Independent domain census

I redid this census from the residual line alone before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency. The residual line I worked from was: harm from a climate hazard, indexed per community and hazard, under a forecast that remains uncertain.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: many slots, not one global number. Symmetry: asymmetric, since the cost of a miss differs from the cost of a false alarm and differs between exposed groups. Scale: local. A global error near zero is compatible with one live community slot, so the average is not a sufficient statistic.
- **Domain A, the forecasting frame.** Actors: models, agencies, warning messages. Currency: skill, probability, lead time, reach. Anchor: the hazard as it unfolds, which the frame does not control. Uncertainty belongs to this frame.
- **Domain B, the habitat and its local readings.** Actors: community, local institutions, indicators, terrain, infrastructure. Currency: contact with the residual and readings already returned. Anchor: harm outcomes and survived lineage. Capability lives here as agent-and-habitat.
- **Domain C, the translation compound.** Actors: both frames, communicators, decision-makers, outcome feedback. Currency: residuals, agreement, actions. Anchor: harm at the ground, never agreement.
- **Partition or union:** union. The four conditions (skill, dissemination, interpretability, capacity to act) overlap, so a single lever clears only one region. I reached this from Fig 9 :54-56 rather than from the primary's section 0.
- **Order by dependency:** perception, then a vocabulary of affordances, then planning, then learning from outcomes. Refuse the "uncertainty alone is the problem" premise before minting anything (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

My census matches the primary's three domains and union reading.
