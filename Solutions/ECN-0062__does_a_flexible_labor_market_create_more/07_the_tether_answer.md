# The Tether's answer: does flexibility create jobs or shift risk?

**Mode: `general` on refusing the either/or, `specified` on the shape that replaces it, `gap` on the empirical choice.** No measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which of two effects a flexibility intervention mainly produces: a higher count of jobs, or a transfer of income and employment risk from employers to workers. Restated in effect terms, what fails to resolve is what changes on the ground when hiring, dismissal and contract rules are loosened. The two offered predictions are not competing readings of one quantity. One is an ordering (more or fewer jobs), the other is a directed transfer (risk from one party to another). Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

The residual is indexed by slot. A national employment average can be near zero while a live risk residual sits on one contract form (Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state").

## 2. The shape

1. **Refuse the either/or; split the union.** The two outcomes are not a partition of one variable. The same lever changes hiring and contract form, so they overlap. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The answer must be two verdicts with two grounds, plus an account of the intersection.
2. **Never average the slots.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." "More jobs" is exactly the headline average that hides the risk signal.
3. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Flexibility can be job-supporting for one residual and risk-shifting for another; both can hold at once. The question's "or" is therefore false.
4. **The two effects carry different bonds.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The count is production; the transfer is subtraction plus a directed arrival. Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." The comparison bond ⋛ is refused here: Operators table :50 "a threshold relation between two quantities", and these are not two quantities of the same kind. The supporting derivation is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
5. **State the objective before the instrument.** Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Having terms for flexibility and measures of employment does not settle what would count as progress. The debate stalls at this middle link because it has no obvious instrument (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").
6. **Unbundle the term and enumerate the habitat.** "Flexibility" bundles dismissal rules, fixed-term contracts, agency work, on-call hours and dependent self-employment. Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Demand conditions, benefit systems, bargaining coverage and the legal framework are in contact with the residual and cannot be composed away.
7. **Range each frame against an anchor, not against agreement.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Most flexibility studies share the EPL evidence pool, so their agreement is weak (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Lower-overlap sources are needed.
8. **The empirical choice stays `gap`.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The figures give the shape; a country-specific employment effect needs imported data and a ground reading. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,".

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that flexibility has no effect on employment. The literature is mixed, not null (Rodgers 2007; World Bank 2024).
- Not that flexibility only shifts risk. Both effects can be present on different slots.
- Not a country estimate, a causal effect size, or a policy recommendation.
- Not that expert agreement settles anything. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- Not a derived order between the two effects. That is `gap` until an event study timestamps them.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was measured.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A per-slot, source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the ambiguity. Here:

- **Employment slot:** matched firm-level hiring and separation records, with contract form attached, in a defined jurisdiction and period. What is the change in net matches attributable to the rule change?
- **Risk slot:** administrative income and benefit records per worker, by contract form. Who bears the variance before and after?
- **Intersection:** the same worker-level panel linking the new match to the change in income variance. This is where the union's overlap is visible.
- **Order:** an event study that timestamps hiring and the transfer of variance (closes the `gap` in `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- **Standing refuters:** any of the six pre-registered conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` firing, especially a single quantity that is both count and incidence, or a fixed valence independent of residual.

The ground does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"). A pooled employment rate is a stale channel, not the anchor.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The either/or is malformed. A count of jobs and a transfer of risk are two quantities on two slots, produced by the same lever, and the same flexibility arrangement can help one and harm the other. The Tether therefore refuses to average them. It says the answer must be two verdicts with two grounds, read per contract and per worker rather than through a national average, with the objective stated before the instrument, flexibility unbundled by contract form, and the surrounding habitat enumerated. The count is a production relation, the transfer is a subtraction with a direction, and calling their comparison a threshold between two quantities is a type error. The figures give this shape and no country-level sign: whether flexibility actually raised employment or mainly moved risk in a given place and period is left `gap` until per-slot, source-discriminating data range against the ground. Agreement among economists is not such data.
