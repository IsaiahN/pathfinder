# Supporting derivation: are the two outcomes isomers, and what is their order?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`:

1. Are "more jobs" and "risk shifted" **isomers** under Fig 12, that is, the same atoms arranged differently, or are they different molecules that share only one atom?
2. What is the **order** between them, if any? Does the employment change precede the risk transfer, follow it, or are they one event read twice?

Order matters because Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." If the bond or the order changes, the term changes even when the ingredients do not.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the labor frame: hiring rules, dismissal rules, contract form, benefit coverage, income variance, employment count, job tenure. Bonds available: the seven (conjunction, disjunction, identity, sequence, production, subtraction, comparison) plus negation. No new atom is available without import. The anchor is measured per-slot outcomes, not agreement among economists. This is a partition question at the bond level and a union question at the outcome level, exactly as in `01`.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

As commonly written, the two outcomes are:

- `flexibility ⇒ more jobs` (production: a job that did not exist before)
- `flexibility − employer risk` plus a directed addition of that risk to the worker (subtraction: an operand's absence is the point)

These share only one atom, flexibility. Their other atoms differ (job count versus income variance), so under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers as written**. They are two different molecules built over one shared atom.

The stronger form is testable. Write both over the full shared atom set {hiring rules, dismissal rules, contract form, benefit coverage, income variance, employment count}. Then both outcomes are compositions of the same atoms with different bonds. Under that writing they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. This is the claim worth sending to a check.

Falsifier, written before the run: if either outcome can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading below stands instead.

### Q2 - What bond is each outcome?

Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before" with the test Fig 12 :77 "is there a B before A fires? No." A new match, a new employment spell, is production: before the firm hired, that job was absent. So the employment outcome carries ⇒.

Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent" with the test Fig 12 :83 "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes." The risk shift carries − on the employer side: the point is that the employer no longer bears the variance. On the worker side it is a directed arrival, which is why the valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).

The tempting bond is ⋛ comparison, "jobs versus risk". That is refused. Operators table :50 defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities." A count and an incidence are not two quantities of the same kind, so applying ⋛ here is a type error, the same strain recorded in `01` F4.

### Q3 - What is the order?

Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" is sequence, and sequence is ordered: Fig 12 :92 "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." If the risk transfer requires the employment change first (hire, then load the risk onto the new contract), the bond is →. If both arise from the same contract change with no dependency, the bond is conjunction (both hold at once) and the ordering is not licensed.

The world material does not settle this. Eichhorst et al. (2019) say the flexibility and the insecurity arise "at the same time"; Rodgers (2007) says the distributional effect is firmer than the aggregate one; neither establishes a temporal order. So the order is `gap`. Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Leaving the bond unlabelled is not neutral, it is an unpriced arrangement.

Falsifier: a per-contract event study that timestamps hiring and the transfer of income variance. If one consistently precedes the other, the `gap` closes and the bond is named; if they are simultaneous, conjunction is admitted.

### Q4 - Analogues that bear

- **Functional group.** Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Both outcomes can be written `employer -> contract form -> worker`. That shared substructure is a functional group; "fixed-term" or "on-call" is the qualifier. This is the weaker, safer claim if the isomer test fails.
- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A flexibility regime survives each hiring cycle that uses it; a one-off derogation does not. Treating them as the same term hides the difference across uses. `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Flexibility -> higher turnover -> greater worker risk -> political demand for more flexibility is a candidate positive loop. Whether it closes depends on its sign, which the literature does not settle (World Bank 2024 reports both reallocation benefits and dual-market costs). Recorded as open, not asserted.
- **Two currencies.** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Describing a reform is cheap; establishing its per-slot effect is expensive. Summing them hides the shortfall.
- **Recursion.** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A contract form whose effects are settled against the ground becomes an atom for the next institutional arrangement. This is why unbundling by form is not a merely semantic exercise.

## CONCLUSION

The two outcomes are **not isomers as usually written**; they are different molecules sharing the atom "flexibility". They become a genuine isomer claim when both are written over the full shared atom set, and that version has a stated falsifier. Their bonds differ (production for the job count, subtraction plus directed transfer for the risk), and the ⋛ comparison reading is a type error. Their order is `gap`: the sources say "at the same time" and do not timestamp the events, and under Fig 12 that unlabelled arrangement is a real omission, not a harmless simplification. The functional-group reading is the safe fallback.
