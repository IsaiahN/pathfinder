**Mode: `general` on the framing refusal, `specified` on the shape, `gap` on the empirical choice.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes material already in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the question it reports on. ## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, between two claims about labor-market flexibility. Claim J: it creates more jobs. Claim R: it mainly shifts risk from employers to workers. What each leads to: J treats a count as the objective and reads the effect through an employment average; R treats the distribution of variance as the objective and reads the effect through contract form. A third option, derived below, is that the question is a union compound that must be split into two slots with two grounds, and that the empirical choice between them stays `gap` until per-slot data settle it. The sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term before the run (`THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction; `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** What fails to resolve is the effect of flexibility on the ground. Arity: at least two distinct quantities, a count (ordering, spectrum-like) and a directed transfer (gradient-like, employer to worker). Symmetry: the transfer is ordered, so swapping operands changes the claim; the count is a comparison. Scale: micro (one firm's hiring, one worker's income) to macro (national employment rate). Fig 9 calls this a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, firms and employers.** Actors: employers, managers, HR. Currency: hiring and firing cost, adjustment speed, profit. Anchor: firm payroll and hiring decisions. Question type: part of the union.
- **Domain 2, workers and households.** Actors: workers, job seekers, families. Currency: wages, income variance, tenure, benefit coverage. Anchor: administrative employment spells and income records. Part of the union.
- **Domain 3, the matching system.** Actors: vacancies, job seekers, matches. Currency: job creation and destruction, unemployment and vacancy rates. Anchor: statistical agency counts. Part of the union.
- **Domain 4, rule makers.** Actors: legislators, regulators, courts. Currency: statutory flexibility, EPL strictness, benefits, active labor policy. Anchor: written law and the OECD EPL indicator. Part of the union.
- **Domain 5, research and verification.** Actors: labor economists, statisticians. Currency: estimates and identification strategies. Anchor: reproducible datasets and measured outcomes, never agreement. The frame that ranges the others against the ground.
- **Not a domain:** "flexibility" has no nameable anchor of its own. It is a bundle of arrangements inside D1 to D4, and its valence is relative to the residual.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception (EPL indices, employment statistics, income data exist) then vocabulary (contract forms) then objective (what counts as progress, the contested link) then planning (identification strategy) then learn and carry. Effort above the break cannot rescue a step whose input never arrives.

Strain test: ⋛ on "jobs versus risk" was tried and failed because comparison needs two quantities of the same kind; re-census moved it to different bond types. "Flexibility" as a domain failed the anchor test. The partition reading failed Fig 9 :54. Each is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6, not smoothed over.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures do not choose between "more jobs" and "shifted risk". They refuse the choice. The residual is a union compound spanning two slots, the national employment average is the wrong instrument, and the valence of flexibility is relative to the residual rather than fixed. The count is production, the transfer is subtraction with a direction, and ⋛ is a type error here. Both effects can hold at once. The empirical question of which dominates in a given jurisdiction and period is `gap`: it needs imported per-slot data and a ground reading. This verdict is `general` on the refusal and `specified` on the shape, and ungrounded throughout.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a theory instead of a split and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its falsifiers. If the split is wrong and the two outcomes are one slot, the whole packet is withdrawn.
- **undoable:** yes. The framing claim can be replaced by a single-slot claim without touching the world sources; the refuter list makes the replacement testable. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and no policy has been asserted, so there is nothing irreversible to unwind. A sent packet can be superseded by the corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A firm-worker panel that measures employment and income variance on the same slots would settle both halves; an event study that timestamps hiring and the transfer of variance would close the order `gap`; a demonstration that the two residuals are perfectly correlated would break the per-slot finding; a single quantity that is both count and incidence would break the split; a fixed valence independent of residual would break Fig 11. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
