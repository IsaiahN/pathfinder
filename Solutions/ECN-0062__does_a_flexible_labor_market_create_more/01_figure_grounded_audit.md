## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

The question offers two outcomes for one intervention:

> Flexible labor markets create more jobs, **or** mainly shift risk from employers to workers.

Restated as an effect: what fails to resolve is what a change in hiring and dismissal freedom, contract form and social protection actually does on the ground. Two predictions are offered. Prediction J: the count of jobs rises. Prediction R: the incidence of income and employment risk moves from the employer side to the worker side. The question asks us to choose one.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** at least two. A level or count (jobs) and a directed transfer (risk from employer to worker) are different quantities. "More jobs" is an ordering (spectrum: more versus less). "Risk shifted" is a directedness (gradient: from one party to another). Under Fig 12 these need different bonds, so they cannot be compared as one number.
- **Symmetry:** the transfer has a direction (employer to worker), so swapping operands changes the claim. The count is a comparison over an ordering. A single flat formula cannot hold both.
- **Scale:** micro (one firm's hiring decision, one worker's income stream) to macro (national employment rate). The residual at a single slot can be live while the aggregate is near zero.

**Domain census, written before choosing any figure line.** One line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 firms and employers | employers, managers, HR | hiring and firing cost, adjustment speed, profit | firm payroll and hiring decisions | part of a union with D2 to D4 |
| D2 workers and households | workers, job seekers, families | wages, income variance, tenure, benefit coverage | administrative employment spells and income records | part of the union |
| D3 the matching system | vacancies, job seekers, matches | job creation and destruction, unemployment and vacancy rates | statistical agency counts (OECD, Eurostat, BLS) | part of the union |
| D4 rule makers | legislators, regulators, courts | statutory flexibility, EPL strictness, benefits, ALMP | written law and the OECD EPL indicator | part of the union |
| D5 research and verification | labor economists, statisticians | estimates, identification strategies | reproducible datasets and measured outcomes | the frame that ranges the others against the ground |

"Flexibility" itself is **not** a domain. It has no anchor of its own; it is a bundle of contractual arrangements living inside D1 to D4. Its valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."). Treating it as a self-standing thing with a fixed sign was my first census error and is retracted in section 6.

**Partition or union.** The two outcomes are not a partition of one quantity (mutually exclusive labels on one variable), and they are not independent. The same flexibility lever that may change hiring also changes contract type and therefore risk incidence. The split lands on a **union**, so it must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (can we measure flexibility, jobs and risk? yes: EPL indices, employment statistics, administrative income data) then vocabulary (hiring, dismissal, fixed-term, agency work, on-call, self-employment) then objective (what counts as progress? this is where the either/or hides a disagreement) then planning (identification strategy) then learn and carry (name what worked, verify, carry forward). The chain usually breaks at the middle link, which has neither obvious tests nor obvious failures (Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."). That is exactly the popular debate: it argues about flexibility while leaving the objective unstated.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The either/or is a union compound that must be split, not one question with two competing answers (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The two outcomes do not compete as answers to one question. They are effects on different slots produced by the same lever. Asking which one is "main" is asking us to average two quantities that are not commensurable. Supported across two low-overlap passages of Fig 9 plus Fig 1 :19, so `triangulated`.

Refuter: show that a single measured quantity is simultaneously the count of jobs and the incidence of risk, so the two are one slot under two names. Then the split was wrong.

### F2 - Averaging across slots destroys the signal (general, single-source)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."

A national employment rate near zero residual can coexist with a live risk residual on a specific contract or worker. The headline "more jobs" is precisely the average the figure forbids as an instrument. World corroboration: Rodgers (2007) reports the evidence does not support higher employment from flexibility but does support an effect on the distribution of employment among groups; Eichhorst et al. (2019) and the ILO (2016) report risk moving to workers. `single-source` for the figure line, corroborated in world.

Refuter: a demonstration that employment and risk residuals are perfectly correlated across all slots, so averaging loses no signal.

### F3 - The same actor has different valence relative to different residuals (general, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Flexibility is not intrinsically job-creating or risk-shifting. It can be a benefactor to the employment-level residual in one configuration and a malfactor to the risk residual in another. This dissolves the either/or: both effects can hold at once on different slots. The ILO (2016) line that a defining characteristic of precariousness is that the worker bears the risks rather than the business is a world-side instance of a valence attached to a relation, not to a kind. `triangulated` across Fig 11 passages and world.

Refuter: a measured fixed valence of flexibility independent of the residual under measurement.

### F4 - The two outcomes are different bond types, so they cannot be compared as one quantity (specified, single-source)

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :101 "A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."

"More jobs" is a comparison over an ordering (spectrum-like, more or less). "Risk shifted" is a directed transfer (employer loses, worker gains a burden). Under Fig 12 these are different arrangements, and the flat ingredient "flexibility" records half of the story. The Operators table defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities"; "jobs" and "risk" are not two quantities of the same kind, so ⋛ is a strain here and is refused. The supporting derivation is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

Refuter: a single bond type that correctly expresses both effects without loss, which would collapse the isomer distinction.

### F5 - The objective link is where the question actually breaks (specified, single-source)

Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :25 "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests."

The debate has instruments for flexibility (the OECD EPL indicator) and for jobs (employment statistics), but the objective is contested: is the goal a higher count, or a fairer distribution of variance? Fig 3 says the link between having terms for what a situation affords and saying what counts as progress attracts the least attention. Refusing to state the objective is what keeps the either/or alive.

Refuter: a single stated objective under which both effects are measurable components, with an instrument for each.

### F6 - "Flexibility" must earn its keep or be unbundled (specified, triangulated)

Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Fig 5 :44 "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

"Flexibility" bundles individual dismissal rules, collective dismissal rules, fixed-term contracts, temporary agency work, on-call hours and dependent self-employment. The ILO (2016) and the World Bank (2024) report materially different effects by form, including dual labor markets. If one flat term hides those differences, it fails the bargain. The two currencies also matter: describing a reform is cheap in bits, finding out whether it paid is expensive in actions, and summing them hides the shortfall. `triangulated` (Fig 5 plus Fig 12 plus world).

Refuter: evidence that all forms have the same sign and magnitude of effect on both slots, so the bundle compresses without loss.

### F7 - The verdict needs an anchor, not agreement (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

The anchor is measured employment and measured risk outcomes, which do not move because an economist prefers a conclusion. Pro and anti camps must each range against that anchor separately. Rodgers (2007), the OECD (2025) and Eichhorst et al. (2019) largely agree that the aggregate employment effect is mixed and the distributional effect is firmer; because they draw on overlapping policy literature, that agreement is weaker than it looks (Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."). `triangulated`.

Refuter: a ground reading that changes its answer when a frame is persuaded, which would mean we had mistaken a frame for an anchor.

### F8 - Low overlap is required for the two halves to corroborate anything (general, triangulated)

Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Many flexibility studies use the same EPL datasets and the same cross-country panels, so their agreement is one pool talking. The genuinely low-overlap witnesses here are worker well-being (Green and Leeves, 2013), employment relations (Rubery et al., 2016) and political philosophy (Bieber and Moggia, 2021). Agreement across those and the econometric literature would mean more than agreement within the econometric literature. Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." The residual was described in section 0 before these frames were chosen.

Refuter: a demonstration that the low-overlap frames draw on the same underlying data as the EPL studies.

### F9 - The empirical either/or is unreachable from the figures (specified on shape, gap on the choice, single-source)

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :32 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The figures can settle the shape of a correct answer (split the union, measure per slot, treat valence as relative to the residual, keep the anchor outside the frame). They cannot settle whether flexibility raised net employment in a given jurisdiction in a given period. That needs an import from measured data and a ground reading. Reporting `gap` here is a verdict, not a silence. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,".

Refuter: a derivation from the figures alone that returns a country-specific employment effect without data. That would mean the closure contained more than we census.

### F10 - The macro claim carries a round trip gap (specified, single-source)

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Abstracting a macro slogan ("flexibility creates jobs") from micro evidence and then concretising it into a policy does not return the same thing. What drops out: per-slot risk incidence, contract-form heterogeneity, and the difference between turnover and reallocation. The gap is recorded qualitatively in `11_audit.md`. Treating the slogan as identical to the evidence would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error.

Refuter: a policy translation in which every micro constraint survives the trip, demonstrated with the same instrument at both scales.

### F11 - A reform that does not change contact changes nothing (specified, triangulated)

Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Seat Map :281 "An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."

A flexibility reform that only relabels contracts without changing actual hiring or income risk changes nothing on the ground. Enumerate the habitat rather than compose it: demand conditions, benefit systems, active labor market policy, bargaining coverage, the legal framework. The ILO (2016) and the World Bank (2024) both note that effects depend on the surrounding protections, which is a habitat claim, not a property of flexibility alone. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Refuter: a relabeling reform that moves measured outcomes with no change in contact, which would break the capability claim.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287 "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim (split into two slots, per-slot measurement, valence relative to the residual, empirical choice left to the ground) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single measured quantity is simultaneously job count and risk incidence, so the two outcomes are one slot under two names (breaks F1).
2. Employment and risk residuals are perfectly correlated across slots, so aggregation loses nothing (breaks F2).
3. Flexibility has a fixed valence independent of the residual at issue (breaks F3).
4. A ground reading shows, on one per-slot instrument, that flexibility changed employment with no change in risk incidence, or the reverse (then one half is settled and the question decomposes as predicted, so this confirms rather than refutes the split but ends the `gap`).
5. "Flexibility" explains all outcomes by saying nothing, with no differences among contract forms (breaks F6).
6. A derivation from the figure set alone returns a country-specific employment effect without imported data (breaks F9).

Until one is run, no part of the empirical claim may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Refuse the either/or. Answer in two slots with separate verdicts and separate grounds: (i) effect on employment level, (ii) effect on risk incidence. Keep the union at the intersection visible (F1).
2. Report per slot, not as a national average (F2).
3. State the objective before the instrument. "More jobs" and "less risk" are two objectives, not one (F5).
4. Unbundle flexibility by contract form and by the surrounding habitat (F6, F11).
5. Range each frame separately against measured outcomes and do not pool agreement from a shared evidence pool as corroboration (F7, F8).
6. Mark the empirical choice `gap` until a ground reading arrives; do not let a filter hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
7. Lead outreach with the residual, the split and the refuter, not with a theory (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").

## 7. Bottom line of the audit

The figure-grounded result is a refusal of the question as posed and a shape for any answer that replaces it. Split the union into two slots, measure each on its own ground, treat the valence of flexibility as relative to the residual, and keep the empirical either/or marked `gap` until data settles it. Nothing is averaged, and no agreement is counted as a witness. Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the refusal, `gap` on the choice.
