## The question as an effect

What is being decided is whether one intervention (more flexibility in hiring, dismissal and contract form) produces one effect or two. Option A says the count of jobs rises. Option B says a burden (income and employment risk) moves from the employer to the worker. An answer would look like a statement of which quantity changes, by how much, on which slot, measured against what anchor. The framework's first move is to refuse the either/or as a union compound and bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching (Formula, Direction).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the framing.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The outside frame must not hold the interiors of the derivation.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
