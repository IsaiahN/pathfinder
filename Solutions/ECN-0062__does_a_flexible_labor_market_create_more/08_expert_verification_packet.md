# Expert verification packet: the Tether on flexibility, jobs and risk

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labor-market specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the framing refusal, `specified` on the shape, `gap` on the empirical choice.** No measurement at the country level is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does flexibility create more jobs or shift risk" is a union compound, not one question with two competing answers. The count of jobs is an ordering; the transfer of risk is a directedness. They are effects on different slots, they can both hold at once, and the same flexibility arrangement can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. Any adequate answer therefore has two verdicts with two grounds, read per contract and per worker, with flexibility unbundled by contract form and the habitat enumerated. The figures return that shape and refuse the comparison of the two outcomes as one quantity; they do not return a country-level sign. The empirical choice is `gap` until per-slot, source-discriminating data settle it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The two outcomes are a union compound that must be split (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 9)

- **Statement:** the either/or is malformed. Where each rule keeps working on a different subset, the problem is several well-formed ones, not one hard one. Because the lever (flexibility) changes both hiring and contract form, the split is a union, and the intersection must be handled explicitly.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Also Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." and Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Refuter:** show that a single measured quantity is simultaneously the job count and the risk incidence, so the two are one slot under two names. Or show the two effects are independent (a partition), so no intersection exists to unbundle.

### C2 - Per-slot measurement and relative valence (framework-internal, checkable in Figures 1 and 11)

- **Statement:** a national employment average cannot carry the risk signal; valence is a relation to the residual, not a property of flexibility. Both effects can be true at once without contradiction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- **What to check against the literature:** Rodgers (2007) reports weak evidence on aggregate employment and stronger evidence on distribution across groups. Eichhorst et al. (2019) report that shifting risk to workers creates insecurity "at the same time as it provides employers with greater flexibility." Do those findings match the two-slot reading?
- **Refuter:** employment and risk residuals perfectly correlated across all slots (so averaging loses nothing), or a measured fixed valence of flexibility independent of which residual is at issue.

### C3 - Different bonds; ⋛ is a type error (framework-internal, checkable in Figure 12 and the Operators table)

- **Statement:** the count is production, a job that did not exist before; the transfer is subtraction on the employer side plus a directed arrival on the worker side. Comparing them with ⋛ is not licensed, because comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities of the same kind.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before" and Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent" with its test Fig 12 :83 "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Operators table :50 "a threshold relation between two quantities."
- **What to check:** is "a new job" genuinely a production (absent before the firm acted), and is the employer's shed risk genuinely a subtraction (absence is the point)? If both reduce to one bond type, the distinction collapses.
- **Refuter:** a single bond that expresses both effects without loss, or a valid ⋛ typing of count against incidence under a declared common unit.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that flexibility has no employment effect, and not that it always shifts risk. Both are open empirical questions here.
- Not a country estimate, a causal effect size, or a policy recommendation.
- Not that expert agreement settles either slot. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a derived order between job creation and risk transfer. That is `gap` with a stated instrument in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not a claim that the two outcomes are strict isomers as usually written. They share one atom, not a full atom set; the stronger isomer form is stated and falsifiable in `06`.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading of green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the ambiguity. Here:

1. **Employment slot:** firm-level hiring and separation records with contract form attached, in a defined jurisdiction and period.
2. **Risk slot:** worker-level income and benefit records by contract form, measuring who bears the variance before and after the reform.
3. **Intersection:** the same panel linking the new match to the change in income variance.
4. **Order:** an event study timestamping hiring and the transfer of variance.
5. **Refuters:** any of the six in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :5, :54 and :57 (three short passages). Is the question a union compound that should be split, or one slot under two names?
2. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 11 :49, :50. Check C2 against Rodgers (2007) and Eichhorst et al. (2019): does the two-slot, relative-valence reading fit the evidence you know?
3. Read Fig 12 :76, :82, :83, :117 and Operators table :50. Is the bond typing in C3 right, and is the ⋛ refusal correct?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the employment effect from the risk effect on the same workers, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
