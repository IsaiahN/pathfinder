**Subject:** One checkable output on the hierarchy problem, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **First, a correction to the question as usually posed.** The hierarchy problem is often stated as "why is the Higgs particle mass so small compared with the Planck mass". Matt Strassler puts it precisely: "This is incorrect. The problem is with how big the non-zero Higgs field is." Wikipedia adds: "The problem cannot even be formulated in the strict context of the Standard Model, for the Higgs mass cannot be calculated." So the residual is not a measured number that a theory predicts badly. It is that the theory cannot predict it, and the mass-squared parameter is quadratically sensitive to the cutoff.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** three conditions are bundled in this question and must be separated. (1) The mass-squared parameter is radiatively unstable. (2) The weak scale and the Planck scale are about 10¹⁷ apart. (3) The Standard Model has no derivation of the scale. From the framework's closure rule it follows that condition (3) cannot be closed by composing more Standard Model diagrams: the wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. A resolution therefore has the shape of an **import**, not a bigger calculation. The framework gives the shape that import must have: it must explain the observed gap, arrive with provenance, and be settled by an instrument. It does **not** name the import. Supersymmetry, extra dimensions, compositeness and anthropic selection are candidates from the physics side, not outputs of the derivation.
>
> There is a second live branch. The residual has persisted and not trended down, and the expected new phenomena have not appeared at the scales where they were expected. The framework has a bin for exactly that: the term held may be the wrong one, and what is owed is a **refusal**, not another theory. The candidate for refusal is naturalness as a demand on theory choice. I am not asserting that nature is or is not natural. I am recording that the prior is refusable, with a test below.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I know which mechanism is right; that naturalness is false; that the hierarchy problem is solved; or that anything here is `grounded`. No measurement at the relevant scale is claimed.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. Derive the weak scale by composing Standard Model atoms, with no imported primitive and no fine-tuned input.
> 2. Show that a proposed import needs no source gate and no shadow test and is still an answer.
> 3. Find a stabilising mechanism at accessible scales so the residual starts to trend down (this kills the refusal branch).
> 4. Show that naturalness has been settled by an instrument rather than assumed by a community.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
