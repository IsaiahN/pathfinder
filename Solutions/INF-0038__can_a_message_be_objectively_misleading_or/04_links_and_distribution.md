## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Definition disagreements are a live problem in the field itself.** The HKS survey (2023) reports that 43% of experts chose "false and misleading information" while 7% chose "misleading information" alone, and that qualitative and quantitative researchers differ on intentionality. This is corroboration that the property is not a single settled object, not a second witness to the figure verdict.
- **True can mislead.** The Nature Medicine review's Chicago Tribune headline is a real case where the events were true in isolation and the report misled. This is the empirical counterpart of Fig 12's "same atoms, different bond, different substance".
- **The law already operationalises the receiver.** The FTC test fixes a reasonable consumer in context and, for targeted audiences, a reasonable member of that group. It is an instance of the shape the figures predict, arrived at in a different closure. It is an *instance*, not a derivation by the Tether.
- **Pragmatics separates saying from misleading.** Wayne Davis's SEP entry shows that a speaker can mislead while making no false statement, because implicature depends on context. Same shape, different evidence pool.
- **Animal signalling gets the same grammar without mental states.** Searcy and Nowicki define deception functionally by the receiver's response and the state of the world. This is the lowest-overlap witness in the set.
- **No conflict triggered.** None of the world sources contradicts the figure verdict. All are secondary and are reported as corroboration only. Where they share the misinformation-research pool (HKS and Nature Medicine), their agreement is one witness, not two.
