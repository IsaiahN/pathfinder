## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29-30/:36-38, Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 9 :4/:32, Fig 12 :94-96, Fig 10 :47/:49.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand used in `07` and `09`: "misleadingness is a receiver-indexed relation over a union of accuracy and uptake; declared definitions are conventions."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** an operational form, e.g. the FTC test: likely to mislead consumers acting reasonably under the circumstances, material, evaluated in context.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- which authority fixes the reference audience (a convention, not a figure);
- which decision counts as material (a legal threshold, not a figure term);
- the threshold for "likely" (an evidentiary standard, not a figure term);
- the source of the underlying facts W (a measurement or a fact-check, not a figure term).

One thing survives cleanly: the verdict cannot be a property of the message alone. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists for a conceptual claim; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "a metric is the condensed form of the verdict" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a stated gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` now records.
