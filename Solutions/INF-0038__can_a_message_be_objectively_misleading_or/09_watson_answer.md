## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether "misleading" is a property of a message alone or a relation to a receiver and context. Option A: it is objective and message-borne, so a message-only metric is sufficient. Option B: it is receiver-indexed, so any operationalisation must declare a reference audience and context. What each leads to: A averages over receivers and mistakes a declaration for a ground; B keeps the objective accuracy condition intact while locating the rest of the property in a union compound with its own split and its own instruments.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** A message m, world facts W, and a receiver with prior b in context c. Arity: 3, directed (m acts on the receiver), so not symmetric under swapping message and receiver. Scale: token/arrangement level, receiver belief level, adjudication level. Fig 9 names this a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, arrangement and content.** Actors: tokens, bonds, the message as used. Currency: what m conveys in its context. Anchor: the message and its context of use, a fixable object but not the ground for the whole question. Question type: partition of which arrangements carry which content.
- **Domain 2, receiver uptake.** Actors: audience with prior b, context c. Currency: belief update and inference. Anchor: the receiver's belief state ranged against W; human judgment is legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38). Question type: partition by receiver slot.
- **Domain 3, adjudication.** Actors: fact-checkers, regulators, the reasonable consumer. Currency: verdicts of misleadingness. Anchor: ground truth plus a declared standard. Question type: a convention that must not decide the outcome (Fig 10 :47).
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The accuracy condition and the uptake condition overlap in the case called misleading. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :4).
- **Order by dependency.** Fix what m conveys (D1), then assess uptake (D2), then adjudicate (D3). Refuse the term "objectively misleading" before minting a replacement metric (Formula step 2). Describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
- **Habitat (Fig 11).** Enumerated, not composed: speaker, message, platform, receiver, fact-checker, regulator, reasonable-consumer standard, underlying facts, context, prior, source credibility, repetition, correction relations.

Strain test: Fig 2 :37 could be strained to cover all messages; re-census restricts it to questions constitutively about people, which this is. Fig 10 :18 could be strained to deny all objective content; re-census restricts it to declared meanings (conventions). No strain survives re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**B, with a qualification.** A message cannot be misleading on its own. Misleadingness is a receiver-indexed relation over a union of an objective accuracy condition and a context-bound uptake condition. A message-only verdict fails the anchor test; a declared definition of "objectively misleading" is a convention until it names its reference audience. The objective component is real and fact-checkable, but it does not determine the whole property, so the correct claim is relational, not arbitrary. The framework returns that shape with four pre-registered refuters and does not name a correct audience or threshold. Mode `general`/`specified`, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The verdict splits the property and states four refuters, so an error can be found rather than hidden. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a fully built framework before they record a verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn. Adding a reference-audience operationalisation, or an invariance result, changes the verdict without deleting the derivation. What is recorded only grows; what is reachable is derived, so a refusal adds a term and does not erase one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Specifically: (1) a message-only metric invariant across independent audiences that still tracks belief divergence or harm (refuter 1); (2) identical verdicts across audiences sharing no evidence (refuter 2); (3) a demonstration that valence is carried by the message across residuals (refuter 3); or (4) a demonstration that falsity alone determines the verdict and the union collapses (refuter 4). Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
