# The Tether's answer: can a message be objectively misleading?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the operationalisation prediction. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A message m, facts W, and a receiver with prior b in context c. The question asks whether "misleading" is anchored in m alone or indexed to the receiver. The two leading options lead to different practices:

- **Option A (objective property of m):** build a message-only metric, fact-check the literal proposition, and label. This averages over receivers.
- **Option B (receiver-indexed relation):** split the property, fix a reference audience explicitly when operationalising, and test the verdict against independent receivers.

## 2. The shape

1. **A message-only "misleadingness" fails the anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Change the receiver or context and the verdict moves. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it"*; this question is about a receiver, so the receiver is the legitimate anchor, ranged against the facts.
2. **Valence is a relation, not a property carried.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* The same message misleads one audience and informs another.
3. **The property is a union compound, not one hard thing.** Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Condition one is factual accuracy (objective). Condition two is uptake (receiver-indexed). "Misleading" is their compound.
4. **The arrangement carries the content.** Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* A token-level or truth-value-level record misses the difference between false content and true content under a misleading implicature.
5. **A declared definition is a convention, not a ground.** Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* "Objectively misleading" is only coherent after the implicit reference audience is declared.
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a standard.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* It cannot name the right audience or threshold.

## 3. The answer, stated plainly

**B, with a qualification.** Misleadingness always depends on an audience and context. There is an objective component (whether the content matches the facts), and it is properly fact-checkable, but it does not by itself determine the whole property. "Objectively misleading" is a shorthand that works only when a reference audience and context have been fixed and declared; without that, it is a convention pretending to be a measurement.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that misleadingness is arbitrary or merely subjective. One of its two conditions is objective.
- Not that content-based measures are useless. They settle condition one; they just do not settle the compound.
- Not that the Tether names the correct "reasonable consumer" or threshold.
- Not that a committee declaration grounds a definition.
- Not that the field's definitional disagreement proves the conceptual claim. That disagreement is one evidence pool and is corroboration only.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles a conceptual claim here.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color reading *"cannot comment on the source"* and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Here:

1. **Invariance test:** a metric of misleadingness that does not move when receiver and context change, yet still tracks actual belief divergence or harm across independent audiences. This is refuter 1 in `01` §4.
2. **Independence test:** the same content, facts and context, same verdict across audiences sharing no evidence (Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"*). This is refuter 2.
3. **Discriminator test:** an instrument that distinguishes false content, true-but-misleading implicature, de-contextualisation and receiver-prior effects, so the source of the reading is identified rather than guessed.
4. **Collapse test:** demonstrate that factual falsity alone determines the misleading verdict, dissolving the union (Fig 9 :4). This is refuter 4.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The derivation stops where the instruments cannot distinguish among sources of the same reading, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Underivable in principle and unreached at this budget are different facts with different instructions; no import against a budget limit is warranted here.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A message cannot be misleading on its own. The Tether's answer is that misleadingness is a relation among the message, the facts and a receiver in a context: the accuracy of the content is objective and fact-checkable, but whether the message misleads depends on who reads it and in what circumstances. The same true headline can mislead one audience and be read correctly by another, so a verdict that moves with the receiver fails the anchor test. The two conditions form a union that has to be split before it is measured, and the difference between a false claim and a true claim with a misleading implication is a difference of arrangement, not of ingredients. "Objectively misleading" is therefore only coherent once a reference audience has been declared, and a declared definition does not ground itself. What would settle this is a discriminating instrument, not a unanimous panel: a measure that stays invariant across independent audiences while still tracking real belief divergence would refute the claim. No such measure is held here, so the answer is given as a shape with its refuter, not as a verdict.

## 8. Standing falsifiers (pre-registered, copied from `01` §4)

1. A message-only metric invariant under receiver and context changes that still tracks the phenomenon and fixes no hidden reference audience.
2. The same content, facts and context yielding the same verdict across audiences sharing no evidence.
3. A message's misleading valence shown to be invariant across receivers, so Fig 11 :49-51 fails.
4. Factual falsity alone shown to determine the misleading verdict, so the union collapses.

If any fires, the answer is withdrawn. Until one is run, this stays `general`/`specified`, candidate.
