**Subject:** One checkable claim about "objectively misleading", and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about misinformation measurement that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the operationalisation, not `grounded`):** "misleadingness" is not a property carried by a message. It is a relation between a message, the facts, and a receiver in a context. The factual-accuracy condition is objective and fact-checkable; the uptake condition is receiver-indexed. Together they form a union compound. So a message-only metric of "objective misleadingness" fails a straightforward test: the verdict moves when you change the receiver or the context, and a thing that moves when you push on it is not an anchor.
>
> This leaves an objective component intact. A true or false relation between content and the world is measurable. What fails is the idea that this component alone yields "misleading": the same literally true headline misleads one audience and is read correctly by another who knows the timing, and the same words under a different arrangement convey something different.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that misleadingness is arbitrary, that no content-based measure is useful, that the framework can name which audience is the right one, or that a committee's definition grounds itself. A declared definition is a convention; it becomes a verdict the moment it decides an outcome unless it declares the reference audience it is using.
>
> **What would refute this, written before I wrote to you:**
> 1. a measure of misleadingness invariant under changes of receiver and context that still tracks actual belief divergence or harm, without fixing a hidden reference audience;
> 2. the same content, facts and context producing the same misleading verdict across audiences that share no evidence;
> 3. a demonstration that a message's misleading valence is carried by the message across audiences, rather than being relative to the receiver;
> 4. a demonstration that factual falsity alone fully determines the misleading verdict, so the two conditions are one.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are three sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - [Figures text with line numbers (local framework copy)](local: C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md)
> - [Canonical figures README (local)](local: C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\FIGURES_README_canonical.md)

>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
