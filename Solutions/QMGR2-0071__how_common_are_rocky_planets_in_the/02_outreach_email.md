# Outreach draft: check the shape of the claim before deciding whether the framework matters

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output cannot be defended.

---

**Subject:** Would you check one claim about eta-Earth before we talk about the framework behind it?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structural rule, `specified` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the question "how common are rocky planets in the habitable zones of different kinds of stars?" does not have one number as its answer. It has a table. The residual is indexed by star kind, not aggregated, and the framework's rule is that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. So the answer form is one occurrence rate per star-kind slot (FGK, early M, mid to late M and so on), with the definition of "rocky", the definition of the habitable zone, and the instrument that measured the rate attached to each cell. The question is also a union of three conditions, rocky AND in the habitable zone AND counted per host kind, so those conditions must be unbundled into columns before any number is compared. None of this comes from measurement; it is the shape an answer must have.
>
> **What the framework does not say:** it does not say the value of any cell. It has no stellar luminosity function, no completeness map and no climate model in its closure. The number has to be imported from the measurement literature, with provenance and error bars. When a mission baseline adopts a working value for planning, that is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. If you were told the framework returns a number, that is wrong.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a single aggregate rate preserves everything needed for both mission design and a physical claim about star type;
> 2. the accepted rate is fixed by consensus or a baseline rather than by a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it;
> 3. two analyses offered as independent corroboration turn out to draw on one catalog and one pipeline with no separate derivation;
> 4. a bin with zero detections is reported as a zero population while its completeness is unmeasured;
> 5. two rates with different radius or habitable-zone definitions are compared as if they were the same quantity.
>
> I wrote these down before contacting you. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public source links and the figures. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Distribution notes

- One recipient at a time. No mail merge, no shared thread, no list of people selected for likely agreement (Fig 2 :40).
- Record the verdict on stage 1 **before** any theory or link is shown. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- The response this wants is a correction on shape or a named failure class, not an endorsement.
