## Wider literature that bears on the question (read as summarized by the 2025 review, not independently opened in full)

These are cited because the opened review records their role and numbers. They are marked in the ledger as read via the review, not as separately opened primary pages.

- **Bryson et al. 2021**, final Kepler team DR25 result: conservative HZ occurrence between 0.37 and 0.60, optimistic between 0.58 and 0.88 planets per star for Sun-like stars. The review reports these bounds and the reason they are wide (few detections).
- **SAG13** (NASA Study Analysis Group 13, 2017): consolidated η⊕ near 0.6 for mission yield planning. A budget input, not a ground verdict.
- **Pascucci, Mulders and Lopez 2019**, ApJL 883:L15: excluding short-period planets, many possibly stripped cores, drops η⊕ by factors of about 4 to 8. This is why the "rocky" condition must be unbundled explicitly (F7).
- **Dressing and Charbonneau 2015**: early M-dwarf estimate of about 15.8 percent conservative and 24.3 percent optimistic; Bergsten et al. 2023 show the underlying sample changed after Gaia (F6).
- **Hsu et al. 2019/2020, Kunimoto and Matthews 2020, Bergsten et al. 2022**: named in the review's comparison figure; their disagreement is the evidence that this is a live residual, not a settled slot.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described what they would count as a failure sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see in a per-star-kind rate table before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* If independence is bought, buy it across instruments (transit, radial velocity, imaging), not across press summaries of the same Kepler result.
- **Fig 9 :60** *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A mission baseline or a consensus value is a filter.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which definition of η⊕ they use; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, before any theory:** the shape claim (per star-kind slot, union unbundled, number imported) and the pre-registered refuter. Record their verdict here.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious after recording a verdict, send the three links above. Their later agreement is never counted as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If a reviewer shows that a single aggregate rate is sufficient, or that the number can be derived from the figures, the shape claim is withdrawn and nothing is sent onward.
