# Solution QMGR2-0071: rocky planets in habitable zones, by star type

**Question:** How common are rocky planets in the potentially habitable zones of different kinds of stars?

**Source:** QM & GR Questions.txt line 232

**Section:** H. Exoplanets and the search for life

**Mode:** `general` on the structural rule (an occurrence rate is indexed per star-type slot and settled by the ground, never by agreement), `specified` on the shape claims derived below, `grounded` only on the numbers reported by the world sources, which are measurements with large credible intervals. This folder is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the numeric question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The residual in effect terms

A mission planner or an astronomer needs a number of rocky habitable-zone planets **per star**, reported separately for each kind of star (FGK dwarfs, early M, mid to late M, and so on). Option A collapses the kinds into one aggregate "how common" figure. Option B keeps one rate per slot, names the instrument that measured it, and lets the ground settle each slot. A hides a live signal in an average (Fig 1 :22). B leaves a checkable, falsifiable statement. The figures decide between A and B. They do **not** decide the value of any rate. That is imported from measurements, with provenance.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers. Public institutional forms, registration pages and journal submission systems only.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- No bulk-send plan, and no claim that agreement among analysts is a second witness. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- No claim that this derivation picks a number. The figures give a shape and a checking discipline. The number comes from the ground, reported by the cited literature with its uncertainties intact.
