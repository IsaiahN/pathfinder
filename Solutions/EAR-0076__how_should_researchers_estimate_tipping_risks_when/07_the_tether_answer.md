# The Tether's answer: estimating tipping risk under short observations and incomplete geology

**Mode: `general` on the method shape, `specified` on the five falsifiable predictions. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No measurement settles any specific component's risk here.

## 1. The residual it answers

A researcher must decide whether an Earth-system component carries a tipping risk and how large it is, holding a short observational series and an incomplete geological record. The failure has arity two (short observations AND incomplete geology), so it is a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Its anchor is the component's future behaviour, which no frame can move. With short data the frame cannot distinguish depth (the risk is there but unresolved) from genuine absence (no risk), and the figures say the two look the same from inside (Fig 6 :27).

## 2. The shape

1. **Report per component, never as a global average.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Each tipping element is a slot; the one with live positive mass is the one that matters.
2. **Treat the short record as a budget gap, not evidence of low risk.** Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* The remedy is more search in the same closure, not a new tipping theory. Importing an atom against a budget limit "adds an atom that was not needed". And Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
3. **Triangulate across low-overlap evidence in proportion to irreversibility.** Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Paleo evidence, instrumental monitoring, process models and robust decision methods must be genuinely separate frames. Agreement among studies reusing one proxy network is one witness.
4. **Describe the effect before choosing the indicator, and pre-register the refuter.** Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*, and Fig 5 :65 *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."* Write down what would count as a tipping point and what observation would retract the estimate before running CSD or any other method.
5. **Sharpen instruments already returning something.** Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* For short records: higher-frequency monitoring, additional proxies, spatial indicators where temporal ones are too short, reanalysis of existing cores. Keep SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD as three distinct questions (Formula step 7).
6. **Measure the round-trip gap, and label the mode.** Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap between the method shorthand and the headline number is the honest report of what was dropped. Nothing is `grounded` until the ground pays.
7. **Where the ground cannot be reached, decide robustly rather than wait.** Fig 9 :42 *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."* When it does not decide in time, the decision field (read this session) supplies robust rather than optimal strategies. This is a separate loop, not a correction to the estimate.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a tipping probability. The framework returns a shape, not a name (canonical README; Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that a clean short record proves low risk. Fig 8 :39 forbids the inference.
- Not that early-warning indicators uniquely identify a bifurcation. Rietkerk et al. 2025 and Lenton et al. 2024, both opened this session, document non-tipping resilience loss.
- Not that components can be averaged for policy convenience. A filter is not a witness (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not `grounded`. No Planck-scale-style instrument, here no source-discriminating observation, was run.
- Not that the world literature corroborates because it agrees. Most of it is one evidence pool (the tipping-points community); the decision-science sources are the low-overlap witness.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was *"proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a rising variance or autocorrelation trend in a short record come from an approaching threshold, or from non-tipping resilience loss or non-stationary forcing? A discriminating observation would name the source.
- Does a component with a century to millennial response show any resolvable trend in a 40-year record at all? If nothing registers at any resolution, Fig 6 :34 *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from"* applies, and the honest answer is unreachable, not zero risk.
- Do two frames that share no proxy network reach the same per-component estimate separately? That is Fig 2 :31-32 independence, not pooled agreement.
- Standing falsifiers from §4 of `01`: a unique probability from short data; aggregation that preserves the slot signal; corroboration from a shared pool; a trend that uniquely identifies a bifurcation; a lossless headline number.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures forbid treating a clean short record as absence. The world sources independently show the timescale mismatch (Lenton et al. 2024), the ambiguity of EWS (Rietkerk et al. 2025), the recovery of meaningful signals under non-ideal conditions with rigorous assumption testing (Chapman et al. 2026), and the decision field's methods for acting while uncertain (IPCC AR6 WGII Ch17). None settles a specific component here. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a tipping probability, and it does not claim a short clean record proves low risk. It says that risk must be estimated per component, never averaged into one number; that a short observation window and a sparse geological record are a budget gap, not evidence of absence, so the remedy is more search and more independent evidence rather than a new theory; that the irreversibility of tipping is exactly why independent paleo, observational, model and decision frames must be bought and kept separate, since agreement from a shared proxy pool is one witness; that the criterion for a tipping point and the refuter must be written down before the indicator is chosen; and that the gap between a neat headline number and the messy method must be published rather than hidden. What it offers is the shape any defensible estimate must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The estimate stays `candidate` until the ground, which is the system's future behaviour, settles it.
