## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual), Fig 8 :39-43 (no proof of absence; budget versus underivability), Fig 2 :40-42 (independence priced by cost), Fig 6 :32-34 (instrument extension), Fig 4 :30 (round trip).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`: estimate per component, treat scarcity as a budget gap, triangulate, pre-register, measure the gap, stay `candidate`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete risk report for one component, for example a headline "probability of AMOC tipping this century" with a confidence interval.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** collapses when a policy audience asks for one number;
- the **budget/absence distinction** disappears when a confidence interval is read as absence of risk;
- the **independence requirement** is lost when several studies share one proxy network and are counted as separate lines;
- the **round-trip gap itself** is dropped when the headline is written, because publishing the dropped constraints is exactly what the shorthand demands and the headline omits.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the refusal to treat a clean short record as proof of low risk, and the requirement to pre-register the tipping criterion. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the method condensed into a risk score" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
