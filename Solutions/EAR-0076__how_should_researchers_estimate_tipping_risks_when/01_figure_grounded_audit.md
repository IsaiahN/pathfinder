## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A researcher must decide whether a given Earth-system component carries a tipping risk and how large that risk is. What they hold: a short observational series (decades), an incomplete geological or paleo record, a family of dynamical-systems models, and generic resilience indicators. What fails to resolve: whether the absence of a warning signal means low risk or merely an observation window too short to see one, and whether a measured loss of resilience is an approaching threshold or a non-tipping change. The anchor that would settle it is the component's actual future trajectory (does it tip, when, at what forcing), which cannot be pushed experimentally (the neighbouring question "How can researchers estimate thresholds in systems that cannot be experimentally pushed through them?" is question 72) and does not move when any frame changes its mind.

**Arity / symmetry / scale of R.** Arity: two conditions, short observations AND incomplete geology, so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*, Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Symmetry: the quantity at issue is a threshold relation, which is ordered, not a symmetric join (Operators table: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). Scale: instrumental decades versus geological millennia, first order inside a component versus second order across cascading components. The mismatch of scales is the defect, not a footnote.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction").** Before searching, bind the sought-for shape: a risk estimate that is (a) per component, (b) mode-labelled `specified`/`candidate`, (c) triangulated across at least three low-overlap evidence streams, (d) accompanied by a written refuter, and (e) explicitly silent where the ground has not settled. That shape is the thing that accrues a residual like any other term. Without this binding, the preference for "a number" is unbound, predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it (Fig 5 :62-63; read in the essay, see `05`).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | no anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 observation / instrument frame | satellites, proxies, ice cores, lake sediments, monitoring networks | observations, variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, recovery rate | the component's actual state, only partially reachable; no pushable experiment | anchored, but thin |

| D3 decision / verification frame | researchers, reviewers, risk assessors, decision makers | risk estimates, agreement, residuals | future system behaviour, never agreement | anchored |

**Partition or union.** The two scarcity conditions overlap (short instrumental records and sparse paleo evidence share the "small sample" condition) and the question also unions estimation with decision under deep uncertainty. This is a union, so it is unbundled before search: (i) is a tipping mechanism present at all, (ii) is the signal in hand adequate, (iii) what decision follows if it stays unresolved. Each has its own loop and its own ground. The word "tipping" in a short record is a label applied after the fact by a frame, not a standing kind.

**Dependency order (Fig 3, :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).** Describe R in effect terms → SEARCHED (is the signal already in the data and unfound?) → INWARD (extend an instrument already returning something) and OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs), never read against each other → settle at the ground → decide robustly. Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* is the test for which link the chain stops at.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** An early reading tried to force Fig 6 :32 (instrument built from a worse instrument) onto the decision problem in D3. That is a strain: the line governs instrument extension in D1, not robustness in D3. Re-census sent it to D1. The decision half is governed by Fig 2 :42 and Fig 9 :60. ## 3. Findings F1..F10

### F1 — The governing distinction: unreachable at this budget, not underivable in principle

Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* And: *"the remedy is more search, not another atom. Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed"*. Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*

Short observations and incomplete geology are primarily a **budget** failure, so the remedy is more search in the same closure (longer series, more proxies, reanalysis), not a new theory of tipping. A new atom imported against a budget limit "costs more than starting from primitives" (Fig 8 :43). Caveat, stated honestly: from inside the frame a genuine absence of tipping looks identical to depth. The figures do not let us declare which without more contact. Mode `general`, **triangulated** (Fig 8 and Fig 6, independent routes).

**Refuter:** show a short record plus sparse paleo record that licenses a unique tipping probability without further data. If that is possible, the residual is settled and this finding fails.

### F2 — Absence of a signal is not evidence of absence of risk

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

A clean series with no rising variance or autocorrelation, over 40 years, does not close the question for a system with a 1,000-year response (the observed timescale mismatch is documented in the world sources below). No Earth component is a sealed room. Mode `general`, **triangulated**.

**Refuter:** produce a sealed-room case in Earth systems where a finite search exhausts the space and absence is proved. The figures say such a room cannot be a frame; a genuine one would break the claim.

### F3 — Keep the residual per component; never aggregate to one number

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Symbols table: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*

Each tipping element (Greenland ice sheet, AMOC, Amazon, and so on) is a slot. A single global "tipping risk" average hides the one component with positive live mass. Mode `general`, **triangulated** (Fig 1 and Symbols table, and the per-element structure of the world literature).

**Refuter:** show that aggregation preserves the per-slot signal, that is, that a global average retains the information needed to locate where to look. If it does, the average is not destructive and this finding fails.

### F4 — Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

Irreversible change makes the cost of being wrong high, so the estimate must draw on genuinely low-overlap frames: paleo evidence, instrumental monitoring, process models, and where appropriate decision analysis. Agreement among studies that reuse the same proxy network is one witness. Mode `general`, **single-source** for the price rule (Fig 2), corroborated from a low-overlap field (decision science, see `09`).

**Refuter:** show that two frames sharing the same evidence pool provide independent corroboration of a risk verdict. Fig 2 :18-21 defines why they do not; a demonstration otherwise breaks the finding.

### F5 — Describe the residual before choosing the method

Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

If a researcher starts with "run a CSD analysis" and then looks at the data, any dataset will seem to fit the method. The effect to describe first is: "a relatively short, possibly non-stationary record from a component with a poorly constrained threshold, where resilience loss may have causes other than tipping." Only then pick the indicator. Mode `specified`, **triangulated**.

**Refuter:** show a selection procedure where choosing the frame first does not bias which datasets appear to fit. The figures and the tacit-knowledge essay both deny this; a counter-example breaks it.

### F6 — Extend an instrument already returning something; do not specify one from a description

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :34: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

For short records the practical route is not "invent a perfect early-warning sensor". It is to sharpen what already registers: higher-frequency monitoring, additional proxies, spatial indicators where temporal ones are too short, and reanalysis of existing cores. Mode `specified`, **single-source** for the instrument rule (Fig 6), corroborated by the paleo-CSD study in `09`.

**Refuter:** specify, from a description alone, a working instrument for a quantity that has never registered at any resolution. Fig 6 :34 says none exists; producing one breaks the finding.

### F7 — Take the round trip; measure the gap R_T between method shorthand and concrete claim

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.

A method shorthand ("rising autocorrelation means tipping risk") compressed into a report and then cashed out as a concrete probability loses constraints on the way down. The loss is the measurement, not an error to hide (Fig 4 :31 *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*). Mode `general`, **triangulated** (Fig 4, Formula step 6, and the tacit-knowledge essay's round-trip passage).

**Refuter:** show a compression of the method into a headline risk number that loses nothing (R_T = 0). If a lossless shorthand exists, the dead-reckoning warning does not apply.

### F8 — Bind the sought-for shape and pre-register the refuter

Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."* `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."*

Write down before analysis what would count as a tipping point, what would count as a stable trend, and what observation would force a retraction. Otherwise the frame will refuse the outcome it did not want rather than the belief it holds (Fig 5 :63-64). Mode `specified`, **triangulated**.

**Refuter:** show a frame that certifies its own direction without an ordinary term for the sought shape. Fig 5 :67 calls this a limit; a working self-certification breaks it.

### F9 — Split the union before searching; use filters for budget only

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

"Short observations", "incomplete geology", and "how to decide" are three conditions. A single lever that clears one leaves the intersections standing. A method ranking (tier, tractability, data availability) selects what gets tried first and says nothing about whether the risk estimate is true. Mode `general`, **single-source** for the split rule (Fig 9); the filter/witness rule is corroborated by the ranking plan's own use.

**Refuter:** show that the three conditions are one well-formed question whose difficulty shrinks monotonically with effort. Fig 9 :4 denies this; evidence otherwise breaks the finding.

### F10 — The ground settles; settlement is necessary and not sufficient

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 5 :69: *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."*

The anchor here is future behaviour, which no frame can move. We cannot run the experiment, so no estimate in this folder is `grounded`. Even if one component is settled, that does not license composing over it at the cascade level without checking the composition. Mode `general`, **triangulated**.

**Refuter:** move the anchor by agreement among frames and show the risk estimate changes with it. If that succeeds, the thing was a frame, not a ground, and the finding breaks.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule quoted in the exemplar: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The method shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A short record plus sparse paleo record, with no further data, licenses a unique tipping probability. (Breaks F1, F2.)
2. Aggregating risk across components preserves the per-slot signal needed to decide where to look. (Breaks F3.)
3. Two frames drawing on the same evidence pool provide independent corroboration. (Breaks F4.)
4. A rising autocorrelation or variance trend in a short record uniquely identifies a bifurcation, excluding non-tipping resilience loss. (Breaks F2, F5.)
5. A method compressed into a headline number loses nothing relevant (R_T = 0). (Breaks F7.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Report risk **per component**, mode-labelled `specified`/`candidate`, with the written refuter attached (F3, F8).
2. State explicitly that the short record is a budget gap, not a proof of low risk, and that depth and absence are indistinguishable from inside (F1, F2).
3. Run the three questions in order: SEARCHED on existing data, then INWARD (better proxies, higher-frequency monitoring, spatial indicators), then OUTWARD (paleo analogues, other systems, robust decision methods). Keep them separate (Formula step 7; F1, F6, F9).
4. Triangulate across low-overlap evidence streams in proportion to the irreversibility of being wrong; do not pool agreement from a shared proxy network (F4).
5. Describe the effect before choosing the indicator; pre-register what would count as a tipping point (F5, F8).
6. Measure the round trip between the method shorthand and the concrete risk claim; publish the dropped constraints (F7).
7. Where the ground cannot be reached, decide robustly rather than wait for settlement (F10; world corroboration from decision science in `09`).

### One honest conflict from the world pool, sent back to census

Dakos et al. 2024 report that *"in the majority of the case studies we reviewed, the performance of most early warnings was positive in detecting tipping points"*, while Rietkerk et al. 2025 argue that *"alternative explanations can lead to resilience loss in the absence of tipping points"*. Read as a contradiction, one would have to be discarded. Re-census dissolves it: the two govern different quantities. EWS detects resilience loss (D1, measurement), not the existence of a tipping mechanism (D2, structure). Both are true in their own scope. This is exactly the WATSON conflict rule: a conflict sends us back to the census, never flips the figure verdict. It strengthens F2 and F5 rather than weakening them. The full record is in `09_watson_answer.md`.

### Updated verdict

The method shape survives: per-component, mode-labelled, triangulated, refuter-bearing, budget-aware, and silent where the ground has not settled. What does not survive is any claim that a short clean record proves low risk, that components can be averaged, that agreement among overlapping studies corroborates, or that a headline probability carries no round-trip loss. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**; the only live strain is the acknowledged inability to distinguish depth from genuine absence from inside the frame (Fig 6 :27), which is recorded as the honest stop rather than smoothed over.
