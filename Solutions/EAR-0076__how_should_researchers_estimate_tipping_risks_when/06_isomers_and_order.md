# Union compounds, ordered thresholds and dependency order

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

Three things are bundled in "how should researchers estimate tipping risks when observations are short and geological evidence is incomplete": a measurement problem (short record), a provenance problem (sparse paleo evidence), and a decision problem (what to do while unresolved). Are they one question, a partition, or a union? And what is the order in which they must be worked?

- Atoms held: residual R per slot, anchor, closure(Γ), import, instrument, round-trip gap R_T, threshold relation ⋛, union/partition. Bonds: → sequence, ⋛ comparison, ¬ negation. No new primitive is available without import.
- Domains D1 (observation), D2 (theory/model), D3 (decision/verification) as in `01` §1.

## Q1 — Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The three conditions overlap (short records and sparse paleo share small-sample structure; the decision problem consumes both). They are a **union compound**, not a partition and not a list. Each condition needs its own loop, and the intersections need theirs. A single lever (for example, "get more data") clears the first condition and leaves the second and third standing, because they still bind.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that one intervention monotonically reduces the difficulty of all three conditions, so the bundle behaves as one well-formed question. Fig 9 :4 denies this; a demonstration otherwise breaks the union reading.

Triangulated for the union rule (Fig 9, and the decision-science field's separate treatment of deep uncertainty as a distinct condition).

## Q2 — Is the threshold relation symmetric?

Operators table :47-51: ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* It is one of the **ordered** bonds (the symmetric ones are +, ∥, ≡). Fig 12 :92: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*; under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms.

Risk is directional: the question is not "are two quantities both present" but "which is larger, the forcing or the threshold". Swapping the operands changes the claim. So a risk statement written as a symmetric conjunction ("forcing and threshold") has recorded the ingredients and not the relation, the flat-formula failure of Fig 12.

- **Falsifier:** show that a tipping-risk statement is invariant under swapping forcing and threshold, and that the two orderings license the same decision. If so, the relation is symmetric and this reading fails.

Single-source for the bond type (Operators table, corroborated by Fig 12's symmetry rule).

## Q3 — What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The working order is:

1. **Perception** (D1): can the record tell a change from what was there before? A short record often cannot. This is where the chain usually breaks for slow components (Lenton et al. 2024, opened this session).
2. **Vocabulary / objective** (D2): do we have terms for what a short record affords, and can we say what counts as progress? Fig 3 :25-26 says this middle link has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the least attention. Binding the sought-for shape (Fig 5 :65) is the instrument for this link.
3. **SEARCHED** before INWARD or OUTWARD (Formula step 7): *"Asked FIRST — it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer."*
4. **INWARD** (extend an instrument already returning something, Fig 6 :32) and **OUTWARD** (import from a frame whose closure differs, Fig 6 :30 *"Cast it in effect terms."*). Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*
5. **Settle / decide** (D3): Fig 9 :42 *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."* Where the ground is unreachable, decide robustly (IPCC AR6 WGII Ch17, opened this session).

- **Falsifier:** show that effort at step 4 rescues a chain broken at step 1, that is, that more imported theory compensates for a record that cannot perceive the change. Fig 3 :19 denies this; a case where it happens breaks the order.

Triangulated for the order rule (Fig 3 and Formula step 7, independent statements).

## Q4 — Analogues that bear, and their limits

- **Metamerism (source discrimination).** *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"if several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* A rising autocorrelation is a reading with several sources (tipping, non-tipping resilience loss, non-stationary noise). The spectrometer analogue licenses asking for a discriminating instrument, not a name. This is an analogue from human perception/instrumentation, a low-overlap field relative to Earth systems.
- **Decision making under deep uncertainty.** NOAA SAB 2021, opened this session: deep uncertainty includes cases where the uncertainty *"cannot be reduced... by gathering additional information"*. This is Fig 8 :41 *"Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found"* restated in a policy frame. Low overlap, so genuine corroboration of F1.
- **Are these isomers?** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* The metamerism case and the tipping case share the atom "lossy reading of a source" but differ in the bond (spectral discrimination versus threshold crossing). They are isomers in the figure's sense: same abstraction, different arrangement, different substance. The analogy transfers structure, not content. Claiming more (that an instrument from one literally solves the other) would be the error the canonical README warns against when it returns a shape, not a name.

- **Falsifier for the analogue:** show that the source-discrimination analogue predicts the wrong direction for a tipping case (for example, that a better spectral instrument would resolve a threshold it cannot touch). If so, the analogue is decorative and is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union compound (Q1), the threshold relation is ordered (Q2), and the work has a fixed dependency order that cannot be rearranged by putting decision first or importing theory before perception is checked (Q3). Two low-overlap analogues corroborate the shape but transfer structure only (Q4). No new primitive is minted, no seventh risk score is added, and the one live strain (depth versus absence indistinguishable from inside, Fig 6 :27) is recorded, not resolved.
