# Outreach draft — check the estimation rule before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim below survives the reader's own check. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about estimating tipping risk from short records

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific methodological output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, please stop there.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the predictions, not `grounded`):** when observations are short and the geological record is incomplete, a tipping-risk estimate should have five properties.
>
> 1. It is reported **per component**, not as one global average. A global average hides the one system with a live signal.
> 2. It states that a clean short record is a **budget gap**, not evidence of low risk. "No warning signal found" is not "no risk". A component with a 1,000-year response cannot be closed by a 40-year series.
> 3. It draws on at least three **low-overlap** evidence streams, because the cost of being wrong is irreversible. Agreement among studies reusing the same proxy network is one witness, not three.
> 4. It **pre-registers** what would count as a tipping point and what observation would retract the estimate, before the indicator is chosen.
> 5. It publishes the **round-trip gap** between the method shorthand and the headline number, that is, which constraints were dropped to make the number speakable.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this produces a tipping probability, that early-warning indicators identify a bifurcation uniquely, or that any specific component is close to a threshold. The shape is a constraint on any defensible estimate, not an estimate. It stays `candidate` until the ground, which is the system's actual future behaviour, settles it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a short record plus sparse paleo record that licenses a unique tipping probability with no further data;
> 2. an aggregation across components that preserves the per-component signal needed to decide where to look;
> 3. two frames drawing on the same evidence pool that give independent corroboration;
> 4. an autocorrelation or variance trend in a short record that uniquely identifies a bifurcation and excludes non-tipping resilience loss;
> 5. a compression of the method into a headline number that loses nothing relevant.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file)
> - Canonical figures README (GitHub, branch `general-agent`)
> - The wider literature I actually read is listed in the source ledger in the packet
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result **before** you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage (in practice three-stage) offer

1. **Stage 1, before any theory:** send the shape claim and its five refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record that verdict before showing anything else.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the figure links and the source ledger. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they have now seen the interior (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, offer a method only:** the formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode.
