# Expert verification packet — EAR-0076 tipping-risk estimation under short records

**What this is:** a methodological claim in a form a researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the predictions. Not `grounded`.** No instrument result is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a risk number.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When observations are short and geological evidence is incomplete, a defensible tipping-risk estimate must be per component (never a global average), must treat the short record as a budget gap rather than evidence of low risk, must triangulate across genuinely low-overlap evidence streams in proportion to the irreversibility of the decision, must pre-register what counts as a tipping point and what would retract the estimate, and must publish the round-trip gap between the method and the headline number. It stays `candidate` until the ground (the system's future behaviour) settles it. Where the ground cannot be reached in time, the decision is made robustly in a separate loop.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 8 and 9

- **Statement:** risk is per component, and absence of a signal is not proof of absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44 *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*
- **What to check:** are these four lines present verbatim in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, and do they license the two statements without strain?
- **Refuter:** show an aggregation across components that preserves the per-slot signal needed to decide where to look, or a sealed-room Earth case where a finite search proves absence. Either breaks C1.

### C2 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 6 and 9

- **Statement:** the scarcity is mostly a budget problem, so independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and instruments are sharpened from ones already returning something.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :40-43 *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* and *"the remedy is more search, not another atom. Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed"*. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **What to check:** is the budget/absence distinction drawn correctly, and does the independence rule follow from the cost of being wrong rather than from a preference for consensus?
- **Refuter:** show a short record plus sparse paleo record that licenses a unique tipping probability with no further data, or show two frames sharing one evidence pool giving independent corroboration. Either breaks C2.

### C3 — cross-domain; checkable against the world literature

- **Statement:** the world literature independently supports the measurement half (timescale mismatch and EWS ambiguity) and the decision half (robust choice under deep uncertainty), with low overlap between those two fields.
- **World evidence, opened 2026-11-22:** Lenton et al., Nature Communications (2024): *"For slower-responding tipping systems, the relatively short satellite era of ~50 years is insufficient."* Rietkerk et al., Nature Climate Change (2025): *"alternative explanations can lead to resilience loss in the absence of tipping points"* and *"better testing of the existence of tipping points, beyond the application of EWSs"*. IPCC AR6 WGII Ch17 (2022): *"Deep uncertainty relates to circumstances in which data are too sparse, experts are in too much disagreement or time is too short to model the uncertainty."*
- **What to check:** are these quotations accurate, and are the two fields genuinely low-overlap (Earth-system observation versus decision science)?
- **Refuter:** show that the decision-science sources draw on the same evidence pool as the EWS literature, so their agreement is one witness wearing two names. Or show that EWS trends uniquely identify a bifurcation, excluding non-tipping resilience loss. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a tipping probability for any named component. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that a clean short record proves low risk, or that no signal means no threshold.
- Not that CSD or any EWS uniquely identifies a bifurcation.
- Not that components can be averaged for convenience. Convenience is a filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not that the world sources corroborate by agreement. Their overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`; most of the tipping literature is one pool.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was run.
