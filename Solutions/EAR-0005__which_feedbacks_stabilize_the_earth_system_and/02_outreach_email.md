**Subject:** One checkable claim about climate feedback signs, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The residual (in effect terms).** A climate feedback is a closed loop whose output re enters as input. Given a perturbation from a stated reference (radiative equilibrium, a preindustrial baseline, a model control), we need to say which loops reduce the gap and which grow it. The question is whether "stabilizing" and "amplifying" are properties a feedback carries, or a relation to a chosen residual, region and scale.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. A feedback is a closed loop, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes; it is not a one way forcing chain.
> 2. Stabilizing versus amplifying is a valence relative to the residual, not a kind. The same actor can damp one residual and amplify another, so no standing list of "good" feedbacks survives a change of reference.
> 3. The classification is per slot and per scale. A global average sign can hide a live regional signal, and a sign at one scale need not hold at another.
> 4. The figures do **not** name which physical feedback dominates or supply a watt per square metre ranking. That part is a gap owed to instruments, observations and model ensembles, not something the framework settles.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derives the standard list (Planck response stabilizing, water vapour and ice albedo amplifying, clouds uncertain). Those names and magnitudes come from climate science, not from the figures. I am claiming only the shape any such list must have. Agreement with a known list is not evidence for the framework; it would be a retrodiction from a shared pool.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. show that a feedback carries a stabilizing or amplifying sign independent of any residual, region, scale and reference;
> 2. show that a "stabilizing" classification survives a change of reference state;
> 3. show the network is adequately modelled as an open chain rather than a closed loop;
> 4. show that a first order, direct interaction account fully determines sign, with no role for cascades;
> 5. produce the ranking of named physical feedbacks from the figures themselves.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
