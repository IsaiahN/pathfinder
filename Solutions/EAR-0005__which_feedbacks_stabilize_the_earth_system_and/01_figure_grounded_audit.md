## 0. The residual, in effect terms

We are handed a set of closed causal loops (feedbacks: Planck response, water vapour, lapse rate, ice albedo, clouds, ocean carbon uptake, weathering, permafrost methane, and so on) and asked to sort them into two classes: those that reduce a perturbation (stabilize, damp) and those that increase it (amplify).

**R, stated as an effect:** a perturbation is a gap between the Earth system's state and some reference (radiative equilibrium, a preindustrial baseline, a model control state). A feedback is a closed loop whose output re enters as input. The question asks what sets the sign of that loop, and whether "stabilizer" or "amplifier" is a property the loop carries or a relation to a chosen residual, slot, scale and anchor.

**Two options and what each leads to:**

- **Option A, a standing list.** Sort named feedbacks once into "good" (stabilizing) and "bad" (amplifying). Leads to a portable list, but one whose dependence on an unstated reference and scale is invisible, so the list silently changes meaning when the reference changes.
- **Option B, a relation.** Treat sign as a loop relation to a stated residual, assessed per slot and scale, with the ranking of named feedbacks left to a ground. Leads to a checkable classification procedure, a per slot verdict, and an honest open slot where measurement is required.

The figures decide B, as shown below. Note that Option A is not "false" as a summary of a particular study; it is a category error when offered as the figure derived answer.

**Binding the sought for shape before the run** (formula Direction rule): an answer is a function from (reference, residual, slot, scale) to a signed loop classification. That is the ordinary term we will let accrue a residual, rather than smuggling in a preferred list.

## 1. Domain census (done before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** the residual is indexed by slot (region, subsystem, variable), not a single global number. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single global sign for "the ice albedo feedback" is an aggregate that can hide a live regional signal.
- **Symmetry:** the sign is directed (damping versus amplifying), not a symmetric join. Directedness is not recoverable from magnitude alone: Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* So the same actor can carry opposite signs in different bonds.
- **Scale:** feedbacks span fast and slow, local and global. The kernel (loop and naming rule) is invariant, while actors, currency and anchor change with size. Fig 4 :19 20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* A sign at one scale does not transfer to another by fiat.

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D1 and D2 each have an instrument anchor. D3 has the figures. D4 has the ground. All four qualify.

### Partition or union

This is a **union compound**, not a partition. "Stabilizing versus amplifying" bundles at least two conditions: (a) the sign of the loop relative to the chosen residual, and (b) the slot and scale at which it is assessed. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 56: a bundled problem is "usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones", and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Each named feedback is a relation, not a kind (Fig 11 :50), so a single global list is the wrong object.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order is:

1. Fix the anchor and reference state (D1/D2 instrument, Fig 2 :29). Without it "progress" and "damping" are undefined.
2. Enumerate the habitat of the residual by contact, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43 44).
3. Classify the sign of each closed loop relative to that residual (Fig 12 :121).
4. Only then ask which named feedback dominates at which scale (a ground question, not a figure question).

Effort spent at step 4 cannot rescue a broken step 1: Fig 3 :19, "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

### Strain test applied before concluding

An early reading tried to force Fig 13 :32 (amplification compounding is chaos) to be the claim that the Earth system's *net* feedback is positive and chaotic. That strains the line: the net feedback is described in the world sources as negative overall but becoming less negative. Re census: Fig 13 :32 governs the internal behaviour of a positive loop (a remainder compounding through a chain), not the net sign of the sum of loops. It governs another quantity, so it is not used to assert Earth's net sign. Recorded in the retraction log.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 A feedback is a cycle, and its sign is a property of the loop (decisive for shape)

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A feedback cannot be modelled as an open forcing chain. Classification applies to a closed loop's sign. A chain "cannot close by construction", so any account that lists feedbacks as one way influences without returning the output as input has already dropped the thing being classified. Fig 12 :119 adds the catalyst case: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Some loops persist through their own operation and some are spent, so a sign assigned once may not survive repeated use. Corroborated by control theory (Negative feedback, loop gain AB negative) and by chaos theory (feedback loops are named as a feature of chaotic systems). Mode: `general` structural, triangulated across Fig 12 and the control / chaos frames (low overlap: bond grammar versus control mathematics).

### F2 Stabilizer / amplifier is a valence relative to the residual, not a kind carried by an actor

Fig 11 :49 :50 :51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Apply to Earth feedbacks: ice, water vapour, lapse rate and clouds are actors. Whether an actor damps or amplifies is a relation to the residual and reference, not a property of the actor. Fig 11 :51 is the direct refusal of Option A, a standing list.

World corroboration, read this session: the lapse rate feedback is negative in general but positive in polar regions; clouds act as a negative feedback in some latitudes and a clear positive feedback globally; and "Naming a feedback positive or negative does not imply that the feedback is good or bad." Positive feedback article: "Positive and negative in this sense refer to loop gains greater than or less than zero, and do not imply any value judgements as to the desirability of the outcomes or effects." This is strong corroboration from a different field (control and systems theory) and from within climate science itself. Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 11 valence frame + Fig 12 loop-sign frame + world control theory, low overlap).

### F3 The sign is per slot, never a single global number

Fig 1 :19 and :22, quoted in section 1. A global mean sign hides the live regional signal. The world sources make this concrete: lapse rate flips sign at the poles; clouds flip sign by latitude; polar amplification is exactly the regional residual that a global average would average away. Mode: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 perception frame + Fig 13 independence condition :38 39 + world latitude dependence).

### F4 Scale changes the answer because the currency and anchor change

Fig 4 :19 20, quoted in section 1. Also Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fast feedbacks (Planck, water vapour, lapse rate, clouds) and slow ones (ice sheets, chemical weathering, carbon sinks) are not the same object at the same level, so a sign at one level is not a sign at another. Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* Mode: `specified`, single source for the scale claim (Fig 4), corroborated by Fig 12 and the world distinction between fast and long term feedbacks.

### F5 Contact cascades set the effective sign, and first order enumeration misses it

Fig 11 :52 53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :43 :44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* and *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* A feedback's effective sign can be fixed by an indirect path two or three steps out (for example ice loss altering regional lapse rate, water vapour and cloud feedbacks, as the world source notes). Scoping to direct interactions misclassifies. Mode: `specified`, single source (Fig 11) with a concrete world case cited in the same figure and in climate literature.

### F6 A positive loop compounds a remainder into chaos; this governs the loop, not the net sign

Fig 13 :32:

> **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."**

Fig 12 :121 already says the sign decides whether the loop closes. Together: amplifying loops are where small remainders grow and where the system approaches instability or a new state. This is corroborated by control theory ("positive feedback tends to instability via exponential growth, oscillation or chaotic behavior") and by chaos theory ("Chaotic behavior exists in many natural systems, including weather and climate"). Strain handled in section 1: this line governs the inside of a positive loop, not Earth's net sign. Mode: `general` structural, triangulated (Fig 13 + Fig 12 + mathematics / control frames).

### F7 The two domains have different currencies, so the sign question is framed differently in each

Fig 13 :76, quoted above. D1's currency is directed energy flux (energy and gradient compose a flow). D2's currency is carbon concentration and ordering. Fig 4 :20 says only actors, currency and anchor change across size; here they change across domain too. So a "positive carbon cycle feedback" and a "positive albedo feedback" share a sign word but not a currency, and cannot be summed without an explicit conversion that the figures do not supply. Mode: `specified`, single source for the currency distinction, with world sources confirming the two feedback families are treated separately (physical versus biogeochemical).

### F8 Naming and ranking named physical feedbacks is a gap after a genuine census

No figure line ranks Planck response above ice albedo, or assigns a watt per square metre per kelvin value, or says which feedback dominates. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The anchor for the ranking is the instrument and model ensemble, not the figures. The honest verdict at this level is `gap`, and the gap is owed to the ground, not hidden by a figure line strained to fit. The world sources supply the missing magnitudes as *secondary* evidence (Planck about minus 3.8 W m^-2 K^-1, water vapour about plus 1.8, surface albedo about plus 0.35, clouds about plus 0.42, with cloud uncertainty the largest), but those are imports with provenance, not figure derivations (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 52).

## 4. Pre registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument". The shape claim (Option B) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A feedback can be shown to carry a stabilizing or amplifying sign independent of any chosen residual, slot, scale and anchor (breaks Fig 11 :49 51).
2. A classification of a feedback as stabilizing survives a change of anchor or reference state (breaks Fig 11 :51).
3. The feedback network is adequately modelled as an open chain rather than a closed loop whose output re enters as input (breaks Fig 12 :121).
4. The classification is invariant to spatial or temporal scale (breaks Fig 4 :19 20).
5. The sign is fully determined by direct first order interactions with no role for cascades (breaks Fig 11 :52 53).
6. A figure line is produced that ranks named physical feedbacks by magnitude or names which one dominates. That would remove the F8 gap and move the ranking from `gap` toward figure governance.

If any of 1 to 5 fires, the shape is wrong and this package is withdrawn. If 6 fires, F8 is revised. Until one is run, no ranking claim may be labelled `grounded`.
