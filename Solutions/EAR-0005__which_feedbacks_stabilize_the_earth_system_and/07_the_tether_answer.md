# The Tether's answer: which feedbacks stabilize, which amplify

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the classification rule, `general` on the metatheory. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A feedback is a closed loop whose output re enters as input. Given a perturbation from a stated reference (radiative equilibrium, a preindustrial baseline, a model control), we want to know which loops reduce the gap and which grow it. The residual is not "what is the list"; it is "what does the sign depend on, and can a sign be assigned independent of a reference, a slot and a scale?" The question bundles two conditions, so it is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54), not one partition. Each condition needs its own loop.

## 2. The shape

1. **A feedback is a cycle, and the sign is a property of the loop.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A one way forcing chain is not the object being classified.

2. **Stabilizing versus amplifying is a valence relative to the residual, not a kind an actor carries.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Ice, water vapour, lapse rate and clouds are actors. Their sign is a relation to a chosen residual, region and scale. This is the direct refusal of a standing list.

3. **The classification is per slot, never a global average.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The world makes this concrete: the lapse rate feedback is negative in general but positive in polar regions; clouds are negative in some latitudes and a clear positive feedback globally.

4. **The sign is scale relative.** Fig 4 :19 20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fast feedbacks and slow ice sheet or weathering responses are not the same object at the same level, so a sign does not transfer across scale by fiat.

5. **Indirect cascades can set the effective sign.** Fig 11 :52 53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A first order account can misclassify a loop whose decisive path is two or three steps out.

6. **The two feedback families have different currencies.** Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." The physical radiative family trades in directed energy flux; the biogeochemical family trades in carbon ordering and concentration. They are a functional group (signed closed loop), not isomers over a shared atom set (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q2). Do not silently sum watts and carbon.

7. **Amplifying loops compound a remainder; this governs the loop, not Earth's net sign.** Fig 13 :32: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Fig 12 :121 says the sign decides whether the loop closes. An early reading that made this a claim about a positive *net* feedback was retracted after re census (`01` section 1).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a standing list of stabilizers and amplifiers. No feedback is "good" or "bad" in itself; sign is relative (Fig 11 :49 51).
- Not a derivation of the standard magnitudes. Planck near minus 3.8 W m^-2 K^-1, water vapour about plus 1.8, surface albedo about plus 0.35, clouds about plus 0.42 with the largest uncertainty: these come from the climate frame and are imports with provenance, not figure results (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 52).
- Not that the figures rank which feedback dominates. That is a `gap` after a genuine census (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that a shift of sign by latitude or scale is a contradiction. It is the predicted behaviour of a relational sign.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

The anchor for the *shape* is the figures; the anchor for the *ranking* is the instrument and the model ensemble (Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."). What would close the gap:

- Sustained top of atmosphere flux and outgoing longwave observations that discriminate the sign of each named loop at a stated reference and slot.
- Independent model ensembles and paleoclimate constraints that agree on the magnitude ranking without sharing a single evidence pool (Fig 2 :31 32).
- A source discriminating instrument in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that can tell which loop arrangement produced a given radiative reading, rather than naming the loop from the reading (metamerism).
- The falsifiers in section 5, written before this conclusion.

## 5. Falsifiers, written before the run

1. A feedback carries a stabilizing or amplifying sign independent of any residual, slot, scale and anchor (breaks Fig 11 :49 51).
2. A "stabilizing" classification survives a change of reference state (breaks Fig 11 :51).
3. The network is adequately modelled as an open chain rather than a closed loop (breaks Fig 12 :121).
4. The classification is invariant to spatial or temporal scale (breaks Fig 4 :19 20).
5. The sign is fully determined by direct first order interactions, with no role for cascades (breaks Fig 11 :52 53).
6. A figure line is produced that ranks named physical feedbacks by magnitude (removes the F8 gap).

## 6. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the ranking.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The figure set returns the form any valid answer must have, written refuters, and the instruments that would close the remaining gap. It does not return a name or a number, and the stop is recorded so a reader can locate it. Agreement from people who share the same evidence pool is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). The canonical README adds the caution that indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand you a list of good and bad climate feedbacks. It says that a feedback is a closed loop whose sign is decided by the loop, not by the actor (Fig 12 :121); that stabilizing versus amplifying is a relation to a chosen residual, region and scale, so no standing list survives a change of reference (Fig 11 :49 51); that the classification must be made per slot, because a global average hides the live regional signal (Fig 1 :19); that sign does not cross scale without a measured round trip (Fig 4 :19 20); and that indirect cascades can set the effective sign (Fig 11 :52 53). It refuses to rank Planck response against ice albedo or clouds, because the figures return a shape, not a name, and the ranking needs instruments and model ensembles. What it offers is the form any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the ground that would finish the job. The ranking stays open until that ground pays.
