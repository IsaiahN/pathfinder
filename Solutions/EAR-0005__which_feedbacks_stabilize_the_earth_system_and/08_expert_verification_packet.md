# Expert verification packet: the Tether on stabilizing and amplifying feedbacks

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate feedback researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the classification rule, `general` on the metatheory. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the ground is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A feedback is a closed loop whose output re enters as input, and its sign is a property of the loop (Fig 12 :121). "Stabilizing" and "amplifying" are a valence relative to a stated residual, slot and scale, not properties carried by an actor, so no standing list of stabilizers and amplifiers survives a change of reference (Fig 11 :49 51). The classification must be made per slot, because a global average hides the live regional signal (Fig 1 :19). It does not cross scale without a measured round trip (Fig 4 :19 20), and indirect cascades can set the effective sign (Fig 11 :52 53). The figures do not name which physical feedback dominates or supply a watt per square metre ranking; that is a gap owed to instruments and model ensembles. What is offered is the shape any valid answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the ground that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub claims

### C1 Framework internal, checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** a feedback is a closed loop (not an open chain) and its sign is a relation to a residual, not a property of the actor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." and Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** are these three lines present verbatim in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`? Does the inference from them to "sign is relational, not carried" follow without adding an unstated premise?
- **Refuter:** show a feedback carrying a fixed sign independent of any residual, slot, scale and anchor; or show the network is adequately modelled as an open chain whose output never returns as input.

### C2 World facing, checkable against the climate feedback literature

- **Statement:** a single global sign list fails because the same named feedback flips sign with region or scale; and the two feedback families have different currencies (energy flux versus carbon concentration).
- **Expected observation if the claim is true:** the lapse rate feedback negative in general but positive in polar regions; clouds negative in some latitudes but a clear positive feedback globally; physical and biogeochemical feedbacks reported in separate sections with different units.
- **What to check:** open https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_feedbacks (which cites IPCC AR6 WG1) and verify those latitude and unit claims. Is the "no standing list" prediction borne out?
- **Refuter:** produce a reference and scale under which every named feedback has one invariant sign and one common currency across the physical and biogeochemical families, with the ranking unchanged everywhere.

### C3 Framework internal derivation, checkable by reading Figures 1, 4, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the classification is per slot, scale relative, and the question is a union that must be unbundled before search; the magnitude ranking is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 4 :19 20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 11 :52 53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** does the dependency order in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3 to Q5 follow from these lines, or is it imported from outside the figures? Is the F8 gap honest, i.e. is there really no figure line that ranks the named feedbacks?
- **Refuter:** find a figure line that assigns a magnitude or dominance ranking to named physical feedbacks; or show that the union claim is a partition after all, so unbundling is unnecessary.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of stabilizers and amplifiers. Sign is relative, not carried (Fig 11 :49 51).
- Not the standard magnitudes as figure results. Planck, water vapour, albedo and cloud values are imports with provenance from the climate frame (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 52).
- Not that ice albedo is "bad" or the Planck response is "good". Positive and negative here are loop gain signs, not value judgments (control theory and the climate page both say so).
- Not that the Earth system's net feedback is positive and chaotic. Fig 13 :32 governs a remainder compounding inside a positive loop, not the net sign of the sum.
- Not that the two feedback families are isomers over a shared atom set. They are a functional group with different currencies (`06` Q2).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a test of the framework. This packet is a candidate for checking, not a report of a check that was run.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Sustained top of atmosphere flux and outgoing longwave observations that sign each named loop at a stated reference and slot.
2. Independent model ensembles and paleoclimate constraints agreeing on the ranking without a shared evidence pool (Fig 2 :31 32).
3. A source discriminating instrument in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: one that tells which loop arrangement produced a given radiative reading instead of naming the loop from the reading.
4. The six falsifiers in `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 5. Any one that fires moves the claim toward retraction.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :121, Fig 11 :49 51, Fig 1 :19 and Fig 4 :19 20 in the local figure text. Four short passages. Confirm the quotes are verbatim.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of climate feedbacks: do the lapse rate and cloud sign flips, and the separate units for physical and carbon feedbacks, hold?
3. Ask yourself the key question: can you name any feedback whose sign is independent of a reference, region and scale? If yes, C1 is refuted.
4. Name one instrument that would tell which loop produced a given radiative reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
