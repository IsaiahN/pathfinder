## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, the shape is:

> "A valid answer is a rule that assigns a signed classification to each closed feedback loop, relative to a stated residual, slot and scale, with the magnitude ranking left to a ground."

A standing list of "stabilizers" and "amplifiers" is a name pretending to be a shape. The distinction is exactly the one in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F2 and F8.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recursively. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Applied here: start with "feedback", "damping", "gain", "residual", "reference"; narrow toward "loop sign relative to residual", "per slot", "cascade", "currency".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." The outreach email therefore asks what a recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true, before giving them the theory.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works* on stopping: the loop "stops when nothing you have registers a difference from it", and "indiscernibility is not identity". Here the stop is the F8 gap: after a genuine re census, no figure line discriminates the named feedback ranking, so the honest report is a gap plus the instrument that would close it.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is the union compound in section 0 of `01` (sign relative to residual is one condition, slot and scale another).

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The essay *Allostasis* is not needed for this; the figures say it. For the ranking, the ground is the flux observation, the model ensemble, the paleoclimate constraint.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The watt per square metre magnitudes on the climate feedback page are such imports. They must be recorded as coming from IPCC AR6 and climate models, not from the figures.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism for why a name from a reading is not enough. An instrument reads green and "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." Several paths (green, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK) create the same reading: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Applied here: a sign read off a global mean temperature time series cannot comment on which loop produced it. The same reading can come from several loop arrangements (Fig 12 :94, "The arrangement is the substance"). Naming the source loop from the reading is the metamerism error; what is owed is a discriminator, not a name. This is why F3 (per slot) and F5 (cascades) are constraints on any valid answer.

## 4. Why the standing list is the specific forbidden error

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the general valence rule in framework language: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malefactor to another." That is Fig 11 :50 almost verbatim, and the essay ties it to the practical rule that bad news must travel in the same lane as good news, with decay so an old verdict can be retested.

The climate evidence makes the error concrete. The lapse rate feedback is negative in general but positive in polar regions. Cloud feedback is negative in some latitudes and a clear positive feedback globally. A list that says "lapse rate: stabilizer" is not wrong forever; it is wrong for a particular slot, and silent about the condition. That is the shape error, not a factual error about one study.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* adds why an endorsement cannot repair it: "High contact builds exactly the shared understanding that makes communication work, but that same factor makes the learner's agreement with the teacher worthless as a check." Two readers of the same figure extract agree because they share the extract, not because the extract is true (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. Q: Does "shape, not a name" forbid using the standard feedback names?

**No, with a condition.** The README's phrase governs a *missing* thing, a primitive not held in the library. The standard names (Planck, water vapour, ice albedo, clouds, carbon cycle) are not missing primitives; they are bindings from the climate frame onto descriptions of loops. Naming them is legitimate as a **binding with provenance**, recorded as coming from that frame. What is forbidden is presenting them as figure derivations, or turning them into a standing list whose dependence on reference and slot is invisible. This corrects the over strict reading that the exemplar itself records in its `05` section 5b for Gradient and Spectrum, applied here to climate loops.

**Does this folder commit the error?** No at the shape level: `01` F8 declares the ranking a gap and marks the magnitudes as imports. It would commit the error if it asserted "the figures show ice albedo is an amplifier" as a result. That sentence does not appear in any file here.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach, plus the honesty of this derivation

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers well known climatologists to agree with the shape produces a convention, not a check. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried, let the ground settle it, and record the refutation if one is found.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of anything. It cannot settle the question it reports on, and it cannot certify which climate feedback dominates.

**Second seat caveat.** *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: "Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times." The independent audit in `11_audit.md` is a simulated second seat drawing on the same evidence pool. Its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. No ground reading was taken in producing this folder.
