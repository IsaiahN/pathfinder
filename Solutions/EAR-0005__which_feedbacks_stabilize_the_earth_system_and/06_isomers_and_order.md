## DERIVATION

### Q1 What is the structure of the object being classified?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :48: *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."*
- Therefore a feedback is not a list of links (a chain) but a closed molecule. The "sign" is not an extra ingredient added to the atoms; it is the bond property that makes the loop close in one direction or the other. Same atoms, different sign, different behaviour, which is Fig 12 :117's isomer idea applied to the loop's closure rather than to its atoms.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that a feedback can be fully represented as an open chain whose output never re enters as input, with no loss in the classification. If that holds, the cycle reading fails and the loop sign is a metaphor, not a structure.

### Q2 Are the physical and biogeochemical feedback families isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Physical radiative feedbacks (D1) and biogeochemical feedbacks (D2) do **not** share a full atom set. D1's currency is directed energy flux (Fig 13 :76, energy and gradient compose a flow); D2's is carbon ordering and concentration. Each family has its own anchor. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a **functional group** (a signed closed loop relative to a residual) with different actors, currency and ground in the slots.
- The stronger form would be: write both families over the full shared six term set and arrange them differently. Then they would be isomers. But the currency difference is load bearing in the world sources, and Fig 4 :20 says actors, currency and anchor change across frames. So the stronger isomer claim is **not licensed** here. It is recorded as a road not taken, not as a hidden result.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a single currency and a single anchor under which both families are the same molecule with only the bond changed. If that succeeds, the isomer claim replaces the functional group claim.

### Q3 Is the question a partition or a union, and what follows?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55 56: a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, it becomes a specific compound.
- "Stabilizing versus amplifying" bundles at least two conditions: (a) sign relative to the residual, (b) slot and scale. A single global sort tries to clear both at once and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.
- The unbundling is: classify sign relative to a stated residual first; then re run for each slot; then re run for each scale. The compound (for example "polar lapse rate feedback") is then a specific object with its own loop, not an instance of a general list.
- *Library of Babel* names the same move: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually." Corroboration from a different frame (search), low overlap with the figure text.

### Q4 What is the dependency order, and why can a sign not be assigned early?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :5 (link 1, perception): the system can tell what is in front of it and tell it apart from what was there before. Without a measured change there is no residual to sign.
- Fig 3 :7 (link 2, vocabulary): it has terms for what things afford. Without terms for loop, gain and damping there is nothing to assign a sign to.
- Fig 3 :10 (link 3, objective): it can say what would count as progress here. *Progress for a stabilizing loop is reduction of the gap to the reference.* Without a reference the word "stabilize" has no referent.
- Only then does sign classification (link 4, planning) become possible. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Effort spent ranking named feedbacks before the reference is fixed lands at a step that cannot use it.
- Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* The habitat is enumerated, not composed (Fig 11 :43).

### Q5 What is the order of scale, and can a sign be promoted across it?

- Fig 4 :19 20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom."*
- So the classification procedure crosses scales (the kernel is the same), but the *sign* of a particular loop does not automatically cross with it, because the actors, currency and anchor change. A settled per slot classification can become an atom for a regional or global composition, but only after settlement at its own level.
- Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Promoting a sign from region to globe without measuring the gap R_T is dead reckoning. The world source makes the cost visible: lapse rate negative globally, positive at the poles; clouds negative in some latitudes, positive globally.
- **Falsifier:** show a feedback whose sign is invariant across a change of region, time horizon and anchor, with the same currency and the same measured residual. One such case would not break the rule (the rule says sign is *relative*, not that it must always flip), but a general invariance theorem would.

### Q6 Analogues from low overlap fields (corroboration only)

- **Chemistry, Le Chatelier's principle:** "If the equilibrium of a system is disturbed by a change in one or more of the determining factors (as temperature, pressure, or concentration) the system tends to adjust itself to a new equilibrium by counteracting as far as possible the effect of the change." A stabilizing loop outside its equilibrium assumptions can fail: "In scenarios outside thermodynamic equilibrium, there can arise phenomena in contradiction to an over-general statement of Le Chatelier's principle." Corroborates F2 and F4: valence depends on the assumed conditions.
- **Biology, allostasis:** "The key goal of physiological regulation is not rigid constancy; rather, it is flexible variation that anticipates the organism's needs and promptly meets them." Corroborates the point that "stabilize" is not constancy of a number but a relation to a moving reference.
- **Engineering and control, negative and positive feedback:** "In a psychology context, on the other hand, negative refers to the valence of the feedback - attractive versus aversive, or praise versus criticism." And "Positive and negative in this sense refer to loop gains greater than or less than zero, and do not imply any value judgements as to the desirability of the outcomes or effects." Corroborates the relation reading of sign and the refusal of a good/bad list.
- **Mathematics, chaos theory:** "Chaotic behavior exists in many natural systems, including fluid flow, heartbeat irregularities, weather and climate." Corroborates Fig 13 :32 for the inside of an amplifying loop.
- **Ecology, Fig 11's own DDT case:** "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The support derivation for F5.

None of these analogues is a figure line. All are secondary. Where one seemed to strain (Le Chatelier outside equilibrium), the note is kept, not smoothed away.

## CONCLUSION

The classified object is a closed cycle whose sign is a bond property, not a list of links. The two feedback families are a **functional group**, not isomers, because their currencies and anchors differ. The question is a **union** that must be unbundled into sign relative to residual, then slot, then scale. Dependency order forbids assigning a sign before a reference and a measured residual exist (Fig 3). Scale promotion requires a measured round trip gap (Fig 4 :30) and cannot be assumed from kernel identity. Each claim has a falsifier above.

This framing rescues what is defensible (the sign relation and the per slot rule) without the part that fails (a standing global list). The magnitude ranking remains a ground question, exactly as in `01` F8.
