## What the wider literature actually bears on

1. **The sign is a relation, not a value judgment.** Three independent frames say so: the climate page (positive/negative does not mean good/bad), the positive feedback page (loop gain sign carries no desirability), and the psychology usage on the negative feedback page (valence: attractive versus aversive). This is the low overlap corroboration of Fig 11 :49 51.
2. **The same actor flips sign with region or scale.** The lapse rate and cloud examples are the concrete instance of "no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51). They also show why the per slot rule (Fig 1 :19) is not cosmetic.
3. **Stability is not rigid constancy.** Allostasis and Le Chatelier both define stability relative to a reference and a set of conditions, and Le Chatelier explicitly warns that the over general statement fails outside its assumed conditions. That corroborates F2 and F4: change the reference or the assumptions and the sign can change.
4. **Amplification has a characteristic internal behaviour.** Control theory says positive feedback tends to instability, oscillation or chaos; chaos theory names feedback loops inside chaotic systems. This corroborates Fig 13 :32, while the strain note in `01` keeps it from being misread as a claim about Earth's net sign.
5. **Magnitudes are an import, not a derivation.** The watt per square metre values on the climate page come from IPCC AR6 and model ensembles. Under Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :50 52 they are adopted imports with provenance. They may corroborate a shape but they are not a figure result, and they must not be presented as one.

- **Stage 1, before anything else:** the residual, the shape claim, the refuter. This is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source", and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
