### Q1 - Is entropy a first-order derivative? CORRECTION TO MY OWN `05` SECTION 3

- The figures README: *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Entropy is not held as a primitive, but it is also not missing in the sense the phrase governs: it is a composition.
- Fig 13 :43-44: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or / smoothness over space and time."* A generating set must not contain what its members produce. Entropy is likewise produced, so it cannot be a seventh member.
- The load-bearing paths from Fig 13's minimality table:
  - network, :59, *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"*;
  - energy, :68, *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"*;
  - gradient, :71, *"no direction to drive the flow, and no remainder left after each step"*;
  - time, :65, *"no time-translation symmetry, and no finite operations to leave a remainder"*;
  - space, :62, *"no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything"*;
  - spectrum, :56, *"no ordering, so no equivalence over thermal state, so no transitivity"*.
- So entropy reads as a **composition across both kinds**, roughly `network(count) + energy(finite) + gradient(directed) + time(remainder)`, with space supplying the isolated region and spectrum the ordering. That is a derivative, not a member. Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

Retracted as applied to derived quantities. Triangulated (README + Fig 13 minimality table).

### Q2 - What order does the "why" chain have?

- Fig 12 :30, Operators table :30: **→ sequence**: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with the test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* The chain `prior regime → low entropy turn → arrow of time` is a sequence: the later term needs the earlier one to have happened.
- Fig 12 :76, Operators table :36: **⇒ production**: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, test *"is there a B before A fires? No."* A mechanism claim ("inflation produced the smoothing") uses production, not mere sequence. The two bonds are not interchangeable, and the figures price the choice (Fig 12 :99-101).
- Fig 12 :86, Operators table :48: **⋛ comparison**: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* The word "unusually" is a comparison. Its operands are the registered state and a measure. The measure operand is not currently held, which is why F3 records a `gap`.
- Fig 7 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."* The dependency order is: register the state, supply the comparison, then propose the production. Proposing a production before the comparison is the apophenia named in Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* So the chain advances only when a new room differs and matches. Repeating the same mechanism at a smaller scale is motion, not progress (Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress"*).
- **Falsifier, written before this run:** if two successive "why" answers can be shown to share the same closure and the same residual, the chain is a mirror chain and the progress claim fails.

### Q3 - Are competing mechanisms isomers, functional groups, or something else?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Inflation, a bounce and a de Sitter fluctuation do not share the same atoms. They invoke different fields, different phases and different prior regimes. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. I attempted that reading in the first census and retracted it; the strain was recorded in `01` section 6.
- What they do share is the functional group: each is a `prior regime → low entropy turn → arrow` molecule. The group says what kind of answer; the qualifier says which candidate. Fig 12 :124 is the figure that governs, and it is single-source for this application.
- The stronger isomer claim would require writing all candidates over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which transition. That is a checkable structural claim, not an established one.
- **Falsifier, written before this run:** if a candidate cannot be written over the shared atom set without importing a new atom, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - Where do catalyst, cycle and functional group appear in this question?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** The vacuum energy in the Carroll-Chen picture plays a catalyst-like role: it is not consumed by the local arrow it supports. This is a physics-facing analogy, `specified`, unmeasured, single-source for the figure and single-source for the physics.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** A cyclic or bouncing cosmology is the figure's cycle, and the sign of the loop (whether entropy resets or accumulates) decides whether the arrow survives a turnaround. This is another checkable sub-claim, not a figure verdict about any actual model.
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."** Used in Q3 for the family of candidate mechanisms.
- Fig 13 :121-124 records that *"different routes can arrive at the same substance"* and that *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* So the observational sameness of smoothness across candidate mechanisms is expected and carries no discriminating power. That is exactly the metamerism point in `05` section 4.

## CONCLUSION

Entropy is a **first-order derivative** of the six-term set: a composition across both kinds, assembled from counting, finiteness, directedness, remainder, region and ordering. It is neither a primitive nor a forbidden name. The "why" chain is a **sequence** using production for mechanism claims and comparison for "unusually"; its order is dependency, not importance, and its rooms advance only when they differ and match the residual. Competing mechanisms are **functional groups**, not isomers, on the figure's definition, although the stronger isomer claim over the shared atom set is a real check. Catalyst and cycle name further structural options, each with its own falsifier.

This rescues the derivative reading of the low entropy state without claiming a final mechanism. It needs no seventh primitive and no import that moves the framework's wall. What remains open is the comparison operand in the ⋛ relation and the source-discriminating instrument that would name the first slot.
