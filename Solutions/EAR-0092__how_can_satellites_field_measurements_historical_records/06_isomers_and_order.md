# Supporting derivation: union, order and analogues for four observation frames

**Mode: `specified` on the union and order claims, `general` on the structural rule, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The four named sources are presented as a list to be combined. Two questions are owed before any combination is attempted:

1. Is the list a **partition** (four disjoint contributions that can simply be added) or a **union of overlapping conditions** (four frames whose overlap must be measured and whose intersections form compounds with their own loops)?
2. If it is a union, in what **order** must the work proceed, and are the frames related as **isomers** over a shared structure or only as members of a **functional group**?

The residual that drives both questions is the per-slot gap between what a frame returns and what the earth system does (Fig 1 :19).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms and relations held: the six substrate terms of Fig 13 (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures), the seven bonds plus negation, and the rule that composition runs across the two kinds and never within one. No new primitive is available without import.

The four frames are **frames**, not atoms. Each is a bounded system with a library, a currency and an anchor. A frame can be given a *reading* as a first-order molecule, `state → structure → state`, one crossing. That reading is a binding from the observation frame onto held substrate atoms, not a derivation of a new atom. It is `specified`, ungrounded, and it must not be mistaken for the framework naming which structure sits where.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The four fail in different ways: a sensor drifts, a field site moves, an archive has a gap, a knowledge holder is excluded or extracted. Those are overlapping conditions. A single lever, for example "add more satellites," clears only the region where the satellite condition holds alone and leaves the intersections standing. If a catchment is both poorly observed from orbit and absent from the documentary record, adding satellites does not fix it. The union reading is therefore `specified` and, on this census, decisive.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate that the four failure conditions are disjoint, so that a single lever clears all four and no compound remains. That would dissolve the union and make flattening safe.

### Q2. What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 orders links by dependency: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Applied to this question:

1. **Perception.** Each frame must tell what is in front of it before it can be combined. Radiance is calibrated; field instruments are inter-compared; archives are dated and their gaps recorded; knowledge holders identify what they are observing and under what conditions. This link has instruments.
2. **Vocabulary.** Terms for what an observation affords must exist before a shared objective is declared. Here the currency mismatch is severe: a radiance value, a soil sample, a documentary entry and a relational observation about a place are not the same kind of thing. There is no instrument for "what would count as progress" across them, which is exactly Fig 3 :27's unnamed link.
3. **Objective.** Each frame must say what would count as progress in its own terms. A single top-down metric declared before step 2 is a convention, and *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47).
4. **Planning.** Only now choose a route: which frames bear on this residual, which are silent, which overlap.
5. **Learn and carry.** Carry the method (how the residual was indexed, how overlap was measured, how provenance was kept), not the record of one successful combination (Fig 4 :22).

The world-side Multiple Evidence Base gives a low-overlap analogue of this order: Tengö et al. (2014) describe three phases, joint definition of problems and goals, generation of an enriched picture on an equal platform, then joint analysis and evaluation. That maps onto links 2 to 4 above, arrived at from ecosystem governance rather than from the figures. It corroborates the order but does not decide it.

### Q3. Are the four frames isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

A first-order reading of each frame as `state → structure → state` gives, as **bindings**, something like:

| frame | a possible first-order reading | bond that differs |
|---|---|---|
| satellite remote sensing | Space → Spectrum → Time | an ordering of radiance across space, revisited in time |
| in-situ field measurement | Space → Gradient → Time | a directed, local reading with a threshold against a standard |
| historical and documentary records | Time → Spectrum → State | an ordering of entries across time used to name a state |
| Indigenous and local knowledge | Space → Network → Time | relations among observers and a place carried across generations |

These are **not** figure-derived names. They are candidate bindings, the same kind of move the exemplar makes when it reads GR as `Space – structure – Time`. Their value is to show that the four need not be treated as four versions of one quantity. Over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` they would be **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the stronger claim worth testing. If the binding is rejected and each frame is written over only the atoms it needs, the four still share the substructure `state → structure → state`, so the weaker **functional-group** claim stands: they are the same *kind* of thing with different qualifiers.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any frame cannot be written as a first-order `state → structure → state` molecule at all, or if one requires an atom that none of the others can use and no shared substructure remains, both the isomer and the functional-group readings fail. The frames would then be incomparable closures, and a union would not be a legitimate object of combination until a bridging import is found.

### Q4. What is the order of the union itself?

Two orders must be kept distinct and not read against each other (Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*):

- **Order of discovery (search):** residual first, frame second (Fig 8 :53). Describe the per-slot gap in effect terms, then look for the frame whose closure predicts its shape. Starting from a preferred fusion method is the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 forbids.
- **Order of settlement (decision):** refuse before mint (Formula step 2). A frame that is held and predicting wrongly gets a refusal before a new fusion term is minted. The refusal is a negation, which is not one of the seven bonds (Operators table :70-74). A vocabulary with subtraction but no negation can say what is missing and cannot say that a reading is wrong.

Then, after arrival: narrowing happens at the ground, and *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"* (Fig 13 :138-139). Every cut is kept ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41).

### Q5. The cascade analogue

Fig 11 :52-53 is the canonical example of order in a habitat: *"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

A data product has the same structure. Its first-order contacts are its users and the variables it reports. Its second-order contacts are the decisions those users take. Its third-order contacts are the consequences to people and places that never saw the product. A responsible combination that stops at first-order contact has enumerated the wrong habitat. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A knowledge holder, a satellite and an archive each change valence as the residual changes, so no standing roster of roles survives a change of question.

**Falsifier:** a habitat boundary drawn at first-order contact that still predicts a third-order harm on a held-out case. If it does, the cascade requirement adds nothing for that domain.

### Q6. Two currencies, not one

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. In this question, the four frames sit at opposite ends: a satellite product is cheap to describe at scale and expensive to settle at a specific place; a knowledge holder's observation is expensive to describe in a portable format and cheap to settle among people who were there. A single "data quality score" that sums them is the error Fig 12 :130 names, not the fix.

## CONCLUSION

The four frames form a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition, so the union is unbundled and verdicts are kept per domain. The dependency order runs perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry, with the middle link currently the one without an instrument. Over a shared substrate the frames can be read as first-order `state → structure → state` molecules; the isomer reading holds only while the full atom set is shared, and the functional-group reading is the honest fallback. Discovery order (residual first) and settlement order (refuse before mint) are separate tracks. The habitat must be enumerated to cascades, and description cost and settlement cost cannot be added. No figure line selects the fusion algorithm, so that remains a declared `gap`.
