**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether "same information" means anything

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the split, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on one domain):** the question "do different physical systems instantiate the same information, or only structurally similar representations?" is a bundle of four questions with four different anchors, not one binary.
>
> 1. **Measure:** the unit of comparison is a per-slot residual against an anchor that does not move when the comparing frame is pushed. A global "same information" averages away the slot where equality fails.
> 2. **Arrangement:** the bond carries information the operands do not. Same atoms with a different typed bond is a different substance (a chemical isomer), not a similar representation of the same one. Identity has a substitution test: two names, one referent, and nothing lost on substitution.
> 3. **Carrier:** different physical carriers can reach one term by convergent derivation, so substrate identity is not required for reach. But the reach is not free. Bits price the description and actions price finding out whether it holds; those are two currencies that do not add.
> 4. **Source:** a match at the level of a reading does not determine a match at the level of the source. Metameric colours match under one illuminant and fail under another. The framework returns a shape here, not a name. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument.
>
> So the honest answer to the question is neither of the offered options. Different physical systems instantiate the same information only when the same bonded arrangement is settled against the same ground-anchored per-slot residual with a recorded lineage. Structural similarity alone is not enough. Substrate difference is admitted in the reach and priced in the cost.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all universal computers carry literally identical information in every respect (energy and material costs differ), that a reading match establishes a source match, that any consensus of experts makes two representations the same, or that the specific source in a case can be named by the framework. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Agreement within one evidence pool is a convention, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. two systems with identical atoms, bonds and arrangement, settled against the same anchor, that yield different per-slot residuals;
> 2. two systems with different bonded arrangements that carry identical settled information;
> 3. an instrument that reads the source rather than the projection, so a reading match entails a source match under change of illuminant and observer;
> 4. a single frame that certifies its own sameness verdict with no differing closure and no ground reading;
> 5. derivation of one of the six terms from the other five, or of a typed bond from its operands alone;
> 6. one accounting in which bits and actions add to a single sameness figure with no residual;
> 7. two systems with different carriers that instantiate one term at identical description cost, identical action cost, and zero round-trip gap.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding verdict is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace.
> - Foundational Questions Institute, public essay and grant competitions: https://fqxi.org
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Information: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Semantic Conceptions of Information: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information-semantic/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the four domain verdicts and R1 to R7. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figures and the two SEP pages. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A Stage 1 refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated as if it came from any other source.
