## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether two systems *look* alike. It is what would have to be true for the phrase "the same information" to be licensed at all. Write the two options as rival predictions:

- Option A, "different systems instantiate the same information": leads to the prediction that a difference in physical carrier is not a difference in the information, so an instrument checking sameness need only find the invariant structure and need not match the substrate.
- Option B, "only structurally similar representations": leads to the prediction that resemblance is the strongest available claim, so an instrument checking sameness finds only a graded similarity and never identity.

A third possibility, not offered by the question, is that the binary is a **bundle** of distinct quantities with distinct anchors. That is the reading derived here.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions under which "same information" is licensed across two systems, plus an instrument that would discriminate a sameness of carrier from a sameness of arrangement, from a sameness of source, and from a sameness of measure. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` refuses A or B as a forced choice. It splits the question into four domains, keeps their verdicts separate and never averaged, and reports one declared `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the information question, and no claim that the framework is the only correct account of information.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
