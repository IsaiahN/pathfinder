## Five findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The measure is blind to arrangement.** SEP Information notes that a string of five zeros followed by five ones carries the same Shannon information as a mixed string with the same symbol counts. This is the flat-formula failure Fig 12 names from inside the framework. It is why an information-theoretic equality is the weakest of the four senses of "same" and cannot by itself settle the question.
2. **Sameness is a provenance claim as much as a pattern claim.** SEP Semantic Conceptions of Information quotes Evans requiring the same initial informational event for two states to embody the same information, and Fodor noting that the same information can be transmitted over many different kinds of channels. These are the lineage and reach conditions the figures derive as D3 and D1.
3. **Substrate independence is admitted at the reach and contested at the cost.** SEP Multiple Realizability and SEP Structural Realism support the reach claim (same function, different realizers; same structure, different ontologies). Thagard 2022 supports the cost objection (bits and joules, neuromorphic versus digital, energy-dependent tipping points). The derivation keeps both and refuses to sum them, under Fig 12's two-currency rule.
4. **Reading-level equality is not source-level identity.** Wikipedia Metamerism gives the engineering case: two spectral power distributions, one tristimulus reading, and a match that fails under a different illuminant or observer. The framework essay makes the same point about an instrument that reads green but cannot comment on the source. This is the D3 `gap`.
5. **Chemistry supplies the exact structural analogue.** Wikipedia Isomer confirms the historical case the figures cite: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have identical composition and different properties, which forced structure into chemical notation in 1824 to 1830. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is a low-overlap corroboration of Fig 12 :117, not an analogy imported into the framework.

No world source was treated as a verdict witness. All corroborate or conflict with shape claims that were derived from the figures first. The Thagard conflict was returned to the domain census and narrowed the D1 verdict rather than flipping it.

## Distribution - offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four verdicts and R1 to R7; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Selecting recipients by likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* This question is foundational, so the cost of being wrong is high and independence is worth paying for.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four domain verdicts and R1 to R7; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only if asked, the formula as a method to reuse with the user's own refuter and mode.
