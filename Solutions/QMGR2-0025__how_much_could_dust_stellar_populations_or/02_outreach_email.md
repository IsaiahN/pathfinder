**Subject:** One check on a per-slot error-budget claim before you decide if the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about
> distance-ladder systematics. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not
> asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the disagreement between the local distance ladder
> and the CMB-inferred Hubble constant should not be reported as a single systematic error budget.
> The residual is indexed per object and per slot, so the three effects you know as dust extinction,
> stellar-population and crowding differences, and distance-ladder choices (rung, anchor,
> photometric system) must be kept as separate live slots. A total near zero with one live slot is
> a legal state, not a resolved one. The figures I work from state this directly: *"The residual is
> indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal
> disappears into an average."*
>
> Two consequences follow and are checkable without the theory. First, the ground does not decay,
> the channel does, so these three effects are defects in the measurement channel, not changes in
> the expansion rate. Second, agreement between two analyses that share Gaia parallaxes, WFC3 zero
> points or the same SN compilation is not corroboration; it is one evidence pool talking. The
> figure rule is *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an
> evidence pool."* The TRGB-versus-Cepheid comparison with no measurements in common is the clean
> case of the opposite.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a numerical size for any slot. The framework returns
> a shape, not a number, so I am not saying dust contributes X km/s/Mpc or crowding contributes Y.
> I am not claiming the tension is solved by systematics, nor that it is definitely new physics. I
> am not claiming these three effects are independent of each other; they share calibration. Null
> tests are bounds at the resolution reached, not proofs that a slot is dead.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this email):**
> 1. a figure line that returns the numerical magnitude of a systematic in km/s/Mpc;
> 2. a demonstration that dust, populations and ladder choices are one slot with one mechanism;
> 3. a case where two frames sharing all calibration inputs still count as independent;
> 4. a demonstration that a dust or population change moves the ground and not the reading;
> 5. a sealed frame in which a null search would be a proof of absence.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to
> me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know
> well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it,
> and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> - Isaiah (framework author)
