## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a controller must fully understand the internal structure of a complex networked system in order to control it. Option A: yes, full internal understanding is a prerequisite, so control is deferred until the model is complete. Option B: no, control is possible when a ground anchor fixes the target, a lever in the habitat changes the per-node residual, the channel keeps readings unpooled, and a differing closure supplies the lever when it is not derivable locally. What each leads to: A makes control unreachable in principle or at budget for an open system and treats completeness as a verdict; B makes it a conditional yes with four separable tests, and treats completeness as a budget filter.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what the controller's model predicts and what the system does, `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`, indexed per node slot. Arity: controller, action, observation, plus a structure operand because the system is a network. Symmetry: the target is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join; interventions are ordered (Fig 12 :70, :76) and closed control is a cycle (Fig 12 :121). Scale: single node loop, network collective, and the membrane between them (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, controller loop and model.** Actors: controller, `Γ`, `b`, action `a`, per-slot `R`. Currency: residual reduction at the ground. Anchor: the measured target variable (Fig 2 :29). Admitted.
- **Domain 2, system as habitat.** Actors: nodes, edges, conditions, relations, introduced levers. Currency: contact, cascade, introduction. Anchor: what the ground returns when the system runs (Fig 11 :12-19). Admitted.
- **Domain 3, reach and knowledge limit.** Actors: frames, `atoms(Γ)`, `closure(Γ)`, imports. Currency: reach versus library size. Anchor: ground verification of an imported lever. Admitted.
- **Domain 4, verification channel.** Actors: instruments, metrics, seats, percepts. Currency: maintained contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **X1, full internal understanding as prerequisite.** No nameable actors, no currency, no anchor. It is a preference held as a shape, not a term (Fig 5 :61-65). Not a domain yet.
- **X2, control as negotiation with the model.** Absorbed into Domains 1 and 4; agreement is not an anchor.
- **X3, the system as a sealed room, fully knowable in principle.** Refused by Fig 9 :44 and Fig 8 :32.

**Union, not partition.** Control is C1 AND C2 AND C3, plus C4 when the lever is imported. The conditions overlap: an unobserved lever fails both C2 and C3. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Measure the target per slot, state the objective in the system's own terms, plan the lever, verify and carry the method. Refuse the completeness preference before minting (formula step 2).

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :40 (contact) and Fig 6 :23 (closure) govern different objects, capability in a habitat versus composition inside a frame, so no strain after re-census. Fig 8 :32 (no search adds an atom) and the existence of data-driven control are compatible because input-output data are an import, not internal composition.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict: yes, conditionally, and `specified`, not `grounded`.** Full internal understanding is not necessary for control. Necessary and jointly sufficient, per the census: C1 a ground anchor that fixes the target and does not move with the model; C2 an introduced lever that changes the per-node residual; C3 a channel that keeps readings per-node and unpooled; C4 an import from a differing closure when the lever is not derivable locally. The metric is per-slot residual at the ground, not model coverage. Capability belongs to controller-and-habitat, so enlarging `Γ` without changing contact changes nothing. Missing leverage is an import, not a failure of understanding. Control is a maintained cycle, not a one-shot plan. Which lever works for a particular network is `gap`, to be closed by measurement and provenanced import. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The Liu et al. apparent conflict was returned to the census and re-mapped; it did not change the verdict. The low-overlap world fields corroborate the mechanism shape, not the specific claim for any one system.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected effort. A system failing C3 would be sold a better model, or a system failing C4 would be sold more internal search. Because the four conditions are kept separate and the refuters are recorded, an error is findable rather than invisible. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is bounded and largely reversible.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five refuters are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: identical contact but different model completeness yielding different outcomes; a target with no fixed anchor; a lever that changes no slot while contact is held; leverage underivable and unreachable at budget (which converts the specific case to `unreachable` and refutes a universal yes); or a new atom arising from composition alone inside a closed `Γ`. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot controllability is fully predicted by model coverage would also reverse it.
