# The Tether's answer: controlling a complex networked system without full internal understanding

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A controller acts on a complex networked system and observes `o'`. The question is whether the controller must hold a complete internal model of the system for its actions to drive the target. Restated as an effect: must `Γ` contain all atoms and bonds of the system, or can `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` be driven down at the controlled slots with a partial `Γ` that nevertheless has an anchor, a lever and a channel?

This is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*). Its conditions have to be answered separately and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The metric is at the ground, not in the model.** Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Coverage, compression and number of predicates are frame-internal (Fig 1 :24-27) and are filters for budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
2. **Capability is controller-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* An enlarged internal model that changes nothing at the ground changes nothing.
3. **A missing lever is owed from outside the closure.** Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Searching the model harder cannot manufacture a lever. Import can, with a source gate and a shadow test.
4. **The channel fails first.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26 names *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Most losses of control are channel failures, not model failures.
5. **Control is a cycle, not a plan.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A one-shot plan cannot close under disturbance.
6. **The specific lever is not named.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Which nodes to pin, which edges to change, and how much control energy is required are `gap` items in this package, to be closed by measurement and provenanced import.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that control is always possible. The verdict is conditional on C1 to C4; where no anchor exists, the answer is no, and where no lever is reachable at budget, the answer is `unreachable` for that case.
- Not that observability implies controllability, or that a driver-node set is a complete controller. Those are claims from a world frame and need their own ground checking.
- Not that model-based control is useless. A model is a head start when it changes contact (Fig 4 :22); it is a filter, not a witness.
- Not that agreement among experts settles it. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a system was taken by this agent.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A ground check, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read this session), an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Anchor test:** does the target reading stay fixed when the controller's internal model is deliberately changed? If it moves with the model, C1 fails.
2. **Lever test:** introduce a candidate lever and measure `R` per slot. If contact is maintained and no slot moves, C2 fails and Fig 11 :40 is refuted.
3. **Channel test:** check for saturated metrics, pooled readings and stale percepts (Fig 10 :26). A lost signal here is a channel failure, not a controllability verdict.
4. **Import test:** can a differing frame supply a lever that the controller cannot derive, with a source gate and a shadow test? If the leverage is underivable and unreachable at budget, the case is `unreachable`.
5. **Standing refuters from `01` §4:** identical contact but different model completeness yielding different outcomes; a target with no fixed anchor; a lever that changes nothing; a new atom from composition alone inside a closed `Γ`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, for the general question.** The four conditions are reachable in many real systems, and the world sources opened this session show parts of them in practice (data-driven control, structural controllability, adaptive management, feedback control without internal models). But no figure line names a lever for a particular network, and this agent took no measurement. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that internal structure is irrelevant, and it does not name a control algorithm. It says that the metric of control is the per-node residual against a ground anchor, not how much of the system the controller can describe; that capability belongs to the controller-and-its-habitat, so a bigger model that changes nothing at the ground changes nothing; that a missing lever must come from a different frame, because searching inside a closed model cannot add one; and that most failures of control are failures of the measurement channel rather than of understanding. Control is a maintained cycle of anchor, lever and unpooled readings, not a one-shot plan. The framework returns that shape, writes its refuters in advance, and leaves the choice of lever to measurement. The answer is yes, conditionally, and the conditions are the content of the claim.
