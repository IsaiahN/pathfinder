## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond carries causation?

- Fig 12 :77, production: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Test: *"is there a B before A fires? No."*
- Fig 12 :72, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Test: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- Fig 12 :87, comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.
- Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* gives the structural atom for influence.

Causation in the strong, physical sense maps to **production ⇒** plus network influence. Folk causal judgment often tracks regularity or counterfactual dependence, where both relata are already present in the description, which fits **sequence →** better than production. The figures do not decide between these two uses of one word; they define bonds. **Binding, not derivation.**

**Strain, named:** the "did not exist before" clause of ⇒ is not satisfied by a regularity judgment. Whether a regularity-based causal concept is a composition over ⇒ or over → is an instrument question (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).

### Q2 - Which bond carries agency?

- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.
- Fig 1 :11: *"residual R indexed by object slot"* gives the tracked actor/patient slot.
- Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"* describes the gradient, which fits directed effort once a state is bonded to it.

So agency maps to **comparison ⋛ (goal)** over an **actor slot**, with a gradient-like directedness. Again a binding. The comparison bond's threshold reading (Fig 12 :87) is not obviously the folk sense of "wants", so this mapping carries the same strain type as Q1.

### Q3 - Are agency and causation isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As written from the folk concepts:

- Causation uses `{actor/outcome states, Network influence, ⇒ production}`.
- Agency uses `{actor slot, Gradient directedness, ⋛ comparison}`.

They do **not** share the same atom set, so by Fig 12 :117 they are **not isomers**. They are two members of a functional group: both are directed compositions over states and structures in which one operand is tracked and another is compared or produced (Fig 12 :124, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*).

The stronger form: if both are rewritten over the **full shared six-term set** `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair and which bond is charged, then they **are** isomers - same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the checkable structural claim worth carrying into the expert packet. It mirrors the exemplar's treatment of GR and QM: the weaker functional-group reading as written, the isomer reading over the full shared set.

### Q4 - What order does the comparison have?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"*. The order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The universal/core question must be answered at the perception and vocabulary links before the cultural-carving question can be asked at the carry link. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A concept that has not settled against the ground cannot serve as an atom for a cross-cultural comparison.

**Analogue from the world literature (secondary).** Berlin and Kay's basic colour terms, as recorded in the public reference page *Color term*: *"there are a limited number of universal basic color terms which begin to be used by individual cultures in a relatively fixed order"*, while *"other languages have between 2 and 12"*. That is exactly the shape predicted here: a universal order of availability (structure) plus a cultural choice of how many cuts to make (convention). It does not decide the agency or causation question; it shows that the shape "universal constraint plus cultural carve" is a real, documented pattern.

### Q5 - Why the mappings are bindings, not derivations

Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* They're Scanning Books: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."*

The six are already held atoms, so naming Network influence or the ⇒ bond is permitted; claiming that the framework derives the folk concept "cause" as a primitive is not. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Arrangements are unbounded, so a particular carve has to be provenanced from a frame, not declared from the substrate.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

If either a regularity-based causal judgment or a goal-ascription judgment can be shown to require an atom the other does not, over the full shared six-term set, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If swapping ⇒ for → or ⋛ for a non-comparative bond changes no ground prediction in a cross-cultural task, the specific bond bindings in F3 are decoration and are withdrawn, while the universal-as-shape claim in F2 remains untouched. If a single culture is found whose carve order contradicts the predicted universal order while still solving the same ground task, the analogue in Q4 is narrowed to colour naming only.

## CONCLUSION

Causation binds to production ⇒ plus network influence; agency binds to the goal comparison ⋛ plus the actor slot and a directed gradient. Both are `specified` bindings with a named strain on the bond type, not derivations. As folk concepts they are **not isomers**; over the full shared atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The order of comparison is set by Fig 3's dependency chain, with a documented analogue in Berlin and Kay's colour-term hierarchy. What this rescues is the structural claim without the overreach: no new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no claim that the framework names a folk concept.
