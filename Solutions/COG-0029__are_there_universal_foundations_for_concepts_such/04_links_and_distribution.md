## Wider literature that bears on the question

These are secondary corroboration only. The figures decide. Each source was opened this session and is recorded with a quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

1. **Cross-cultural causal attribution.** Choi, Nisbett and Norenzayan, 1999, *"Causal attribution across cultures: Variation and universality."* Psychological Bulletin 125(1), 47-63. The title itself holds the two halves of this question. Morris and Peng, 1994, *"Culture and cause: American and Chinese attributions for social and physical events."* JPSP 67(6), 949-971. Verified through Crossref metadata records. These predict a real learned layer in attribution and a shared core in physical-event reasoning. Corroborates the split in F1.
2. **A contested result.** Ge, 2025, *"Second Thoughts About Culture and Cause: Why and How Do the Chinese and Americans Differ in Causal Attributions?"* Personality and Social Psychology Review 30(1), 29-54. The abstract says *"empirical studies yield mixed findings."* This corroborates F7: the middle link lacks an instrument, and even a large literature does not settle it. It does not flip the figure verdict; it sends us back to the census for a cleaner task.
3. **Universal constraints with cultural carving.** Berlin and Kay's basic colour terms, as recorded in the public reference page *Color term*: *"there are a limited number of universal basic color terms which begin to be used by individual cultures in a relatively fixed order"*, with *"other languages have between 2 and 12"*. This is the closest well-documented analogue for F2 and F4: a universal constraint on carve order plus a cultural choice of where to cut.
4. **Agency as a predictive stance.** Dennett's intentional stance, as recorded in the public reference page *Intentional stance*: *"first you decide to treat the object whose behavior is to be predicted as a rational agent; then you figure out what beliefs that agent ought to have, given its place in the world and its purpose."* The page also gives Rosch's criterion that a working category system corresponds to *"the perceived structure of the real world"* and *"maximum information with the least cognitive effort"*. Corroborates the ground-tracked reading of agency in F2 and F3.
5. **Folk psychology as a universal practice.** The public reference page *Folk psychology*: *"When perceiving, explaining, or criticizing human behaviour, people distinguish between intentional and unintentional actions."* Corroborates the actor/outcome core, while leaving carving to culture.
6. **Causal structure as a portable shape.** The public reference page *Causal inference*: *"The approaches to causal inference are broadly applicable across all types of scientific disciplines"*, listing *"Pearl's structural causal model (causal diagram + do-calculus)"*. Corroborates the claim that causal structure is a shape that travels across frames, which is the Figure 6 effect-term requirement in F5.
7. **Comparative frame.** Public reference pages *Theory of mind* and *Animal cognition* describe the comparative case. These are low overlap with the framework's author and are useful for the task design in F8, though neither settles the question.

The eight essays share an author with the figures, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight. The cross-cultural, linguistic, philosophical and comparative sources above are the genuinely low-overlap witnesses. Where they conflict, the conflict was recorded (see item 2) and sent back to the census; it did not flip a verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if a universal core existed before giving them the article.
- **Fig 9 :30-31** *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"* - do not ask them to compare the words for agency across languages.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Never select recipients by likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* - a later endorsement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the split and the shape claim; (2) the pre-registered refuter and the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record the answer; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with a third optional stage

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a recipient holds the interiors, their agreement stops being evidence.
2. **Stage 2:** if curious, read the source links. The figures govern; the essays and the world literature only corroborate.
3. **Stage 3:** the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending methods. Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because Formula step "Direction" says a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The bond mappings in F3 are currently `specified` and strained. Send them as bindings with provenance or omit them; do not present them as derivations.
