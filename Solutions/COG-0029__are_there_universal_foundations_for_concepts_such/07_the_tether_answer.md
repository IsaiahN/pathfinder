# The Tether's answer: universal foundations or culturally learned concepts?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route to a ground. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Human frames everywhere track an actor and an outcome, and cultures differ in how they carve, name and assign responsibility for those events. The question offers one choice, universal foundations or substantially culturally learned. Fig 9 :54 says: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The residual is a union compound: a perceptual core, an agency-ascription layer, and a cultural convention layer, each with a different anchor. One answer to A or B cannot settle all three (F1).

## 2. The shape

1. **The either/or fails; the question must be split.** Causation is Fig 12 :77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*; agency involves a goal, Operators table :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. These are different bond types, and the cultural layer is a third question about conventions. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
2. **The universal is a constraint, not a name.** Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* The universal is the requirement that any frame in contact with this habitat land on a ground-tracked actor/outcome structure, and the requirement that it do so at low overlap with other frames. It is not a list of folk concepts.
3. **The framework cannot name which folk concept is universal.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Selecting production ⇒ for causation or comparison ⋛ for agency is a binding from the cognitive frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation. Those bindings carry a named strain (F3).
4. **The cultural layer is a convention, not a failed verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* A culture's carve and name are a real product of shared meaning. They become evidence about the world only if they change a ground prediction.
5. **Comparison must use effects, not words.** Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* The travelling description is: given this event, which actor is tracked, which outcome is predicted, what changes when the actor is removed.
6. **Independence decides what agreement means.** Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Low-contact convergence supports a universal; a shared translation or media channel makes the populations one witness.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "all humans are born with the concept of cause". The universal is a constraint over held atoms and a convergence criterion, not a folk name.
- Not "culture is irrelevant". The cultural layer is the product of a language community and has a real function (Fig 2 :41).
- Not a derivation of the folk concepts from the six-term set. The bond mappings in F3 are bindings, `specified` and strained.
- Not an empirical finding that a universal core exists here. That row is `gap` at this budget (F9): no cross-cultural ground task was run.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles the boundary between the two layers in this package.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

The anchor is a **low-contact, cross-cultural and comparative ground task**, not researcher agreement and not translation equivalence. It must satisfy three conditions:

- (a) the populations compared have independent contact histories, so agreement is not one evidence pool;
- (b) the task is stated in effect terms (which actor is tracked, which outcome predicted, what changes when the actor is removed), not in translated words;
- (c) the task yields a residual that persists and does not trend down under one option and shrinks under the other. That is the shrinking test of Formula step 2, read with the opposite sign.

Standing falsifiers, written before the run (§4 of `01`): low-contact populations with no actor/outcome distinction in a ground task; a naming-only manipulation that changes the ground prediction; a shared channel explaining the convergence; no differential task at all, which is a `gap`, not an answer. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six"* (They're Scanning Books). Declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the instruments available here and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. How Intelligence Actually Works: *"the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "universal or learned" with a single word. It splits the question. Causation and agency use different bonds, and the cultural carving is a third thing again. What may be universal is not a folk concept but a shape: any frame in contact with this habitat must land on a ground-tracked actor and outcome structure, and it must do so by convergent derivation from frames with independent evidence. What is culturally learned is the carve, the name and the attribution of responsibility, which are agreements about shared meaning and are the product of a language community, not a failed verdict about the world. The framework returns that shape, not a name, so it does not say which folk concept is universal. What it offers is the split, the effect description that can travel between cultures, the refuters written in advance, and the kind of low-contact ground task that would close the boundary. The empirical existence of the core stays open here: no such task was run.
