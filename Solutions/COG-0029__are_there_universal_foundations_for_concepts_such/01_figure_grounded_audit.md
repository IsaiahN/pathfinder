## 0. The residual in effect terms

R is the observation that human frames everywhere form concepts of agency (someone acted, with a goal) and causation (something produced something else), while the carve, the name, and the attribution of responsibility vary across cultures. The question asks to choose:

- **Option A, universal foundations:** the shared outline is a ground-tracked constraint that any frame in the human habitat must land on. Predicts convergence at maximum independence, invariance under renaming, and a core that survives contact with the world even when language is stripped away.
- **Option B, substantially culturally learned:** the shared outline is a convention produced by shared language and history. Predicts variation that tracks cultural contact and naming, and no invariant that survives an independent ground test.

What an answer would look like, bound before the run as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :65-66 *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*): **a universal foundation is a constraint that survives convergent derivation at low overlap, not a name in a vocabulary.** A culturally learned layer is a convention about shared meaning that changes no ground prediction. The verdict is which sub-residuals fall under which definition, and what instrument would separate them.

The two options are not exhaustive. That is the first finding.

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least three sub-residuals, (i) the perceptual core of causation, (ii) agency detection and goal-ascription, (iii) the cultural carving, naming and responsibility layer. They do not share one ground. The Library of Babel essay names this case: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*
- **Symmetry:** neither concept is a symmetric join. Causation is directed production, Fig 12 :77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Agency is an actor/patient asymmetry with a goal, Operators table :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. The question's "or" treats two different bond types as interchangeable alternatives.
- **Scale:** individual frame (one lifetime), population transmission (many agents across generations, Fig 4), ground and habitat (selection, Fig 11), and the research frame that tries to compare them.

### One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, individual perceptual frame | object slots s, percepts, beliefs b, priors w, actions a | per-slot residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|, prediction then correction | the ground o', what actually happened | yes: experiment / correction |
| D2, cultural transmission | agents, shared library, language, conventions | methods vs recordings, head starts, shared meaning | only while the convention still ranges against the ground | conditional: if a culture only checks against itself, no anchor (Fig 2 :13-21, :33-34) |
| D3, ground and habitat | habitat, sensorium, conditions, relations, selection | contact, what the ground kept | the ground, which does not update | yes: cross-cultural data, comparative data |
| D4, research / verification frame | researchers, instruments, cultures as frames | agreement vs residual | experiments and cross-cultural measurements, not researcher consensus | yes, but in this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool |

D2 has a **conditional** anchor. When a cultural frame only updates on itself, the anchor disappears and the frame becomes Fig 2's panel 1: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* leading to *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"* (Fig 2 :13-15). No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain that can settle a world question. It is still a domain for the question about meaning, because Fig 2 :41 makes agreement about shared meaning the product of that frame.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The universal question and the learning question overlap: a concept can have a ground-shaped core and a conventional carving at the same time. A single lever that clears the region where only one holds leaves the intersection standing. Split before searching.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the frame tell what is in front of it and tell it apart from what was there before (Fig 3 :4-5).
2. **Vocabulary:** does it have terms for what situations afford, what can be acted on and caused (Fig 3 :6-7).
3. **Objective:** can it say what would count as progress in its own terms (Fig 3 :9-10).
4. **Planning, then learn and carry** (Fig 3 :12-15).

The chain typically breaks between 2 and 3. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Both cultures have words for agency and causation; neither has an instrument for what would count as a change in the underlying concept. That is why the argument does not converge.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The either/or is a union compound, so the question must be split (decisive, structural)

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The Library of Babel essay gives the same instruction in plain terms: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

Applying it: "agency and causation" are at least two different bonds. Causation is Fig 12 :77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*; agency involves a goal, Operators table :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. The "culturally learned" layer is a third question about conventions, not about either bond. One answer cannot settle all three.

**Refuter:** exhibit one measurement that changes the verdict on both the perceptual core and the cultural carving without splitting them, or show that the three sub-residuals share one anchor. Until then, answering A or B as written is a category error.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 + Fig 3 + Library of Babel essay, low overlap between a composition law, a dependency chain and a search essay).

### F2 - The universal layer is a habitat-shaped constraint, not a list of folk concepts

Fig 13 :3: *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."* Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

Under the six-term substrate, causation and agency are compositions over states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), not primitives. Fig 13 :24 gives the structural atom for causal influence: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*.

What makes the outline universal is not that everyone has the word. It is Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* And Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

The RSI Trap essay ties this directly to the question: *"Unless you tie it to something all humans have. Something that does not change because culture changes. The priors and primitives are the thing you tie it to, but you grow the learning aligned by the ground and the architecture."*

Crucially, the framework returns a shape. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. So the universal is the **requirement** that a ground-tracked composition sit between state and state, plus the requirement that frames in contact with a shared habitat converge on it at low overlap. It is not the claim that every culture uses the word "causation" or the same bond.

**Refuter:** derive one of the six from the other five; or find a population in full contact with the same habitat that converges on no actor/outcome distinction at all in a ground task; or show a shared contact channel that explains the apparent convergence (then it is one pool).

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 13 structural census + Fig 8 independence law + Fig 11 selection law; low overlap between them).

### F3 - The specific bond mappings are bindings, not derivations, and one is strained

Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* is the production bond ⇒. Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

So the natural figure-side bindings are: causation to production (⇒) and network influence (Fig 13 :24), agency to the goal comparison (⋛) and to the actor slot s (Fig 1 :11 *"residual R indexed by object slot"*). These are **bindings from the cognitive frame onto atoms already held**, not derivations from the six. Fig 5 :65-66 forbids treating the sought-for shape as a hidden term; here the shape is held and the bond choice is a provenanced import.

**Strain, named:** ⇒ is defined as producing an operand that *"did not exist before"*. Folk causal judgments frequently track regularity or counterfactual dependence where both events already exist in the description. Whether ⇒ is the right bond for a given causal concept is an instrument question, not an argument. The same applies to ⋛ as a goal: Fig 8's threshold reading of comparison may not be the folk sense of "wants".

**Refuter:** show that swapping ⇒ for sequence (→) or ⋛ for a non-comparative bond changes no cross-cultural prediction and no ground task. Then the mapping is decoration, not content.

**Status: single-source** (Fig 12 / Operators table for the bond definitions), with the applicability strain left open. Not averaged with F2.

### F4 - The culturally learned layer is a convention, which is a different quantity, not a defect

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*.

So Option B is partly right in a way that does not threaten Option A. A culture's shared carving of agency and causation is a convention about shared meaning. Conventions are the product of a language community; they are not failed verdicts about the world. Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

The Tacit Knowledge essay names the mechanism: *"Culture is a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge. Its members can hold very different mindsets; what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against."* What travels across generations is Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*, not the experience that grounded the original carving.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where a purely conventional naming choice changes a ground prediction in a causal or agency task. Then the layer is not only a convention.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 valence rule + Fig 10 grounding rule + Tacit Knowledge essay on collective tacit knowledge).

### F5 - Cross-cultural comparison must describe effects, not words

Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*

Comparing the word for "agency" in two languages compares terms borrowed from the two frames. The effect description that travels is: given this event, which actor does the frame track, which outcome does it predict, and what changes if the actor is removed. The Tacit Knowledge essay: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*

**Refuter:** show a word-level comparison that predicts a ground difference without any effect description. Then the words carry world information and this finding narrows.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 + Fig 6 + Tacit Knowledge essay).

### F6 - Independence decides what cross-cultural agreement means

Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 7 :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Therefore: isolated cultures arriving at the same actor/outcome structure is evidence for a universal; cultures with a shared missionary, translation or media channel arriving at the same structure is one pool talking. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A failed search for a universal is not a proof that none exists.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that the apparently isolated cultures shared the relevant channel, or that they differed on a ground task while agreeing in word use.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 + Fig 7 + Fig 8, three different figures with different mechanisms).

### F7 - The debate sits at the middle link, which has no instrument

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :25-26 describes why: perception and planning have obvious failures and tests, but *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

Every participant in this debate has terms (agency, cause, responsibility). Nobody has an instrument for what would count as a change in the concept underneath the term. So effort lands above the break, on more vocabularies and more surveys, and the middle link stays unmeasured.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that measures what counts as progress in a concept before the concept is named, and show the two options make different readings on it.

**Status: single-source** (Fig 3), with Fig 12 :115 as corroboration rather than a second witness to the same quantity.

### F8 - The ground that would settle it, named before the run

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

The anchor here is a **cross-cultural, cross-species ground task**, not agreement among researchers and not translation equivalence. Three instruments already exist in the literature and are read in `09_watson_answer.md`: universal carve patterns in colour naming (Berlin and Kay), early theory-of-mind and causal perception tasks, and comparative work on animal causal cognition. None settles this question alone. What would settle it is a task that (a) is run at low contact between populations, (b) reports the effect description rather than the name, and (c) yields a residual that persists and does not trend down under one option and shrinks under the other.

**Refuter, pre-registered before this conclusion:** if no task can be specified on which A and B predict different effects, then the question is underivable at this budget, not open. Fig 8 :39 stops us from calling that a proof of absence. If a task is specified and B's prediction survives while A's core fails to converge at low overlap, Option A is withdrawn. If A's core converges and B's predicted cultural variance appears only in naming and boundary carving, Option B is narrowed to the conventional layer. If a bond mapping from F3 fails to change any prediction, it is withdrawn as decoration.

**Status: specified; the run itself was not performed here.**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Fig 5 :65-66 (*"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working"*). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One of the six substrate terms is derived from the other five, or a composition across the two kinds is shown not to be required (breaks F2).
2. Two genuinely low-overlap populations, both in contact with the relevant habitat, converge on no actor/outcome distinction in a ground task (breaks the convergence criterion in F2).
3. A naming-only manipulation changes the ground prediction in a causal or agency task with the effect description held fixed (breaks F4 and F5).
4. The bond mappings in F3 are swapped with no change in any prediction (breaks the specific bindings, not the shape).
5. No differential task can be specified (then the question is underivable at this budget, recorded as `gap`, not answered).

If any of 1 to 4 fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. Until a task is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One question or several?** Several, a union compound. Fig 9 :54-58 + Library of Babel bundle passage.
2. **What is the universal in effect terms?** A cross-kind constraint that frames in contact with a shared habitat converge on at low overlap, not a name. Fig 13 :74; Fig 8 :50; Fig 11 :36.
3. **Does the substrate name agency or causation?** No. It holds network influence and cross-kind composition; the folk concepts are bindings onto held atoms. Fig 13 :24, :135.
4. **Which bonds?** Causation to ⇒ and network influence; agency to ⋛ (goal) and the actor slot. Single-source and strained, F3.
5. **What is the learned layer?** Conventions about shared meaning and collective tacit knowledge, carried as methods and head starts. Fig 2 :41; Fig 10 :18; Fig 4 :22.
6. **How must comparison be described?** In effect terms, before picking a frame. Fig 9 :27-31; Fig 6 :30.
7. **What settles it, and where is the stop?** A low-contact ground task with a persistent residual under one option, not agreement. No such run here, so `gap` for the empirical existence claim. Fig 2 :29; Fig 8 :39; Fig 13 :135.

**Verdict of this audit:** the structural claims survive and are triangulated; the specific bond bindings are `specified`, single-source and strained; the empirical existence of a universal core is `gap` at this budget. Re-census after the canonical check: one strain (bond type), named, none hidden.
