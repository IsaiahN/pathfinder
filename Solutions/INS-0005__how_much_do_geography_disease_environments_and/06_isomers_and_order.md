# Union, currency and bond: the supporting derivation for INS-0005

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `gap` on the aggregate magnitude.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Given conditions (geography, disease, endowments) and political choice are asked to yield one comparative magnitude for institutional development. Three supporting questions are owed: is the bundle a union or a partition (Q1), are the operands measurable in one currency (Q2), and what bond is actually being asserted when we say "compared with" (Q3). A fourth question asks whether cases share a functional group or are isomers (Q4), and a fifth locates the dependency break (Q5).

Atoms held: four named conditions and an institutional outcome. Bonds held: the seven, including conjunction `+`, sequence `→`, production `⇒`, subtraction `−` and comparison `⋛`; plus negation as the unary operator. No common currency is available without an import, and no import with a source gate and shadow test has been supplied for one.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- The Library of Babel essay puts the same rule in search terms: a bundle of several problems *"masquerading as one"* must be searched individually and then combined.
- Disease depends on geography; settlement depends on disease; endowments shape inequality; inequality shapes political choice; choice rewrites institutions. The conditions overlap. So this is a **union**.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a design shows the four conditions are independent, with no interaction term and additive shares that reproduce the outcome, the union finding fails and a partition is licensed. Triangulated across Fig 9 and the essay (low overlap between a dependency rule and a search essay).

### Q2 — Two currencies or one?

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126-127: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."*
- Fig 12 :129-130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- Material constraint is priced in settlement feasibility and yield. Political choice is priced in a recorded decision and its contingent sequence. There is no conversion factor in the library.
- Therefore a percentage split is a **sum across incommensurable accountings**. Fig 12 names the sum as the error. This is why "three-quarters" (AJR), "about half" (Diamond) and "50 percent" (Diamond reporting the review position) are not rival estimates of one quantity.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a single unit in which both a mortality rate and a political decision are measured, with a conversion that both frames accept as an anchor. If that exists, F3 fails and the magnitude becomes tractable. Triangulated with Fig 3 (different dependency reason for the same separation).

### Q3 — What bond is asserted by "compared with"?

- The Operators table :48-52 defines `⋛` comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* And :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- "How much A compared with B" states a goal in `⋛` form. But `⋛` needs **two quantities**. Here the operands are four overlapping conditions and an institutional outcome, not two quantities at one level. The comparison is **mistyped**: the question asks for a threshold relation between operands that have not been reduced to quantities.
- The symmetric alternative `+` (conjunction, *"both hold at once"*, Operators table :11-15) would say that all four conditions hold together, which is true but is not the comparison the question asked for. Substituting `+` for `⋛` would dodge the question, not answer it.
- **Falsifier:** show that both sides of the comparison are quantities at the same level, so `⋛` is well typed. Until then the comparison stands as a goal, not a measurement. `single-source` on the Operators table definition; corroborated by Fig 12's bond list.

### Q4 — Functional group or isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Two cases of institutional development typically do **not** share all four atoms. One case may be endowment-heavy with weak political choice; another may be choice-heavy with a benign disease environment. Across cases the constant is a **substructure**: contact between a material condition and a rule-making seat. That is Fig 12's functional group. The group says what kind (an institutional-development path); the qualifier says which member (which atom fills which slot).
- The stronger isomer claim would require both cases to be written over the full shared atom set, differing only in arrangement. That is a real but unmeasured claim. As usually written, the cases are **not isomers**; they are members of a functional group.
- **Falsifier:** if two cases can be written with identical atom sets and only the bonds differ, and the behavioural outcomes diverge, the isomer claim holds for that pair. If either case needs an atom the other lacks, the functional-group reading stands. Triangulated for the figure definitions; the case-level application is `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q5 — Where does the dependency chain break?

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*
- Perception works: mortality, land quality, institutional indices are all readings. Vocabulary fails: there is no term for what a disease environment and a political decision **jointly afford** in one accounting. Objective fails with it: no common statement of what would count as a comparative share.
- So the break is between links 2 and 3, not at measurement. More data lands above the break.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a working vocabulary and objective for the joint affordance, so the chain reaches planning. If that exists, the break claim is withdrawn. `single-source` on Fig 3, corroborated by the currency finding.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions form a **union**, not a partition (Q1). Their two accountings **do not add** (Q2). The question's "compared with" is a `⋛` comparison mistyped over operands that are not two quantities (Q3). Across cases the constant is a **functional group**, not an isomer pair, unless a shared atom set is supplied (Q4). The chain breaks at vocabulary and objective, so extra measurement lands above the break (Q5).

What survives is a structural claim: any adequate answer must unbundle the conditions, price each in its own currency, supply a common measure before comparing, and keep the lineage of what was imported. What does not survive is the request for one aggregate share. The falsifiers above are stated before the run; none has fired.
