## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for a comparative magnitude: how much of institutional development is due to geography, disease environment and resource endowments, as against political choices. Stated as an effect, a reader wants a function like

```
institutions = f(geography, disease, endowments, political_choice)
```

with a weight or share assigned to each argument, so that they can decide where an intervention could act. Given conditions cannot be changed; choices can. So the practical stake is: if the weights are known, we know whether institutional reform is a matter of changing rules or of accepting fate.

The residual R is the gap between the observed institutional arrangement and what any one of those arguments predicts on its own. It does not shrink when a single argument is refined. The same disease environment is reported to have produced different institutional paths (Botswana and Sierra Leone share diamonds under different rules, per the Acemoglu/Robinson NYRB letter, 2012). The same geography is reported alongside a "reversal of fortune" in which formerly prosperous colonies became relatively poorer, so the geography did not change while the fortunes did. Both directions are in the world record. R therefore persists under each frame separately.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run:** a shape with four properties, (i) the conditions are unbundled rather than ranked, (ii) each object slot is measured against its own anchor, (iii) the frames are kept independent, (iv) the result stops where the instruments stop and records the lineage. A single number is not the sought-for shape; it is one possible filling of a slot, and the figures do not license it. This binding is written before the derivation so the preference can be refused like anything else (Fig 5 :62-66, THE_FORMULA "Direction").

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** four named conditions, not one. Geography, disease environment, resource endowments and political choice are four arguments of R. This is a **union compound**, not a single hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Symmetry:** the arguments are not interchangeable. A geographic condition persists and constrains settlement; a political choice is an event that selects among rules at a different scale. Swapping them changes the claim. They are not symmetric operands.
- **Scale:** biophysical conditions operate over centuries and continents; political choices operate within polities over years. Two different scales in one comparison is a dependency question, not an averaging question.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 environmental-material | terrain, climate, pathogens, soils, crops, minerals | settlement feasibility and material constraint | yes: mortality rates, land quality, latitude, measured directly | partition boundary (what can physically obtain) |
| D2 institutional-formal | constitutions, property rights, courts, suffrage, bureaucracy | rules and enforcement, a convention that has decided outcomes | yes: an actually observed rule, checked not agreed | partition (which rule is in force) |
| D3 political-agency | leaders, coalitions, voters, revolutions | deliberate choice and contingent sequence | yes, weakly: a recorded decision or documented juncture | union (overlaps D1 and D2) |
| D4 verification and meta | researchers, journals, instruments, indices | agreement and residuals | the ground = institutional outcomes that can be checked, but no controlled origin experiment exists | union (all three above draw on one evidence pool) |

No anchor means no domain. D1 to D3 each have a nameable anchor, so each is a domain. D4's anchor is weak but nameable as the checkable outcome, so it stays, with its independence recorded as near zero.

### Partition or union

Union. Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Disease depends on geography; settlement depends on disease; endowments shape inequality; inequality shapes political choice; political choice rewrites institutions. The intersections are the interesting cases. A question that demands one share per condition is a demand to treat a union as a partition.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

1. **Perception:** are the conditions measured at all? Settler mortality, land quality and institutional indices are all attempted readings.
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what each condition affords? Here is the break. "Institution" and "political choice" have no shared unit with "mortality rate".
3. **Objective:** what would count as institutional change, in a common currency? Not stated.
4. **Planning:** a design that isolates one condition.
5. **Learn and carry:** a method that transfers to the next case.

Most published effort lands at step 1 (more regressions, better mortality data) because that is where instruments exist. Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."* Effort above a break cannot rescue a link that does not exist.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 — The question is a union compound, not one magnitude (controlling finding)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54-55:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The Library of Babel essay (Isaiah N, 2026-10-02) says the same in search terms: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

The geography/institutions debate does not shrink with effort. Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson report that institutions explain *"approximately three-quarters of the income per capita differences across former colonies"* (NBER WP 7771, 2000). Diamond replies that the Neolithic Revolution *"merely"* accounts for *"about half of prosperity differences today around the world as a whole"* (NYRB, 2012). Acemoglu and Robinson answer that geography and disease ecology *"are not the major factors shaping institutional variation today"* (same exchange). Albouy finds *"infinite confidence intervals"* (AER 2012 comment). Four careful parties, four different magnitudes. That is the signature Fig 9 names: split, do not search harder for the single number.

**Verdict on D4 (verification): `triangulated`** across Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay (low overlap: a dependency-chain rule and a search-process essay). The world sources corroborate the persistence of disagreement but do not decide it. Mode `specified`.

### F2 — No anchor settles the comparison

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

Settler mortality was offered as an anchor for the institutional channel. Albouy (AER 2012) reports that *"36 of the 64 countries in the sample are assigned mortality rates from other countries"* and that estimates become *"unreliable, often with infinite confidence intervals"*. Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson reply that the alternative coding is *"coded inconsistently"* and that limiting outliers *"significantly strengthens"* their results (AER 2012 reply). The instrument is real and it is contested. By Fig 2 an instrument that moves when a frame pushes on it is not yet an anchor.

Institutional development is also not a sealed room: there is no experiment that randomly assigns endowments and political rules to comparable polities. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Verdict on D4: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 anchor rule plus the Albouy exchange, which is a different field and a different method from the figure). Mode `specified`.

### F3 — Two currencies do not add (the decisive structural bar)

Fig 12 :125:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126-127:

> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**

Fig 12 :129-130:

> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

D1 to D3 are not two measurements of one quantity. Material constraint is priced in feasibility and yield; political choice is priced in a recorded decision and its contingent sequence. To ask "how much A compared with B" is to ask for a sum in one currency. The figures name that sum as the error, not the fix. This is why "50 percent institutions" (Diamond, quoting the review position in NYRB 2012) and "three-quarters" (AJR) are not two answers to one question: they are readings in different accountings, and neither figure converts the other.

**Verdict across D1/D2/D3: `triangulated`** across Fig 12 and Fig 3 (the dependency order gives a second, independent reason why the arguments cannot be added). Mode `general` on the structural rule.

### F4 — Aggregate residual destroys the per-slot signal

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A global "institutions score" regressed on a global "geography score" is exactly the aggregation Fig 1 forbids. The live signal is per slot: which institution, which disease channel, which endowment, which decision. Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*. A country-level average can look explained while a specific slot is still burning.

**Verdict on D4: `single-source`** for the figure line (Fig 1), corroborated directionally by Fig 9's split rule but not triangulated at maximum independence. Mode `general`.

### F5 — Institutions are a convention that has already decided outcomes

Fig 10 :47:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

An institution starts as a rule people could have chosen otherwise. Once it decides who gets property, schooling and suffrage, it is a constraint on subsequent choices, and subsequent political choices compose over it as over a settled atom. This is why D2 cannot be collapsed into D3: the "political choice" that matters today is often made on top of an institution that was settled long ago. Engerman and Sokoloff (NBER h0066, 1994) document exactly this persistence: *"the roots of these disparities in the extent of inequality lay in differences in the initial factor endowments of the respective colonies"*, with government policies then *"maintain[ing] these conditions along the respective economy's path of development."*

**Verdict on D2: `triangulated`** (Fig 10 rule plus Fig 12 recursion, low overlap between a seat rule and a composition rule). Mode `specified`.

### F6 — Given conditions are habitat, not verdict

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :73:

> **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

Geography, disease and endowments are enumerated habitat (Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*). They are real, they are measurable, and they are where the actors come from. But the habitat is not what settles the institutional question; the outcome is. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Diamonds are a curse in one habitat and a blessing in another, which is precisely the Acemoglu/Robinson observation that *"it is not characteristics of a natural resource but the institutions that determine whether it is a curse or a blessing"* (NYRB 2012).

**Verdict on D1: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 habitat/valence plus Fig 2 anchor test; different figures, low overlap). Mode `specified`. The magnitude is unmeasured.

### F7 — Political choice is an actor in the habitat, not an external switch

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 11 :61-62:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Political choice cannot be subtracted from the habitat; it can only be introduced into an account that already contains it. A comparison that treats it as a separable switch is performing an exclusion the figures forbid. The 2011 PNAS study by Laitin, Moortgat and Robinson finds a positive association between continental axis orientation and linguistic persistence, while explicitly noting that *"state makers have had agency both in determining the borders of their states and in influencing cultural homogenization in them"* (PNAS 2012, published online 2011). The agency is in the same habitat as the axis.

**Verdict on D3: `single-source`** for the figure rule (Fig 11), with the world source corroborating but sharing the geography/institutions evidence pool. Mode `specified`. Magnitude unmeasured.

### F8 — The two frames draw on one evidence pool

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

The environmental frame and the institutional frame in this debate repeatedly use the same colonial sample, the same income data and the same historical cases. Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson's own letter concedes that institutional development *"has been influenced by geography or disease ecology (as our own academic research joint with Simon Johnson has documented)"*. That is an overlap, not an independence. Agreement between such frames is one pool talking.

**Verdict on D4: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 independence plus Fig 7 room rule). Mode `general`.

### F9 — Strain test: the physical six-term mapping is declined here

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

The loop, the anchor rule, the union rule and the two-currency rule are domain-agnostic and apply here. But mapping geography to "space", resources to "energy" and coalitions to "network" would be a strained reading of Fig 13's physical six-term set. Strain test: if a line only fits with a strain, it governs another quantity, so re-census. Re-census result: Fig 13 governs the substrate of physical composition; it is not the authority for a social-science variable list in this question. No six-term mapping is asserted. This is recorded as a named strain, not a hidden one.

**Verdict: no domain derives from the strained mapping.** The verdicts above rest only on the domain-agnostic rules.

### F10 — The numeric magnitude is `gap`, not silence

Fig 8 :39 and Fig 13 :134:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

After a genuine re-census, no figure line licenses a single comparative magnitude. There is no common currency (F3), no shared anchor (F2), a union rather than a partition (F1), and a per-slot residual that aggregation destroys (F4). The requested number is therefore reported as `gap`: unreachable with the concepts and instruments held here. This is a verdict about the form of the question, not a claim that geography or institutions have no effect.

**Verdict on D4: `triangulated`** (Fig 8 and Fig 13, two different limit arguments). Mode `specified`.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single well-defined instrument returns the comparative magnitude and both environmental and institutional frames accept it as an anchor that does not move when either pushes on it (breaks F2).
2. The four conditions are shown to be separable, with additive, non-overlapping shares and no interaction terms, so the union is actually a partition (breaks F1).
3. Material constraint and political choice are shown to be measurable in one common currency, so the two readings add (breaks F3).
4. A figure re-census admits an aggregate residual over all object slots as the legitimate quantity for this question (breaks F4).
5. The institutional rule at issue is shown to be a live convention that has never decided an outcome, so Fig 10 :47 does not apply (breaks F5).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 8. Round trip gap `R_T`

`R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` (Symbols table :75-77). Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

1. **Sent up (x):** Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-57 (split a union), Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot residual), Fig 12 :125-131 (two currencies), Fig 2 :29-30 (anchor).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** "the comparison has no common currency or anchor, so split it and keep per-slot residuals."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete deliverable in `07` and `08`: four domain verdicts, a `gap` on the number, three falsifiers.

**`R_T` is nonzero and mostly benign.** What survives the trip: the union finding, the per-slot rule, the refusal to add currencies. What is dropped: the actual magnitudes (three-quarters, about half, 50 percent, infinite intervals), which are estimand-specific and do not travel. One thing survives with a caveat: "anchor" returns as "contested instrument" rather than "settled measurement". `R_T` was not measured numerically, because the ground does not settle the comparison; it is recorded qualitatively as the set of dropped constraints. Treating a condensed formula as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
