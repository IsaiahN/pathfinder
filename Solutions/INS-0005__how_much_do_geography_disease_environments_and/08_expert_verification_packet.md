# Expert verification packet: the Tether on geography, disease, endowments and political choice

**What this is:** a claim in a form a geographer, economic historian or institutional economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the per-slot rule, `gap` on the single comparative magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No measurement here settles the comparison.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how much do geography, disease environments and resource endowments influence institutional development compared with political choices?" is a union compound, not one well-formed question. The four conditions overlap, so they must be unbundled rather than ranked; each is measured against its own anchor, and settler mortality is an instrument for one slot rather than a verdict on the whole; material constraint and political choice are priced in different currencies that do not add; and a single aggregate percentage averages away the per-slot signal. Institutions are a convention that has already decided outcomes and therefore constrains later political choice, while political choice is an actor inside the habitat, not an external switch. The framework returns that shape and five refuters written in advance. It does not return a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 12

- **Statement:** the bundle is a union, the residual is per slot, and the two currencies do not add. Therefore an aggregate "how much" is not licensed.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
  - Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
  - Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit an additive variance decomposition with no interaction terms that reproduces the institutional outcome (breaks the union and the two-currency finding), or show that an aggregate residual over all slots is the legitimate quantity here.

### C2 — world-facing; checkable against the literature on colonial origins and factor endowments

- **Statement:** the published magnitudes are readings in different accountings drawn from one evidence pool, not rival estimates of one quantity. AJR's NBER WP 7771 says institutions explain *"approximately three-quarters of the income per capita differences across former colonies"*; Diamond in the NYRB exchange says the Neolithic Revolution *"merely"* accounts for *"about half of prosperity differences today around the world as a whole"*; Acemoglu and Robinson in the same exchange say geography and disease ecology *"are not the major factors shaping institutional variation today"*; Albouy (AER 2012) reports *"infinite confidence intervals"* after correcting the mortality data.
- **What to check:** are those four characterisations faithful to the sources? Does the Albouy critique change the estimand or only the estimate? Is the settler-mortality instrument an anchor by Fig 2 :29-30, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"*, given that the answer moves with coding choices?
- **Refuter:** show that all four statements estimate the same quantity on the same sample and that the disagreement is sampling error, not a difference of estimand. Or show that the instrument is invariant to the coding choices Albouy disputes. Either would undermine C2.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation about institutions and choice

- **Statement:** institutions are a settled convention that constrains later political choice; political choice is an actor in the habitat with relative valence.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
  - Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** is there a case in which the institutional rule at issue has never decided an allocation, so Fig 10 :47 does not apply? Is a political choice ever exogenous to the habitat described in Fig 11, rather than one of its actors?
- **Refuter:** an institution shown to be a live convention that has never decided an outcome; or a political choice demonstrably outside all contacts enumerated for the residual. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that geography is destiny, and not that institutions explain a fixed share.
- Not that disease, terrain and endowments have no effect. They are habitat and are enumerated.
- Not that political choice is free, or that it can be subtracted from the habitat.
- Not a derivation of one side from the other, and no fifth condition or new primitive.
- Not that the physical six-term set maps onto geography, resources and coalitions. That mapping was tried and declined as strained (`01` §4).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A common-currency instrument in which material constraint and political choice are both expressible and that both frames accept as an anchor.
2. An additive, non-interacting decomposition that turns the union into a partition.
3. A disease instrument whose estimates survive the coding corrections Albouy proposes.
4. A well-typed `⋛` comparison: both operands shown to be quantities at one level.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5 and :54, Fig 1 :19, and Fig 12 :125. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the linked abstracts and the NYRB exchange. Are the four characterisations faithful?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a geographic contribution from a political one, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
