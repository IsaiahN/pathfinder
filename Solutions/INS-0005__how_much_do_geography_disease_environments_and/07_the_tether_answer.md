# The Tether's answer: geography, disease, endowments versus political choice

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the per-slot rule, `gap` on the single comparative magnitude. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question wants one magnitude: how much of institutional development is attributable to given conditions (geography, disease environment, resource endowments) as against political choices. The residual is the gap between the observed institutional arrangement and what any one of those arguments predicts alone. That gap does not close when a single argument is refined. The same disease environment is associated with different institutional paths; the same geography is associated with a reversal of fortune; the same resource is a curse in one place and a blessing in another. So R persists under each frame.

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

1. **Environmental-material conditions are real habitat and a checkable constraint, not a verdict.** Fig 11 :39-40: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Mortality, land quality and latitude are nameable anchors for their own slots. An actor's valence is relative to the residual: diamonds are not a curse in themselves. **Verdict: `triangulated`, `specified`; magnitude unmeasured.**
2. **Institutions are a convention that has already decided outcomes, and so now constrain later choice.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* This is why institutions cannot be folded back into "political choice": the choice that matters today is made on top of a rule settled earlier. **Verdict: `triangulated`, `specified`.**
3. **Political choice is an actor in the habitat with relative valence, not an external switch.** Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Introduction is available; exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62). A comparison that treats political choice as separable is performing an exclusion the figures forbid. **Verdict: `single-source`, `specified`; magnitude unmeasured.**
4. **The "how much" itself is a union compound with no common currency and no shared anchor.** Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Published shares *"three-quarters"* (AJR), *"about half"* (Diamond) and *"not the major factors"* (Acemoglu and Robinson, NYRB 2012) are readings in different accountings drawn from one evidence pool. **Verdict: `triangulated`, `specified` on the form; `gap` on the number.**

These four verdicts are not averaged. A union of overlapping conditions has no single total.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that geography is destiny, and not that institutions are everything. Both are resource claims in a debate whose terms are being refused.
- Not that disease, terrain and endowments have no effect. They are habitat and are enumerated, not dismissed.
- Not that political choice is free or unconstrained. It is an actor in a habitat it did not author.
- Not a derivation of institutions from climate, or of political choices from institutions. No atom is derived from the others here.
- Not a fifth condition, a hidden variable, or a new primitive. Fig 13 :43: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* The list is read off the world, not invented.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument here discriminates a geographic contribution from a political one in a single institutional outcome.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **A common-currency instrument:** a design that returns a single measure in which material constraint and political choice are both expressible, accepted by both frames as an anchor that does not move when either pushes on it (refuter 1).
2. **A separability test:** a demonstration of additive, non-overlapping shares with no interaction terms, turning the union into a partition (refuter 2).
3. **A clean disease instrument:** settler-mortality data whose estimates do not depend on coding choices, with confidence intervals that both camps accept (addresses F2).
4. **A typed comparison:** show that the operands of `⋛` are two quantities at one level (refuter 3).
5. **A vocabulary for joint affordance:** a term for what a disease environment and a rule-making seat jointly afford, so the dependency chain reaches planning (Fig 3 :29-30).

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the number.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The single comparative magnitude is a `gap` with the concepts and instruments held here. That is a verdict about the form of the question, not a claim that the world lacks an answer. The six-term physical substrate was not mapped onto these variables; the strain is recorded in `01` §4. Another vocabulary could make the comparison well typed, and that would be a change of frame, not a late conversion of units.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that geography is destiny or that institutions are everything. It says that the question "how much one compared with the other" is not one well-formed question. The four conditions overlap, so it is a union of conditions that must be unbundled; each is measured against its own anchor, and settler mortality is an instrument for one slot, not a verdict on the whole; material constraint and political choice are priced in different currencies that do not add; and a single percentage averages away the per-slot signal that says where to look. Institutions are a convention that has already decided outcomes and now constrains later political choice, while political choice is an actor inside the habitat, not a switch outside it. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would make the comparison well typed. The aggregate number stays open until such an instrument exists.
