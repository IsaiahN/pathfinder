## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting checkers for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **A room does not add an anchor.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence."* A larger list of agreeable readers is a bigger room, not a better check.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in submission venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.

## Suggested next step

Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. The audience that can help is the one that has opted in to receiving claims and is positioned to refute them, not a purchased list of believers. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
