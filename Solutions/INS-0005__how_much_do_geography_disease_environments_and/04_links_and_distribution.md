## Wider literature that bears on the question

Two findings are worth separating from the headline coefficients.

1. **The instrument is contested.** Albouy's comment and the AJR reply show that a central measurement in this literature, European settler mortality, changes with coding choices andsample definitions. By Fig 2 :29-30 an anchor *"does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"*; this one does. That is a reason to keep it as an instrument and withhold verdict status, not a reason to discard the whole line of work.
2. **Two geography-side tests disagree with each other.** The 2012 PNAS paper finds support for a linguistic-persistence implication of Diamond's axis thesis; the 2024 Evolutionary Human Sciences paper finds no ecological advantage for Eurasia and warns against single-factor explanations. They are different tests with different estimands. A conflict sends the reader back to the domain census (F1), never to a flipped verdict.

These two points are the most useful things to put in front of a checker, because they are checkable without accepting the framework.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the four domain verdicts and the five refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* — ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Seat Map :163** — *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
