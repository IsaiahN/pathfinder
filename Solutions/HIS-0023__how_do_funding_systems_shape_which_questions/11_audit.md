## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order and the residual line alone. The residual line I worked from was: there is a gap between the allocation a ground-pressured selection would keep and the allocation the funding-and-attention system actually keeps.

- **R, by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: at least three live quantities, the ex ante selection of questions, the ex post selection of findings, and the habitat conditions coupling them. One variable with two values will not carry the observation. Symmetry: ordered, because money enables a question, the question produces a finding, and the finding's attention feeds later criteria; a `→` sequence in the operators table, not a `+` conjunction. Scale: per grant, per paper, per career slot, then field and portfolio. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **D1, ex ante allocation.** Actors: funders, programme officers, panel reviewers, applicants. Currency: grant money and review scores. Anchor: the eventual ground contact of the funded question, in principle an experiment or dataset that does not move because a panel liked the proposal. Nameable, so D1 is a domain. Its operational criterion is a candidate, not a settled instrument.
- **D2, ex post attention.** Actors: editors, reviewers, indexers, citers, communicators. Currency: citations, visibility, journal prestige. Anchor: replication or measurement of the finding. The citation count is an instrument for that anchor and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Nameable, so D2 is a domain.
- **D3, compounding across time.** Actors: past winners, past nonwinners, later panels reading past awards. Currency: cumulative funding and status. Anchor: an external ground introduced by a room that differs from the last one. Inside the loop alone no anchor is present, which is the mirror-chain failure. Nameable as what selection between rooms would reach, so D3 is a domain.
- **D4, the funder's seat and stake.** Actors: the funding organisation and its governance. Currency: reputation, mission, accountability. Anchor: consequences for knowledge read by a checker who cannot author those consequences. A ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified, so this anchor must be sought outside the funder. Nameable, so D4 is a domain.
- **Rejected as a domain.** Public opinion about science. No anchor can be named that does not itself update on the funding system's own reports. Habitat, not a verdict-bearing domain.
- **Partition or union.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." D1 and D2 overlap because past attention is a merit input to future funding and funding constrains which findings can exist. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Verdicts stay separate per domain, never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The suspected break is at the objective link: inputs and outputs are measured, but whether a funded question changed contact with a ground usually is not. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

My census matches the primary's four domains, its union reading and its rejected domain. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. The Formula defines it as the error signal across scales, and Fig 4 :30 says "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29 and :33 (non-updating anchor), Fig 9 :54-55 (union), Fig 7 :35-36 (differing rooms) and Fig 11 :49 and :51 (valence relative to residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` and `07`: funding sets reach, not settlement; the two selections are a union; reused criteria become mirrors; counts are filters; no standing list survives a changed residual.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete answer in `07` and `08`, including the claim that a near-threshold divergence is a selection artifact and that an objective-link instrument would test the shape.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down without loss:

- the **identity of the ground at each slot**, which the shorthand compresses into importance or truth. Fig 2 :29 requires a measurement that does not move when pushed; a citation count or a peer score is an instrument whose axes must be declared, and that qualification is dropped in the shorthand.
- the **union's intersection**, which the shorthand names but does not measure. Fig 9 :57 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, so an average over the two selections is not a return trip to the same object.
- the **conditional form of the mirror and no-list claims**, which become categorical when compressed. Fig 7 :36 requires the next room to differ and match the surfaced residual; Fig 11 :51 is relative to a residual. Stated without those conditions they overclaim.

One thing survives cleanly: a budget sets reach, and only a non-updating ground settles. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists in this derivation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the condensation as identical to the verbatim figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a stated gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` now records.
