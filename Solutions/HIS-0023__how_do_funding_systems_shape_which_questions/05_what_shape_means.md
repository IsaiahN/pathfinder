## 1. The ordinary-language "shape" and the canonical "shape" are the same operation

The question asks how funding systems *shape* which questions researchers ask. In ordinary use, "shape" means constrain the form of something without fully determining it. The canonical use in the figures is narrower and agrees: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 says "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and that the system can characterise what is missing in effect terms.

So a shape here is a constraint on the form of an explanation that can be stated before the specific funding system has been measured. It constrains; it does not fill.

- A shape for this question: "funding sets reach, not settlement; the two selections are a union; reused criteria become mirrors; counts are filters, not witnesses."
- A name would be: "the NIH R01 system causes X" or "double-blind review is the answer." Those are bindings that must be provenanced, not outputs of the derivation.

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The same essay adds that a final book that fails to answer means either the question was unanswerable or "perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one." That second clause is the direct warrant for splitting the question into its two selections (Fig 9 :54-57) instead of averaging them.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Applied here: the held vocabulary is "funding", "question", "finding", "attention"; the recurring new terms from the opened sources are "threshold", "cumulative advantage", "preferential attachment", "publication bias", "metric saturation", "room". Each one narrows the space.
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So we describe the effect (near-threshold proposals diverge; top-ranked papers stay on top; positive results get published) and let the figure names attach afterwards. We do not start by naming "the Matthew effect" and then look for confirmations.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is why the outreach email leads with the shape and the refuter, not with the framework.
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." If we had picked the funding-bias literature first, any funding system would have seemed to fit. The census came first (audit section 1).

## 3. What licenses a name, and the correction to my own overstatement

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The canonical README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52) with a source gate and a shadow test.

**Correction, kept visible.** An earlier reading of this rule, carried into the exemplar and initially repeated here, treated "shape, not a name" as a ban on naming any concrete mechanism. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in the library. A lottery, an independent panel, a registered report and a citation count are all available bindings from the world frame, not new primitives the framework invented. Naming one is provenance work; presenting it as the framework's derived answer is the error. I record the overstatement here rather than erasing it, because the correction is part of what makes the answer checkable (Fig 10 :62-64: a ruling carries provenance so a later reader can locate the error).

## 4. The source-discrimination point, applied to funding metrics

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. The reading does not determine the source. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument", and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

Applied to this question: a citation count, a grant total or an impact factor is a reading, not a source. Several histories can produce the same count: a genuinely important result, a fashionable field, a self-reinforcing ranking, a well-connected lab. Reading importance off the count is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, not a new argument. Registered reports, replication records and held-out datasets are candidate discriminators (Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"). This is why the answer in `07` is a shape plus an instrument request, not a policy recommendation.

Fig 10 :24 adds the time dimension: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". A metric can saturate or pool readings while the thing it was meant to track has not changed. The failure is in the channel, and it "looks exactly like a working one" until the drift is large.

## 5. Prestige is not action: the decisive essay line for this question

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, section "Prestige Is Not Action": "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." And the failure: "The ground is never consulted. The error compounds. The lineage is corrupted. Nobody notices because the prestige loop looks like agreement, and agreement feels like truth."

This is the ordinary-language question's core. Funding gives a question a budget to be tried. It is not a verdict on the question. When a panel treats a past award as proof that the question was worth asking, prestige has been allowed to validate an action directly, which the essay names as the root collapse. Fig 2 :29 states the figure-side version: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 12 :127 adds that "the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."

The two sentences are one claim at two scales: the budget sets reach; only the ground settles. That is why the answer to "how do funding systems shape which questions?" is about reach and selection, not about truth.

## 6. Independence, convention and drift

From *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: two people who only check against each other "drift, and they drift together, so neither notices"; "Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources." The three safeguards the essay names map exactly onto the figure answer: a ground both are corrected by separately, separate experience that renews independence, and claims in effect terms.

Fig 2 :39-41 supplies the valence: "Correlation has valence", agreement is "disqualifying for a verdict" but "efficient for a convention". A funding community needs agreement about what a grant means (a convention), and must not use that agreement as evidence that the question was important (a verdict). Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLMs "can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"; "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the claim about funding, and they must be labelled as such. Mode on the substantive claims is `specified`; on the structural shape, `general`; nothing here is `grounded`.

The honest stop is the one the canonical README states: stop where instruments can no longer distinguish, record the lineage, and report what was unreached as a verdict rather than as silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).
