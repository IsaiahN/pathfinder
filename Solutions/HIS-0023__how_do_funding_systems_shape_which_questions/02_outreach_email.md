# Outreach draft  -  check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01` and `08` have been checked against the figure text. The offer collapses if the first-stage output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how funding selects research questions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about research funding that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the two selections, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`):** the way a question gets funded and the way a finding gets attention are two different selections, not one. They form a union of overlapping conditions: past attention feeds into future funding criteria, and funding constrains which findings can exist. The shape claim has five parts. Funding sets the reach of what can be tried, not the verdict on what is true or what mattered. A selection criterion that uses its own past awards as evidence of merit is a mirror rather than an anchor, so its agreement is variation, not a verdict. A metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. There is no standing list of good funding rules, because an actor's value flips with the question being asked. And counts of grants or citations are filters for a budget, not witnesses for a finding. The effect is per slot (per field, career stage and question type), never an average over all research.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any specific funding system is the best one, that funding determines which findings are true, that a particular rule should be adopted, or that this is settled by measurement. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Concrete mechanisms such as independent review rooms, lotteries or registered reports are bindings I would have to provenance, not results the derivation produced.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A funding criterion that settles question choice, does not move when the funding body is pushed, and whose per-slot residual trends down as funding rises while attention tracks that same non-updating measurement rather than prior attention.
> 2. A single lever that clears both selections, including their intersection, so the compound is a partition rather than a union.
> 3. A metric that decides an outcome yet stays checkable against a non-updating ground and produces no coherent drift across repeated use.
> 4. A funding rule that improves question choice across several changed residuals without changing valence or adding contact.
> 5. A global attention average that predicts per-slot question choice as well as per-slot residuals, checked at the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The Matthew effect in science funding, Bol, de Vaan and van de Rijt, PNAS 2018: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5948972/
> - The Matthew effect in empirical data, Perc, Journal of the Royal Society Interface 2014: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4233686/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a funding problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Do not ask for trust | Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." |
| Verdict recorded before theory | Seat 4 sees the residual and shape only. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40). |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Two selections, not one | Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." |
| No funding rule named as the answer | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The README narrows this to missing primitives, so a named binding is provenance, not a derivation. |
| Counts as filters | Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." |
| Stake declared | Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask users to write their own refuter and mode | Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the sought shape is not a term it holds. |
| No bulk send | Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that voids their agreement as a check. |

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their stage-1 verdict is never pooled as corroboration afterwards.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Every user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
