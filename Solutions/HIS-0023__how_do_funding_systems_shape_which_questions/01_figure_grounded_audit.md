## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Observation to be explained. Research attention is not distributed as a function of truth or importance alone. Two selections happen. Before the work, a funding panel chooses which questions receive a budget and therefore which questions get worked on. After the work, editors, indexers and citers choose which findings are visible and repeatedly cited. A near-threshold cut in a grant competition produces a large later divergence in funding even when review scores were almost identical (Bol, de Vaan and van de Rijt 2018). Ranking algorithms that weight past citation counts make already-cited papers more visible and therefore more cited (Wikipedia, "Google Scholar"). Positive results are published at roughly three times the rate of null results at equal quality (Wikipedia, "Publication bias").

Stated as an effect: there is a gap between the allocation a ground-pressured selection would keep and the allocation the funding-and-attention system actually keeps. Actors are panels, applicants, editors, indexers and citers. The currency is money and attention. The question is whether the selection is ranged against a ground that does not update on what the frame thinks, or whether it updates on its own previous decisions and converges on whoever had the most influence.

An answer would look like this, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a **mechanism shape**, that is, a list of the selection relations, the currency of each, the location of the anchor for each, and the conditions under which the residual index is per slot rather than aggregate. What an answer would *not* be: a single universal funding rule, a percentage, or a rank order of funding systems. Those are preferences, not terms, and the loop cannot check its own direction (Fig 5 :62-63).

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least three live quantities: the ex ante selection of questions, the ex post selection of findings, and the habitat conditions that couple them. "Which questions" and "which findings receive attention" are not one variable with two values.
- **Symmetry.** The coupling is ordered, not symmetric. Money enables a question, the question produces a finding, and the finding's attention feeds back into later criteria. This is a `→` sequence in the operators table ("A then B; B needs A to have happened"), not a `+` conjunction. Swapping the order produces a different process.
- **Scale.** Per grant, per paper, per career slot; then field and portfolio level. Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by slot. A headline such as "funding shapes science" aggregates across slots and throws away the signal about where to look (Fig 1 :22).

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if a nameable anchor can be stated, even if that anchor is currently unreached. Where no anchor can be named, the object is not yet a domain and the question must be re-censused.

- **D1, the allocation frame (ex ante).** Actors: funders, programme officers, panel reviewers, applicants. Currency: grant money and review scores. Anchor: the eventual ground contact of the funded question, in principle an experiment, dataset or result that does not move because a panel liked the proposal. This anchor is nameable, so D1 is a domain. Status of its operational criterion: a candidate that may have drifted into panel 1.
- **D2, the attention and circulation frame (ex post).** Actors: editors, reviewers, indexers, citers, communicators. Currency: citations, visibility, journal prestige. Anchor: replication or measurement of the finding. The operational metric (citation count) is an instrument for that anchor, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37-38). Nameable, so D2 is a domain.
- **D3, the compounding frame across time.** Actors: past winners, past nonwinners, later panels reading past awards. Currency: cumulative funding and status. Anchor: an external ground introduced by a room that differs from the last one. Inside the loop alone no anchor is present, which is exactly the mirror-chain failure. The anchor is nameable as the ground that selection between rooms would reach, so D3 is a domain.
- **D4, the funder's seat and stake.** Actors: the funding organisation and its governance. Currency: reputation, mission, accountability. Anchor: the consequences of the funding decision for knowledge, read by a checker who cannot author those consequences. A ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified (Fig 10 :93), so this anchor must be sought outside the funder. Nameable, so D4 is a domain.
- **Rejected as a domain:** "public opinion about science." No anchor can be named that does not itself update on the funding system's own reports. It is treated as habitat, not as a verdict-bearing domain.

### Partition or union

D1 and D2 are a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. The ex ante criterion feeds on ex post attention (past citations and awards as merit evidence), and ex post attention is constrained by what was funded in the first place. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." One lever, such as more money or double-blind review, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Verdicts therefore stay separate per domain and are never averaged into a single "effect of funding".

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perception: can the selection see the residual per slot? (Fig 3 :4-5)
2. Vocabulary: does it have terms for what a proposal or a finding affords? (Fig 3 :6-8)
3. Objective: can it say what would count as progress, in its own terms? (Fig 3 :9-11)
4. Planning: is there a route to that objective under incomplete information? (Fig 3 :12-14)
5. Learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem. (Fig 3 :14-16)

The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19). The suspected break in funding systems is between links 2 and 3: they have a rich vocabulary of inputs and outputs but no instrument for "did this change contact with a ground". That link attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :25-26), and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27). This is a reading of the chain, not a property of it; Fig 3 :18 says where a chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain.

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  Funding is a budget, not a ground (decisive for the shape; `general`)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 12 :127: "the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Funding buys the attempt. It determines which questions enter the reachable set, how much of the arrangement space is searched, and how many people can work on a slot. It does not determine which finding is true or which question mattered. The essay makes the same separation in non-figure language: prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it.

Applied to D1 and D2, this gives the first half of the answer: the funding system shapes the **reach**, not the **verdict**. A metric that is used as if it were the verdict has been promoted past its office. Cost if wrong: if this is false, a funding body could legitimately certify a finding by funding it, which no instrument in the ledger supports.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural separation, `specified` when applied to a named funding system. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 6, Fig 12 and the operators/symbols tables, which use different objects (anchor, closure, budget).

**Refuter:** exhibit a funding criterion that settles a slot without moving when the funding body is pushed, and whose per-slot residual trends down as funding increases while attention tracks that same non-updating measurement rather than prior attention.

### F2  -  Self-updating criteria are panel 1, not triangulation (`specified`)

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

A panel that treats prior awards as evidence of merit, and a citer who selects from a ranking built on prior citations, are both ranging against a record produced by the same process. Their agreement is the pool talking, not two frames ranging against a ground. Bol, de Vaan and van de Rijt 2018 found that winners just above a funding threshold accumulated more than twice as much funding over eight years as near-identical nonwinners just below it, and that the gap was partly produced by nonwinners applying less often. That is a compounding selection, not a ground reading. Perc 2014 gives the general mechanism in network data. Wikipedia's Google Scholar entry reports that ranking by citation counts strengthens the Matthew effect because highly cited papers appear at the top and gain more citations.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 7 on rooms), with world corroboration from three different fields (science funding, network science, information science). The three world sources share the Merton/price pool in part, so they count as one witness for the mechanism shape, not three.

**Refuter:** show a funding panel whose criterion is an external measurement, not a prior decision of the same panel, with independence renewed by separate experience (Fig 10 :80), and show that the per-slot residual trends down.

### F3  -  The effect is per slot, never aggregate (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

"Does funding shape research?" is not one question. The effect differs by field, career stage, question type and finding type. An average across all slots is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60). This is why the policy literature can report opposite results in different settings without either being wrong; the disagreement does not shrink with effort because each rule works well on a different subset.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` for any named slot. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 9, two distinct figures with different objects (slot indexing versus union splitting).

**Refuter:** show that a global attention average predicts per-slot question choice as well as or better than per-slot residuals, with the comparison made at the ground and not inside a shared evidence pool.

### F4  -  The broken link is the objective (`specified`)

Fig 3 :25-26 describes the middle link: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 12 :114-115: having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar.

Funding systems have precise terms for inputs and outputs (money spent, proposals received, papers published, citations accrued). They typically have no term, or no instrument, for "did this funded question change contact with a ground". So effort lands above the break, where measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30), and the system optimises link 4 while link 3 remains unread. The NSF merit-review page, fetched this session, is a concrete instance: it states three portfolio principles and four review elements, and its measurable criteria are about quality, qualification and resources, not about post-award ground contact. That is a description of a real institution, not a finding about its effects.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 3 plus Fig 12 plus Formula step 7, different objects).

**Refuter:** exhibit a funding system with a working instrument at the objective link, show the measurement, and show that improving the planning link afterwards changes the per-slot residual rather than only the reported count.

### F5  -  A metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (`general`)

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Fig 2 :41 says agreement is "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it", while Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Review criteria and bibliometric thresholds start as shared meanings. Shared meaning is the product of a convention and is not corrupted by high agreement. The moment a criterion decides who gets funded or which finding is published, it is a verdict and the second sentence governs. Publication bias (Wikipedia, "Publication bias") and funding bias (Wikipedia, "Funding bias") are the world-side names for this promotion: the outcome of a study, or the identity of its sponsor, enters the decision to publish or to believe.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2 with the seat map :163, different offices: convention versus verdict versus correlation).

**Refuter:** show a metric that decides an outcome, remains checkable against a non-updating ground at the slot, and produces no coherent drift across repeated use.

### F6  -  Attention compounds in a mirror chain (`specified`)

Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom".

A funding body that books the same kind of room each round, selected by the same criteria, travels in place. The room's internal agreement is variation, not a verdict. Progress requires the next room to differ from the last and to match the residual that the last one surfaced. The two factors named in Fig 7 :38-39, "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and "the ground's pressure on the room", are the quantities to instrument; zero either and the chain runs in place.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 plus Fig 6 plus Fig 8, all different objects), with world corroboration from Bol et al. 2018, Perc 2014 and the Google Scholar entry. These world sources partly share one evidence pool.

**Refuter:** show a sequence of funding rooms with low overlap whose next room is chosen against the residual surfaced by the previous one, and whose per-slot residuals trend down across handoffs. That would refute the claim that the chain necessarily runs in place when criteria are reused; it would not refute Fig 7 itself.

### F7  -  There is no standing list of good funding rules (`general`)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The same actor, for example a prestigious reviewer, is a benefactor to a residual that needs discrimination and a malfactor to a residual that needs novelty. So the honest answer to "which funding design is best?" is not a list. It is a rule for enumerating the habitat (funders, panels, applicants, journals, indexers, conditions and relations) and for re-reading valence whenever the residual changes. What a seat can do is introduce actors, for example an independent room, a lottery, a registered-report option, or a new instrument. It cannot subtract contact, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult it (Fig 11 :61). A synthetic harness that proves a rule works proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 11), corroborated in a different field by the Wikipedia funding-bias and publication-bias entries, which show that the effect of sponsorship flips with the question being asked. Single-source is recorded as single-source; it is not upgraded by a shared pool.

**Refuter:** exhibit a funding rule that improves per-slot question choice across several changed residuals without changing valence and without adding contact.

### F8  -  The ground does not decay, the channel does (`specified`)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

What fails in a funding system is the channel, not the truth. Metrics saturate, pooled readings replace per-slot readings, and transients are dropped. When the output stops reporting contact, the system says "you have lost touch" rather than a verdict (Fig 10 :27-28). The remedy is to keep the anchor reachable, not to negotiate a new definition of importance.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 11 plus Fig 2, different objects: channel versus selection versus anchor).

**Refuter:** show a metric that does not saturate under increased use, does not pool across slots, and whose failures are detected at the ground rather than by a seat's reputation.

### F9  -  The funder's stake is structural and must be declared (`general`)

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." Seat Map :171: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

A funding body cannot be its own anchor. Its interest in the outcome (a successful portfolio) is unavoidable; its interest in the reading (which questions count as important) is disqualifying. Declaring the stake does not remove it, but it makes the model visible so that it can be challenged. The world-side funding-bias entry corroborates at a different scale: studies supported by an interested sponsor are more likely to report outcomes favourable to that sponsor.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 10 plus seat map, same framework), with low-overlap world corroboration from research ethics.

**Refuter:** exhibit a funding body whose reading is demonstrably unaffected by its portfolio's reputation across changed residuals, checked by a party that cannot reconstruct the criterion.

### F10  -  Metrics are filters for the budget, witnesses only if ranged against the ground (`general`)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :48-49: two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names.

Citation counts, impact factors and grant counts price the budget. They tell you how much of the space a field has visited. They do not tell you which finding is true or which question mattered unless they are separately ranged against a ground that does not update on them. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, which is why a second room with low overlap is worth more than a second reviewer from the same pool.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 2 plus Fig 8, different quantities: filter, independence, surplus).

**Refuter:** show a metric that functions as a witness at the ground without a non-updating anchor and without low-overlap frames.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is withdrawn if any of the following fires:

1. **Anchor refuter.** A funding selection is found whose criterion settles question choice, does not move when the funding body is pushed, and whose per-slot residual trends down as funding rises while attention tracks that same non-updating measurement rather than prior attention. This would break F1 and F2.
2. **Union refuter.** A single lever is shown to clear both selections, including their intersection, so that the compound is a partition rather than a union. This would break the census and F6.
3. **Convention refuter.** A metric is shown to decide an outcome while remaining checkable against a non-updating ground at the slot and producing no coherent drift across repeated use. This would break F5 and F8.
4. **Standing-list refuter.** A funding rule is shown to improve per-slot question choice across several changed residuals without changing valence and without adding contact. This would break F7.
5. **Aggregation refuter.** A global attention average predicts per-slot question choice as well as per-slot residuals, tested at the ground. This would break F3.
6. **Sufficiency refuter.** A funding intervention is shown to change contact with an external ground while the ground itself demonstrably moves in response to the funding body. That would break the anchor definition in Fig 2 :29 and leave the claim without a ground.

Until one of these is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the shape, not the rule. The answer is a set of selection relations and anchor locations, not a universal funding design. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
2. Split the question into its two selections and keep the verdicts separate. Do not average them into "funding helps" or "funding hurts" (Fig 9 :54-57).
3. Instrument the objective link before adding budget or reviewers. Fig 3 :27 applies; optimising above the break cannot rescue it.
4. Keep the channel reachable: per-slot readings, a non-updating ground, and at least one room drawn from a closure that differs from the last (Fig 7 :36, Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :42).
5. Declare the funder's stake and refuse the claim that the funding body settles importance (Fig 10 :59-60).
6. Treat counts as filters for the budget. Never let a count hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
7. Send the residual and the refuter, not the theory. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
