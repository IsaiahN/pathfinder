## 1. The residual it answers

The ordinary question treats one lever with two signs. In effect terms the residual is a **union of four overlapping conditions with different anchors**, indexed per species and patch (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The conditions are what the seat introduces, whether the cascade was enumerated, whether the anchor is reachable and non-updating, and whether the room still matches the question. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." One lever clears the places where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, so both outcomes can be true at one site for different slots. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The four per-domain verdicts

### D1, habitat and seat: the separator is introduction with an enumerated cascade

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

A seat can introduce but cannot subtract, so there is no solved state. Management strengthens resilience when it keeps an enumerated habitat in contact with a ground that pays, and it prevents adaptation when it substitutes a habitat or introduces an actor whose cascade was never enumerated. The two silent failures are named in the figure: Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." **Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 habitat, Fig 12 catalyst and cycle). Not `grounded`.

### D2, measurement: refuse the averaged score and the updating anchor

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

A resilience index produced by the same program it grades is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."). If the index moves when the program's report changes, it is another frame, not an anchor, and the drift is invisible from inside. What is owed is a per-slot reading against a measurement that does not move with the program's opinion. **Mode `general` on the structure, `specified` at a named site, triangulated** (Fig 1, Fig 2, and the low-overlap cybernetics witness).

### D3, time: adaptation is selection between rooms, and a fixed room fails

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

An ecosystem adapting on its own is selection between rooms: each generation meets a different world and the next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced. A management regime that keeps the same target, species list and success metric while climate and interactions move is a mirror chain. It produces motion, not progress. **Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 7 room chain, Fig 10 ground maintainer, two low-overlap routes). World corroboration from novel ecosystems and shifting baselines.

### D4, targets and imports: a convention stops being one when it decides an outcome; an import needs provenance

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

A target species list or desired condition is a convention until it decides which outcome counts. Then it is a verdict and autonomous selection is not allowed to move it. The companion failure is channel decay: stale percepts, saturated metrics and pooled readings cut the seat off from a ground that never decayed (Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"). Introductions and assisted migrations are imports: they add an atom to the manager's closure, not to the world, and need a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explaining a gap already described). **Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 10).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that non-intervention is generally better than intervention. The figures do not rank the two modes; Fig 11 :56 describes unmaintained mode without endorsing it.
- Not that any technique has a standing positive valence. Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not that a restored historical structure equals a restored function. A recording of one occasion cannot generate a new success (Fig 4 :25-26).
- Not that a successful pilot plot licenses landscape application. Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."
- Not a number, a target list, or a universal rule. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No intervention in this derivation was measured against a ground.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. The corresponding discriminators here are:

1. **Contact test.** Does the intervention change contact with the ground, or only the program's internal bookkeeping? A ground that moves only when contact moves is the discriminating instrument.
2. **Cascade test.** Was the introduction's cascade enumerated out to where it stopped mattering, including second and third order effects? Record what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced.
3. **Anchor test.** Does the success measure stay fixed when the program's report changes? If it moves, it is a frame, not an anchor.
4. **Room-fit test.** When the question changed, did the room change? A target held constant through a regime shift fails this.
5. **Per-slot test.** Does the pooled index predict each species and patch as well as the per-slot readings, across a real regime shift?

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4: contact unchanged but persistence improves; subtraction possible after withdrawal; fully enumerated cascade still reduces persistence; fixed regime tracks every change and preserves selection; pooled index matches per-slot prediction; unprovenanced atom pays better than starting from primitives; a figure line returns a universal level.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The habitat is enumerated from the world, not composed from the library (Fig 11 :43-44). Until a site's actors and cascades are enumerated and a non-updating anchor is installed, no per-domain verdict can be applied to it. Fig 13 :138-139: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides." The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "is active management good or bad", because that is one dial where the question is actually a union of four conditions with different anchors. A manager is a seat acting on a habitat: it can introduce an actor but cannot subtract the habitat, so management is permanent work. It strengthens resilience when the cascade was enumerated before the introduction and the ground is read by a measurement that does not move with the program's own reports. It prevents autonomous adaptation when it substitutes a habitat, turns a target into a verdict, pools the residual into one averaged "ecosystem health" score, or holds the room fixed while the question moves. No actor has a standing positive valence, and no figure line returns a universal intervention level. What the framework offers is the separator, the per-slot indexing, and seven falsifiers written in advance. The site stays open until a ground settles it.
