## 0. The residual in effect terms

The ordinary-language question is "when does active management strengthen resilience, and when does it prevent ecosystems from adapting on their own?" That phrasing hides three things: an actor with a seat, a habitat the seat acts on, and two different outcomes that are not opposite ends of one dial.

Restated in effect terms:

- There is an ecosystem, a field of contact among species, conditions and relations. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
- A manager is a seat that can act on that field. Fig 11 :58: "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."
- Two candidate effects are being compared. Effect A: the seat's action changes contact so that the system keeps meeting its ground through disturbance. Effect B: the seat's action substitutes or fixes the habitat so that the system's own selection stops doing the adapting. "Strengthens" and "prevents" are an ordered comparison, not a symmetric join, so a pooled correlation between "management intensity" and "resilience" cannot choose the arrow. Operators table :51 on comparison: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- The residual is indexed per species, patch and relation, never averaged into one score. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- The ground is whatever settles persistence and recovery, and it does not move because the manager's report says it did. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

**What an answer would look like (binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"):** a separator with conditions. A list of conditions under which a seat's action keeps the ground reachable and the habitat unauthored, and a different list of conditions under which it substitutes a habitat, fixes a room while the question moves, or promotes a convention into a verdict. The separator must be checkable per slot, and it must not pretend that the two lists are complements of one variable. The sought-for shape is written down here before the figure lines are chosen so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

**Why the either/or framing fails before any figure is read.** "When does A strengthen, and when does A prevent" treats one lever with two signs. Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The world literature already shows several well-formed subsets: active versus passive rewilding, passive versus active adaptive management, managing against versus managing for a historical baseline, intervention bias in environmental decisions. Those are different conditions with different anchors.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

I describe R by arity, symmetry and scale, then give one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet. I then mark partition or union and order the domains by dependency (Fig 3).

**R, the residual.**

- Arity: a union of at least four overlapping conditions, not two values of one variable. The conditions are (i) what the seat introduces or removes, (ii) whether the cascade was enumerated, (iii) whether the anchor is reachable and non-updating, and (iv) whether the room still matches the question on the timescale in view. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Symmetry: "strengthens" and "prevents adapting" are ordered effects, not a symmetric relation. Operators table :30-32 on sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" with symmetry "no". A single measured association cannot orient them.
- Scale: per slot first (species, patch, interaction), population or ecosystem only after per-slot verdicts exist. Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

**Domain 1, the habitat and the seat.**

- Actors: the manager as seat, plus every actor in contact with the residual (species, disturbances, conditions, relations).
- Currency: contact and cascade. What the seat adds or fails to reproduce, what propagates three steps out.
- Anchor: the ground, which settles whether the lineage kept meeting its goal. Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- Question type: a partition boundary between maintained and unmaintained modes, with a compound where both are present. Fig 11 :55: "Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."

**Domain 2, measurement and the anchor.**

- Actors: managers, monitors, models, peer frames.
- Currency: residuals and readings.
- Anchor: a measurement that does not update on what any frame thinks, for example survival, recovery time, or persistence of function.
- Question type: partition (is this reading admissible as an anchor or is it a frame pretending to be one). Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

**Domain 3, time and autonomous adaptation.**

- Actors: rooms over successive handoffs, the ecosystem's own selectors, the manager's fixed regime.
- Currency: selection between rooms versus motion inside one room.
- Anchor: the ground's pressure on each room.
- Question type: union with Domain 1 (a maintained habitat still undergoes selection; an unmaintained one still has a seat-shaped history). Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

**Domain 4, import and novelty.**

- Actors: introduced actors, provenance records, shadow tests, peer frames.
- Currency: atoms imported across a closure wall, debited against independence.
- Anchor: the ground's payment for the new atom, plus the source gate.
- Question type: composition plus import, not a partition. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**Dependency order (Fig 3).**

1. Perceive and index the residual per slot (Fig 1 :19, :28). Without this there is nothing to enumerate.
2. Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44).
3. Establish an anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :33, :29).
4. Only then choose an intervention, and refuse before minting where a held term is false (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2).
5. Check room fit over time (Fig 10 :72, Fig 7 :36).
6. If an atom must cross a closure wall, run source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-14, Fig 8 :50-52).

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Most management effort lands at step 4 (planting, culling, reintroducing) when the chain actually stops at step 2 or 3 (no vocabulary for the cascade, no non-updating anchor). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Partition or union verdict:** union. The four domains overlap (an introduced actor is both a habitat act and an import; a metric is both an instrument and a possible updating frame). One lever cannot clear the intersections (Fig 9 :56).

**Strain test applied at the census.** I initially tried to read the whole question on Fig 11 alone. That forces "active management" and "adapt on their own" into two values of the maintained/unmaintained distinction, which makes Fig 11 :36 ("selection ran") contradict the idea of adaptation. Re-census shows the contradiction is in my reading: Fig 11 :36 describes what happens when no seat maintains anything, and Fig 11 :56 restates it descriptively. No figure line needs forcing. Recorded as S1 below and kept visible.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The seat's power is asymmetric, so management is permanent work (governing finding, Fig 11)

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

Fig 11 :64: "And in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author."

**Reading.** A manager can introduce an actor, an instrument or a disturbance regime. The manager cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. So there is no "we fixed it and can leave" state. The seat's work continues or the system returns to pure contact. This directly bears on the question: active management strengthens resilience when its continuing work changes contact in a way that keeps the ground reachable, and it prevents adaptation when the continuing work replaces the habitat's own selection. Both outcomes are the same seat, so the variable is not "management yes or no" but what the seat does and what it fails to reproduce.

**Refuter.** Show a managed ecosystem where the seat can remove a contact relation rather than only add an actor, and still keep the ground reachable after the seat withdraws. That would refute the asymmetry and with it the permanent-work consequence. Also: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the contact claim, exactly as Fig 11 :72 states: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated. Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 12 :119 (catalyst, a part that survives its own use) are low-overlap routes to the same shape. World corroboration: rewilding's "self-sustaining" goal and restoration's "abandonment may be the wisest option" are different target states, not a refutation of ongoing seat work.

### F2 - Introduction without an enumerated cascade is the named failure mode (Fig 11)

Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"

Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

**Reading.** The figure itself cites ecosystem engineering (Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994) as the legitimate version of introduction, then names cane toads and kudzu as the same move done without enumerating the cascade. So the separator is not "introduce versus do not introduce." It is "cascade enumerated and ground-checked before introduction" versus "cascade unenumerated." Two silent failures follow: what you failed to reproduce shows up only when the goal fails, and what you brought with you shows up only when it acts.

**Refuter.** A controlled case where every contact of an introduced actor was enumerated out to the point where the cascade stopped mattering, including second and third order effects, and the intervention still reduced persistence. That would break the figure's claim that enumeration is what separates engineering from cane toads. A weaker refuter: the cascade was enumerated but the ground reading was unavailable, so the enumeration made no difference.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated. Fig 11 :44 (enumerate outward) and Fig 12 :121 (cycle, sign of loop) are separate routes. World corroboration: ecological traps (animal-behaviour field) and the novel-ecosystem "secondary invaders" finding are genuine low-overlap witnesses for unenumerated cascades.

### F3 - Isolation is substitution, not removal (Fig 11)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

**Reading.** A protected area, a fenced reserve, a translocation site, a greenhouse propagation trial or a model-based "reference condition" is not a habitat with the problem taken away. It is a different habitat, with actors the manager did not choose. "Prevent ecosystems from adapting on their own" is often exactly this: the system is moved into a substituted habitat where its own selection runs against different actors. The figure names two invisible costs: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced.

**Refuter.** A substituted habitat that reproduces every condition the original goal required, inherits no unchosen actors, and still lets the system's own selection track a moving question at the same rate as the original. That would refute the substitution law. A simpler refuter: a case where moving the residual removed a contact rather than substituting one, which the figure says is impossible.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 :66-70 and Fig 7 :42 (a chain approaches truth only as the world runs out of ways to refuse it). World corroboration: novel ecosystems and shifting baselines.

### F4 - Valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of good interventions (Fig 11)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

**Reading.** This is the structural refusal of the request for a universal answer. A keystone predator, a fire regime, a grazing regime, an invasive species that provides habitat for an endangered species: each is a benefactor or malfactor only relative to a named residual. When the residual changes (new climate, new pathogen, new target), the valence flips. So "active management strengthens resilience" cannot be a standing verdict about a technique. It is a per-residual judgment.

**Refuter.** Exhibit an actor that is a benefactor across every residual in a habitat, with the same mechanism, measured against a non-updating ground. That would refute the relativity claim. It is not enough to cite a "keystone species" label, because the label is a name, not a per-residual measurement.

**Status:** `general` structural, `specified` per case, triangulated. Fig 12 :123 (functional group: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member") is a low-overlap structural route. World corroboration: conservation biology's shifting baselines and the novel-ecosystem management split.

### F5 - A fixed room fails when the question moves (Fig 10 and Fig 7)

Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

**Reading.** "Ecosystems adapting on their own" is selection between rooms: each generation meets a slightly different world, and the next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced. A management regime that keeps the same target, the same species list and the same success metric while climate and interactions move is a mirror chain. It produces motion (monitoring, reports, interventions) without progress, because every room agrees with the last. Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."

**Refuter.** A fixed management regime that nevertheless tracks every change of the ecological question and preserves autonomous selection, with a non-updating anchor showing that it did. That would refute the room-fit claim. Equivalently, show a mirror chain that made progress against the ground without differing from the previous room.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 (ground maintainer) and Fig 7 (room chain), two figures with low conceptual overlap. World corroboration: novel ecosystems (baselines unattainable under climate change) and ecological restoration (shifting baseline).

### F6 - A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10)

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

**Reading.** A restoration target, a desired future condition, or a "reference state" is a convention: a declared meaning that makes the bug statable. It becomes a verdict the moment it decides which outcome counts, for example when a site is judged failed because it does not match a historical list even though the ground is paying for persistence and function. "Prevent ecosystems from adapting on their own" is often this: the target fixes value and the system's own selection is not allowed to move it. The companion failure is channel decay: the ground does not decay, but stale percepts, saturated metrics and pooled readings cut the manager off from it. The output is then not a verdict, it is "you have lost touch" (Fig 10 :27-28).

**Refuter.** A target that fixes value without deciding any outcome, demonstrated by a case where the target was revised against a non-updating ground and the manager accepted the revision. Also: a metric that stayed saturated through a real change in persistence would refute the channel-decay claim only if the manager still read the ground correctly from it.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2 :33-34). World corroboration: adaptive management's process failures ("data are analyzed and presented, but are not used for decision-making") is a direct low-overlap witness for channel decay.

### F7 - Per-slot residual, never an averaged resilience score (Fig 1 and Fig 2)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

**Reading.** An "ecosystem resilience index" produced by the same agency that runs the intervention cannot be the anchor: it is a quantity the frame also produces. If the index moves when the manager's report changes, it is not an anchor and the whole system drifts coherently, which is invisible from inside. The live signal is per species, per patch, per interaction. Ashby's requisite variety (opened this session) independently states the same arithmetic: "no amount of variety recording the patients' average temperature would detect this small signal which might have a big effect. Monitoring is required on individuals thus amplifying variety."

**Refuter.** A pooled index that predicts every per-slot outcome as well as per-slot readings, across a real regime shift, measured against a ground the index does not update. That would refute the per-slot claim. In practice this is a discriminator, not an argument.

**Status:** `general` on the structure, `specified` when a site is named, triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 2. World corroboration: cybernetics (low overlap) and adaptive management's monitoring-failure list.

### F8 - Import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 and Fig 8)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 6 :26: "An import adds no atom to the world."

Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

**Reading.** Assisted migration, reintroduction and engineered feedback loops are imports: they add an atom to the manager's closure, not to the world. They are licensed only if (i) provenance is shown (source gate), (ii) the imported atom explains a gap that was already described (shadow test), and (iii) the peer frame is independent enough that copying it does not just import an error. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. The order matters: describe the residual first, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. Picking a technique and then finding a place to use it is the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 forbids.

**Refuter.** A case where an introduced atom was adopted without provenance, without explaining a pre-described gap, and still paid against a non-updating ground at lower cost than starting from local primitives. That would refute the source-gate requirement as a necessary condition.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8. World corroboration: rewilding and novel ecosystem management both record that interventions are controversial and need reference points, which is the shadow-test role in practice.

### F9 - A recording of one occasion cannot generate a new success (Fig 4)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

Fig 4 :26: "Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."

Fig 4 :34: "The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

**Reading.** Restoring to a historical species list or structure is a recording of one occasion carried across the membrane and replayed downward. Under unchanged conditions it may work; under changed conditions it repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. What should cross is the method: enumerate the habitat, read the ground, introduce with a shadow test. A head start is allowed; a replay is not. The restoration literature's "carbon copy" myth, where a plan that worked in one area is applied to another with the same results expected, is the same finding in a different field.

**Refuter.** A historical recipe replayed into a genuinely different habitat that still produces the same function against a non-updating ground, with no method carried and no reinterpretation. That would refute the recording/method distinction at this scale.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 12 :104 (level-relative irreducibility). World corroboration: ecological restoration's carbon-copy myth and shifting-baseline discussion.

### F10 - Settling is necessary but not sufficient, so "the intervention worked once" is not a license (Fig 5)

Fig 5 :33: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist."

Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."

Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

Fig 5 :69: "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."

Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."

**Reading.** An intervention that paid once is settled, but whether it composes into the next season's problem is a separate structural question. A term that explains one occasion perfectly fails the acceptance test (Fig 5 :44). This is why a successful pilot plot does not license landscape-scale application: the arrangement may not be composable over, and the two currencies (bits to describe, actions to test, Fig 12 :125-126) do not add.

**Refuter.** Demonstrate that a settled intervention composes over arbitrary next problems without re-enumeration, with the ground paying each time. That would erase the necessity/sufficiency distinction.

**Status:** `general` structural, `specified` at a site, triangulated across Fig 5 and Fig 12. World corroboration: adaptive management's "learning is only useful where management decisions are repeated" is a direct match.

### F11 - No figure line returns a universal rule or an intervention level (gap)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name"

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

Fig 13 :136: "What it can do is narrow. It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**Reading.** After a genuine re-census across all 13 figures, the operators and symbols tables, no line says "active management strengthens resilience when X percent of the habitat is managed" or "intervention is always better than non-intervention" or "minimal intervention is always better." The figures return the separator and the refuters. A universal number would be a name where the framework returns a shape. This is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported plainly rather than filled with a plausible percentage.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a figure line that returns a universal intervention level or a universal sign for the question. If one exists, this gap finding is withdrawn. Until then, unreachable is the verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).

**Status:** `gap`, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and the canonical figures README.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The whole shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Contact.** A controlled intervention changes only the manager's internal bookkeeping, leaves contact with the ground unchanged, and still improves persistence against a non-updating anchor. This breaks Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
2. **Asymmetry.** A seat can subtract a contact relation from the habitat and still keep the ground reachable after withdrawal. This breaks Fig 11 :61-63.
3. **Cascade.** Every contact of an introduced actor was enumerated out to where the cascade stopped mattering, including second and third order effects, and persistence still fell. This breaks Fig 11 :44/:65.
4. **Room fit.** A fixed management regime tracks every change of the ecological question and preserves autonomous selection, measured against a ground that does not update. This breaks Fig 10 :72.
5. **Per-slot.** A pooled resilience index predicts every per-slot outcome as well as per-slot readings across a real regime shift. This breaks Fig 1 :19/:22.
6. **Import.** An unprovenanced atom with no shadow test pays better than starting from local primitives. This breaks Fig 6 :24 and Fig 8 :51.
7. **Gap.** A figure line is exhibited that returns a universal intervention sign or level. This breaks F11.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not the verdict. Ask which condition is present at a site before asking whether to intervene.
2. Index the residual per species and patch. Refuse any single averaged "ecosystem health" number as the anchor (F7).
3. Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering before any introduction (F2). Record what you failed to reproduce and what you brought with you (F3).
4. Treat a target as a convention until it decides an outcome, then refuse it (F6). Rebuild the room when the question moves (F5).
5. Carry methods across scales, not recordings (F9). Run source gate and shadow test on every import (F8).
6. Report `gap` where no figure line closes the residual (F11). Do not invent a universal intervention percentage.
7. Keep the two verdicts separate. "Strengthened resilience" and "prevented autonomous adaptation" can both be true at the same site for different slots, and must never be averaged into a net score (Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

### Search route at the limit

No figure line closes the universal rule. The three questions are kept distinct:

- **SEARCHED.** Is the universal rule already in the closure and merely unfound? Fig 6 :48-49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." A search across all 13 figures was run; nothing of that shape appeared. Answered, so the other two assume this answer.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from one already returning something. Here the per-slot residual is already returning something, so sharpen it: a source-discriminating instrument that distinguishes "persistence because the seat kept contact" from "persistence because the system's own selection kept running." ## 7. Updated verdict

The figures return a separator, not a verdict on active management. A seat strengthens resilience when it keeps an enumerated habitat in contact with a non-updating ground, carries a method rather than a recording, and rebuilds the room when the question moves. It prevents autonomous adaptation when it substitutes a habitat, fixes a convention into an outcome, pools the residual into an average, fixes the room while the question moves, or imports an actor whose cascade was never enumerated. Both outcomes can hold at one site for different slots, and are kept separate and never averaged. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the separator, `gap` on a universal rule. Nothing is `grounded`: the refuters in section 4 are written, not fired.
