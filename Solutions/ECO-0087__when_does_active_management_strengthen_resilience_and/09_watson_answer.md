# WATSON answer: when does active management strengthen resilience and when does it prevent adaptation?

**Mode: `specified` on the four per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural separator, `gap` on a universal rule or intervention level. Not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes material already in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule, from Isaiah N's *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened 2026-11-23), "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." This run cannot settle the question it reports on. No ecosystem was measured and no intervention was run.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a manager acting on an ecosystem. Option A: treat "active management strengthens resilience" and "active management prevents adaptation" as opposite signs of one dial and report a universal balance point. Option B: refuse the single dial, split the residual per species and patch into overlapping conditions with different anchors, and return a separator with refuters while reporting no universal level. What each leads to: A delivers an averaged verdict whose success metric is produced by the same frame it grades, and it hides the per-slot signal; B delivers checkable per-domain verdicts and an honest `gap` where no figure line closes the universal rule.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: a union of at least four overlapping conditions (what the seat introduces, whether the cascade was enumerated, whether the anchor is reachable and non-updating, whether the room still matches the question). Symmetry: "strengthens" and "prevents" are ordered effects, not a symmetric join, so an association cannot orient them. Scale: per species, patch and relation first, ecosystem only after per-slot verdicts. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Domain 1, habitat and seat.** Actors: manager as seat, species, conditions, relations. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground that settles persistence. Partition boundary between maintained and unmaintained, with a compound where both are present.
- **Domain 2, measurement.** Actors: managers, monitors, models, peer frames. Currency: residuals and readings. Anchor: a measurement that does not update on the program's report. Partition question about admissibility of an anchor.
- **Domain 3, time and adaptation.** Actors: rooms over handoffs, the ecosystem's own selectors, a fixed regime. Currency: selection between rooms versus motion in one room. Anchor: the ground's pressure on each room. Union with Domain 1.
- **Domain 4, import and novelty.** Actors: introduced actors, provenance records, shadow tests. Currency: atoms crossing a closure wall. Anchor: the ground's payment plus the source gate. Composition plus import, not a partition.
- **Order by dependency.** Index per slot (Fig 1 :19), enumerate the habitat outward (Fig 11 :44), establish a non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :33), refuse false terms before minting, choose the intervention, check room fit over time (Fig 10 :72), then run source gate and shadow test for any import (Fig 6 :12-14). Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

Strain test at the census: a first pass tried to govern the whole question with Fig 11 alone, which made Fig 11 :36 appear to contradict adaptation. Re-census showed the line says selection ran, so there is no contradiction. Four domains with different anchors are required; Fig 11 governs only Domain 1. Recorded in the retraction log of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures return a separator, not a verdict on active management. A seat strengthens resilience when it keeps an enumerated habitat in contact with a non-updating ground, carries a method instead of a recording, and rebuilds the room when the question moves. It prevents autonomous adaptation when it substitutes a habitat, promotes a target convention into a verdict, pools the residual into one averaged score, holds the room fixed while the question moves, or introduces an actor whose cascade was never enumerated. Both outcomes can hold at one site for different slots, so per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. No technique has standing positive valence, and no figure line returns a universal intervention level. The universal rule is a declared `gap`. The whole result is `specified`, ungrounded and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Each finding carries its own refuter, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices directly: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four verdicts are independent; falsifying one does not withdraw the others. A sent packet can be superseded, no primitive has to be withdrawn, and the gap can be filled by a measurement without changing the separator.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates seat-maintained persistence from self-selected persistence would settle D1; an anchor test would settle D2; a room-fit record would settle D3; a provenance and shadow test would settle D4; a figure line returning a universal level would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
