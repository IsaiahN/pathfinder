**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can check the shape claim in stage 1 without holding the framework's interior. If the first-stage output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why standards of convincing evidence differ

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about evidence and standards of proof. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the features, not `grounded`):** the question "how have different societies defined convincing evidence, and why do the definitions differ?" does not have one answer to find. It is a union of at least four sub-questions, each with its own ground: what may be admitted as a reason, how much is enough, who is entitled to decide, and what is pinned as the final settle-er. Internal convergence on a standard is a convention, not a verdict about the world. The differences across societies are located in the actors, the currency, the anchor and the habitat at each scale, not in one society reasoning better than another. A standard of proof is a threshold comparison, and because "convincing" is constitutively about people, its anchor is human judgment, which updates, so there is no fixed measurement that makes one standard correct and another wrong.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one society's standard is the rational one and the rest are errors; that the framework returns the name of the correct threshold (it returns a shape, not a name); that a famous court or a prestigious textbook settles anything; or that this is a result of historical measurement. It is a shape any complete explanation would have to carry, written before the historical search.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this message):**
> 1. a case where "convincing evidence" is settled by an instrument whose reading does not depend on human judgment, giving a fixed anchor;
> 2. a case where all four sub-questions collapse into one and the disagreement shrinks with effort;
> 3. a case where the variation is fully explained by better or worse reasoning in otherwise identical habitats;
> 4. a case where a standard's internal consensus is shown to be a verdict about the world rather than a convention.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download: `FRAMEWORK/figures/` in this project, canonical text at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
> - The legal concept of evidence (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evidence-legal/
> - Evidence (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evidence/
> - What Are the Origins of "Reasonable Doubt"? (James Q. Whitman, History News Network): https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/what-are-the-origins-of-reasonable-doubt
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading.
> [name]

## What is deliberately not asked

- Not "do you agree?"
- Not "do you trust the framework?"
- Not "here is why you should believe the conclusion."

Asking any of those before the stage-1 verdict spends the only thing the reader contributes: their independence. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
