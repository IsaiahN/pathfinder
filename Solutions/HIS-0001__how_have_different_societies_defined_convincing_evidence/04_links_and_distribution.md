## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The concept fills distinct roles.** The SEP Evidence entry says different theories emerge from "different emphases on the competing demands that have been placed on the concept." That is the world-side expression of F5: the single question is a bundle. Cite it when explaining why the four-way split is not an arbitrary imposition.
2. **Standards are thresholds and vary by system.** The SEP Legal Concept of Evidence entry says the legal concept is "neither static nor universal" and treats standards of proof as probabilistic thresholds. That is the world-side expression of F2. Cite it when explaining why "convincing" is a comparison, not a world-fact.
3. **A standard's content comes from its habitat.** Whitman's account of the theological roots of reasonable doubt, and Rosendahl's comparison of three traditions adopting doubt standards "for distinct and overlapping reasons," are world-side expressions of F3 and F6: differences live in habitats, and the lineage must be recorded because end states look alike.
