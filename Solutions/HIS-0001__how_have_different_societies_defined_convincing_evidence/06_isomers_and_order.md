# Structure, order and analogues: the union of four sub-questions

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the order claim, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks what explains differences in standards of convincing evidence. Before any cause is named, two structural questions are owed: (1) is the composite one question or several, and (2) what is the dependency order among the parts? A third question follows: do the standards of different societies stand as isomers, as a functional group, or as unrelated terms under Fig 12?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms in play: a reason E, a claim H, a threshold ⋛, a decision seat, an anchor, and a habitat. Bonds in play: ⋛ comparison (threshold), → sequence (dependency), + conjunction (conditions that co-hold), ≡ identity (two names for one standard). No new primitive is available without import. Comparatively, the standard of proof in two societies may be reached by different routes (convergent derivation) or by adoption (import), and the artefact alone cannot separate the two (Fig 8 :50-52).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the composite one question or four? (structure)

- Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- The four sub-questions are: **admissibility** (what may count as a reason), **sufficiency** (how much is enough, the ⋛ threshold), **seat** (who decides), and **anchor** (what is pinned as final). A fifth, the habitat that maintains the standard, is the condition under which the other four hold.
- Each has a separate ground: a rule of admission can be checked against a case, a threshold against outcomes, a seat against a procedure, an anchor against what the community actually treats as final. The disagreement does not shrink when they are blended. So the composite is a **union compound**, with its own loop.
- Fig 9 :57-58: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." So one lever, for example "literacy caused the change," clears only one condition and leaves the rest. That is why single-cause explanations in this field keep failing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single lever moves all four sub-questions together in more than one society, the union claim fails and the single-question reading stands. Triangulated (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-59), corroborated by the SEP Evidence entry on the distinct roles of evidence.

### Q2: What bonds hold the four together?

- **⋛ comparison** (Operators table :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities") states sufficiency: support is set against a threshold. Fig 12 :112: "A goal is a comparison." A standard of proof is a goal stated as a comparison.
- **→ sequence** (Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened") states the dependency: a reason must be admitted before it can be weighed; it must be weighed before a seat can decide; the seat's decision presupposes an anchor.
- **+ conjunction** (Operators table :13: "both hold at once") states that the four conditions co-hold in any actual trial.
- **¬ negation** (Operators table :55: "this does not hold") is what a refusal of a claim uses. A vocabulary with ⋛ but without ¬ can set a threshold and cannot record that a standard itself is wrong. Fig 12 :112: "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The four sub-questions are not a list of ingredients; the bonds between them are the explanation.

### Q3: What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Order: **habitat supplies the vocabulary of reasons (D2), the frame applies it (D1), the comparator compares across frames (D3)**. This maps onto Fig 3's links: vocabulary (link 2) before objective (link 3, "what would count as progress here"), before planning and learn-and-carry (links 4 and 5).
- Fig 3 :25-27 warns that the middle link is where chains break: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." For the history of evidence, the middle link is sufficiency: how much is enough. The SEP Legal Concept of Evidence entry reports that courts "have eschewed any attempt at authoritative quantification" of the criminal threshold, which is exactly a link with no instrument.
- **Falsifier:** if a comparison across societies can be made before the habitat vocabulary is enumerated, the dependency order is wrong. Triangulated (Fig 3 and Fig 9).

### Q4: Are the standards of different societies isomers, a functional group, or unrelated?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Two societies whose standards use the same four atoms (reason, threshold, seat, anchor) but bind them differently, for example a jury + high threshold versus a professional judge + intime conviction, are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is a real, checkable structural claim.
- Where they share only some atoms, they are members of a **functional group** (a threshold-and-seat structure) with different qualifiers. That is the weaker claim.
- Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." So two societies can reach the same threshold independently. Fig 8 :50-52 says the artefact cannot tell this from adoption; only the record can. The isomer question and the lineage question are the same question read at two levels.
- **Falsifier:** if a society's standard can be written without one of the four atoms the other requires (for example no distinct seat, or no threshold), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the comparative-law sources as unmeasured world checks.

### Q5: What order is the ground?

- Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
- For this question the anchor is human judgment, which updates (F1). So the ground does not sit at the end of a chain that converges; it is continually re-set by the frames. Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- The practical consequence for order: you cannot finish by appealing to "what people find convincing," because that is the moving thing being explained. You must stop at the limit of the instruments and record the lineage. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that measures conviction independently of the community holding it would give a fixed anchor and restore panel-3 triangulation.

## CONCLUSION

The composite is a **union of four sub-questions plus a habitat condition**, held together by ⋛, →, + and ¬, ordered by dependency as habitat, then frame, then comparison. Standards that share the four atoms but bind them differently are **isomers**; those that share fewer are a **functional group**. The ground is not at the end of the chain, because for this question it updates with the frames. This framing rescues what is checkable without naming a single cause for the differences.
