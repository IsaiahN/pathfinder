## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," because naming a missing primitive would mean already holding it.

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, the shape is the six features in `07_the_tether_answer.md`. A single named cause, for example "the standard is set by religious belief," is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel* instructs the searcher to use the vocabulary they already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the discipline: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." The remedy is a better instrument, not a confident name.
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place," and the paradox is that by the time you can state the exact terms you have "already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

The effect-term discipline matters here. Naming "religion," "technology" or "political structure" as the cause borrows the historian's vocabulary before the residual is described. Describing the effect first, namely what a standard must do for a community that anchors conviction in human judgment, is the figure-ordered move (Fig 9 :53: "Residual first, frame second").

## 3. Why the bundle must be split before naming anything

*The Library of Babel* names the failure mode directly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." Fig 9 :4-5 says the same in the primary text: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

For this question, the bundle is four sub-questions: admissibility, sufficiency, decision seat, and anchor. The SEP entry on Evidence independently reports that "the concept of evidence has often been called upon to fill a number of distinct roles," which is why a single definition keeps failing. The shape is the claim that the bundle must be split, not a claim about which single cause wins.

## 4. What licenses a name in this question

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For this question, that means an actual historical reading of the record, with dates, provenance and outcomes.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Comparative law and legal history are such imports here. They are recorded as imports, not allowed to masquerade as deductions from the figures.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the warning that applies when a historian reads a standard off an artefact: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." Several habitats can produce the same threshold. Reading the habitat off the threshold is the metamerism error. "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

## 5. Why the anchor is not an instrument for this question

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says "The Tether ties alignment to the ground. Not to human preferences. Not to a reward model trained on human feedback. To the thing that settles a question regardless of what any frame thinks. An outcome. A measurement." And: "Humans in the 1800s had different values, customs, and cultures than humans of today. What does it mean to align an AI to human values when human values are a moving target? You cannot align to something that changes."

That is exactly the F1 strain. For the question "is this evidence convincing?", the object being measured is human conviction, which is a moving target. Fig 2 :36-38 licenses human judgment as the anchor for a question about people; Fig 2 :33-34 says an anchor must not update. The resolution is not to declare the question unanswerable. It is to report the derived consequence: for this question kind, the triangulation that would settle a world-fact is not available, so what remains is convention and coherent drift. *How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the drift in plain terms: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

## 6. Why agreement and prestige are not witnesses here

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly" and "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." Also: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* adds the mechanism: "the agreement is the pool talking" and "how little they draw on the same evidence" is what makes independence worth buying. So a list of historians who already agree with the shape is a convention, not corroboration.

## 7. My own derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality. Anything else is drift." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output" while "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a history of evidence and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on. What they offer is the shape any answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 8. Correction carried forward

I initially framed the whole question as a search for the correct standard of convincing evidence. That is the name-seeking error the README forbids. The corrected framing is: describe the effect, split the bundle, and return the shape. The earlier framing is retained here as evidence of the correction, not silently erased.
