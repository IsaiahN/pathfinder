# Expert verification packet: the Tether on convincing evidence across societies

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of evidence can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the six features, not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how have different societies defined convincing evidence, and what explains the differences?" is a union of at least four sub-questions, each with its own ground: what may be admitted as a reason, how much is enough, who is entitled to decide, and what is pinned as final. A standard of proof is a threshold convention, not a verdict about the world, and it becomes answerable to the ground at the moment it decides an outcome. The differences across societies are located in the actors, the currency, the anchor and the habitat at each scale, not in one society reasoning better than another. Because "convincing" is constitutively about people, its anchor is human judgment, which updates, so no measurement makes one standard correct and another wrong; what remains is convention and coherent drift. A standard's history is a lineage question, because convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the end state. The framework returns a shape, not the name of a correct standard.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the composite must be split before it is searched, and a standard of proof is a convention until it decides an outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **Refuter:** show that a single lever moves all four sub-questions together in more than one society, or that a standard's internal consensus is a verdict about the world rather than a convention.

### C2: history-facing, checkable by a historian against the record

- **Statement:** cross-societal variation is explained by a census of actors, currency, anchor and habitat, not by a difference in reasoning. Standards sharing the four atoms but binding them differently are Fig 12 isomers; those sharing fewer are a functional group.
- **What to check:** take two societies (for example a common-law jury with beyond a reasonable doubt, and a civil-law judge with intime conviction). Enumerate who decides, what counts as a reason, what is final, and what the habitat supplies. Are the differences fully captured there, or is there a residual difference in reasoning quality that the census cannot hold? Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."
- **Refuter:** a case where two otherwise identical habitats differ only in reasoning quality, with the census unable to register the difference; or a standard that can be written without one of the four atoms the other requires.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a named strain

- **Statement:** the anchor for "convincing" is human judgment, which updates, so triangulation is unavailable and the result is coherent drift.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- **Refuter:** an instrument that measures conviction independently of the community holding it, giving a fixed anchor and restoring triangulation. Until one is shown, the strain between "anchor must not update" and "the anchor here updates by nature" is named, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final room.
- Not a name for the correct standard of convincing evidence. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a ranking of societies by rationality. Fig 4 :19-20 puts variation in actors, currency and anchor.
- Not "religion caused it" or "technology caused it." Each is one condition in a union, and a single lever leaves the intersections standing.
- Not a history of evidence. No dates, cases or records were measured in this packet.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a historical instrument.
- Not confirmed by expert agreement. A reply that agrees is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a further argument:

1. A study that splits a society's standard into the four sub-questions and enumerates the habitat actors for each, with dates and provenance.
2. An instrument that measures conviction independently of the community holding it (would name the anchor and restore triangulation).
3. A case where a single lever moves all four sub-questions together in more than one society (would break the union claim).
4. A provenance trail showing whether a shared threshold was independently derived or imported.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 10 :46-47 (three short passages). Do the four sub-questions actually have different grounds in the case you know best?
2. Read Fig 2 :33-38. Is the updating-anchor consequence right for "is this evidence convincing?", or is there a fixed anchor we have missed?
3. Take one pair of societies and try the four-atom census. Does anything important fail to fit? Name it.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
