## QUESTION

Option A: treat it as one hard question with one correct standard still to be found, and explain differences as errors or degrees of rationality. ## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Communities converge internally on standards of convincing evidence and the convergences differ (ordeal, oath, witnesses, documents, probability thresholds, intime conviction). Arity: two-place (a body of reasons E stands to a claim H for a community) plus a second-order comparison across communities. Symmetry: not symmetric, and the acceptance relation is an ordered threshold comparison. Scale: individual fact-finder, institution, and historical comparison. Fig 9 names the composite a union compound: a case in several conditions is not a harder case of one.
- **Domain 1, the fact-finding frame.** Actors: jurors, judges, elders, oracles, parties, witnesses, instruments. Currency: what counts as a reason to accept a claim. Anchor: whatever the community treats as final (a claimed divine verdict, a jury verdict, intime conviction, a measurement). Question type: partition member, one room per community.
- **Domain 2, the habitat that maintains the standard.** Actors: institutions that train and select fact-finders, the case pool, religion, literacy, technology, political stakes. Currency: the conventions and rules of proof transmitted. Anchor: the ground filtered through the habitat (the habitat is not the ground). Question type: union, overlaps D1 and D3.
- **Domain 3, the comparing frame.** Actors: the historian or philosopher and the surviving sources. Currency: corroboration across independent sources, residuals. Anchor: the historical record plus human judgment, which is legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people. Question type: union, overlaps both.
- **Order by dependency.** D2 supplies the vocabulary of reasons, D1 applies it to reach a verdict, D3 compares across rooms. Settle each sub-question before blending (Fig 9 :4-5), and describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the question's anchor is both legitimate (because the question is about people) and updating (because conviction is what changes). ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a single-cause explanation and any ranking of societies do not. The composite is a union of four sub-questions plus a habitat condition, held together by threshold, sequence, conjunction and negation bonds, ordered habitat then frame then comparison. A standard of proof is a convention until it decides an outcome. Differences live in actors, currency, anchor and habitat. Because the anchor is human judgment and it updates, no measurement makes one standard correct and another wrong; what remains is convention and coherent drift. A standard's history needs a provenance record because convergence and import look identical in the end state. This is a `general`/`specified`, ungrounded result. Strains after re-census: two, both named (the updating anchor, and the convention that decides an outcome).

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states four refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is presenting a simulation as a history and spending a reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the four-way split and the refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape is a filter on explanations, not a historical claim. It can be narrowed or withdrawn without discarding any historical finding. The four refuters are the levers for doing so.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A historical study that splits a standard into the four sub-questions and enumerates the habitat actors with dates and provenance would test features 1, 3 and 5; an instrument that measures conviction independently of the community holding it would give a fixed anchor and restore triangulation; a case where one lever moves all four sub-questions together in more than one society would break the union claim. Prestige or consensus is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :40).
