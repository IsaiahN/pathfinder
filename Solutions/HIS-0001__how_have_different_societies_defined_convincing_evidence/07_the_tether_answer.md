# The Tether's answer: how different societies define convincing evidence

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the six features, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route through the question, not a result of historical measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Communities converge internally on standards of convincing evidence and the convergences differ. The question asks what explains the differences. Option A treats the variation as one hard question with one correct standard still to be found. Option B treats it as a union of well-formed questions whose standards are conventions anchored in human judgment. A sends a search that cannot measure its own anchor. B sends a checkable shape with a stated refuter. The verdict is B, at the stated mode.

## 2. The shape (six features)

1. **The composite is a union, not one question.** Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." At least four sub-questions: what may be admitted, how much is enough, who decides, and what is pinned as final. A single lever clears one condition and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57-58).

2. **A standard of proof is a threshold convention, not a verdict about the world.** Operators table :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." Internal agreement on a standard is "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" (Fig 2 :41). But Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

3. **The differences live in actors, currency, anchor and habitat, not in different reasoning.** Fig 4 :19-20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." The explanation of cross-societal variation is a census of who decides, what counts as a reason, what is final, and what the habitat supplies.

4. **The anchor for "convincing" is human judgment, and it updates.** Fig 2 :36-38 licenses human judgment because the question is about people. Fig 2 :33-34 says an anchor must not update, and that when it does "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." So no measurement makes one standard correct and another wrong. What remains is convention and coherent drift. This is the feature that most distinguishes the question from one about physical evidence.

5. **A standard's history is a lineage question.** Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Two societies reaching the same threshold independently and one importing it from another look identical in the end state. Only provenance separates them.

6. **Verdicts stay per domain, never averaged.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Each of the four sub-questions gets its own verdict against its own ground. There is no single score for "how convincing a society's evidence standard is."

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any society's standard is the rational one and the rest are errors. That is the name-seeking error the README forbids.
- Not that the framework returns the name of the correct threshold. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that "religion," "technology" or "the economy" is the single cause. Each is one condition in a union; a single lever leaves the intersections standing.
- Not a history of evidence. No dates, cases or records were measured here.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument settles the claim that human conviction is the moving anchor.
- Not that agreement among experts confirms the shape. Agreement inside one evidence pool is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

A ground reading, not a further argument. Concretely:

- A historical study that splits a society's standard into the four sub-questions and enumerates the habitat actors for each, with dates and provenance, would test features 1, 3 and 5.
- An instrument that measures conviction independently of the community holding it would give a fixed anchor and restore triangulation, reversing feature 4.
- A case where a single lever moves all four sub-questions together in more than one society would break feature 1.
- A case where internal consensus about a standard is shown to be a verdict about the world rather than a convention would break feature 2.

Until one of these fires, the answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." *Indiscernibility is not identity.*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the correct standard of convincing evidence, and it does not rank societies by rationality. It says the question is a bundle of at least four sub-questions, what may be admitted, how much is enough, who decides and what is pinned as final; that a standard of proof is a threshold convention, not a verdict about the world; that the differences across societies live in the actors, currency, anchor and habitat at each scale, not in one group reasoning better; that because "convincing" is constitutively about people, its anchor is human judgment, which updates, so no measurement makes one standard correct and another wrong; and that a standard's history is a lineage question, because convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the end state. What it offers is the shape any complete explanation must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The question stays open until the ground, a real historical reading, settles it.
