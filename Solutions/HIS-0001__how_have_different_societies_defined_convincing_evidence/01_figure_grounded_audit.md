## 0. The residual, in effect terms

Observed: communities converge internally on what counts as convincing evidence, and the convergences differ (ordeal, oath, witnesses, documents, probability thresholds, intime conviction, expert consensus). The question asks for the explanation of that variation.

Stated as R:

- **Arity.** At least two-place: a body of reasons E stands in a relation to a claim H such that a community accepts H. The explanation is many-place because many communities are being compared. There is also a second-order relation between communities, so R is a compound, not a single slot.
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. E convincing H does not make H convincing E. The acceptance relation is an ordered threshold comparison (Operators table :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities"). Direction matters.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in play: the individual fact-finder, the institution that maintains the standard, and the historical comparison across societies. Fig 4 :19-20 says the kernel is unchanged across size and "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before searching: a **shape** with six features, namely a union of sub-questions; a convention-threshold; a location of variation in actors, currency, anchor and habitat; an updating-anchor constraint; a provenance requirement separating derivation from import; and per-domain verdicts kept separate. This binding is written down before the figure search so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Formula "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ordinary term).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale is given above. One line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the fact-finding frame | fact-finders (juror, judge, elder, oracle), parties, witnesses, instruments | what counts as a reason to accept a claim (testimony, document, oath, ordeal outcome, likelihood, expert consensus) | whatever the community treats as the non-negotiable settle-er: a claimed divine verdict, a jury verdict, intime conviction, a measurement | one room per community, a partition member |
| D2, the habitat that maintains the standard | institutions that train and select fact-finders, the case pool, religion, literacy, technology, political stakes | the conventions and rules of proof that get transmitted | the ground, filtered through the habitat; the habitat itself is not the ground (Fig 11 :73) | overlaps D1 and D3, so a union member |
| D3, the comparing frame (historian or philosopher) | the researcher and the surviving sources | corroboration across independent sources, residuals | the historical record plus human judgment, which is legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38) | overlaps both, so a union member |

The three are a **union**, not a partition. A standard of proof is simultaneously a procedure, a habitat-maintained convention, and an object of comparison. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." So each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict; verdicts are never averaged (Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.").

**Order by dependency (Fig 3, the chain is ordered by dependency not importance).** D2 supplies the vocabulary of reasons, D1 applies it to reach a verdict, D3 compares across rooms. So the order is D2, then D1, then D3. Effort spent at D3 before D2 has a vocabulary lands at a step whose input never arrives; Fig 3 :22: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."

No nameable anchor was invented for a fourth domain. None is claimed.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is constitutively about human judgment, and that anchor updates

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**

"Is this evidence convincing?" is a question about what settles a person. So the legitimate anchor is human conviction, not an instrument that measures the world. This is not a weakness of the question; it is the question's kind.

But Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."** And Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Conviction is exactly the thing that updates when evidence arrives. So the anchor for this question updates by nature. The appropriate reading is therefore panel 1, mutual update and coherent drift, not panel 3, triangulation against a fixed ground. A society's standard can drift coherently without any member noticing, because the thing that would correct it is the thing that is changing.

This is the deepest figure-grounded point in the answer, and it explains something the world sources also report: courts can ask what "beyond a reasonable doubt" means and get no definitive answer. Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

**Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 2 :36-38 (anchor legitimacy) and Fig 2 :33-34 (anchor must not update). Low overlap: one is a constitutive rule about question kinds, the other is a dynamical warning about drift. **Strain noted:** the same object (human judgment) is both the legitimate anchor and an updating anchor. Re-census in §6 resolves which quantity each clause governs.

### F2: A standard of proof is a threshold convention, not a verdict about the world

Operators table :48: **"a threshold relation between two quantities."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
Fig 10 :46: **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."**

"Convincing" is a ⋛ comparison: a quantity of support is set against a threshold. The threshold is set by convention. Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** That is why internal convergence on a standard is not a corruption: it produces the shared meaning the standard is.

But Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** When a standard of proof determines a verdict, it is no longer a pure convention; it now makes a claim about the world and is answerable to the ground. This is the hinge in the history of evidence standards: thresholds that began as theological or ritual rules end by deciding liberty and death.

**Mode `general`. Triangulated** across the Operators table and Fig 10 (low overlap: one defines the operator, the other governs the seat's authority).

### F3: Variation lives in actors, currency and anchor across scales, not in different reasoning

Fig 4 :19-20: **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**
Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The loop is the same in a jury room, an inquisition, a peer review panel and a laboratory. What differs is who decides, what counts as a reason, what is pinned as final, and what the habitat supplies. So the explanation of differences is a census of actors, currency, anchor and habitat, not a claim that one society reasoned better. This is the core answer to the second half of the question.

**Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 4 (across size) and Fig 11 (across habitat), low overlap.

### F4: Internal agreement is variation; differences survive where evidence pools differ

Fig 8 :47: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**
Fig 8 :48-49: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

A society's consensus about what counts as convincing is a convention produced inside one pool. It is not evidence that the standard is correct. Two societies whose fact-finders draw on different evidence, different institutions and different stakes will differ, and the difference is real information about their habitats rather than an error to be averaged away. Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

**Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 8 and Fig 7 (low overlap: surplus/independence vs room selection).

### F5: The composite is a union of well-formed questions; split before searching

Fig 9 :4-5: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

"Convincing evidence" does not decompose into one threshold. It decomposes into at least four sub-questions: what may be admitted as a reason, how much is enough, who is entitled to decide, and what is pinned as the final settle-er. Each rule works well on its own subset and the disagreement does not shrink when the four are conflated. So the explanation must be unbundled before the historical search. World corroboration: the SEP entry on Evidence says "the concept of evidence has often been called upon to fill a number of distinct roles" and that "Different theories about the nature of evidence might thus naturally emerge from different emphases on the competing demands that have been placed on the concept."

**Mode `general`. Single-source for the figure ruling (Fig 9), corroborated by a world source outside the framework (Thomas Kelly, SEP).**

### F6: A standard's history is a lineage question; derivation and import look identical in the contents

Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 13 :126-127: "WHY THE LINEAGE IS THE PROOF" / "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."

Two societies can arrive at the same threshold independently (corroboration at maximum independence, Fig 8 :50), or one can adopt the other's threshold (import). The end states look the same. Only the provenance record separates them. That is why the explanation of differences must be a lineage, not a comparison of snapshots. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," applies to the parts of that lineage the record cannot recover.

**Mode `specified`. Single-source for the figure ruling (Fig 8), with Fig 6 and Fig 13 corroboration.**

### F7: Prestige and agreement cannot settle which standard is right

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**
Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

A famous court or a prestigious textbook does not make a standard correct. Agreement selected for its own sake manufactures the very correlation that disqualifies a verdict. What settles a claim about a standard is a ground reading of the historical record and of outcomes, not a headcount of endorsements.

**Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 1 (low overlap: cost/independence vs metric).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 10 :59: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." And the seat-map rule carried in `02`: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A case where "convincing evidence" is settled by an instrument whose reading does not depend on human judgment, giving a fixed anchor (breaks F1).
2. A case where all four sub-questions collapse into one and the disagreement shrinks with effort (breaks F5).
3. A case where variation is fully explained by better or worse reasoning in otherwise identical habitats, so actors, currency, anchor and habitat do no work (breaks F3).
4. A case where import and independent derivation are indistinguishable even with full provenance (breaks F6).
5. A case where a society's internal consensus about evidence is shown to be a verdict about the world rather than a convention (breaks F2 and F4).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Re-census, strains and the updating-anchor resolution

**Strain A, human judgment as both anchor and updating frame.** Re-census: Fig 2 :36-38 governs *which object may serve as the anchor for a question kind*. Fig 2 :33-34 governs *what the anchor must do to yield triangulation*. These are different quantities, one constitutive and one dynamical. There is no contradiction; there is a derived consequence. For a question about conviction, the constitutive rule selects human judgment, and the dynamical rule then says the triangulation that would settle a world-fact is unavailable, so what remains is a convention and coherent drift. The strain is named and used, not hidden.

**Strain B, a convention that decides an outcome.** Fig 10 :46 says a convention makes no claim about the world; Fig 10 :47 says a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Re-census: the two clauses govern different phases of the same object's career. A proof standard is a convention at the moment it is formulated and a verdict-bearing device at the moment it is applied. Both are recorded. No smoothing.

**Apparent Fig 6 versus Fig 8 strain on import.** Fig 6 :23 says only contact with a differing closure moves the wall; Fig 8 :50-52 says derivation and import look identical in the contents. Different quantities: one is about what moves a closure, the other is about what a later reader can tell from the artefact. No re-census owed beyond naming them.

**After re-census: two strains, both named, none hidden.**
