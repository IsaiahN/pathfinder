# Outreach draft: check the shape and the refuter before the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two domain verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are checked against the verbatim lines. The output must survive a refuter-first read or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check a one-paragraph claim about founder succession before deciding whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about founder succession. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question splits into two conditions that need separate checks. First, why founders resist: the founder's own assessment of their necessity has no independent anchor inside it, so it converges on what the founder and their team already believe. Their standing was earned on the early success and survives into a stage where the question has changed. That is a structural effect, not a character verdict, and it is not evidence that the founder is right. Second, when a transition is necessary: capability is a property of the leader and the habitat together, never of the leader alone. So changing only the person, with the company's contacts held fixed, changes nothing. A transition is owed when a live slot residual persists against a market the founder's frame cannot move, and the binding constraint has moved past what that frame supplies.
>
> **What the output does not say.** It gives no company size or revenue at which a founder must step down. It does not say "hire an outsider" or "promote an insider" as a rule. It does not name a successor. It says only that the next leader's frame must differ from the founder's in the dimension the residual names, and that handing over the founder's recorded playbook is not the same as handing over a method.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before running the derivation:
> 1. A founder self-assessment instrument predicts the market return without being moved by the founder's standing or by team agreement.
> 2. A controlled succession in which only the leader changes, with the habitat held fixed, persistently moves the ground return.
> 3. A successor whose frame matches the founder's on every residual-relevant axis closes the live slot at the new stage.
> 4. The live slot residual already appears as a leading indicator in the metric the board uses, before any outside frame names it.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Founder succession and entrepreneurship research venue: https://www.aom.org/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the links. Run it on a transition you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own author warns that outputs "must be seen as simulacra" when produced this way, and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Legal and practical note

Use the public institutional channels in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. No personal emails of named individuals, no purchased lists, no automation, no misleading subject lines, and an opt-out line in every message. GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules apply to unsolicited outreach in many jurisdictions. A reply is a conversation, never corroboration.
