## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about founder resistance to replacement and about the point at which leadership transition is necessary. Option A: resistance is a character defect and transition follows a size threshold. Option B: resistance is a no-anchor and standing effect, and transition follows a residual-against-ground rule with a successor whose frame differs in the dimension the residual names. What each leads to: A picks a successor from a chart and treats the founder's disagreement as noise; B describes a live slot first, declares the founder's stake, keeps the checker outside, and returns a shape rather than a name.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: two conditions, R1 resistance and R2 transition, so a union compound rather than one hard question (Fig 9 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Symmetry: asymmetric, because the market anchor does not update on the founder's frame while that frame updates on its own history. Scale: individual frame, organisation habitat, market ground, crossing a membrane with a direction (Fig 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction.").

- **Domain 1, the founder frame.** Actors: founder, belief, weighting, standing, identity. Currency: self-assessment agreement and standing. Anchor: none nameable inside the frame (Fig 2 :13 "no anchor; each updates on the others"), so self-grading is a collapse and becomes checkable only when ranged against the market from outside. Question type: partition boundary between frame and ground; its verdict is `specified`, never `grounded` from inside.
- **Domain 2, the organisation as habitat.** Actors: founder, team, board, processes, market. Currency: capability and contact. Anchor: the market, one of Fig 2 :29's examples, "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Question type: agent-and-habitat composition, with the founder as an actor whose valence changes with the residual.
- **Domain 3, verification and succession.** Actors: board, owners, candidates, founder as an interested party. Currency: verdicts, standing, fit. Anchor: the market outcome, which must not move when the founder frames it. Question type: selection between rooms, with the ground voting (Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes.").
- **Partition or union.** D1 and D2 overlap at the founder, D2 and D3 at the board, D1 and D3 at the founder's stake. Overlaps are compounds with their own loops, so the verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").** Anchor, then vocabulary for what the next stage affords, then the live slot objective, then the successor plan, then methods carried forward. Starting at the successor plan is the named failure.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Founder resistance is a no-anchor and standing effect, not a character defect and not evidence that the founder is right. Capability belongs to the leader and the habitat together, so a handover is a substitution of habitat and changing the person alone changes nothing. Transition is owed when a named live slot residual persists against a market the founder's frame cannot move and the binding link has moved past that frame; the next leader must differ from the founder in the dimension the residual names and match the residual the founder surfaced. The playbook is a recording until distilled into a method, and the checker must sit outside the founder's framing. No size threshold and no named successor are claimed. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and their refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is acting on a chart threshold or a celebrity hire, which buys a recording instead of a generator and spends the board's independence. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the live slot and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A wrong succession decision can be superseded; the shape claim can be narrowed without withdrawing the habitat rule; the refuters can be replaced with better instruments. No primitive has to be discarded, and Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." describes the safe way to narrow: add the refusal, keep the record.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A slot instrument that measures the residual separately from the aggregate, a contact test that moves the founder's contacts while holding the person constant, a room test in which a matching-frame successor closes the residual, a standing test across uncelebrated founders, or a catalyst test on a named slot. Any of the five pre-registered refuters in `01` §3 would also reverse or narrow the verdict. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
