## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134-135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** here is the form a succession answer must have before a successor can be named. It constrains; it does not fill. In this solution:

- Shape: "a frame whose closure differs from the founder's in the dimension the live residual names."
- Name: "hire this particular executive" or "promote this particular insider."

The shape is checkable. The name is licensed only by the ground paying for it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for new terms that overlap or correlate, and recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." The stopping condition is paradoxical: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place".
- Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is exactly why the founder's tacit context cannot be handed to a successor in a document; only a description of effects can be checked.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and "indiscernibility is not identity. The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect." A successor who looks like the founder on paper is not thereby the same frame.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "SETTLE     the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Hiring from outside is such an import; the record of where the candidate's methods came from is what separates a convergent discovery from an adopted practice.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." Applied here: a leadership rubric that declares its axes has made a choice visible, not neutral. That is worth a great deal, and it is not the same as being right.

## 4. Why an instrument, not a further argument, closes the slot

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied to this question: a strong revenue quarter is a reading, not a source. It is consistent with a founder whose contact is still load-bearing and with a founder whose contact has quietly become redundant. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates those sources, not a better narrative about either one. This is the reason F3 and the pre-registered refuter in `01` ask for a named slot residual rather than another explanation.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a successor? ANSWERED, with a correction

**No, with a boundary.** The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Its subject is a primitive not held in the library. A successor is not a missing primitive of the framework; it is an actor in the habitat (Fig 11 :45 "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"). Naming a candidate is therefore not the forbidden move in itself.

**What is forbidden** is treating that name as a derivation, or claiming that selecting it moves the wall. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

**Correction to my own first reading.** I initially wrote this section as if "shape, not a name" forbade any candidate shortlist. That overstates the rule. The rule governs missing primitives and the output of a derivation; it does not forbid an actor being named once the ground pays. The section is retained with the correction visible rather than silently rewritten. The honest form is: the framework returns the **shape** of the successor; the **name** is a provenanced choice made by a seat, and it carries that seat's stake (Fig 10 :60).

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A famous outside CEO, a celebrated founder, and a well connected board are all prestige proxies. None of them settles whether the company's contact changed. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." From *Arrival of the Fittest*: "the outputs generated must be seen as simulacra." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the succession question and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. What the essays add that the figures do not

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the capability rule in the exact words used in this solution: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." It adds that "Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times", and that rooms should be opened with a question and closed with it: "The room composition is a function of the question. When the question changes, the room should change." That is the essay-level grounding for F8.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* adds the warn about two people who "drift away from the truth together if they only ever check against each other", and the remedy: "a ground that both are corrected by, separately." That is the grounding for keeping the founder's framing away from the checker in F11.

All eight essays share an author with the figures. Their agreement with the figure verdict is therefore one evidence pool talking (Fig 2 :40), and is recorded as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`. They are used here to read the framework's own concept of "shape", not as independent witnesses to the business claim.
