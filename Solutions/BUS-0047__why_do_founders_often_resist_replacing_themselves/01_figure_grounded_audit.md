## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual in effect terms

The question is two conditions, not one hard question. They get separate loops and separate verdicts (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").

**R1, the resistance condition.** In a growing company, the founder's prediction that they must remain in the top seat persists and does not trend down even where the market returns a residual against it. In effect terms: a frame that is itself part of the habitat is trying to grade its own necessity. Nothing in R1 is a claim about personality. It is a claim about which reference the frame ranges against.

**R2, the transition condition.** A leadership transition is necessary when the organisation's capability no longer composes over the founder in isolation, that is when the binding constraint has moved to a slot the founder frame cannot close. In effect terms: changing the agent without changing contact changes nothing.

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term).** The sought-for shape is written down and bound before the run, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM so it accrues a residual like anything else. Bound form: *transition is owed when a live slot residual persists against a ground the founder frame cannot move, and the incoming frame's closure differs from the founder's in the dimension the residual names.* This shape is refusable by the same test as everything else.

**Options and what each leads to.**

- **A:** resistance is a character defect and transition follows a size or revenue threshold. Leads to selecting a successor from a chart, treating disagreement as noise, and grading the founder with a metric the founder helps produce.
- **B:** resistance is a standing-and-contact effect and transition follows a residual-against-ground rule. Leads to describing the live slot before selecting anyone, declaring the founder's stake, and keeping the verdict with a frame that cannot reconstruct the claim.

The audit below derives before concluding. B is not assumed; it is what the figure lines govern after the census.

## 1b. Domain census, done before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** two conditions, R1 and R2. Union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54). Each gets its own loop and its own ground reading.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The anchor does not update on the founder; the founder frame may update on its own history (Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."). A symmetric "fit" reading would be a negotiation, not a triangulation.
- **Scale:** individual frame (founder self-assessment), organisation habitat (founder plus team plus processes), market ground (what settles). The boundary between the first two is a membrane with a direction (Fig 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction.").

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

- **D1, the founder frame.** Actors: founder, belief `b`, weighting `w`, standing, identity. Currency: self-assessment agreement and standing. Anchor inside the frame: **none nameable**. Fig 2 :13 "no anchor; each updates on the others". Without an outside anchor this is Fig 2's first collapse, not a domain that can settle itself. It becomes checkable only when ranged against the market from outside (Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."). It is kept as a domain because its effect, persistent resistance, is real and has its own mechanism, but its verdict is `specified`, never `grounded` from inside.
- **D2, the organisation as habitat.** Actors: founder, team, board, processes, market. Currency: capability and contact. Anchor: the market, one of Fig 2 :29's examples: "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." This is a nameable anchor, so D2 is a genuine domain.
- **D3, verification and succession.** Actors: board, owners, candidates, founder as interested party. Currency: verdicts, standing, fit. Anchor: the market outcome, which the founder frame must not be able to move (Fig 10 :42, Fig 2 :46). A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame (Fig 2 :47).

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 overlap at the founder: the founder is both a frame and an actor in the habitat. That overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :55-57), so the two verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. D2 and D3 overlap at the board. D1 and D3 overlap at the founder's stake. The union is not unbundled into a single "leadership problem"; it is split into three loops with one shared market anchor.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).**

1. Anchor link: is there a ground the founder frame cannot move? Without it the next links have no reference.
2. Vocabulary link: terms for what the next stage affords (what counts as progress after growth).
3. Objective link: the live slot residual, stated in effect terms.
4. Planning link: how the successor is chosen and integrated.
5. Learn-and-carry link: methods, not recordings, handed to the next stage.

The common failure is starting at link 4, a successor search, before links 1 to 3 exist. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Effort above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

## 2. Findings

Each finding carries its mode and its own refuter. Verdicts stay per domain.

### F1 - D1: resistance is coherent drift, not a measurement (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 2 :13 "no anchor; each updates on the others"; Fig 2 :14-15 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

The founder's self-assessment is a frame that has spent years updating on its own successes and on the team's agreement. That is Fig 2 panel 1, not panel 3. The market is a separate anchor and does not move when the founder argues. Resistance is therefore not evidence that the founder is right; it is the expected output of a frame with no outside reference.

World corroboration only: *Motivated reasoning* (Wikipedia, 2026) defines the mechanism as "a mental process through which individuals access, construct, and evaluate their beliefs in response to new information or experiences where their biases cause them to arrive at desired conclusions"; *Social identity theory* (Wikipedia, 2026) adds "Social identities are a valued aspect of the self, and people will sacrifice their pecuniary self-interest to maintain the self-perception that they belong to a given social group."

**Refuter:** a founder self-assessment instrument whose output predicts the market return without being moved by the founder's standing or by team agreement. If that exists, the drift reading fails.

### F2 - D1: standing survives the outcome that earned it (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 10 :61 "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on." Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

The founder's authority was earned on the company's early success and survives into a stage where the question has changed (Fig 10 :72 "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."). The interest in the company's outcome is legitimate and unavoidable. The interest in the reading of whether the founder should stay is disqualifying for a verdict. Declare it; do not pretend it away and do not treat it as evidence.

World corroboration only: *Escalation of commitment* (Wikipedia, 2026) records "a project, product, or policy can become so closely tied to the values and purposes of the organization that it becomes almost unthinkable to consider withdrawal."

**Refuter:** founders with no public standing attached to the early success (never named as the founder in public) resist transition at the same rate and for the same stated reasons as celebrated founders. Then standing is not the mechanism.

### F3 - D2: the live slot is hidden in the aggregate (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A growing company can show good aggregate revenue while a single slot, for example executive decision throughput or customer escalation handling, carries a persistent positive residual. The founder reads the aggregate and concludes nothing is owed. The founder also helped design the metric used to grade them, which Fig 1 disqualifies as frame-internal. Fig 5 :43 "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."

**Refuter:** the live slot residual appears as a leading indicator in the aggregate metric the board actually uses, before any external frame names it. Then no slot is being hidden.

### F4 - D2: replacing the founder is not, by itself, a capability change (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

This is the answer to the "when necessary" half at its most basic level. Installing a new CEO changes the agent. It does not automatically change the company's contact with its ground (customers, regulators, cash, technology). If the conditions that made the founder capable are left intact and the new agent is given the same contacts, the capability of the pair is unchanged. The transition is necessary when the habitat itself has moved such that the founder's contact is no longer the binding one, not merely when the founder is tired or the chart says the company is big.

World corroboration only: *Bus factor* (Wikipedia, 2026) states "The 'bus factor' is the minimum number of team members that have to suddenly disappear from a project before the project stalls due to lack of knowledgeable or competent personnel." A bus factor of one names a contact concentration, not a person defect.

**Refuter:** a controlled succession in which changing only the agent, with habitat and contact held fixed, moves the ground return persistently. That would refute the agent-and-habitat claim as applied here.

### F5 - D2: isolation is substitution, and both losses are invisible at first (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails". Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".

Handing the company to an outside leader moves the residual into a substituted habitat. What the founder silently supplied (relationships, judgement under ambiguity, the trust of early employees) is not in the job description, so its absence registers only when the goal fails. What the newcomer brings (their own prior network, habits, incentives) was not chosen by the company and acts later. Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

World corroboration only: *Succession planning* (Wikipedia, 2026) says "No part of the process should be rushed, with the integration process being expected to take roughly two years." *Queen bee* (Wikipedia, 2026) records that "A queen bee that becomes old, or is diseased or failing, is replaced by the workers in a procedure known as 'supersedure'" and that "Balling is often a problem for beekeepers attempting to introduce a replacement queen." The animal analogue is corroboration, not a source of the verdict.

**Refuter:** a transition that reproduces every condition the goal required and introduces no unchosen actor, evidenced by a goal that does not fail and a new actor whose effects were all enumerated in advance.

### F6 - D2: the founder's valence is relative to the residual (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

This dissolves the common framing. The founder is not "good" or "bad" for the company. They are a benefactor to the startup residual and possibly a malfactor to the scale residual. The same person, unchanged, changes valence because the residual changed. So the question "is the founder still valuable?" is malformed; the answerable question is "valuable to which residual?"

**Refuter:** a founder who is a benefactor to every residual the company faces across all scales, with no slot where their presence raises a persistent residual. Then valence is a property after all.

### F7 - D3: transition follows the moved binding link (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :29-30 "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed." Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The "when" is therefore: when the link the company currently stops at is beyond what the founder's frame supplies, and that reading was measured rather than assumed. Revenue size is a filter for the budget (when to start a search), not a witness for the verdict. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

World corroboration only: *CEO succession* (Wikipedia, 2026) states "CEO succession is one of key functions of a board of directors." *Chief executive officer* (Wikipedia, 2026) states "The proper selection and evaluation of the CEO and the executive team is critical to the company's performance. Yet there is no established standard framework to evaluate and govern the CEO performance." The absence of a standard framework is why the live slot has to be described rather than looked up.

**Refuter:** the market ground returns no worsening residual on any slot while a successor is installed, and the board cannot name the link that moved. Then the transition was not necessary on this rule.

### F8 - D3: a successor must differ in the dimension the residual names (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 10 :72 "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom." Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :42 "the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

Difference for its own sake is not the rule. The next frame must differ **and** match the residual the last one surfaced. That is why "hire an outsider" and "promote the loyal insider" are both incomplete answers. The useful question is: which closure does the live slot require that the founder's closure lacks?

**Refuter:** a candidate whose closure matches the founder's on every residual-relevant axis closes the live slot at the new stage. Then "must differ" is false.

### F9 - D3: describe the residual before naming the successor (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :22 "arity · symmetry · scale".

A board that starts with a candidate shortlist has done the frame first. Any candidate will then seem to fit, because the fit test was never written down. The residue of a correct search is a kind of successor (a shape), not a name. The name is licensed only when the ground pays.

**Refuter:** a candidate selected before the residual was written down outperforms candidates selected after it in a controlled comparison of transitions. Then residual-first is not doing work.

### F10 - D3: hand over a method, not a recording (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

The founder's early playbook is a recording of one occasion. Replayed at the new scale it produces "a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one" (Fig 4 :26). From *How a Society of Agents Would Work* (Isaiah N, 2026), the commons holds "only methods distilled from finished rooms", and *Succession planning* (Wikipedia, 2026) records "There is no widely accepted formula for evaluating the future potential of leaders". So the handover should be a generator, not a script.

**Refuter:** a successor who faithfully replays the founder's early playbook settles the new stage's ground persistently. Then the recording is a method after all.

### F11 - D3: the checker must not be reconstructible from the claim (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".

A board composed of people selected by the founder, briefed by the founder, and rewarded by the founder cannot check the founder's necessity. Their agreement is the pool talking. The board that can check is one that ranges separately against the market and is denied the founder's framing of the reading. Fig 10 :59: the interest in the outcome is unavoidable; the interest in the reading is disqualifying, and where a stake cannot be removed it must be declared.

**Refuter:** a board with no founder overlap, no founder briefing and no founder reward dependency returns the same verdict as a founder-selected board on the same disclosed data. Then independence was not doing work.

### F12 - D1 to D2 bridge: the held term owes a refusal, not a further explanation (`general`, triangulated)

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: "REFUTED              a term held is false; what is owed / is a refusal, not an explanation". Operators :57 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 12 :134 "AMENDMENT · a bond is a binary operator, and this figure defines the bonds. It does not define the unary ones."

The founder's held term "I am still essential" is a candidate for the wrong term. It ranks by the quantity already recorded: a residual that persists and does not trend down (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"). What is owed is to refuse that term's standing to compose over the next stage, not to produce a better explanation of why the founder should stay. Nothing is deleted; the reach falls because something was added.

**Refuter:** the residual of the "still essential" term trends down as the company grows, measured on a slot named in advance. Then no refusal is owed.

### F13 - D2: is the founder a catalyst or a spent part? (`general`, single-source)

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A founder may survive their own use, being regenerated by each new challenge, or be spent by it. Fig 12 says nothing records which. This is the one genuinely open sub-question in the derivation: the figure names the distinction but supplies no instrument. `general`, single-source, explicitly ungrounded.

**Refuter:** an instrument that shows the founder's decision quality is regenerated by each new stage rather than degraded by it, on a slot named in advance. Then the catalyst reading is settled and the open item closes.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before the outreach materials, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in §0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor failure.** A founder self-assessment instrument predicts the market return without being moved by the founder's standing or by team agreement. (Breaks F1 and F2.)
2. **Capability failure.** A controlled succession in which the agent alone changes, with habitat and contact held fixed, persistently moves the ground return. (Breaks F4, the decisive finding.)
3. **Room failure.** A successor whose closure matches the founder's on every residual-relevant axis closes the live slot at the new stage. (Breaks F8 and the successor shape.)
4. **Slot failure.** The live slot residual appears as a leading indicator in the aggregate metric the board already uses, before any outside frame names it. (Breaks F3.)
5. **Re-census failure.** A figure re-census shows Fig 11's agent-and-habitat rule governs biological habitats only and does not govern organisations. (Breaks the primary derivation at its root.)

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed to the surviving findings, and the withdrawal is recorded here rather than silently erased. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the question into R1 and R2 before any search. They are a union compound with separate loops (Fig 9 :54).
2. For R1, declare the founder's stake and stop grading the founder with a metric the founder helped produce (Fig 10 :60, Fig 1 :27).
3. For R2, name the live slot in effect terms before naming any candidate (Fig 9 :27-28).
4. Treat company size as a filter for when to start the search, never as the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
5. Select a successor whose closure differs in the dimension the residual names, not one who merely resembles or merely contrasts with the founder (Fig 7 :36).
6. Hand over methods and head starts, not the founder's recorded playbook (Fig 4 :22-25).
7. Keep the checker outside: no founder-selected, founder-briefed, founder-rewarded board as the verdict source (Fig 10 :42).
8. Use the public, opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, ask for a refutation, and record the verdict before showing the framework.
