## What the wider literature contributes, and what it does not

1. **Gross and Blasius (2008)** is the closest world source to the question. It establishes that
   adaptive networks are defined by a state/topology feedback loop, and it documents both regimes:
   networks that freeze into "a final configuration, a so-called network Nash equilibrium", and
   networks that "does not approach a frozen configuration, but remains evolving as links keep
   appearing and disappearing". Its glossary defines a "frozen node" per node, which matches Fig 1's
   per-slot rule. It also states a condition on time scales: "in every scale-separated system there
   has to be a discontinuous transition in the fast dynamics in order to maintain an adaptive
   interplay in the long-term evolution of the system. Otherwise the fast dynamics is simply slaved to
   the slow dynamics." This is corroboration for the dependency order in `01` section 1, not a figure
   line, and it is recorded as such.

2. **Tognoli and Kelso (2014)** and **Roberts et al. (2019)** are a low-overlap field (neuroscience)
   relative to the network-physics literature. They corroborate the structural claim that there are
   qualitatively distinct regimes: phase-locked attractors, metastable dwells and escapes, and
   near-independence. Tognoli and Kelso: "Strong and symmetrical coupling in similar components gives
   rise to stable behavior, whereas weaker coupling, and/or lesser symmetry in the components and
   their coupling gives rise to metastability." Roberts et al.: "no single wave pattern endures."
   These sources supply the **direction** of the sign claim in F3, which the figure itself leaves
   unnamed. They do not supply the figure verdict.

3. **Scheffer et al. (2009)** (ecology and complex systems) corroborates that a regime shift is not
   decided by agreement among observers and that generic early-warning signals can indicate an
   approaching threshold. This supports the F6 measurement rule: watch a trend per slot, not a
   global average at one time point.

The four world sources come from different evidence pools (network physics/biology, coordination
dynamics neuroscience, computational neuroscience, ecological resilience). No two of them quote the
same review. They corroborate; they never overrule a figure. Where two of them are both neuroscience
(S2 and S3), that overlap is declared rather than hidden.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full framework
  before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42**: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has
  absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask
  what they would expect to see in a settled network before giving them any theory.
- **Fig 2 :40**: "disqualifying for a verdict" covers agreement that shares an evidence pool. A mass
  send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22**: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a method, so it
  may be given away. A conclusion is not a method.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the union claim and its four refuters; (2) the question "what
would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the local figure extract
and the three public source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the
authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the shape claim, the mode, and the refuters. Ask for a verdict. Record it before
anything else is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure extract and the two closest world
sources. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A stage-2 reply is a
conversation.

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user must write their own refuter and state
their own mode, because a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question" (`THE_FORMULA.md`,
Direction).

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. The checks in
`08_expert_verification_packet.md` are the gate.
