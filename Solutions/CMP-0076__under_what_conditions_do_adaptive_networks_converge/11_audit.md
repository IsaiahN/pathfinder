## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down",
`gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to
be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per slot), Fig 2 :33-34
   (non-updating anchor), Fig 12 :121 (cycle sign), Fig 12 :106 and Fig 13 :117-118 (settlement
   versus closed reach), and the `THE_FORMULA.md` Closure sentence.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: settling is a union of conditions,
   the reference must not update, the sign decides, measure per slot, maintenance is work, and there
   is no internal criterion.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a specific adaptive network: it converged because
   rewiring slowed past a threshold, with a global edge-change rate as the evidence.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot requirement** is dropped when the global edge-change rate is substituted as evidence
  (Fig 1 :19, :22);
- the **non-updating-anchor requirement** is dropped when the threshold is measured against a
  reference the network itself produces (Fig 2 :33-34);
- the **no-internal-criterion limit** is dropped when threshold crossing is treated as a certificate
  rather than a candidate (Closure, Fig 6 :27).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the network is a cycle and its sign is the deciding variable.
That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading
exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the
formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error
Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap.
