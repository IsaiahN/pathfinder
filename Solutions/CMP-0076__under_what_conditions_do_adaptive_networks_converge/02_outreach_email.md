**Subject:** One checkable claim about when adaptive networks settle

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about adaptive networks I would like you to check against what you
> already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with
> anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an adaptive network is a cycle, node states change,
> links rewire in response, and the rewired topology feeds the next state update. The claim is that
> settling is not produced by one lever such as a coupling strength or a rewiring rate. It is a union
> of conditions that must hold together:
>
> 1. There is a reference that does not update on the network. If the only reference is the network's
>    own last output, the system converges by mutual adjustment, and what it converges on is whoever
>    had the most influence. That is drift, not settlement.
> 2. The rewiring loop has the sign that closes it. The same output re-enters as input in both regimes;
>    the sign decides whether the loop closes or amplifies.
> 3. A composed structure is settled against that reference and then admitted as a part that later
>    structure can be built on. Without this, continued rewiring is a search through arrangements
>    inside a closed set.
> 4. Convergence is measured per node or per link, not as a network-wide average. A global residual
>    near zero with one live slot is a legal state. Under averaging, the live signal disappears.
> 5. If a maintainer is steering the network, a stable structure is permanent work, not a solved
>    state. An in-silico result is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability.
>
> **What the framework does not supply:** a convergence criterion of its own. The loop stops when the
> residual stops arriving, and from inside the frame a perfect prediction and a closed channel look
> the same. So "converged" is offered as a `candidate` verdict, not a measurement. I record that as a
> genuine limit, not as silence. I also do not claim that continual reorganization is always driven
> by residual. The system can manufacture its own gradient when nothing is scoring.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. A network correctly called converged while at least one node or link still has positive residual
>    that does not trend down.
> 2. A network that demonstrably settles against a reference that itself updates on the network, with
>    that result shown to be settlement rather than coherent drift.
> 3. A frame that can tell, from its own instruments alone, a perfect prediction from a closed
>    channel.
> 4. A single lever, used alone and without a ground reading, that provably decides whether settling
>    occurred.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no
> reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract):
>   `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Gross T, Blasius B, "Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review", J R Soc Interface 2008:
>   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2405905/
> - Tognoli E, Kelso JAS, "The Metastable Brain", Neuron 2014:
>   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3997258/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on an adaptive network
> you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before*
> you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a
> reported success.
>
