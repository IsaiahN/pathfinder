# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the neutron star equation of state

**What this is:** a claim in a form a nuclear physicist or high-energy astrophysicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. Send this one, not the whole folder.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the phase name. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at `2 to 10 n_sat` is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The origin of the neutron star equation of state is a union of three conditions, not one question with one answer: a low-density chiral-EFT band that returns a controlled result up to about one to two saturation densities; a high-density perturbative-QCD pressure boundary at roughly thirty to fifty saturation densities that currently has errors too large to constrain star densities; and observational posteriors from gravitational waves and X-ray timing that form a non-updating anchor. Between them, at two to ten saturation densities, the phase name is an explicitly open slot. Over a fixed atom set, a first-order-transition EOS and a smooth-crossover EOS are isomers, same degrees of freedom arranged with a different bond, and the difference is decisive in behaviour. The framework returns that shape and its falsifiers, not the name of the phase.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable by reading Fig 2 and the sign-problem literature

- **Statement:** the intermediate-density slot has no anchor that settles the phase, so the name is a `gap` recorded as unreachable at this budget, not a silence and not underivable in principle.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."` `Fig 6 :34-35: "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."` `Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"` `Fig 13 :134: "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"`
- **What to check:** is there any instrument at `2 to 10 n_sat` that currently returns a phase reading with controlled error? The sign-problem review (Nagata 2021) states *"the Euclidean action becomes complex, and the importance sampling is not applicable"* and *"However, the phase realized in a neutron star is currently unknown."*
- **Refuter:** a first-principles or quantum-simulation calculation of `P(ε)` at `2 to 10 n_sat` with controlled error that names a phase, passes a source gate and has a shadow test. Or any instrument at any resolution already returning something that fails to resolve.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable against the two boundary frames and the observations

- **Statement:** low-density χEFT is a settled band that becomes an atom for the next band; high-density pQCD is a boundary condition with provenance, not a ground reading at star densities; the observations cut a corridor that many EOS constructions share.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"` `Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."` `Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"` `Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"`
- **What to check:** does the χEFT band really return controlled uncertainties only to about `1 to 2 n_sat`? Does pQCD currently constrain star densities, or only a thermodynamic bound? Do the GW170817 and NICER posteriors rule out models without selecting one phase? Alarcón et al. 2025: *"the intermediate density region is more complicated, and normally requires some modeling."* Zhou 2024: *"the large pQCD uncertainties preclude any meaningful bounds on the neutron star EOS at the moment."* LIGO/Virgo: *"ruling out several equation-of-state models at the 90% credible level."*
- **Refuter:** the low-density band failing below `1 to 2 n_sat`; the high-density boundary failing above `30 to 50 n_sat`; or a measurement that selects one phase over another with a common `(M, R, Λ)` reading.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a physics check

- **Statement:** over the full shared atom set, a first-order-transition EOS and a smooth-crossover EOS are isomers; over the observational corridor they are a functional group. The union of the three conditions is not a partition, so one lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."` `Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."` `Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."` `Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."`
- **What to check:** do the two constructions use the same degrees of freedom? If yes, the difference is the bond and the isomer claim holds at that level. If one requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. Is there a discriminating observable (latent heat, post-merger spectral peak) that makes the difference decisive in behaviour?
- **Refuter:** one construction requiring an atom the other does not; or identical posteriors with no discriminating observable at any resolution, which removes the "decisive in behaviour" half.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final EOS. `Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room"`
- Not a name for the phase. Quark matter, hyperons and meson condensates are candidates in the habitat, not the answer.
- Not that the sign problem is solved, or that lattice QCD returns the core EOS.
- Not that pQCD measures `5 n_sat`; it is a boundary condition with provenance.
- Not that a posterior is the ground, or that agreement among models is corroboration (`Fig 2 :40`).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement at `2 to 10 n_sat`.
- Not an import that moves the wall: naming an already-held degree of freedom is a binding from the physics frame, not a new atom (`Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."`).
