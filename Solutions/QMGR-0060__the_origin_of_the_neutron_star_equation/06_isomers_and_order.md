## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are a transition EOS and a crossover EOS isomers?

- `Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."`
- `Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."`
- `Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."`

Write two candidate EOS constructions over the same atom set `{nucleons, quarks, chemical potential, pressure, energy density}`:

- **EOS-T:** atoms joined by a first-order transition bond (a segment of constant pressure while the density jumps, a latent heat).
- **EOS-C:** the same atoms joined by a smooth crossover bond (a steep but continuous sound-speed band).

Same ingredients, different typed bond, different macroscopic behaviour: a latent-heat segment and a possible post-merger frequency shift in one, a smooth band in the other. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not variants of one curve. The difference is invisible in a table that records only the ingredients and the endpoint pressures, and decisive in the behaviour. Mode `specified`: the figure definition is `general`, the identification of transition versus crossover as the bond is a binding from the physics frame, not a figure line.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the same endpoint pressures and the same measurement posteriors can be produced by both bond arrangements with no discriminating observable at any resolution, the isomer claim loses its "decisive in the behaviour" half and stands only as a notation distinction. If one arrangement requires an atom the other does not, the two are not isomers at all.

### Q2 - Are they a functional group instead?

- `Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."`

If the comparison is made at the level of "a pressure band that supports `2 M_sun` and passes the tidal constraint", the shared substructure is the kind (a stiff-enough band between two boundary conditions), and "first-order" or "crossover" is the qualifier that says which member. Both readings are legitimate at different levels, which is the level-relativity of `Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"`. At the level of the full atom set they are isomers; at the level of the measurement corridor they are members of one functional group. Kept separate, not averaged.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order, and where does the chain break?

- `Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."`
- `Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."`
- `Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."`

Dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception:** D4 observations reached. Masses, radii and tidal deformabilities are read.
2. **Vocabulary:** partly reached. D1 and D2 have terms for what the matter affords; D3 has a list of candidate phases (the habitat) but no selector.
3. **Objective:** **not reached.** What would count as "the origin"? No instrument states what would count as progress toward naming the phase. This is the break.
4. **Planning:** over the break. More EOS parameterizations and more phenomenological phase models land above the break and cannot be used by link 3.

`Fig 3 :19` applies directly: optimizing link 2 (more candidate phase models) cannot rescue link 3 (no objective or instrument). The break is between 3 and 4, drawn by the missing discriminator, not by a lack of models.

### Q4 - What do the two currencies price?

- `Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."`
- `Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."`

An EOS model with many parameters is expensive to describe (bits) and may be cheap to refute with one mass measurement (actions). A short, clean two-boundary shape is cheap to state and may take a whole observational campaign to settle. Neither figure predicts the other. `Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."` A model-count or a chi-squared is a filter for which calculation to try next, never a witness for which phase is real.

### Q5 - Does the union have a surplus?

- `Fig 8 :50: "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."`
- `Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"`

D2 (pQCD) and D1 (χEFT) share the atom "pressure". They reach it by separate routes at opposite density ends, so their agreement on the need for a monotonic connection is corroboration at maximum independence, provided the record shows the separate provenance. Zhou 2024 reports the two sets of constraints are *"orthogonal"*, which is the surplus case: two frames together delimit a region neither reaches alone. But D2 is imported into the star-density question through a thermodynamic bound, so the record must show the source gate. If the provenance is lost, the same curve would be read as convergent derivation when it is an adopted import.

### Q6 - What is the analogue in another field?

The inverse-problem article (mathematics, opened 2026-11-23) gives the structural analogue: *"operator F is not likely to be one-to-one, therefore there can be more than one pre-image."* EOS-T and EOS-C are two pre-images of the same forward map `F` (the TOV equations composed with the observation operator). This is not a metaphor imported as evidence; it is a low-overlap corroboration of the many-to-one shape already derived from `Fig 9 :32` and the metamerism essay. The SEP underdetermination entry (philosophy, opened 2026-11-23) adds the general form: *"the evidence available to us at a given time may be insufficient to determine what beliefs we should hold in response to it."*

## CONCLUSION

Transition and crossover EOS constructions over a fixed atom set are isomers under Fig 12: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. Over the measurement corridor they are members of one functional group. The dependency chain breaks at the objective link, where no instrument says what would count as naming the phase; adding more models lands above the break. The two currencies, description bits and settling actions, do not add, and the pQCD/χEFT pair has a surplus only if their separate provenance is recorded. All of this is `specified` on the physics binding and `general` on the figure rules, and none of it names the phase.
