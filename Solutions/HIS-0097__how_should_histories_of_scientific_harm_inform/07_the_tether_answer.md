# The Tether's answer: how harm histories should inform current decisions

**Mode: `specified` for the policy shape and for each world-facing claim, `general` for the structural rules. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A decision-maker has a record of past scientific harm and must decide what it licenses now. The bad outcomes are symmetric in their failure, not in their content: ignoring the record repeats the harm; treating the record as a verdict on all research throws away the signal that says where the harm occurred. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The answer has to preserve that index.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split the question before answering it.** The question is a union of four conditions: is the claim correct, was the process acceptable to those it affected, were benefits and burdens justly distributed, was the checking independent. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.

2. **Treat the harm record as a scoped refusal, not a global verdict.** Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Negation acts on one operand. Name the claim being refused. Do not negate the library.

3. **Keep the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global trust score for "research" is the aggregation error. The record tells you which protocol, which funding relation, which institution, which affected group. That is where to look.

4. **Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, at the joints the record shows were corrupted.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* If the institution supplies both the claim and the judgment that the claim is safe, the anchor has become a frame. Independent review, registered protocols and affected-community participation are the graded response, not a declaration that research is untrustworthy.

5. **Give the ethics question an anchor that is about people, and the correctness question its own ground.** Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38 warns that an instrument *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Human judgment is the right anchor for acceptability and an instrument for correctness. Mixing the two produces both blind trust and blanket distrust.

6. **Carry methods, not recordings.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The transferable object from Tuskegee, Belmont or Asilomar is the checking procedure with its provenance, not the list of harms. Take the round trip and measure the gap (Fig 4 :30).

7. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Harm is often second or third order: who funded, who could have treated, who inherited the false result. Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

8. **Declare every stake that cannot be removed.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*

9. **Stop where the instruments stop.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework names the properties a policy must have, not the specific policy. That needs a ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that research is inherently untrustworthy. That is the aggregation error (F3) and the arity error (F1).
- Not that a harmed community's judgment is unreliable. It is the anchor for the acceptability question (F4).
- Not that an institution can certify its own safety. Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
- Not that harm histories are irrelevant to correctness. They are a strong instrument for finding corruption of the checking channel (F5), but they are not a measurement of any particular claim.
- Not a general policy that fits all four conditions with one lever. The conditions form a union (F8).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this run settled whether any proposed rule reduced harm.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating check, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the case was resolved by a better instrument. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Slot test:** does keeping the residual per slot locate a harmful protocol that a global trust score misses? A blind comparison of the two policies on a set of known cases would fire here.
- **Independence test:** does buying independent checking at a corroded joint change outcomes at equal cost? Fig 2 :42 is falsifiable in principle.
- **Union test:** does a single lever clear all four conditions, or does a specific compound remain after it fires? Fig 9 :55-57 predicts the latter.
- **Cascade test:** does first-order habitat scoping miss a harm that a second- or third-order enumeration finds? Fig 11 :52 gives DDT and the eagles as the known case.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a figure line that licenses global averaging without signal loss; intrinsic trustworthiness independent of the residual; a controlled case where added independence made outcomes worse at equal cost; a harm record that cannot refuse one claim without refusing all.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* From *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The four conditions and the six figures used to split them are a visible choice, not nature's own list. Another frame may hold a better decomposition. The answer stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that research is untrustworthy, and it does not say to ignore a history of harm. It says the question is a bundle, so split it into correctness, acceptability, justice and independence before proposing any single fix. The harm record is a refusal of a specific claim, not a verdict on the whole library: keep it indexed to the protocol, funder, institution and affected group it concerns, because averaging across slots destroys the signal that says where to look. Buy independent checking in proportion to the cost of being wrong at the joints where the record shows independence was corrupted, give the ethics question an anchor that is constitutively about people, carry the checking method rather than the list of past harms, and enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering. The framework returns that shape, not a named policy and not a ground verdict. What would settle it is a comparison at the ground: does per-slot, provenance-bearing, independence-buying checking actually locate harms that a global verdict misses, at acceptable cost? Until that runs, the claim is `specified` and ungrounded.
