## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how a record of past scientific harm should bear on a current research decision. Option A: ignore the record and judge the claim on its own ground. Option B: treat the record as proof that all research is inherently untrustworthy. What each leads to: A repeats a harm that was already visible and refuses to range the claim against a known failure; B averages a per-slot residual into a global verdict, loses the locating signal, and refuses every claim including the instruments that could discriminate the harmful case from the harmless one. The sought-for shape, bound before the run so it can accrue a residual, is a per-slot, provenance-bearing, independence-buying, stake-declared, habitat-enumerating rule that refuses a named term rather than a whole library.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a research institution predicts about its own claim and what the historical record shows happened. Arity: many slots (protocol, funder, institution, affected group, claim). Symmetry: asymmetric. Harm and benefit are relations to the residual, not properties carried by an actor (Fig 11 :49-51). Scale: first order at a claim and its protocol, second order at a policy about research generally. The question as asked is a union of four overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55-57).
- **Domain 1, the historical record.** Actors: historians, archives, harmed communities, documents, review panels. Currency: documented evidence and its provenance. Anchor: what actually occurred, a record, a withheld treatment, a patient outcome (Fig 2 :29-30). Genuine domain, nameable anchor.
- **Domain 2, the current research decision.** Actors: researchers, review boards, funders, journals, regulators. Currency: protocols, predictions, claims, funding. Anchor: the ground for the current claim, an experiment, an outcome, a measurement. Genuine domain, nameable anchor.
- **Domain 3, the verification and policy frame.** Actors: decision-makers, expert checkers, affected communities, the public. Currency: agreement, residual, trust. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :40). Genuine domain, nameable anchor.
- **Partition or union.** D1 to D2 to D3 is a dependency chain; D2's four conditions are a union, not a partition. One lever clears the regions where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of the harm, then vocabulary for its mechanism, then the objective of a safe decision, then the plan, then carrying the checked method. Harm histories enter most strongly at the vocabulary and objective links, the middle of the chain where Fig 3 :27 says a link with no instrument is not a link that works.
- **Strain test applied.** Fig 2 :36 makes affected judgment the legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about people, while Fig 2 :37-38 calls human judgment an instrument on axes it does not measure. Re-census: the two lines govern different questions, acceptability and correctness. Two objects, so no strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; neither extreme does. A harm history is a scoped refusal of a named term with provenance, kept per slot, not a verdict on research as a category. The decision is a union of four conditions, so it must be unbundled before any lever is applied. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong at the joints the record shows were corrupted; acceptability has a ground that is constitutively about people, while correctness has its own ground; stakes are declared; the habitat is enumerated outward; the method rather than the recording transfers. The framework names those properties and stops. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest single figure-grounded point is Fig 1 :22, because it gives the "without treating all research as untrustworthy" clause a structural reason rather than a rhetorical one.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, three refuters and its provenance before its conclusion, so an error can be found rather than absorbed. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a global verdict or a theory instead of a per-slot claim and refuter. Fig 2 :42 prices that: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The two extremes are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A published verdict can be superseded by a scoped one, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled comparison in which per-slot, provenance-bearing, independence-buying checking fails to locate harms that a global verdict catches, at equal cost, would reverse the main claim. A figure re-census that permits global averaging without signal loss would break F3. A case where a harmed community's judgment is shown to be an instrument rather than the anchor for acceptability would break F4. A case where no cascade exists beyond direct contact would narrow F10, not reverse it. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
