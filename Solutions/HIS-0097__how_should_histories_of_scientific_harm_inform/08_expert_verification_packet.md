# Expert verification packet: the Tether on histories of scientific harm

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, research-ethics scholar or science-studies researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claim, `general` on the structural rules. Not `grounded`.** No measurement in this run established that any proposed policy reduced harm.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A history of scientific harm should be used as a scoped refusal of the particular claim that failed, with provenance, not as a global verdict on research. It should stay indexed by slot (protocol, funding relation, institution, affected group), because Fig 1 :19-22 says a per-slot residual disappears when averaged. The decision question is a union of four conditions: correctness, acceptability, justice and independence, so it must be unbundled before any single lever is applied. Independent checking should be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong at the joints the record shows were corrupted, every stake that cannot be removed should be declared, and the checking method rather than the list of harms should be what transfers between cases. The framework returns that shape. It does not name a specific policy and it is not a ground verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** a harm record expresses a negation of one term, not a negation of the library, and averaging across slots destroys the locating signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Operators table :57 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.
- **Refuter:** show a figure rule that licenses a global verdict over all research without signal loss, or a harm record that cannot be expressed as a refusal of one claim without refusing every claim alongside it.

### C2. Structure of the decision; checkable against Fig 9's union rule and any real decision

- **Statement:** the question is a union of at least four overlapping conditions. A lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing. The conditions are correctness, acceptability, justice and independence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **What to check:** take a harm case you know. Does a single intervention (training, a code of conduct, a dismissal) clear all four conditions, or does a compound remain? Is "is the result correct" answerable on the same ground as "was the process acceptable to those it affected"?
- **Refuter:** show that one of the four conditions is strictly implied by another, so they form a partition and one lever does clear the intersections.

### C3. World-facing, graded response; checkable by a practitioner against known cases

- **Statement:** the right response is graded independence, bought where the record shows the anchor was corrupted, rather than a constant refusal. Harm histories are instruments for finding the corrupted joint, including the second- and third-order cascade.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- **What to check:** in a known case, was the corruption at the funding joint, the consent joint, the publication joint, or downstream? Did a graded response exist, as at Asilomar, where containment was matched to estimated risk? Was the relevant consequence first order or several steps out?
- **Refuter:** a controlled case where a constant refusal dominates every graded rule at equal cost, or a harm case where no cascade existed and the first-order scope was sufficient.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that research is inherently untrustworthy, and not that all research should be distrusted.
- Not that histories of harm are irrelevant to judging a current claim.
- Not that a harmed community's judgment is unreliable. It is the anchor for acceptability (Fig 2 :36) and an instrument for correctness (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not that an institution can judge its own safety (Fig 10 :93).
- Not a single lever that solves the union.
- Not a specific policy. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment or an outcome.

## 5. What would settle it

A comparison at the ground, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. **Slot test:** does per-slot tracking locate a harmful protocol that a global trust verdict misses, on a set of cases where the answer is already known independently?
2. **Independence test:** does added independent checking at a corroded joint change outcomes at equal cost?
3. **Union test:** after a single lever fires, does a specific compound remain that needs a second, different lever?
4. **Cascade test:** does a second- and third-order habitat enumeration find a harm that a direct-contact scope misses?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19-22, Fig 5 :53, Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 2 :42. Four short passages.
2. Choose one harm case you know well. Name the term it refuses, and name the slot it concerns.
3. Say whether a single lever cleared all four conditions in that case, or whether a compound remained.
4. Say whether the independence failure was at the funding, consent, publication or downstream joint, and whether the response was graded.
5. Name one instrument that would distinguish a correct refusal from a global verdict on that case, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
