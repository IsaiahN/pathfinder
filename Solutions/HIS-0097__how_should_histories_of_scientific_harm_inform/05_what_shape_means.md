## 1. A shape is a constraint that can be stated before the name is available

The canonical limit is in `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, then stops at the limit of what instruments can distinguish and records the lineage. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The essay adds that convergence is a bootstrapping process: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*

For this question the shape is the set of properties a policy must have: it must keep the residual indexed by slot, carry provenance, buy independence where independence was corrupted, declare stakes, unbundle the union, and refuse a specific term rather than the library. It is not a specific policy. "Mandatory preregistration" is a name; "a per-slot, provenance-bearing, independence-buying rule" is a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained: proximity, effect terms and the bundle test

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. The essay's stopping condition is that *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and the paradox is that by then *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*
- *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* The chosen axes are visible, which makes them revisable, but visibility is not neutrality.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is the reason the outreach email leads with a checkable effect and withholds the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on a failed search: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the essay's own version of Fig 9 :54 and it governs HIS-0097 directly. The question as asked is a bundle.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles a minted term.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* In this question, whether a given policy reduced harm is a ground question and requires an outcome, not an argument.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The historical record of Tuskegee, the Belmont Report, and Asilomar are imports from the frame of research ethics and science governance into the decision frame. Each must carry its provenance. None moves the wall by declaration.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* A harm history tells you a reading was green and someone was hurt; it does not by itself tell you the source of every future green reading.

## 4. Why "all research is inherently untrustworthy" is the specific forbidden error

Three separate essays converge on this, and none of them is a figure line, so this is corroboration, not the verdict.

- *How a Society of Agents Would Work* restates Fig 1: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A blanket verdict is the aggregate. It loses the locating signal.
- *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A blanket refusal gives a policy a budget to be tried only if the ground can pay for it; refusing everything pays nothing.
- *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* "Research caused harm" is a projection; the source could be a funding relation, a consent failure, a publication bias, or a cascade the first-order scope missed. Naming "research itself" as the source from the projection is the metamerism error.

## 5. Correction to my own §3 and §4 (made before the conclusion)

I initially wrote that "the framework returns a shape, not a name" forbids naming any specific entity in a slot. That overstates the rule. The canonical phrase governs a *missing* thing, one not held in the library. In this question the relevant entities are not missing primitives: informed consent, independent review and conflict of interest are already in the decision frame's vocabulary, and the historical sources name them directly.

So naming "informed consent" or "independent review" is **not** forbidden. What is forbidden is (a) treating that name as derived from the figures when it was imported from the ethics frame, and (b) claiming the import settles a slot at the ground without a source gate. The correction narrows the rule; it does not erase the metamerism point in §4, which still holds because the projection does not determine which of several mechanisms produced the reading. Both statements are kept visible so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* A policy that cites famous cases or famous critics to settle the question is using prestige as proof. The legitimate ask is a check against the ground: what would count as a failure of the proposed rule. Fig 2 :42 puts the resource rule plainly: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**A refusal needs a name for the term it refuses.** Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. A vocabulary with negation can say "this protocol was not acceptable"; a vocabulary without it can only add more cases to a pile. The harm history is useful exactly because it carries a refusal, and the refusal must land on a term. This is why the answer refuses specific claims and not research as a category.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The essay also says *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim, cannot settle it, and every substantive statement in this solution is labelled `general` or `specified`, never `grounded`.
