# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have decided what you would count as a failure of the shape claim. Two-stage contact collapses on first contact if the first stage carries theory instead of output.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how planets form, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "How do planets form from disks of gas and dust around young stars" is not one question with one mechanism as its answer. It is a union of overlapping conditions. Terrestrial cores, giant cores, direct gravitational collapse, pebble accretion, and migration overlap, so a single lever that clears one region leaves the intersections standing. The figures I am working with say that when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, the bundle must be unbundled before the search is run.
>
> What the figures do decide is the shape any answer must have. A list of ingredients cannot name a route, so the answer has to be a sequence of typed transitions. The residual is per object and per region, not a smooth disk average, so the live signal is a local concentration. Each settled body becomes the operand at the next scale, so growth is recursive composition, not one big step. And the thing that settles which transition happened is a discriminating observation, not agreement among models.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures select core accretion, disk instability, or pebble accretion as the mechanism. They do not. A figure line that named one would refute my own claim. The identity of the mechanism is an open ground question, recorded as a gap, and I am offering no verdict on it. I also am not claiming that disk gaps prove planets. In at least one well-studied system, planets and snowlines can produce the same gap, which is exactly why the source stays open until an instrument discriminates it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a figure line that selects one formation mechanism as the unique route;
> 2. a route given as an unordered ingredient list with no typed bonds;
> 3. a uniform disk observed to produce a bound planet with no local concentration at any scale;
> 4. a result showing disk gaps never trace forming planets anywhere.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Planet Formation Mechanisms, Chris Ormel, arXiv:2410.14430v2, 2024: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14430
> - Giant Planet Formation and Migration, Paardekooper and Johansen, Space Science Reviews 214, 38, 2018: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11214-018-0472-y
> - A tale of planet formation: from dust to planets, Liu and Ji, RAA 20, 164, 2020: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1674-4527/20/10/164/meta
> - The Origin of Gaps in Protoplanetary Disks, AAS Nova, 2018: https://aasnova.org/2018/11/16/the-origin-of-gaps-in-protoplanetary-disks
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an unwritten refuter makes the output unfalsifiable.
>
> Thank you for reading this far. A correction is a useful outcome.
>
> [name]

**Contact discipline:** one recipient at a time. Public institutional or opt-in channels only. No personal emails harvested from private posts, no purchased lists, no automation, and an opt-out line in every message. A reply is a conversation, never corroboration.
