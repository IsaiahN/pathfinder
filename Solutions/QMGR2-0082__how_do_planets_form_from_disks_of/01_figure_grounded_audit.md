## 0. The residual in effect terms

We hold two readings and one gap.

- Reading A, the starting condition: a flattened, rotating disk of gas and dust around a young star, observed directly in systems such as HL Tauri (ALMA 2014 and 2016, opened this session).
- Reading B, the product: planets, with masses, compositions, and orbits, observed in the Solar System and in exoplanet populations (Liu and Ji 2020, Ormel 2024, opened this session).
- The residual R: the route from A to B is not read off either end. The disk is not the planet, and a catalogue of planet properties is not a route. What is owed is a sequence of compositions, each of which produces a new operand that the next composition can use, and an anchor that settles which route actually ran.

Stated as an effect: **the question asks for a route, not a state.** The framework has a specific name for that failure: Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): a finite ordered list of transitions between characteristic bodies (grain, pebble, planetesimal, protoplanet, planet), each transition licensed by a typed relation, with an instrument that could discriminate among competing transitions. This is the sought-for shape, written down so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

The question is **not** "which named mechanism wins." That is a world-frame binding, and no figure line supplies it. Section 7 records it as `gap`.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** many. The residual is indexed per object and per region, not over a smooth global average. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A disk averaged over all particles has no seed of a planet; the live signal is a local concentration.
- **Symmetry:** the starting disk is close to axisymmetric, but planet formation is the breaking of that symmetry into rings, gaps, clumps, and finally a small number of dominant bodies. The bonds that describe the route are ordered (sequence, production), not symmetric conjunctions only. Fig 12 :111 to :112.
- **Scale:** enormous and recursive. Ormel (2024) reports *"40 magnitudes in mass"*; Liu and Ji (2020) report *"more than 17 orders of magnitude"* in size. The route crosses from micron to planetary, so a settled product at one scale becomes the operand at the next (Fig 13 :103 to :104). Scale is not a nuisance parameter here; it is the structure of the question.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor.

- **Domain 1, the disk as habitat.** Actors: gas, dust grains, ice, pebbles, planetesimals, protoplanets, the star. Currency: mass and angular momentum, moved by gravity, drag, collisions, and migration. Anchor: disk images and spectra (ALMA, SPHERE), meteorites, exoplanet demographics. The habitat is enumerated from contact, not composed: Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **Domain 2, the route as composition.** Actors: characteristic bodies at each scale plus the typed relations between them. Currency: newly produced bodies, each of which becomes an operand. Anchor: Fig 12's bond definitions and the settlement of each first-order product. This is the framework-internal domain and is decidable from the figures as metalaws.
- **Domain 3, the ground and the instrument.** Actors: telescopes, laboratory collision experiments, simulations, meteorite chronology. Currency: readings that discriminate among candidate transitions. Anchor: whatever settles the question and does not move when a frame pushes on it. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: expert readers and public venues. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero. Recorded, not hidden.

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is the first decisive census result. "How do planets form" is **not one well-formed question with one mechanism as its answer.** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

The world frame agrees, as secondary corroboration: Ormel (2024) names two paradigms, disk instability and core accretion, and notes that *"both scenarios could be operating"*; Paardekooper and Johansen (2018) write *"There are two competing scenarios of how giant planets form."* These are overlapping conditions, not a list. A single lever that clears terrestrial-planet formation need not clear giant-planet formation, and the intersection (a giant core formed by accretion that then migrates) is a compound with its own loop. The residual is a union compound. It must be unbundled before a mechanism search is run.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

1. **Perception:** resolve structure in a disk at all (ALMA long baseline, achieved 2014).
2. **Vocabulary:** have terms for what a disk affords (pebble, planetesimal, pressure bump, snowline, isolation mass). This is the middle link, the one with no obvious instrument, and Fig 3 names it as the usual break point.
3. **Objective:** say what counts as progress, that is, which mass and size thresholds were crossed.
4. **Planning:** order the transitions into a route.
5. **Learn and carry:** each settled body becomes an operand at the next scale.

Effort spent on step 4 before steps 2 and 3 are settled arrives at a step that cannot use it. This is why the threshold definitions (what counts as a pebble versus a planetesimal) carry more weight than they appear to.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 : The residual is a route, not a state (decisive, framework-internal)

Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

The six-term substrate and the operators table let the framework state the disk and state the planet. Without typed bonds they cannot state the transition. The answer the question asks for is therefore necessarily a composition, not an inventory. Fig 12 :112 also types the composition: the ordered stages are `→` sequence; each new body that did not exist before is `⇒` production; the thresholds that decide whether growth continues (isolation mass, critical core mass, Toomre Q) are `⋛` comparisons. A term naming only ingredients cannot carry any of these.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line in which an unordered list of ingredients constitutes a route, or show that sequence, production, and comparison are all symmetric bonds. Fails if either holds.

### F2 : The question is a union, so no single mechanism is the answer (decisive)

Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

The two world paradigms are overlapping conditions. Core accretion is bottom-up growth ending in gas accretion; disk instability is direct gravitational collapse; pebble accretion and migration cut across both. Fig 9 says a single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Any answer of the form "planets form by mechanism X" has already committed the error the figure names.

**Refuter:** a verbatim figure line that selects one mechanism as the unique route, or a ground result showing that all planet populations lie in one mechanism's domain with no overlap. Neither is in the figure set.

### F3 : The disk must be enumerated, not composed (framework-internal)

Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

The disk actors are read off the world: gas, solids, ice lines, turbulence, pressure bumps, the star, the surrounding cluster. They are not derived from the six-term substrate. Contact cascades: a pressure bump affects pebble concentration, which affects planetesimal formation, which affects core mass, which affects gas accretion, which opens a gap, which changes the disk. Scoping to first-order contact would miss the loop, exactly as Fig 11 warns.

**Refuter:** derive the full disk habitat from the six primitives alone with no import, or show that a second-order effect in the disk cannot change a first-order formation outcome.

### F4 : Growth is recursive cross-scale composition (triangulated across two figures)

Fig 13 :103 *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
Fig 13 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

A pebble that survives is not a bigger grain in the same sense; it is a new operand on which concentration acts. A planetesimal is a settled pebble clump and becomes the operand for accretion. A protoplanet is a settled planetesimal product and becomes the operand for gas accretion. No new primitive is minted at each stage; a new level is composed over a settled lower one. Fig 4's membrane rule governs what crosses: a method (how to grow, how to concentrate) crosses up; a recording of one success does not.

**Triangulation status:** triangulated. Fig 13 governs the recursion and the level-relativity of the irreducible; Fig 4 governs the direction of the transform between sizes and the lossy round trip. Low overlap, different quantities. The world corroborates (Liu and Ji 2020 identify four characteristic size classes), but the verdict does not depend on it.

**Refuter:** show a growth stage that requires a new primitive not available at the previous level, or show that a settled body cannot serve as an operand for the next composition.

### F5 : The live signal is per slot, not a smooth global average (single-source in the figures, world-corroborated)

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A globally smooth disk has no planet in it. Growth requires local excess: a pressure maximum, a vortex, a snowline pileup, a streaming-instability filament. The world frame is unusually direct here. Ormel (2024) describes the meter-size problem as rapid drift in *"smooth disks"*; Ormel's chapter and Paardekooper and Johansen (2018) both give the streaming instability a threshold local solids-to-gas ratio for clumping. This is Fig 1 read against the ground, not a figure line imported from the world.

**Triangulation status:** single-source for the figure claim (Fig 1 only). World sources corroborate strongly; because they share a literature pool with each other, they count as one witness for the physics, not two.

**Refuter:** a derivation in which a spatially uniform disk spontaneously produces a gravitationally bound body without any local concentration, measured at the ground.

### F6 : The anchor is observation, not agreement among models (framework-internal and outreach-facing)

Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

For this question the anchor is a discriminating reading: a gap in gas and dust at the same radius, a kinematic deviation, a directly imaged embedded planet, a meteorite age. It is not the number of reviews that assert core accretion, and not the number of readers who nod at a diagram. Paardekooper and Johansen (2018) are candid that *"the relative roles of the streaming instability and the vertical shear and baroclinic instabilities in concentrating pebbles is still poorly understood"*; a count of agreeing papers would not settle it.

**Refuter:** show that a widely agreed theoretical preference changes which disk observation counts as a planet signature, so that the anchor moves when a frame pushes on it.

### F7 : A probe is already returning something; the slot stays open until an instrument discriminates (triangulated)

Fig 6 :34 *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*
Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

The inward/outward question is settled cheaply. ALMA is already returning resolved rings and gaps (2014) and gas gaps aligned with dust gaps (2016). There is an edge to extend from. But the reading does not determine the source: AAS Nova (2018) reports that van der Marel and collaborators found that *"either planets or snowlines can cause the gaps and rings in the disk around HD 163296."* That is the framework's metamerism case in astronomical clothing. What is owed is a discriminator (gas density response, kinematics), not a name declared from the projection. The framework returns a shape: something opened the gap and reduced local material; which thing is a ground question.

**Triangulation status:** triangulated. Fig 6 supplies the proto-instrument rule; Fig 9 supplies the shape-not-a-name limit. Different quantities, low overlap.

**Refuter:** a reading of a disk gap that uniquely identifies its cause without a second, independent observable, shown to survive a blind reanalysis.

### F8 : The middle link is the threshold vocabulary (framework-internal)

Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :24 *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."*

Most effort in planet formation goes into detailed growth rates (step 4 planning) because those have instruments. The link that has no obvious instrument is "what would count as progress here": the boundary between grain, pebble, planetesimal, protoplanet, and planet, and the thresholds that switch mechanisms (sticking velocity, Stokes number of order one, Toomre Q near one, pebble isolation mass, critical core mass). Fig 3 :27 of the extract states the rule plainly: a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at, not a link that works. Liu and Ji (2020) classify by exactly these characteristic sizes, which shows the vocabulary exists; the figure warns that having it is not the same as having settled what it means at the ground.

**Refuter:** show that all threshold definitions in the field are operationally settled by a single instrument with no residual, or that planning detail alone can rescue an unmeasured objective link.

### F9 : A multi-stage account pays the bargain (framework-internal)

Fig 5 :43 *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."*

A one-step account ("dust becomes planets") is cheap to state and leaves nearly everything unexplained, so it fails the bargain. A four-stage account costs more to state but removes the meter-size drift problem, the bouncing barrier, the planetesimal birth-size problem, and the gas-accretion timing problem. It pays. The three guards (support, reachability, novelty) are a product: there is positive mass in the residual (the observed disk-to-planet gap); the stages compose from the held operators; and each stage is new relative to the previous one.

**Refuter:** a single-stage account with lower total description length plus remaining residual than the staged route, checked against the same observations.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, as required by the seat discipline: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A verbatim figure line selects one formation mechanism (core accretion, disk instability, or pebble accretion) as the unique route. That would refute the `gap` verdict in §7.
2. A figure re-census admits a route as an unordered ingredient list with no typed bond, or shows sequence/production/comparison are symmetric. F1 fails.
3. A uniform, fully averaged disk is observed to produce a bound planet with no local concentration at any scale. F5 fails.
4. A measurement shows disk gaps never trace forming planets in any system, so F7's instrument claim has no edge to extend from.
5. One of the six substrate terms is derived from the other five, breaking the recursion that F4 rests on.

If any fires, the derivation is withdrawn or rewritten from the census. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
