# Routes, bonds and isomers: are core accretion and disk instability the same molecule?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim says a route is owed and a mechanism name is not. A follow-up question is unavoidable: if two named paradigms both compose over the same held bodies, do they stand to each other as **isomers** under Fig 12, or merely as members of one functional group? And what bond order does the route actually have? This file derives the supporting structure so that `07` and `08` can state a checkable claim instead of a vibe.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What order is the route?

Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
Operators table: `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; `⇒` is *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*.

The four-stage route in Liu and Ji (2020) and Ormel (2024) parses as:

```
grain ⇒ pebble ⇒ planetesimal ⇒ protoplanet ⇒ planet(+ envelope)
```

Each arrow is `⇒` production, not merely `→` sequence. A planetesimal did not exist as a bound 100 km body before the pebble clump collapsed; a protoplanet did not exist before runaway growth; a gas giant did not exist before envelope collapse. The distinction is not cosmetic: Operators table on `⇒` asks *"is there a B before A fires? No."* If a submitted route can be rewritten with only `→`, it has smuggled pre-existing bodies and has not accounted for production.

The checkable sub-claim: **the transitions are productive, not merely sequential.** A falsifier written now: exhibit a stage in the accepted route where the product is already present in the same form before the transition fires, so the bond degrades to `→`.

The thresholds that gate those transitions are `⋛` comparisons. Ormel (2024) gives the Toomre criterion, the sticking threshold, the Roche density, the isolation mass, and the critical core mass. Fig 12 :112 says a goal is a comparison; these quantities are the goals of each stage. They are filters for the budget of a simulation, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

### Q2 : Are core accretion and disk instability isomers?

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two readings, kept separate.

- **As the world frame usually writes them**, core accretion holds `{grain, pebble, planetesimal, protoplanet, gas, time}` while disk instability holds `{disk gas, self-gravity, time}`. They do not share the same atom set. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two different substances with different ingredients. Calling them "competing paradigms" is a claim about outcomes, not about composition.
- **Over the full shared atom set** `{disk gas, solids, self-gravity, time, angular momentum}`, both can be written as productions: CA is `solids ⇒ core ⇒ envelope`; DI is `gas ⇒ clump ⇒ planet`. Same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That **is** an isomer claim under Fig 12 :117. It is the stronger and more interesting version, and it is checkable: it requires showing that neither route needs an atom the other lacks.

The honest report is that the weaker reading is false (atom sets differ as currently written) and the stronger reading is a real but unmeasured structural claim. The world frame itself declines to choose: Ormel (2024) notes that *"both scenarios could be operating"*, and Paardekooper and Johansen (2018) call them *"two competing scenarios"* while keeping both on the table.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either paradigm can be shown to require an atom the other does not hold (for example, CA with no solids, or DI with no self-gravitating gas), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. This falsifier is the one carried into `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

### Q3 : What is the functional group they share?

Fig 13 :103 *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*

Both paradigms are instances of `matter → structure → matter` at more than one scale: gas plus a perturbation forms a clump; the clump settles and becomes an operand for accretion or contraction. The shared substructure is not a mechanism name. It is the functional group that fixes what kind of thing an answer must be: a recursive production chain in which settled products become operands. The qualifier, which member of the group, is what the ground has not settled for any particular disk.

### Q4 : What would move the isomer claim from `specified` to `grounded`?

A ground reading that forces one atom set and one bond arrangement for a labelled system: a planet whose formation history is fixed by meteorite chronology plus disk kinematics, such that the competing arrangement cannot reproduce the observations. Until then the claim is `specified`. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* applies to the isomer reading too: the figures say same atoms plus different bond gives different substance, not which of the two substances a given system produced.

## CONCLUSION

The route is a chain of **productive** bonds (`⇒`), not a mere sequence, with threshold comparisons (`⋛`) gating each stage. Core accretion and disk instability are not isomers as the world frame currently writes them, because their atom sets differ; over the full shared atom set they are a genuine isomer claim with a stated falsifier, still unmeasured. What they share is a functional group: recursive cross-kind production in which a settled product becomes the next operand. This rescues the structural content of both paradigms without letting the framework name a winner.
