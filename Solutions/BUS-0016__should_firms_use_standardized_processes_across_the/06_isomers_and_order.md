# Process variants as isomers, and the order of settlement before replication

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The process inventory asserts that the same process is present in many teams. Two questions are owed: (1) when two teams have the same listed steps, are they running one term or two different substances, and (2) in what order must a process be settled, carried and re-fitted for the union to close?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this question: a process step, a check, a handoff, an interface, a local condition, an actor. Bonds held: the seven, of which `≡` identity, `→` sequence, `⇒` production and `⋛` comparison do most of the work here; negation `¬` is available for a refusal. No new organizational primitive is admitted. The anchor for the verdict layer is the local ground; the anchor for the convention layer is agreement itself (Fig 2 :39-43).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are two teams with the same step list running the same process?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :95: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*.

A standard written as a flat list of steps records `→` by default, whether or not `→` is the right bond. Suppose the central document says "inspect, then assemble, then sign off". One team runs it as a sequence: sign-off waits for assembly. Another runs the inspect step as `⋛` (a threshold comparison that gates assembly) and the sign-off as `⇒` (a production of a new record that did not exist before). The step list is identical; the molecules are not.

**Verdict:** under Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*, the two teams are running **isomers** if they share the full atom set and differ only in bonding. The organization's notation cannot see this, which is why local variation is routinely mislabelled as deviation. Mode `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for the firm-level prediction.

**Falsifier:** find two teams whose step lists and bonds are demonstrably identical and whose ground outcomes are also identical, with no arrangement difference anywhere. If that holds generally, the isomer reading has no object and F7 in `01` falls back to a notation observation.

### Q2: What about the weaker reading, a shared functional group?

- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

If teams do not share the full atom set but share only the pattern "check, transform, record", they are members of one functional group, not isomers. This is the weaker claim and the one that survives without a shared inventory. It still supports the split rule: the functional group is the convention layer (standardizable), the qualifier is the local layer (adaptable).

**Falsifier:** a process family in which no stable substructure can be identified across teams. Then the convention layer has no object and the split rule collapses.

### Q3: What is the order of settlement, carrying and re-fitting?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"* (the line continues past "no bottom"; the quotation is a truncation, not a different line).
- Fig 5 :32: *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*.
- Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

The order is: **settle at one slot against its ground → abstract the reusable method → carry down as a head start → re-range against the new slot's ground → record the round-trip gap → repeat.** Carrying before settling sends an unsettled molecule to serve as an atom; nothing should compose over it yet. Carrying a recording instead of a method sends a description of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).

**Falsifier:** a standard that produces new, ground-verified results in a new slot when it was never settled in the slot where it was written, and whose round-trip gap measures zero. That breaks the settlement order and Fig 12 :104.

### Q4: Catalyst, cycle and the decay of a standard

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

A standard that regenerates across uses is a catalyst; one that is spent is not, and the process document records neither state. A process with a feedback loop can close; a flat chain of steps cannot close by construction. This is why "document the standard" and "keep it alive" are different jobs. The first records ingredients; the second maintains a channel to the ground (Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).

**Falsifier:** a standard whose measured contribution stays constant across dozens of uses and a documented change of residual, with no regeneration work of any kind. Under Fig 12 :119 that should not happen.

### Q5: The union is not a partition

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The convention layer and the verdict layer overlap in the same document. An interface is a convention between two teams and also a claim about what data must be exchanged for the local process to work. A single lever that fixes interfaces everywhere clears the convention condition and can leave the verdict condition standing, or make it worse. The union must be unbundled before a lever is chosen.

**Falsifier:** one intervention that clears both conditions in every observed failure type with no different residue left over. That would make this a partition and remove the need for the split rule.

## CONCLUSION

Two teams with the same step list are one term only if the bond is also the same; otherwise they are isomers, and the flat process document cannot tell you which. The weaker, always-available claim is a shared functional group. A process must be settled against a ground before it is carried, and what crosses must be a method or head start, not a recording. A standard is a catalyst only while it regenerates, and its valence changes with the residual. These three results all support the same shape: standardize the convention and method layer, localize the ground-settled layer, and keep the two verdicts separate.
