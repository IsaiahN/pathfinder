# Outreach draft: check one claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim and the refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The stage-1 output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about standardization and local adaptation, with the test that would break it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a firm-wide either/or between standardized processes and local adaptation is the wrong object of decision. The question is a union of two conditions that overlap in the same process document. The first is a shared meaning, where agreement is the product: a common name for a defect, an interface between two teams, a safety rule, a definition of done. The second is a claim about what works with this customer, on this machine, under this regulator, where agreement tells you nothing because the agreeing teams share the same manual. The proposed rule is to standardize the first layer and localize the second. The framework's own line is that correlation has two valences: it is *"disqualifying for a verdict"* and *"efficient for a convention"*.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a list. It does not say which of your processes belong in which column. That remains open until a local measurement settles it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one best way exists; that every process should be fixed centrally; that every local variation is wise; that a particular adoption percentage is right; or that this is settled by evidence. No measurement was taken here. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute it:** write these down before you look at anything else.
> 1. A process element for which agreement is both the product and a claim about the world, with one instrument that settles both.
> 2. A verbatim replay of a single success that keeps producing new, ground-verified results in every new team, with the round-trip gap measured as zero.
> 3. A process that delivers identical measured outcomes after being moved to a site where none of the original conditions were reproduced and no new actor was introduced.
> 4. One intervention that restores coordination and local fit in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> - The figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The canonical usage rule (why the figures outrank the summary): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The architecture essay that states the simulation limit and the prestige rule: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a process you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the source essay warns that an output *"cannot validate itself"*. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
