# Expert verification packet: the Tether on standardization vs local adaptation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an operations manager, organization scholar or quality professional can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the claim about firms, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No per-slot measurement is claimed. This is a split rule and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A firm-wide either/or between standardized processes and local adaptation is the wrong object of decision. The situation is a union of two conditions in the same process document: a shared-meaning condition, where agreement is the product, and a local-ground condition, where uniformity is disqualifying because it never ranges against the ground. The claim is to standardize the first layer and localize the second. What crosses between teams should be a method or head start, never a recording of one success, and the round-trip gap should be measured at each new site. The framework does not name which specific processes belong in which column; that needs a local measurement. It offers a shape with falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** uniformity has two valences. It is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*; :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Also Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a process element for which agreement is simultaneously the product and a claim about the world, with one instrument that settles both; or show a shared convention that decides outcomes while still functioning only as a convention. Either breaks the split criterion.

### C2: operations-facing; checkable against a real process family

- **Statement:** a process document records the ingredient list, not the bond. Two teams with identical steps can therefore run different processes. The local variants are isomers, not defects, when they share atoms and differ in bonding.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*; :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*; :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :95: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- **What to check:** take one process with two teams that both report "following the standard". Write down the actual steps and then the binding relations (what gates what, what produces an artifact, what is a threshold, what is removed). Are the bonds the same? If not, the teams are not running the same process even though the audit says they are.
- **Refuter:** a process family in which two teams have identical steps and identical bonds and identical ground outcomes across several slots, with no arrangement difference to account for any claimed variation.

### C3: framework-internal derivation, with a measurement hook

- **Statement:** a process must be settled against a ground before it is carried; what crosses is a method or head start, never a recording; the round-trip gap `R_T` must be measured. Capability is team-and-habitat, so carrying the document to a new site substitutes a habitat with invisible losses.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*; :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."*; :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*; :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*.
- **Refuter:** a verbatim replay of one success that produces new, ground-verified results in every new team, with `R_T` measured as zero; or a process that delivers identical outcomes after a habitat substitution where none of the original conditions were reproduced.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that one best way exists, and not that a named production system should be copied everywhere. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- Not that every local variation is improvement. Fig 7 :36 requires the next room to match the residual the last one surfaced.
- Not a standardization percentage, a process list or an organizational chart. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a finding of the management literature. The replication studies share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40); the engineering modularity source is a separate, lower-overlap witness but it is secondary only.
- Not `grounded`. No defect, customer, safety or survival measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Layer test:** for each process element, name the failure that would be counted. If it is "coordination breaks" the element is a convention; if it is "the local ground does not pay" it is a verdict. An instrument that measures both separately would settle the assignment.
2. **Replay test:** carry a standard to a new slot and measure `R_T`. A zero gap on an unvisited slot means it was a recording.
3. **Isomer test:** record the bonds as well as the steps in two teams with the same ingredient list; compare ground outcomes.
4. **Union test:** try one lever that restores both conditions; record any categorically different residue (Fig 9 :57).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :39-43 and Fig 12 :94, :95, :117. Four short passages.
2. Pick one process that is standardized across teams in your organization. List its steps, then list the binding relations. Are two teams running the same molecule?
3. Name one process element where agreement is the product and one where only the local ground can settle correctness. Say what instrument would tell you if you got the assignment wrong.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
