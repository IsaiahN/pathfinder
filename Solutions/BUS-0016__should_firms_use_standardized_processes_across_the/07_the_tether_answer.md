# The Tether's answer on standardization vs local adaptation

**Mode: `specified` on the answer about firms, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A process was settled in one slot and is now being considered for another. The firm must decide what may be repeated and what must be re-settled. The two candidate policies, standardize everything or let every team adapt, are not two answers to one question. The situation is a **union compound**: a shared meaning condition and a local-ground condition overlap in the same process document (Fig 9 :55, :57). It has its own anchor for each condition and needs its own instrument for each.

## 2. The shape

1. **Uniformity has two valences, set by what the agreement is about.** Fig 2 :39-43: correlation is *"disqualifying for a verdict"* and *"efficient for a convention"*, and *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. A defect name, an interface, a safety rule or a definition of done is a convention; agreement there is the product. A claim about what works with this customer, on this machine, under this regulator is a verdict; agreement there is worthless because the agreeing teams share the same manual.
2. **Therefore standardize the convention and method layer; localize the ground-settled layer.** This is the whole answer. It is a split rule with an anchor test, not a percentage and not a process list. A convention that is allowed to decide the outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
3. **What crosses between teams must be a method or a head start, never a recording.** Fig 4 :22-25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A recording *"produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."* Measure the round-trip gap `R_T` (Fig 4 :30) rather than assuming the standard transferred.
4. **Capability is team-and-habitat, not the process document.** Fig 11 :39-40: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Carrying a process to a new site substitutes a habitat; what was lost and what was introduced are invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :66-69).
5. **Same steps, different bond, different process.** Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Two teams with the same step list can be isomers (Fig 12 :117), and a flat standard records the ingredient list but not the bond. Treat a local variant as a re-bonding until shown otherwise.
6. **The break is usually at vocabulary and objective, not planning.** Fig 3 :24, :27, :29. The highest-value question is not "are teams compliant?" but "at which link does this team's chain stop, and was that measured?"
7. **Renewal needs a differing closure; local teams are the channel.** Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :32. A centre that only optimizes its template inside its own library never adds an atom. Fig 7 :36: the next room must differ *and* match the residual the last one surfaced.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that one best way exists, and not that "best practice" should be copied everywhere. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- Not that all local variation is good. Random difference is not selection; it must match the surfaced residual (Fig 7 :36).
- Not a percentage of processes to standardize, not a named production system, not an org chart. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the standardization literature settles it. Netland, Ringov, Winter, Szulanski, March and their cohorts share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No defect count, customer outcome, safety finding or survival test was measured here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

An instrument that discriminates the two layers in a named slot, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (exemplar record): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Layer test:** for each process element, ask what would count as a failure. If the answer is "coordination breaks" it is a convention; if the answer is "the local ground does not pay" it is a verdict. An instrument that measures both separately would settle the assignment.
- **Replay test:** carry a standard to a new slot and measure `R_T`. A zero gap on a slot it never visited means the standard was a recording, not a method (Fig 4 :25, :30).
- **Isomer test:** record the bonds, not just the steps, in two teams with the same ingredient list. If different bonds give different ground outcomes, the local variation is a different substance (Fig 12 :117).
- **Standing falsifiers:** a single lever that restores both conditions with no different residue (Fig 9 :57); a verbatim replay that keeps producing new results at zero gap (Fig 4 :25); identical outcomes after a habitat substitution with no conditions reproduced (Fig 11 :66-69); a team failing at planning with all upper links measured as working (Fig 3 :24).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Which exact process elements belong in which column is a `gap`. The instrument that would name them is a local ground measurement, not a framework argument. The answer stops where instruments can no longer distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *Indiscernibility is not identity.* From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"You may all agree and be wrong... You need ground truth to define where you stand."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say "standardize" or "let teams adapt". It says the question is a union of two conditions that share the same process document. Fig 2 gives the split: agreement is *"disqualifying for a verdict"* but *"efficient for a convention"*. So standardize the layer where agreement is the product (names, interfaces, safety rules, definitions of done) and localize the layer that only the local customer, machine or regulator can settle. Carry methods and head starts across the organization, never recordings of a single success, and measure the round-trip gap at each new site. A capability lives in the team-and-habitat, so a copied document is a substituted habitat with invisible losses. Two teams with the same step list may be running different substances because the bond is not recorded. The framework refuses to name which of your processes belong in which column; that needs a local measurement. What it offers is the split rule, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Mode `specified`, not `grounded`.
