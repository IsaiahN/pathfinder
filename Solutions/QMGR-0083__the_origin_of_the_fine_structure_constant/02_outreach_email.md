# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been checked by an outside reader. The first contact must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Mode of the claim: `specified`, not `grounded`.** The number is a measurement, not a derivation.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why alpha is a pure number, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does
> not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** The electromagnetic coupling alpha is approximately 1/137.036,
> a pure number with no units. Two things are owed about it, and they have different grounds. First,
> why this value, and is it derivable or only measurable? Second, is it truly constant in time and
> space, or does it drift? I keep those in separate loops because one instrument does not settle
> both.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A framework I work with holds six primitives in two
> kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule
> that composition runs across kinds and never within one. From that it follows that a dimensionless
> coupling is not a primitive. It is a quantity left by a composition across the two kinds, at the
> intersection of independently introduced scales. The framework returns that **shape** of an
> origin. It does not return 1/137.036. The number is supplied by a measurement or by an import from
> a frame whose closure differs, and I am not claiming to derive it here.
>
> On constancy: the framework distinguishes an anchor, which settles a question and does not move
> when frames think about it, from a channel, which decays. If alpha really drifted, it was a
> channel, not an anchor. What the current instruments show is stable at their resolution, not
> constant in principle. I record that as a candidate verdict, not a settled one.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derives the value; that a null variation result proves
> constancy; that agreement among physicists confirms anything. Ten clocks calibrated against the
> same standard are one witness unless their sensitivities to alpha differ.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. deriving alpha as a seventh member of the six-term set, or deriving 1/137.036 from those six
>    alone with no measured boundary condition;
> 2. a confirmed, systematics-free drift of alpha seen by two instruments with different
>    sensitivities, which would show alpha was a channel;
> 3. a proof that variation is impossible in principle, which would close the second loop;
> 4. a single instrument or derivation that settles both the value and the drift, which would break
>    the claim that these are two questions.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more
> useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the
> authority.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you
> know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you
> run it, and state the mode of what you get. The architecture's own write-up warns that outputs
> *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A
> reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
