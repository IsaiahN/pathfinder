## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Aggregation is a known loss, not a neutral summary.** The multilevel-model source quantifies the loss (80 to 90 percent of variance possibly wasted) and names the ecological fallacy. This is the closest world-frame match to Fig 1 :22 and Fig 10 :25-26. It is one witness, not a ground settlement.
2. **The instrument may itself be the problem.** The invariance editorial says respondents can attach different meanings to the same items across groups. That supports the per-slot and per-frame discipline, but it also shows why a method name cannot be imported without a source gate: the fix (approximate invariance, alignment, multigroup SEM) is a family, not a single answer.
3. **The invariance literature conflicts with itself.** Welzel and colleagues, as reported in the encyclopedia entry, argue that invariance tests penalise constructs with high between-group variance, while proponents reply that nomological validation assumes comparability in the first place. Per the framework rule, a conflict sends us back to the census, never to a flip of the verdict. Re-census in `01` §7 separates instrument comparability (D1) from slot resolution (D2). Both are live; the figures do not adjudicate the statistical dispute.
4. **Low-overlap corroboration from animal behaviour.** Reaction norms and repeatability measure an individual across an environmental gradient and decompose variance into among- and within-individual parts. This is the same shape in a field with no stake in cross-cultural psychology, which makes it the most independent of the sources read.
5. **The qualitative frame is not an anecdote.** Ethnography's own description is contextual and subject-point-of-view, which matches the habitat-enumeration rule rather than competing with the quantitative frames. It counts as a separate frame only if its evidence does not come from the same instruments.
