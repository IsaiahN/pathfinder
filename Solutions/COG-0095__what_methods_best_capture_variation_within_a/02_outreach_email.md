**Subject:** One claim about within-culture variation, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific methodological claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** cross-cultural studies that report one number per society treat the society as a single slot. The figures this work is built on index the residual per object slot, not per aggregate, and say that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. Applied here, that means within-society variation is not error to be averaged away. It is the residual that says where to look. The method shape that follows is a variance decomposition with persons nested in subgroups, within-society dispersion reported as a result, overlapping conditions unbundled rather than scaled, and laboratory, ethnographic, historical and population frames kept independent until each has ranged against the ground.
>
> **What the framework does not claim:** it does not name a single best statistical method. It returns a shape, not a name. Terms such as multilevel model, measurement invariance test or behavioural reaction norm are imports from the methods literature, provenanced as such. The figures do not select among them. If you think one of them fails the shape, that is the useful reply.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a complete per-slot decomposition that returns no live mass at the person or subgroup level for this question, so the within-society variation is demonstrably measurement error;
> 2. a partition of within-society conditions with no intersections, where one dimension predicts the residual with no residue;
> 3. a proposed method battery shown to draw on one evidence pool, so its agreement is a convention and not triangulation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Open-access measurement invariance editorial: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4516821/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
