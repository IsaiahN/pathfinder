## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's conclusions, in the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the failure of a society-level prediction against observations inside that society. Arity: many nested slots, so a vector, not a scalar. Symmetry: equal slot form, unequal valence, since "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries" (Fig 11 :49). Scale: two at minimum, person and society. A mean is a projection, not the residual itself.
- **D1 Instruments.** Actors: items, scales, respondents, constructs. Currency: whether the same construct is measured across groups. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument. Type: partition (within or between at a declared level; invariant or not).
- **D2 Population as habitat.** Actors: persons, subgroups, school, media, migration, institution. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: behaviour. Type: union, because conditions overlap inside one person.
- **D3 Frames.** Actors: lab, ethnography, history, population data. Currency: independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Type: union of frames with different evidence.
- **D4 Verification.** Actors: readers and venues. Currency: agreement. Anchor: the ground; in this simulation independence is zero.

Order by dependency: resolve per slot, acquire vocabulary for slot and condition, fix the objective as per-slot R reduced, then select instruments, then carry the method across scale. I reached this order from Fig 3 :20-21 and Fig 4 :22 rather than from the primary's §1, but the sequence matches.
