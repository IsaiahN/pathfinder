# Within versus between: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks which methods capture within-culture variation. Two supporting derivations are owed: (1) is "within versus between" a partition or an isomer-like rearrangement, and (2) in what order must the methods be applied, given the dependency chain (Fig 3) and the membrane between scales (Fig 4)?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is "within versus between" a partition?

- Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- A partition assigns each observation to one cell: person in subgroup in society. That is legitimate for the *slots* (D2 in `01` is nested).
- The *conditions* that generate the residual (language, schooling, migration, media, subgroup norm, ecology) are not nested; they overlap. Same observation can be in several conditions.
- ⇒ "Within versus between" is a partition of **slots**, but "variation within a culture" is a **union of conditions**. Treating the second as the first is the error Fig 9 names. A single grouping variable clears one condition and leaves the intersections standing.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a complete census of conditions that shows each observation lies in exactly one condition, with no overlap, and a single grouping variable that predicts R with no residue. If that holds, the union reading fails.

### Q2 : Are the methods isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Consider the family of methods that keep R per slot: multilevel decomposition, behavioural reaction norms, invariance testing, ethnographic contextual enumeration. They share the substructure `per-slot R + declared ground + low-overlap frame`. By Fig 12 :124 that is a **functional group**: the presence of that substructure fixes what kind of method it is. The qualifier (which estimator, which frame) says which member.
- They are **not** isomers in the strict figure sense, because they do not share the same atoms from Γ: the estimators come from different world frames with different primitives (variance components, environmental gradients, item parameters, field notes). The world sources confirm the different primitives (Wikipedia, *Multilevel model*, *Personality in animals*, *Measurement invariance*, all read 2026-11-22).
- The stronger isomer claim would require writing all of them over one shared atom set (slot, R, ground, frame, habitat, method) and differing only in arrangement. That is a checkable structural claim, not yet settled. It needs an instrument that can compare methods across those frames without importing one frame's primitives into another.

**Falsifier:** show that one method in the family can be written without an atom another requires (for example a method with no slot, or no ground), so the functional-group claim fails. If the shared-atom version is tested and fails, the isomer reading is withdrawn and only the functional group stands.

### Q3 : What order do the methods run in?

Dependency order from Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

1. **Perception:** can the instrument resolve within-society variation at all? Below this, no method matters.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the study have terms for slot, subgroup, condition, ground? Without these it cannot say what the situation affords. Fig 3 :25-26: the middle link, "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress", "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments".
3. **Objective:** what counts as progress? R reduced per slot, not a group mean made tidy.
4. **Planning:** select the estimators and frames that serve 1 to 3.
5. **Learn and carry:** carry the method up across scales, not the recording (Fig 4 :22).

Ordering matters because a method chosen at step 4 before step 2 is an import chosen before the residual is described. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

### Q4 : The scale crossing

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

The loop from person to society keeps the same shape: per-slot R, ground, frame. What changes is the cast (persons versus subgroups versus societies), the currency (response versus aggregate rate) and the anchor instrument (survey item versus behavioural measure). Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." A result carried from person scale to society scale without a round trip is a recording treated as a method.

Animal behaviour supplies the low-overlap analogue: behavioural reaction norms measure an individual "over an environmental gradient", giving both an average and a change across contexts (Wikipedia, *Personality in animals*, 2026). That is a method that keeps the loop constant while changing the scale of the gradient, which is the membrane rule in a different field.

## CONCLUSION

"Within versus between" is a partition of **slots** and a union of **conditions**. The methods that keep R per slot form a **functional group** sharing `per-slot R + ground + low-overlap frame`; the stronger isomer reading needs a shared atom set and is untested. Their order is fixed by dependency (Fig 3): resolve, name, set the objective, then choose, then carry the method across scale. The scale crossing is lossy and requires a measured round trip (Fig 4 :30). No method name is derived from the figures; each is a provenanced import.

## FALSIFIER (registered before this conclusion)

If a complete condition census shows a partition with no intersections, or a method in the family is shown to lack a slot or a ground, or a shared-atom comparison succeeds in deriving a unique method name from the figures alone, the corresponding claim above is withdrawn. If a round trip from person scale to society scale returns the same result with zero gap, the warning at Fig 4 :30 is spent effort for this case.
