**Question:** What methods best capture variation within a culture rather than treating each society as internally uniform?

## What is being decided, in effect terms

A study predicts an observation for a unit called "a culture" (one slot) and observes a distribution over persons and subgroups inside it. The choice is between (A) methods that report a society-level mean, which makes the within-society residual disappear into an average, and (B) methods that keep the residual indexed at the slot where it actually carries mass, with separate frames ranging against a ground. A picks a clean number and loses the signal that says where to look. B keeps the signal and pays for more instruments and more independence. The residual, not a theory of culture, decides which is owed.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the conclusion.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, from the framework's own rules:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
3. Asking for trust before checking spends the reader's independence. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
