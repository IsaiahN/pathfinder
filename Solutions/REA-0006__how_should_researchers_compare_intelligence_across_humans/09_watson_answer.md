## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about comparing intelligence across humans, animals and machines with very different abilities. Option A: treat intelligence as an intrinsic quantity and seek the test or currency that ranks the three kinds. Option B: treat the question as a union of conditions and replace the substrate question with the contact question, returning a per-slot protocol, a declared gap for the universal rank, and a pre-registered refuter. What each leads to: A sends a self-scoring scalar, spends the independence of the people checking it, and hides the channel failures that actually produce score differences; B sends a checkable shape, keeps verdicts per domain, and waits for a ground reading before naming anything.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a comparison score and what it claims to be about. Arity: many capacities per subject, indexed by slot, not a scalar. Symmetry: false; comparison is an ordered threshold relation, ⋛, "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :49), not a symmetric average. Scale: the individual agent, the habitat and channel, and the membrane between scales. A scalar rank is a symmetric collapse of an ordered, multi-slot residual.
- **Domain 1, the measurement frame.** Actors: test designer, test-taker, benchmark, human judge. Currency: score and skill. Anchor: none that does not update unless a ground is supplied from outside the test. Human judgment is legitimate when the question is constitutively about people and only an instrument for code. This is a channel failure domain, not a measurement domain.
- **Domain 2, the capability and contact frame.** Actors: agent, habitat, ground, seat. Currency: contact and settlement. Anchor: the ground, which does not update. This is where the answerable question lives.
- **Domain 3, the cross-scale comparison frame.** Actors: the kernel, the actors, the currency and the anchor at each scale. Currency: methods crossing up, head starts crossing down, residuals at each slot. Anchor: the ground at that scale, with a lossy transform between scales.
- **Partition or union: union.** The three conditions overlap; "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57). One lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Install the anchor and check the channel before scoring (Fig 2 :45; Fig 10 :24). Refuse the scalar before minting a method (Formula step 2). Then build an instrument from a worse one already returning something (Fig 6 :32).

Strain test applied: Fig 4 :20 (currency changes with size) strains against the desire for one comparison currency. Re-census assigns currency to a per-domain parameter and kernel shape to the invariant, so the figures do not contradict one another. Two live type strains remain and are named in the verdict.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not one question with a single answer. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not. Capability is a property of agent and habitat together, so researchers should compare per-slot capability against a non-updating ground in a reproduced habitat, using low-overlap instruments and an explicitly named kernel, and should report a universal currency or scalar rank as a shape-not-a-name `gap` rather than inventing one. A shared benchmark is a convention, not a verdict; a harness solve proves wiring, not capability. Over a fixed atom set the three kinds may be isomers with a stated falsifier, otherwise members of a functional group. The result is `specified` on the separator and the protocol, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal rank, ungrounded and a candidate. Two type strains are named and kept (single scalar versus two currencies; human judgment as anchor versus instrument). Verdicts per domain are separate and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a scalar and a shared test instead of a characterised residual and low-overlap instruments. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and its three failures.
- **undoable:** yes. The protocol is a method, not a primitive. A refutation narrows or withdraws it without withdrawing the habitat figure; no term has to be deleted from the record. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one, and the lineage records the change.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measure surviving all three refuters, with a non-updating anchor and held-out ground readings at every slot, would move the contact claim toward `grounded`. A seventh primitive or a cross-kind currency derived rather than declared would break Fig 13 :41. An agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim at Fig 11 :71-72. Two instruments that share evidence and still return the same per-slot verdict would break the independence claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
