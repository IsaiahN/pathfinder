## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

We have readings from three kinds of subject, humans, animals and machines, that are very different in ability, in sensory and motor equipment, in priors and in habitat. We want a comparison that tells us where to look next and that would be wrong if the world were different. What we currently get is a rank or a score. The residual is the gap between that score and what it claims to be about.

Four things are mixed into the score, and the figures separate them:

1. **The agent's contact with a ground**, which is the only thing the question could legitimately be about (Fig 11 :39).
2. **The habitat and the channel** that make contact possible, including sensory, motor and motivational preconditions (Fig 11 :43-48).
3. **The priors and experience** the test-taker brings, which can be bought by a designer and masked as ability (world source Chollet 2019; Fig 1 :27).
4. **The frame's own scoring quantity**, which the frame also produces, so it cannot score itself with it (Fig 1 :27).

The residue is **indexed per capacity and per subject**, not global. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look." A universal intelligence number is exactly that discarded average.

Stated as an ordinary term, so the loop can check its direction (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"): **an answer would look like a protocol that, for each subject and each capacity, names a ground that does not move when a frame pushes on it, enumerates the habitat, keeps independent instruments low-overlap, and returns a per-slot verdict with a written refuter.** If it returns a single number instead, it has answered a different question.

## 1. Domain census

R described by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: many capacities per subject, so a vector indexed by slot, not a scalar. Symmetry: false. Comparison is an ordered threshold relation, ⋛ ("a threshold relation between two quantities", Operators :49), not a symmetric join; swapping two subjects across habitats changes the term. Scale: three, the individual agent, the habitat/channel, and the membrane between scales (Fig 4). A scalar rank is a symmetric collapse of an ordered, multi-slot residual.
- **Domain 1, the measurement frame.** Actors: test designer, test-taker, benchmark, human judge. Currency: score and skill. Anchor: **none that does not update**, unless a ground is supplied from outside the test. Where human judgment stands in for the anchor, Fig 2 :36 makes it legitimate only when the question is constitutively about people and "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they" do not measure (Fig 2 :37-38). This domain currently has a channel problem, not a measurement problem: Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; :26, "saturated metrics, pooled readings"; :28, "you have lost touch".
- **Domain 2, the capability and contact frame.** Actors: agent, habitat, ground, seat. Currency: contact and settlement. Anchor: the ground, which "does not update" (Fig 10 :22). This is where the answerable question lives: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" (Fig 11 :39).
- **Domain 3, the cross-scale comparison frame.** Actors: the kernel, the actors, the currency and the anchor at each scale. Currency: methods going up, head starts coming down, residuals at each slot. Anchor: the ground at that scale, and the lossy transform between scales (Fig 4 :27-30; Fig 10 :68). This is a **union**, not a partition: the three kinds of subject overlap in capacities, and "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Establish the anchor and the channel before scoring (Fig 2 :45; Fig 10 :29). Refuse the scalar before minting a method (Formula step 2). Then pick an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32-34). "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19).

**Verdict on census:** the question as posed is a union of at least three conditions with different anchors, so one lever, one test or one currency cannot clear it. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question asks the wrong object (decisive, `general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :34-41:

> **"What is intelligence?"**
> **"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."**
> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**
> **"Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."**
> **"That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice."**
> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

The question "how should researchers compare intelligence" inherits the refused question. There is nothing in the figure set that licenses an intrinsic quantity attaching to an agent alone. What is licensed is a question about contact: what makes this system capable, in which habitat, against which ground. Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :41: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."

**Refuter:** an instrument that reads a habitat-independent "intelligence" off an agent, with a ground that pays for the reading, would break this. The figure itself names the mirror case at Fig 11 :71-72: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim." Until one is run, the substitution is `specified`, not `grounded`.

**Triangulation:** Fig 11 (habitat/capability) and world source Legg and Hutter 2007 (70 definitions, no consensus object), low overlap between a framework figure and a machine-intelligence survey.

### F2: A global score destroys the only signal (decisive for the measurement frame, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." A benchmark that both defines the task and scores the result produces the quantity it uses to score. Chollet 2019 gives the world-side mechanism: "unlimited priors or unlimited training data allow experimenters to 'buy' arbitrary levels of skills for a system, in a way that masks the system's own generalization power." Colombo and Scarf 2020 quote Hodos 1987: "we should not become bogged down with a general intelligence concept for animals because its measurement is well beyond our grasp", and recommend we "concentrate on specific, definable, and measurable capacities that allow direct comparisons to be made between species".

**Refuter:** one measure that ranks all three kinds on one scale, is per-slot rather than averaged, and has an anchor that does not move when the test designer pushes on it. As stated, the rank must then still predict a held-out ground reading at each slot. If it does, F2 fails.

**Triangulation:** Fig 1 and Fig 2 (frame-internal quantity / non-updating anchor) are low-overlap figure routes; animal cognition and machine intelligence are low-overlap world fields.

### F3: A shared test is one evidence pool, so agreement on it is a convention (triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence" (continued :32, "is what makes agreement mean something"). Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

When humans, animals and machines take the same test, the test is a shared meaning and the comparison of scores is efficient as a convention. It is disqualified as a verdict about capability, because the three readings draw on one designer's priors and one task format. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not".

**Refuter:** two instruments that demonstrably draw on disjoint evidence, each ranged separately against a non-updating ground, returning the same per-slot verdict. That is corroboration at maximum independence, not F3's collapse (Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence.").

### F4: Human judgment is an anchor for people and an instrument for code (live type strain)

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they" (continued :38, "do not measure.").

A cross-kind comparison that uses a human-judged score as its single anchor treats the same quantity as an anchor for one subject and an instrument for another. That is a type error, not a matter of degree. It is recorded as a live strain in §8 and sent back to the census rather than smoothed. Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

**Refuter:** an anchor that is legitimately about all three subjects and does not move when any of the three frames pushes on it. None is supplied by the figure set; this is recorded as `gap`, not filled.

### F5: The channel is the failure, not the ground (triangulated)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; :26: "saturated metrics, pooled readings"; :28: "you have lost touch". Fig 10 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 10 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Schubiger, Fichtel and Burkart 2020 show the mechanism on the animal side: "applying a physically identical task is not sufficient to warrant fair species comparisons", and non-cognitive factors affect performance "via attention"; three preconditions must hold, sensory access, motor access and motivation. Colombo and Scarf 2020 show that when contextual variables are adjusted, "the performance of pigeons becomes indistinguishable from that of monkeys". The observed difference was in the channel, not the ground.

**Refuter:** a score difference that persists after sensory, motor, motivational, attentional and prior-knowledge conditions are reproduced, and that predicts a held-out ground reading. If it persists, the difference is capability and F5 narrows rather than fails.

### F6: A test harness is a substituted habitat (triangulated)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

Chollet 2019 makes the machine-side case: a system can have "unlimited priors" bought by its developer, so "If an AI system has access to extensive, task-specific prior knowledge that is not available to a human, its performance on that task becomes a measure of the developer's cleverness in encoding that knowledge, not the AI's inherent intelligence." The benchmark is a habitat the developers authored, so the solve proves wiring under that habitat.

**Refuter:** a harness that reproduces the goal's full habitat, including the actors the subject did not choose, and whose solve transfers to the unharnessed ground. Then the distinction collapses and F6 fails.

### F7: Comparison is of arrangements over a shared atom set, not of ingredients (single-source on the figure side, with a stated falsifier)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two systems with the same set of capacities but different arrangements stand as isomers (Fig 12 :117). If they share only a `state to structure to state` skeleton while the atoms differ, they are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :123). This is a claim about arrangements, not about a ranking. Over a fixed atom set, "humans, animals and machines" may be isomers; if the atom set changes, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either kind requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3.

### F8: Kernel invariance does not imply a common currency (strain dissolved by re-census, triangulated)

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running" it or a thousand. "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."

An initial reading strains: comparison seems to require one currency, yet the figures say currency changes across scales. Re-census: what is invariant is the loop and the naming rule, not the currency. So the protocol compares kernel shape and ground contact, with actors, currency and anchor named afresh per domain. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

**Refuter:** a currency invariant across all three kinds that carries the ground contact without loss. Its existence would break Fig 4 :20 and Fig 10 :68.

### F9: No universal currency is derivable; the gap is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 13 :136: "What it can do is narrow. It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

No figure line returns a single currency that ranks human, animal and machine ability. Declaring one would be naming a primitive the framework does not hold, and it would be a terminal room: Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room", and Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." The universal ranking is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind.

**Refuter:** a derivation of a seventh kind from the six, or a currency that composes across kinds without import. Either breaks Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."

### F10: The residual is a union; a single lever leaves the intersections standing

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

The measurement domain, the contact domain and the cross-scale domain overlap. A better test (one lever) clears the region where only the measurement condition holds and leaves the habitat and kernel intersections standing.

**Refuter:** a condition that is genuinely a partition, with no shared fraction, such that one lever clears all three at once. The census would then be wrong and F10 fails.

### F11: Filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A test's difficulty, sample size, cost and prestige are filters: they decide how much of the space is searched. They are not witnesses for what a system is capable of. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

**Refuter:** a case where the budget variable itself moves the ground. Then the filter is a witness and the rule fails.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument" (Fig 10 / Seat Map discipline). The protocol in §5 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single measure ranks humans, animals and machines on one scale, is genuinely per-slot rather than averaged, and has an anchor that does not move when any test designer pushes on it, and it predicts a held-out ground reading at each slot.
2. A ground reading shows cross-kind score differences persisting after habitat, priors, sensory and motor access, motivation and attention are reproduced, with the difference attributable to the agent alone (Fig 11 :40).
3. An instrument reads "intelligence" off an agent independently of habitat and ground, and the ground pays for the reading (Fig 11 :36-41).
4. A seventh primitive is derived from the six, or a currency is shown composing across kinds without import (Fig 13 :41, :115).
5. Two supposedly independent instruments turn out to share the same evidence pool, and the per-slot verdicts still hold; then the independence claim in D3 fails (Fig 2 :31-32, :40).

If any fires, the protocol is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation: a six-step comparison protocol

Mode: `specified` on the steps, `general` on the shape. This is what the figures license, not a measured recipe.

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms, per slot.** What failed to resolve, for which subject, against what? Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (from the two adjacent lines). Do not pick the test first.
2. **Enumerate the habitat for each subject.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." List actors, conditions and relations (Fig 11 :45-47). Mark what the harness substitutes (Fig 11 :70).
3. **Install a non-updating anchor per domain and name its legitimacy.** Fig 2 :29, :36-38. Where the question is constitutively about a person, human judgment is legitimate; elsewhere it is only an instrument and can be "wrong on axes they do not measure". A ground that moves when pushed is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30).
4. **Use two or more low-overlap instruments, each ranged separately against the anchor.** Fig 2 :31-32; Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Keep provenance, because "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" (Fig 8 :51).
5. **Build instruments from worse instruments already returning something.** Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34: "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." Run inward and outward as opposed tracks, never read one against the other (Fig 6 :31).
6. **Compare the kernel and the arrangement, keep verdicts per domain, and record the lineage.** Fig 4 :19-20; Fig 12 :94/:117. Never average across slots (Fig 1 :22). Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41). State the round trip gap `R_T` (Fig 4 :30) and the falsifiers.

The deliverable is a shape plus a refuter, not a rank. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 8. Honest stop

No instrument was run and no measurement was taken. The verbatim figure lines support a protocol and a refusal, not a measurement of any species or system. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The universal rank is `unreachable` as a shape-not-a-name gap, not silence and not a proof that no comparison is possible. What would settle the protocol is a ground reading, not agreement: see §4 and `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4.
