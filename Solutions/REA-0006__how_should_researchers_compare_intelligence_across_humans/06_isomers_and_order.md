# Isomers, order and analogues in cross-kind comparison

**Mode: `specified` on the claims that would fire at a ground, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

"Compare intelligence" reads as one quantity over three kinds of subject. Two questions are owed. First, can the capacities of humans, animals and machines be written over a shared atom set so that arrangements, rather than a total, carry the difference? Second, in what order must the comparison be attempted, since a step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22-23)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for the comparison: per-slot residual `R_s`, a non-updating ground, a habitat enumerated from contact, a kernel (perceive, plan, act, predict, name what worked), bonds including ⋛ comparison and → sequence, and negation for refusal. No seventh primitive is available without import.

Bonds: Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A" (continued :47, "recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."). The comparison uses ⋛, defined as "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators :49), which is ordered, not symmetric.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is a global score an isomer of a per-slot profile, or a different term?

- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- A global score and a per-slot profile may share the same ingredient list, the same tasks and the same subjects. What differs is the bond: the global score uses a flat conjunction or a sum across slots, while the profile keeps each slot ranged against its own ground.
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." So these are not variants of one thing under a different name. They are different terms with different behaviour: the profile says where to look (Fig 1 :28), the score does not.
- **Conclusion:** a global score is not an isomer of a per-slot profile. It is a different term whose bond has been dropped, which is the "flat formula" failure of Fig 12. Mode `general`, framework-internal.
- **Falsifier:** if a global score predicts the per-slot ground readings as well as the profile does, the arrangement carries no information and the claim fails.

### Q2: Are humans, animals and machines isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Weak reading.** If each kind is written as its own `state → structure → state` molecule with different atoms in the slots, they share only the skeleton. By Fig 12 :123 they are members of a functional group: same kind of arrangement, different members. The qualifier does the work.
- **Strong reading.** If all three are written over the **full shared atom set** (per-slot residual, ground, habitat, kernel, bonds, refusal), differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is the checkable claim worth sending.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the three requires an atom the others do not, or if the difference disappears when the habitat is reproduced, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. The strong reading is `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Note on overlap.** World sources support the weak reading: Colombo and Scarf 2020 recommend "specific, definable, and measurable capacities" and describe "kernel cognitive capabilities"; Chollet 2019 characterizes intelligence by scope, priors, experience and generalization difficulty, which are parameters of one arrangement, not new atoms. Neither supplies the strong claim.

### Q3: What is the order of the comparison?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 3 :29: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."
- The dependency order for a comparison is therefore:
  1. **Perception:** can the researcher tell what the subject is doing, and tell it apart from what was there before (Fig 3 :4-5)?
  2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what the situation affords, per subject and per capacity (Fig 3 :6-8)? Comparative cognition's "sensory, motor, motivational" preconditions (Schubiger et al. 2020) sit here.
  3. **Objective:** can the researcher say what would count as progress in the subject's own terms (Fig 3 :9-11)? This is the middle link that usually breaks (Fig 3 :24-27), because it has neither obvious failures nor obvious instruments.
  4. **Planning:** a route to the objective under incomplete information (Fig 3 :12-13). This is where low-overlap instruments and a non-updating anchor are chosen.
  5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it as a method (Fig 3 :14-15), never as a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).
- A score collected before step 3 is a reading below the break: "It is a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :23).
- **Falsifier:** a case where the objective link has a working instrument but the comparison still fails at perception or vocabulary. Then this order is wrong and the census must be redone.

### Q4: The analogue that makes the question workable

- Fig 11 :41: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."
- Fig 11 :42: "The lift move is Cayley's, 1799: separating the forces from the flapping is what made powered flight a problem anyone could work on."
- The comparison analogue: stop asking which kind is "more intelligent" and ask what generates the capability, that is, which contact with which ground through which habitat. This is not a metaphor. Fig 11 :38: "That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice."
- Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." So there is no standing list of good tasks for cross-kind comparison either. A task's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- **Falsifier:** a standing battery whose ranking of the three kinds is stable across residuals, with a ground that pays for the stability. That would break the valence-relative claim and the lift analogue.

### Q5: Two currencies, not one (live strain, named)

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them" (continued :127, "and will not notice the shortfall.").
- Chollet 2019 offers one efficiency number, skill times generalization difficulty divided by priors plus experience. That is an attempt to compare in one accounting. It is useful as a `specified`, actionable measure within the machine-evaluation frame, but it strains against Fig 12 :125-130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."
- **Resolution for this solution:** keep Chollet's measure as a world-side instrument, not as the framework's currency. Do not import it as a seventh atom. The strain stays visible and is recorded in `01` §7 S4 and in `09`.

## CONCLUSION

A global score is not an isomer of a per-slot profile; its bond has been dropped. Humans, animals and machines are isomers only under the strong fixed-atom reading, with a stated falsifier; otherwise they are a functional group. The comparison must follow the dependency order, with the objective link explicitly instrumented, because effort above a broken link arrives at a step that cannot use it. The lift analogue replaces "which is more intelligent" with "what generates the capability". One live strain remains: a single efficiency scalar versus Fig 12's two currencies, held apart rather than smoothed.
