## What in the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Public-private classification is provenance, not a source verdict.** The CEPR column's three patent classes (public-private, private-private, public-public) record who funded and who owned. They do not read the source of the primitive off the artifact. This is the strongest world corroboration of Fig 8 :50-52 and Fig 12 :5, and it is the reason a 2% share cannot be promoted to a cause.
2. **Complementarity is the world's version of the union.** Both CSIS and the CEPR column deny that public and private R&D are substitutes. That corroborates the union framing (Fig 9 :54-57) rather than a partition with a winner. The CEPR and LBS pages are one evidence pool, not three witnesses.
3. **The surplus needs low overlap.** Granovetter's weak ties give the mechanism in a different field: information comes from outside your own circle. The LinkedIn result's industry boundary, where strong ties win in less digital industries, is a real sign flip and corroborates Fig 11 :49-51.
4. **Valence is condition-dependent.** Bronstein 1994 says the average outcome hides more than it reveals. That is the ecological analogue of Fig 11 :51 and Fig 1 :22.
5. **A genuine conflict exists.** Ngo and Stanfield find net crowding out at the industry level where others find crowding in. It is kept as evidence that the question is a union of conditions with different currencies, not as a reason to flip the verdict.

Overlap declaration: the three economics sources (CEPR, LBS, CSIS) are one broad innovation-economics pool and count as one witness. Granovetter and Bronstein are genuinely low-overlap corroborators (social networks and ecology). The essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
