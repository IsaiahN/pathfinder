# Supporting derivation: bond type, union structure, and analogues

## RESIDUAL

The question writes "created by private firms, public research, or collaboration." Two derivations are owed: (1) what kind of bond does "created by" name, and does the three-way "or" form a partition or a union? (2) are the two pure closures (private-only, public-only) isomers over a fixed atom set, or do they differ by bond and therefore by substance? An order question follows: which link of the chain breaks first when a source-attribution study fails?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What bond does "created by" name?

- The ordinary phrase is a **production** claim: a new atom exists after the act that did not exist before. That is `⇒`, not `+` (Operators :37 and :56).
- The question's "A or B or C" writes the three institutional forms as a symmetric disjunction (`∥`, *"either suffices; not both required"*, Fig 12 :61-63). But the three are not interchangeable operands of the same operation: two are closures, one is a relation between closures.
- Applying `∥` here is a type error. The test for `∥` is *"remove one. Does it still work? Yes."* (Fig 12 :64). Remove cross-closure contact and the wall-moving operator disappears; the remaining two closures still compose internally but cannot add a new atom (Fig 6 :21). The answer to the test is No, so `∥` is the wrong bond.
- **Conclusion:** the three-way "or" is not a disjunction over one set. It is a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54-57). Mode `general`. **Falsifier:** show that removing cross-closure contact leaves the wall-moving capacity unchanged, with a ground that still pays for a new atom.

### Q2 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- The overlap is not incidental. Public basic research is an operand of the private commercialisation closure through prior imports; private demand is an operand of the public research objective; both share instruments, people and priors. Each overlap is a compound with its own loop.
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** A technology that needs both a basic primitive and a commercialisation channel is not a harder case of "private" or of "public"; it is a specific compound.
- **Conclusion:** union, not partition. Therefore no assignment of a share follows from the question's own structure. Mode `general`. **Falsifier:** exhibit a representative sample of technologies whose source conditions are disjoint, with no shared operands and no cross-closure imports.

### Q3 - Are private-only and public-only innovation isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Over a fixed atom set {a closure, a primitive, an instrument, a paying ground, a slot}, a private-only path and a public-only path use the same atoms but different bonds: the private path bonds the primitive to a market payment (`⋛` against revenue), the public path bonds it to peer settlement (`⋛` against reproduction). Same atoms, different bond, different substance. They are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense.
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The `closure + primitive + paying ground` substructure is the functional group that fixes "an atom was settled"; "market" versus "peer" is the qualifier that says which member.
- The **fixed-atom condition is load-bearing and live.** If the public path requires an atom the private path does not (for example open publication as a separate atom, or a long-horizon tolerance that a firm's closure lacks), the two are not isomers; they are different molecules with different ingredient sets. This is a `specified`, unmeasured claim, not a `grounded` one.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either path can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q4 - What order does the chain have, and where does it break?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Order for a source-attribution study: 1 perception (can the instrument read the capability change?) → 2 vocabulary (does the frame have terms for derivation versus import?) → 3 objective (can it say what would count as a settled source, in its own currency?) → 4 planning (choose a discriminating design) → 5 learn and carry (record provenance).
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The middle link, objective, is the one with no instrument in most of this literature. Patent counts and citation counts are measurements above the break. Funding shares are measurements above the break.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Adding more patent counts cannot rescue a study that cannot say what would count as a settled source.
- **Conclusion:** the break is between vocabulary and objective. The remedy is INWARD (sharpen the instrument) and OUTWARD (import a provenance frame), run as two tracks and never read against each other (Fig 6 :29-31). Mode `general`.

### Q5 - Is public basic research a catalyst or a spent part?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A basic result that is published and remains available for later commercialisation is a **catalyst-like** part: it survives its own use and can be composed over again. A grant that buys a single instrument and is exhausted behaves as a spent part. The difference is not recorded in a funding total.
- CSIS (world source, secondary) notes that public research is *"broadly distributed, openly published"*, which is the property that lets the result survive as a catalyst. This corroborates the mechanism shape, not the economic magnitude.
- **Falsifier:** show a published basic result that is consumed by its first commercialisation and cannot be reused, with a ground that pays only once. Then the catalyst reading fails for that slot.

### Q6 - What order do refusal and mint have?

- Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** The claim "source A mostly creates progress" is a `general`-mode verdict stated without a ground. Formula step 2 routes REFUTED before MINT: what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation.
- The refusal here is of the partition and of the standing list, not of the existence of firms, labs or partnerships. *"Nothing is ever deleted, and the reach falls because something was ADDED"* (Formula step 3). The union shape is added; the either/or loses its standing to be composed over.
- **Falsifier:** show a figure line that reinstates the partition without a new atom and without a ground reading. That would mean the refusal was wrong.

## CONCLUSION

"Created by" is a production bond (`⇒`), not a symmetric disjunction. The three options form a union of overlapping conditions, so no share follows from the question's structure. Over a fixed atom set, private-only and public-only paths are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance; the fixed-atom condition is live and named. The chain breaks at the objective link, where no instrument exists for what would count as a settled source. Public basic results are catalyst-like where they survive reuse, and that property is invisible in funding totals. Refusal of the partition comes before minting the union shape.
