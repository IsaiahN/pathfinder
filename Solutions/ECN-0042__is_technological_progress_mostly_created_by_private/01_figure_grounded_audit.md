## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). The central equation and its indexing rule:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

Here:

- **o′** is a realised change in productive capability, a new settled method entering the library. "Technological progress" names o′ after the fact. It is not a homogeneous stock; it is a set of per-slot observations.
- **b** is what an institutional actor takes to be the case before acting, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what it does: fund, publish, patent, commercialise, licence, collaborate, regulate.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what that actor's library predicts it can produce.
- **s** is the technology or domain slot: a material, a process, a drug, a computing capability, an agricultural practice.

The phenomenon to explain is the *source* of the atom that closes a slot's R. The ordinary word "created" is an ordered production claim, not a symmetric association: `"A produces B; B did not exist before"` (Operators table :37, the ⇒ production bond). Asking who *mostly* creates progress asks for an attribution share over many slots, each with its own ground.

State the residual as: *which closure held the primitive that, on contact with a differing closure or a paying ground, closed this slot's residual, and how would we know the difference between a primitive derived inside a closure and one imported into it?* This is an effect description. It names no winner and no percentage. Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (i) a separator that distinguishes the institutional conditions rather than averaging them, (ii) an anchor per domain that does not update on what the contributor thinks, and (iii) a provenance test that separates convergent derivation from adopted import. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is bound now, so the refuter in section 3 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates directly: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* For D1 (private firms) the market payment is a named anchor and does not move when a firm claims its own impact. For D2 (public research) reproducibility and settlement by a peer frame is the anchor, and citation counts are instruments at best. For D3 (collaboration) the anchor is whatever pays for the combined result, measured per slot. Nobody's reputation is an anchor: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor"* (Fig 2 :30).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a multi-place relation: an institutional closure Γ_i, an action a, an observation o′, indexed by technology slot s and ordered by time t. It is a sequence (`→`, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) carrying a production bond (`⇒`, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Operators :37) and a threshold query ("mostly", `⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87). It is not a symmetric relation: funder and commercialiser are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** "Mostly" is a threshold over an attribution distribution, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89; Operators :46-51). Closing a slot is asymmetric in time: a public basic result and a private product are not recoverable from each other by reversal. The attribution question presumes a symmetric partition, which is the first thing to test.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single research act (one agent loop), the institutional closure (a firm or a university with a shared library and *"something that ranks what they produce"*, Fig 4 :5), and the economy-wide productivity outcome across decades. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Private firm closure** | firms, in-house R&D labs, investors, customers | appropriable return, profit, market share | yes: realised market payment per technology (Fig 2 :29 names a market) | admitted |
| **D2 Public research closure** | universities, public labs, funding agencies, peer reviewers | publications, citations, openly published knowledge | yes: reproducibility and settlement by a peer frame that does not update on the author's claim | admitted |
| **D3 Cross-closure contact (collaboration)** | the boundary object between D1 and D2: licences, joint projects, public-private patents, spillovers | the surplus atom neither closure could compose alone | yes: the ground that pays for the combined result, measured per slot | admitted as a compound, not a third standing source |
| **D4 The temporal / channel link** | instruments, patent classifications, metrics, percepts | contact with the ground across time | yes: "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 "Technological progress" as one homogeneous stock** | none independent of the slots | a single aggregate rate | **no** anchor independent of per-slot measurement; aggregation dissolves the live signal (Fig 1 :22) | not a domain yet |
| **X2 "Genius entrepreneur" or "great lab" as free-floating source** | a named actor | reputation | no anchor independent of the market or peer verdict; reputation is a frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :24-27) | excluded before any figure line is applied |

X1 and X2 fail the anchor test and are excluded before any figure line is applied. That is a structural exclusion, not a claim that firms or labs never matter.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: a firm's closure is one operand of the cross-closure contact. D2 and D3 overlap: so is the public lab's closure. D1 and D2 overlap through shared people, shared instruments and prior imports. D4 overlaps all three through the channel that carries a result from one to the other. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (C1) a closure holds a primitive that can close the slot, (C2) contact with a differing closure or a paying ground carries it across the membrane, (C3) a provenance record distinguishes derivation from import, and (C4) the channel to the ground has not decayed. A technology failing only C1 and one failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure; they are different compounds needing different instruments. Fig 9 :4-5 adds the split rule for genuine disagreement: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the actor read the ground signal?) → 2 vocabulary (does the closure have terms for what the ground affords?) → 3 objective (can it say what progress would count as, in its own currency?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress, is where this question breaks: "mostly" presumes one objective function across three closures with three currencies. Most diagnosis effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). The CEPR patent data is exactly such a measurement above the break: it counts patents by funding and ownership, but it does not by itself settle the source of the primitive.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about innovation, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1 - "Mostly" aggregates slots and throws away the only signal

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1 through D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap: per-slot indexing versus union/partition split).**

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

A percentage share of innovation attributable to one source is a variance decomposition. It is a filter for where to look and where to spend research budget, not a witness for what produced a technology. The per-slot residual is where the source question lives. If one source accounts for 60% of patents but the live residual sits in the other 40%, the aggregate has hidden the signal. Fig 1 :27 makes the frame-internal failure explicit: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A funding agency's own count of its grants is such a quantity.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single attribution share that predicts the per-technology residual for a held-out slot without losing a live slot. If the share predicts no slot, F1 stands and the share is a budget filter only.

**World bearing (secondary only):** the CEPR column reports that publicly funded, privately owned patents are *"just 2% of all patents"* yet account for *"about one-fifth of medium-term GDP and TFP fluctuations"* (Gazzani, Martinez, Natoli and Surico 2025, via CEPR VoxEU). That is a striking budget filter, but it is not a per-slot source verdict. It corroborates Fig 1 :19/:22 and Fig 9 :60 rather than answering the question.

### F2 - There is no standing list of sources; valence is relative to the residual

**Mode: `general` structural refusal, `specified` when applied to a named institution. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 8, low overlap: habitat valence versus union surplus).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)

Private firms are a benefactor to a commercialisation residual and a malfactor to an open-access residual; public research is the reverse for a different slot. Collaboration can be a benefactor to a basic-resource slot and a one-frame wearing-two-names to a slot whose participants already share an evidence pool. The question "which of the three is mostly responsible" asks for a standing list, which Fig 11 :51 structurally refuses. This is the deepest reason the either/or fails: not that each has a share, but that the sign of each source's contribution changes with the residual.

**Refuter:** produce a standing list of institutional sources that is a benefactor to every technology residual across a representative sample, with the ground paid per slot and no sign flips. If a sign flip appears, F2 stands.

**World bearing (secondary only):** Bronstein 1994, *"Conditional outcomes in mutualistic interactions"* (Trends in Ecology and Evolution), reports that *"the costs and benefits that determine net effects to vary greatly in both space and time, inevitably causing outcomes within most interactions to vary as well"* and that the full range of outcomes reveals more than *"the average outcome at a given place and time."* This is a genuinely low-overlap ecological corroborator for Fig 11 :49-51 and for the refusal of an average. It governs a mechanism shape, not the economic fact.

### F3 - A closure cannot add the atom that moves the wall; only cross-closure contact can

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 8, low overlap: closure/reachability versus union surplus).**

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** (Fig 8 :32)
> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** (Fig 8 :45)
> **"Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."** (Fig 8 :46)

This is the structural kernel of the answer. Each institutional closure can compose inside its own library, but it cannot add the primitive it does not hold. A new atom enters a closure only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. "Collaboration" is not a third kind of source with its own standing credit; it is the name for the boundary where the wall moves. That is why it cannot be compared symmetrically with "private" and "public": the first two are closures, the third is a relation between closures. The question's three-way partition mixes kinds.

**Refuter:** exhibit a new atom composed entirely inside one institutional closure that moves the wall for the other closure, with provenance showing no cross-closure contact and a ground that pays for the change. If found, F3 fails and a single closure can be the source of the surplus.

**World bearing (secondary only):** CSIS (Borges 2025) states *"Public and private R&D funding are complementary, and both are necessary to sustain the United States' long-term technological leadership."* The CEPR column adds that *"This collaboration between public funding and private ownership appears to generate powerful spillovers."* Both corroborate the cross-closure surplus. They do not establish that collaboration is "mostly" the source, because they do not range against a per-slot anchor. They are one broad innovation-economics evidence pool, not two witnesses.

### F4 - The surplus needs low overlap and a described residual

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3, condition C2. Status: triangulated (Fig 8 independence rule and Fig 9 describe-first rule, low overlap: union surplus versus lookup discipline).**

> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** (Fig 8 :47)
> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** (Fig 8 :48-49)
> **"Residual first, frame second."** (Fig 8 :53)
> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** (Fig 9 :27-28)

A collaboration whose two sides read the same reports, use the same metrics and share the same evidence pool is one frame wearing two names. Its agreement is the pool talking, and its union adds no atom (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). The residual must be described before the collaborator is chosen, or any collaborator will seem to fit. This is a falsifiable condition on what counts as collaboration for source attribution: a memorandum of understanding is not the surplus, differing closures are.

**Refuter:** show two collaborators with high measured overlap on the same evidence pool who nevertheless produce a surplus atom that neither held, with a source gate showing where the atom came from. If overlap explains the atom, F4 stands.

**World bearing (secondary only):** Granovetter's weak-tie result, as reported by Stanford Report (De Witte 2023), gives the low-overlap mechanism in a different field: *"Your weak ties connect you to networks that are outside of your own circle... They give you information and ideas that you otherwise would not have gotten."* A 2022 Nature-like LinkedIn experiment with 20 million people found *"A person's weakest ties were the most helpful for career advancement."* The same article records a genuine boundary: in less digital industries *"strong ties increased career mobility"*, so the valence flips with the residual, exactly as Fig 11 :49-51 predicts. This is a genuinely low-overlap corroborator (social networks, not economics).

### F5 - Verdicts per domain have different anchors and must not be averaged

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 10 level rule, low overlap: ground definition versus ground maintainer).**

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** (Fig 2 :45)
> **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."** (Fig 10 :68)

D1's claim is settled by a market that pays or does not; D2's by a peer frame that reproduces or does not; D3's by the combined result that the ground pays for or does not. These are different grounds with a lossy transform between them. A single "innovation contribution" score that averages them applies one instrument to three quantities. CSIS makes the currency difference concrete: *"Private research tends to be more narrowly focused, less widely shared"*, while *"Public research, by contrast, is broadly distributed, openly published, and spans a wide range of scientific fields."* Different currencies, different closures, no common denominator.

**Refuter:** produce one instrument that settles the contribution of all three domains per technology with no lossy transform, no residual left unmapped, and no change in its reading when any of the three frames pushes on it. If the reading moves, F5 stands.

**World bearing (secondary only):** all three economics sources (CEPR, CSIS, London Business School) draw on the same Gazzani et al. evidence or the same policy frame. They are one pool. Their agreement is not three independent witnesses.

### F6 - A genuine conflict returns to the census, it does not flip the verdict

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D1, condition C4. Status: single-source conflict, handled by re-census.**

Ngo and Stanfield 2022 (Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis) report: *"Increases in federal spending lead government-dependent firms to expand research and development (R&D) investment whereas industry-peer firms contract. The net result is a reduction in industry-level R&D investment."* This conflicts with the crowding-in finding in the CEPR and CSIS columns.

By the WATSON conflict rule the disagreement is not smoothed over and the verdict is not flipped. It is returned to the census: the two results govern different quantities. Ngo and Stanfield study a **fiscal spending shock transmitted through financial markets and relative-performance incentives**; its currency is financing and its anchor is industry-level R&D investment. Gazzani et al. study **public funding of basic research transmitted through knowledge spillovers**; its currency is knowledge and its anchor is medium-term TFP. This is exactly Fig 9 :4-5: *"not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

**Refuter:** show that the same quantity, same currency and same anchor produce both signs under the same identification. If the objects differ, the conflict is a union signal and the verdict stands.

**World bearing:** recorded as `conflicts` in `10_source_ledger.csv`, with the re-census in `11_audit.md` section 5. No figure line was changed.

### F7 - Attribution is a provenance question, and provenance is not in the artifact

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3, condition C3. Status: triangulated (Fig 8 provenance rule and Fig 12 bond rule, low overlap: record of origin versus composition notation).**

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** (Fig 8 :50-52)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

"Who created this progress" is a provenance question. The artifact, a patent or a paper, records ingredients, not the route that produced them. Fig 12 :5 says the bond is the half nothing was recording. The CEPR study's contribution is precisely a provenance instrument: the Government Patent Register classifies patents by **funding source and ownership structure** (public-private, private-private, public-public). That classification is a record, not a measurement of who created the underlying knowledge. The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the general mechanism: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

**Refuter:** name an instrument that reads the source of a technology off a single artifact without a provenance record, and show it discriminating convergent derivation from adopted import on a held-out case. Until one exists, source claims from artifacts are candidates, not settlements.

### F8 - The channel decays while the ground does not

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: single-source for the figure line, with a world analogue.**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :25-26)
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :27-28)

A citation count is a metric that can saturate; a patent count is a pooled reading; a funding total is a transient. None of them is the ground. The ground is the realised change in capability, per slot. When the channel decays, the source attribution drifts while the ground stays put, and the drift is invisible from inside the funding frame. This is why the "mostly" statistic can move for decades without any change in where progress comes from.

**Refuter:** show a metric whose reading moves exactly with per-slot ground payment across a habitat substitution, with no saturation and no pooling. If it drifts after substitution, F8 stands.

### F9 - The literal "mostly" number is a gap, reported as unreachable

**Mode: `general`. Status: single-source after a genuine re-census, recorded as `gap`.**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** (Fig 13 :135)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

No figure line returns a global attribution share, and no figure line selects a standing winning institutional source. A genuine re-census across all thirteen figures, the Operators table and the Symbols table found no candidate. Therefore the honest report is `gap`: the figures return the shape (a union of conditions with a per-domain anchor and a provenance test), not the percentage. Unreachable is a verdict, not silence, and *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* is the discipline that keeps this from being read as "the question is meaningless." It is reachable in the world by a source-discriminating instrument per slot; it is unreachable from the figures.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that returns a global share or names a standing winning source after re-census. Until one is shown, F9 stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* (Seat Map, field guide). Written here before the conclusion in sections 5 to 9.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A genuine figure re-census produces a line that returns a global attribution share or selects a standing winning institutional source. That breaks F9 and the whole union verdict.
2. One attribution share predicts held-out per-technology residuals without losing a live slot. That breaks F1.
3. A standing list of sources is a benefactor to every residual with no sign flip across a representative sample. That breaks Fig 11 :51 and F2.
4. A new atom is composed entirely inside one closure, moves the wall for the other closure, and has provenance showing no cross-closure contact. That breaks F3.
5. Two collaborators with high measured overlap on the same evidence pool reliably produce surplus atoms neither held. That breaks Fig 8 :47-49 and F4.
6. One instrument settles all three domains per technology with no lossy transform and no change when any frame pushes on it. That breaks F5.
7. An instrument reads a technology's source off a single artifact without a provenance record and discriminates convergent derivation from adopted import on a held-out case. That breaks F7.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, none of this may be labelled `grounded`.
