# Expert verification packet: the Tether on sources of technological progress

**What this is:** a claim in a form an innovation economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the refusal of a global share, `specified` on the per-domain structural claims, `gap` on the percentage. Not `grounded`.** No per-slot source measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a separator, not a result. **It is also a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** Say so before any submission and check the venue's policy on AI-generated work.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are new technologies mostly created by private firms, public research, or collaboration?" presumes a partition with an assignable majority. The figures say it is a union of overlapping conditions. Private firms and public research are two institutional closures with different currencies and different grounds, settled by market payment and peer reproduction respectively. Collaboration is not a third standing source; it is the boundary relation where a new atom can enter, because a closure can compose inside its own library but cannot add the primitive it does not hold. That boundary yields a surplus only when the two sides are low overlap and a residual was described before the collaborator was chosen. Actor valence flips with the residual, so no standing list of sources survives a change of question. The global "mostly" percentage is reported as `gap`, not silence. What is offered is the shape a checkable source attribution must have, a provenance test, and refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the three options are a union, not a partition; R stays indexed per technology; no standing source list survives a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is a source-attribution share a filter for research budget rather than a witness for a cause? Can a source be a benefactor for one technology and a malfactor for another?
- **Refuter:** one share predicts held-out per-technology residuals without losing a live slot; or a standing source list shows no sign flip across a representative sample. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - framework-internal plus economics-facing; checkable by an economist against the two closures

- **Statement:** each closure can compose inside its own library but cannot add a missing atom; the wall moves only on cross-closure contact, and only at low overlap with a described residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :48-49 - *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- **What to check:** is a public basic result (broadly published, reusable) a different kind of operand from a private commercialisation channel (appropriable, market-settled)? Does a high-overlap collaboration add an atom neither side held, or only more spelling?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a new atom composed entirely inside one closure that moves the wall for the other with provenance showing no cross-closure contact; or show two high-overlap collaborators reliably producing a surplus atom neither held.

### C3 - provenance and anchor; checkable against the measurement practice

- **Statement:** attribution is a provenance question; the artifact does not carry its source; each domain has a different non-updating anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50-52 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :68 - *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."*
- **What to check:** the Government Patent Register's public-private / private-private / public-public classes are a record of funding and ownership, not a reading of the primitive's source. Does the study's instrument move when the contributor pushes on it?
- **Refuter:** name an instrument that reads a technology's source off a single artifact without a provenance record and discriminates convergent derivation from adopted import on a held-out case; or show one instrument settling all three domains without a lossy transform.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a winner, not a percentage, and not a new measurement of innovation's sources.
- Not that public and private R&D are substitutes. The world literature says they are complements; this packet agrees with that and gives it a structural reading.
- Not that every collaboration produces a surplus, and not that any partnership is a cross-closure import.
- Not a claim that the Government Patent Register classes measure who created a technology. They are a provenance record.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an instrument.
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation settles the economics question. It cannot.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not an argument. Concretely:

1. For a held-out technology, a provenance chain from the primitive to the closure that held it, plus a ground that pays per slot, surviving a habitat substitution.
2. A sign-flip test across technologies: if the source's contribution changes sign as the residual changes, the standing-list verdict is refused.
3. An independence test: measured evidence-pool overlap between collaborators, with the surplus appearing only at low overlap.
4. A share that predicts held-out slots (this would settle the question the figures leave open, and would break this packet's central claim).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 6 :21 and :23, Fig 8 :48-49 and :50-52, Fig 9 :55, and Fig 11 :51. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one technology you know: can you name the primitive, the closure that held it, and the boundary it crossed? If you cannot, say so; that is the instrument gap the packet reports.
3. Look at the CEPR patent classes and ask whether they are a source reading or a provenance record. A wrong answer here is welcome because it is checkable.
4. Name one discriminator that would name the source per slot, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
