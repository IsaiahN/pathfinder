**Subject:** Check one output on innovation sources before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about innovation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the domain claims, `gap` on the percentage):**
>
> The question "are new technologies mostly created by private firms, public research, or collaboration?" asks for a share over a set of technologies. The framework I am using measures the gap between what an actor's library predicts and what the world returns, per technology slot, never as one average. From that rule it follows that the three options are not a partition with an assignable majority. They are three institutional closures with different currencies and different grounds, plus a boundary relation between them. A public basic result is settled by a peer frame; a private product is settled by a market payment; the cross-closure surplus is settled by whatever pays for the combined result. These verdicts cannot be averaged without losing the signal that tells you where a technology's residual actually closed.
>
> The structural claim is narrower and checkable: a closure can compose inside its own library but cannot add the primitive it does not hold. A new atom enters a closure only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. "Collaboration" is that boundary relation, not a third standing source. And the boundary produces a surplus only when the two sides are genuinely low overlap and a residual was described before the collaborator was chosen. Two collaborators reading the same reports are one frame wearing two names.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any one of the three is the true or main creator of progress; that a funding-share percentage is a cause; that every partnership produces a surplus; or that this derivation is `grounded`. No measurement was taken here. The framework returns a shape, not the answer itself, and it reports the requested percentage as `gap`, unreachable from the figures rather than silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a figure line is found that returns a global attribution share or names a standing winning source after a genuine re-census;
> 2. one attribution share predicts held-out per-technology residuals without losing a live slot;
> 3. a standing list of sources is a benefactor to every technology residual with no sign flip;
> 4. a new atom is composed entirely inside one closure, moves the wall for the other, and has provenance showing no cross-closure contact;
> 5. two high-overlap collaborators reliably produce surplus atoms neither held.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md` (figures README)
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a technology you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its write-ups warn that a reported result could be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
