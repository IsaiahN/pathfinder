## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that a signal cannot carry information if no system can detect or interpret it. Option A: carriage is a property of signal and source, so removing every interpreter changes nothing about what is carried. Option B: information is a three-place relation among signal, world and interpreter, so removing the third term leaves only a physical pattern. What each leads to: A licenses mind-independent information that can be stored, priced in bits, erased at a thermodynamic cost and lost in a black hole. B makes information relative to a reader and collapses it into knowledge, salience or correlation-for-someone, so a manual outlives the craft with nothing left to read it as meant. The residual is whether the third term is constitutive of the first two, or whether the question bundles four different quantities.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: the minimal carriage relation is two-place (signal, source); aboutness adds a typed relation to a state of affairs; interpretation adds a receiving frame. The question stipulates removal of the third term. Symmetry: Shannon mutual information is symmetric, while semantic aboutness and directed information are ordered, so two quantities are called "information" with different operand order (Fig 12 :59, :73). Scale: one slot or bit, an arrangement of atoms and bonds, and a whole corpus are different objects; the residual is per slot (Fig 1 :19).
- **Domain 1, physical carrier.** Actors: source, channel, signal state, noise. Currency: distinguishable states and bits. Anchor: the state of the carrier, measurable; "The ground enters as the only metric." (Fig 1 :23). Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Domain 2, statistical measure.** Actors: source distribution, channel statistics, random variables. Currency: entropy, mutual information, capacity. Anchor: the joint distribution over repeated trials, fixed by source and channel. Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Domain 3, semantic aboutness.** Actors: signal, state of affairs, typed relation, frame holding the closure. Currency: aboutness, truth, meaning. Anchor: the bond is part of the term (Fig 13 :130), but which bond and which referent can be named only with a frame and a ground (Fig 10 :49, Fig 2 :45). Nameable anchor: partial, hence `gap` for content.
- **Domain 4, detection and use.** Actors: detector, instrument, perceiver, habitat. Currency: contact, recoverability, capability. Anchor: an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34). Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. One case instantiates all four conditions (a pulsar pulse has a state, a mutual information, an aboutness and an accessibility), so unbundling is required (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **Order by dependency.** D1 carrier → D2 measure → D3 aboutness → D4 detection. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :21: "Each link consumes what the one above produces". The question reverses this by putting D4 first.

Strain test applied: Fig 8 :39 (no search proves absence) versus Fig 6 :40 (no sensor for something that never registered) dissolves by scope: underivability from a closure versus availability of a proto-instrument (Fig 8 :40). Full re-census in `01` F8 and section 5.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question's binary is refused. A signal carries distinguishable structure and a typed relation to its source without any interpreter, because the bond is part of the term. Its source-channel measures are fixed by source and channel, not by a decoder. Detection is a contact property of agent and habitat; it changes reachability and use, not carriage, and the unperceived is residual rather than nothing. What the signal is about cannot be named from the signal alone without a frame and a ground, so the content is `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence. The four verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. This is `specified` on the four domains, `general` on the split, `gap` on content, and nowhere `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the refuters are used. If the four-domain split is wrong, the cost is a false ontology of information that propagates into storage, erasure and black-hole questions; if the carriage verdict is wrong, the cost is the Fig 1 :22 aggregation error, throwing away a live slot because no global detector fired. The avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework before the output (Fig 10 :52). "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." (Fig 2 :42).
- **undoable:** yes. The split is a convention about which quantities to keep separate, and each domain verdict can be flipped without touching the others. Seven refuters are written down before the run, and the packet in `08` carries them. No primitive must be withdrawn to repair a failure.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A bond-type instrument that reads which relation holds without a reader's closure would close the D3 gap; a contact experiment in which the bond changes when detectors are removed would collapse F2 into F5 and restore Option B for carriage; a channel whose capacity changes with decoder removal would make D2 reader-relative; a demonstration that a global zero entails zero mass on every slot would break F6. A figure re-census showing that D1 to D4 share one anchor would dissolve the split. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
