**Subject:** Check one output on undetected signals before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the split, `gap` on content naming):**
>
> The question "can a signal carry information if no system can detect or interpret it?" bundles four separate quantities. I keep the verdicts separate and never average them:
>
> 1. **Carriage of structure.** A term carries its atoms and the bonds between them. The bond is part of the term, not something a reader adds at read time. So a distinguishable arrangement is there without a reader.
> 2. **Measurability.** Entropy, mutual information and channel capacity are fixed by the source and the channel statistics. They do not change when the decoder is switched off. Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. These are different currencies.
> 3. **Aboutness.** The typed relation from a signal to a state of affairs is carried by the term, but which relation and which referent are not recoverable from the signal alone. With no frame and no ground, I record the content as `gap`, not as "no". Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
> 4. **Detection and use.** Detection is a contact property of an agent and its habitat. It governs reachability and use, not carriage. What cannot be perceived is residual; it simply cannot be read. A global zero with one live slot is a legal state.
>
> So the answer is not yes or no. It is: yes for carriage and measurability, `gap` for naming content, and detection is a separate question about contact. I have not claimed this settles the philosophical dispute. This is a shape and a route, `specified` and ungrounded, produced by a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that a signal with no detector has a *known* meaning; that an undetected signal is therefore useful to anyone; that no detector has ever been found; that information is a substance that flows through a pipe; or that redefining "information" settles anything. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. The signal's typed relation changes when all detectors are removed while signal and source are held fixed.
> 2. The four domains turn out to share one anchor, so one instrument settles all four questions.
> 3. A source-channel capacity changes when the decoder is removed while channel statistics are fixed.
> 4. An instrument reads the bond type and intended referent directly off a signal, correctly across changes of the reader's closure.
> 5. Adding or removing a detector changes the source-to-signal bond.
> 6. A global residual of zero entails zero mass on every slot.
> 7. An interpreter's reading yields a different correct content without adding any bonds.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **One question for stage 1, before any theory is shown:** what would you count as a case where detection changes whether information is *carried*, as opposed to changing whether it is *read*? Your answer on that point is the verdict I want, and I will record it before showing you anything else.
