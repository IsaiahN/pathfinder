**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about biological limits on cultural variation

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in evolutionary anthropology and human universals research: are there limits to cultural variation imposed by shared human biology, and how could such limits be identified? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` on the list; not `grounded`):**
>
> The question is not one question. It bundles universal presence, bounded form-range, and source discrimination. Those have different anchors and overlap, so a single lever cannot settle them (Fig 9 :4-5, :54). The answer I get is that any biological limit, if one exists, is a relation between a shared substrate and a habitat, registered per trait, never a global fraction of "culture" (Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 11 :39). The framework returns that shape; it does not return the names of the genes or traits, so the list of particular limits is a `gap`, stated plainly, not silence (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134).
>
> For the "how" half: a limit is identified by a discriminator that can return either "bound" or "no bound", whose threshold is fixed before the run, and which is sharpened from an instrument already returning a reading (cross-cultural measurement invariance, reaction norms, common-garden designs, comparative method). Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Two frames must range against a non-updating anchor separately; agreement from one corpus is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :31, :40).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular trait is biologically bounded; that there is a standing list of "the constraints on culture"; that a universal presence result proves a biological cause (the source discrimination is exactly what is still owed); or that this is settled by experiment. It is a shape and a protocol, not a result.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A single instrument settles presence, form-range and source for every trait in one measurement.
> 2. A trait's form-bound is invariant across all habitats with no reaction norm.
> 3. A specific biological limit for a specific trait is derived from the substrate alone, with no import and no measurement.
> 4. Two frames counted as independent are shown to draw on the same corpus or author.
> 5. A pre-registered threshold was changed after data inspection without record.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and line-numbered text (local paths): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Cultural universal and the non-nativist caveat: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_universal
> - Reaction norm and common-garden method: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_norm and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transplant_experiment
> - Cross-cultural measurement invariance: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_invariance
>
> **If you want to use the method:** it is in the figures link. Run it on a trait you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before theory | Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The reader checks without holding the framework's interiors. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Do not ask for trust, and do not claim prestige | The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried. |
| State mode on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Split the question before answering | Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* |
| Per trait, never a percentage | Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* |
| Refuse a standing list | Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* |
| Name the `gap` instead of filling it | Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* |
| Instrument before definition | Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* |
| Independence bought, not assumed | Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* |
