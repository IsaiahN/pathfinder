## QUESTION

Are there limits to cultural variation imposed by shared human biology, and how can those limits be identified? Restated in effect terms: given a trait form T, a population P and a proposed shared substrate B, decide whether the form-range of T stays bounded when P changes in a way that does not update on what any frame thinks, and decide which procedure could separate such a bound from a habitat or historical bound. Option A: answer with a list of universals and a percentage. Option B: split the question, return the shape per trait, record the list as a `gap`, and specify a discriminator that can fail. Option A sends a pooled verdict with no source discrimination and spends the independence of the readers. Option B sends a checkable shape, written refuters and an instrument route with an open slot until a ground pays for a name. Source: `cognitive science.txt` line 11, section 1.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency (Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: a limit is a two-place threshold relation between a form-range and a bound; as a research question R is three-indexed `(trait, population, proposed substrate)` and must be read per slot (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: "imposed by" is ordered, substrate to form, not a mutual-update frame; the anchor must not update (Fig 2 :29). Scale: within-population slot, cross-population membrane, species-typical comparative frame, with a lossy transform and a round-trip gap between scales (Fig 4 :30).
- **D1, cross-cultural trait documentation.** Actors: populations, ethnographers, coders, datasets. Currency: trait occurrence and form records. Anchor: published cross-cultural records treated as measurements. Question type: partition boundary for presence and form-range.
- **D2, human universals / evolutionary anthropology.** Actors: human populations, candidate universals, comparative method. Currency: presence across all known societies, inferred homology. Anchor: the census of known societies with a trait-identity criterion, an open set. Question type: existence, always revisable.
- **D3, shared biological substrate.** Actors: genes, developmental systems, sensorimotor and affective machinery. Currency: heritability, reaction norms, canalization. Anchor: experiments, common garden, twin and adoption designs, comparative method. Question type: mechanism, property of agent-and-habitat rather than of atoms alone.
- **D4, identification and discrimination.** Actors: instruments, methods, invariance tests, pre-registered thresholds. Currency: discriminating power between biological-bound and habitat-bound explanations. Anchor: a discriminator that can return either result and was improved from a worse instrument already returning something. Question type: instrument.
- **D5, framework / meta.** Actors: claims in gamma, bonds, seats. Currency: composition, settlement, reach, provenance. Anchor: the figures as metalaws; the ground for a measurement. Question type: shape.

No domain without a nameable anchor was admitted. "Culture" alone was refused: it has no anchor, only a frame-internal abstraction. It appears only as the habitat side of D3 and the population index of D1.

**Union, not a partition.** Presence and form are separate axes and biological and habitat conditions overlap through gene-culture coevolution, so one lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 9 :57-58, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound.").

**Order by dependency.** Perception (can the trait be measured comparably), vocabulary (terms for substrate, bound and alternatives), objective (threshold fixed before the run), planning (which discriminator and which independent frames), learn and carry (provenance and re-census). The break is at objective, where the threshold for "bound" has no instrument in most literatures (Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

Strain test: Fig 13's six physical terms do not sort cultural traits; Fig 12's functional group and isomer structure does. Re-census assigns each figure to its proper object rather than treating the apparent contradiction as a flaw. The one live strain is the operand type of the comparison operator at the cultural scale, named in `06` and `11`, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**Existence half, yes in shape and unresolved in content.** A biological limit, if one exists for a trait, must be a bounded form-range relating shared machinery to a habitat, registered per trait. Universal presence is a separate condition and does not by itself establish a bound. The identities of particular limits are a `gap`, reported as a verdict rather than a silence.

**Identification half, specified.** Fix the trait slot and threshold before the run; sharpen a discriminator that already returns a reading; range two or more low-overlap frames against a non-updating anchor; record convergence versus import; write the refusal condition in advance. Over a fixed atom set, invariant machinery is a functional group and variable cultural forms are isomers.

All verdicts are per domain and are never averaged. Mode `general` on structure, `specified` per domain and on the protocol, `gap` on the list, not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

- **cost if wrong.** Two directions. If a habitat-bound form is labelled biologically bounded, instruments and interventions built from one population are exported to another and misdiagnose cases; the human-universals source records that recurrence alone cannot identify the mechanism. If a biological bound is labelled variable, effort is spent hunting variation that is not there and the shared substrate is missed. The shape claim itself costs little to be wrong because it withdraws cleanly; the real avoidable cost is spending outside readers' independence by sending a list before a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable.** Yes. The shape claim is a generator plus refuters, not a settled list. Withdrawing a per-trait verdict removes standing to compose over it rather than deleting a term from the record (Fig 6 amendment on reach). Each domain verdict can be rewritten independently; nothing has to be retracted globally.
- **reversed by.** (1) A reaction norm or common-garden result showing a form-bound invariant across habitats for a named trait. (2) A single instrument settling presence, form-range and source for every trait, which defeats the union. (3) A trait whose bound is invariant with no reaction norm, which defeats agent-and-habitat. (4) A specific limit derived from the substrate alone with no import and no measurement, which closes the `gap`. (5) Two declared-independent frames shown to share one corpus or author, which removes their triangulation mark. (6) A pre-registered threshold changed after data inspection without record, which makes the convention decide the outcome. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
