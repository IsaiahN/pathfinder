## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Invariant machinery, variable forms: functional group or isomer?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Two readings, kept separate:

1. **Weak reading (functional group).** The shared machinery fixes the *kind* of trait a population can have (a colour lexicon, a display rule, a grammar), while habitat and transmission fix *which member* of that kind appears. The invariant item is the group, not the member. This reading is available for every trait and requires no claim that two cultures share the same atom set beyond the species-typical machinery.
2. **Strong reading (isomer).** If two cultures' colour lexicons are written over the same shared perceptual atom set and differ only in the bond (which contrasts are lexicalised, in which order, under which ecological and historical conditions), then they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a formula that lists only the atoms and decisive in behaviour, exactly as Fig 12 :117 says.

The strong reading is the one worth testing. It converts a vague claim that "culture varies" into a checkable structural claim: hold the atom set fixed, vary the bond, and ask whether the behavioural difference is systematically determined by the bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a cultural form requires a biological atom that the shared machinery does not contain, the isomer claim fails for that slot and an import that moves the wall is owed (Fig 6 :23). If two forms over the same atoms and different bonds behave identically, the "decisive in the behaviour" clause fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q2 : What is the order of the identification procedure?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The dependency chain for a single trait slot:

1. **Perception.** Can the trait form be registered comparably in the two populations? If not, no bound can be measured. This is D1 and the cross-cultural record.
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for the substrate, the bound, and the alternative habitat explanation? This is where gene-culture coevolution makes the question a union. The relevant figure is Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
3. **Objective.** What counts as a bound, fixed before the run? This is the threshold convention of Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. A convention that makes the claim statable is legitimate; a convention that decides the outcome is not.
4. **Planning.** Which discriminator, and which independent frames? Fig 2 :31: **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**
5. **Learn and carry.** Record provenance and re-census. Fig 10 :62: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."**

**Where the chain breaks.** Between steps 2 and 3, for most traits. Comparability instruments exist (measurement invariance), and planning instruments exist (reaction norms, common-garden designs), but the threshold for "bound" is usually chosen after seeing the data. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** A better cross-cultural dataset (step 1) cannot rescue a missing threshold (step 3). This is why the identification protocol in `07` and `08` puts pre-registration before data inspection.

### Q3 : What is the analogue for "import" here?

Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."**

The culture frame cannot derive a biological atom from inside its own closure. It must import from the biology frame. A legitimate import:

- has a **source gate: provenance** (Fig 6 :12), naming where the atom came from and with what evidence;
- passes a **shadow test: explains a gap** (Fig 6 :13), closing something the culture frame could not close;
- is **debited against the importing frame's independence** (Fig 6 :24), so a later agreement with that frame is not counted as a fresh witness;
- is **recorded**, because convergence and adopted import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :51).

An illegitimate import is the standing list: a catalogue of "constraints on culture" presented without provenance or shadow test. Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

### Q4 : Does the unary refusal operator have a role?

The Operators table defines negation: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. A vocabulary that can only list biological constraints cannot say that one of them is wrong for a trait; it can only accumulate candidates. The discriminator protocol therefore owes a refusal condition for each candidate limit (one of the five refuters in `02` and `08`), and a candidate that fails is refused rather than silently dropped. Fig 6 amendment on reach: nothing is deleted from the record; the reach falls because something was added (the refusal).

**Falsifier for Q4:** show a vocabulary of constraints that never needs a refusal because every candidate remains usable for every trait. That would defeat the operator analysis and leave the standing-list reading intact.

## CONCLUSION

The invariant item is a functional group of machinery in habitat contact, and the variable cultural forms are best read as isomers over a shared atom set when that set can be fixed, with the functional-group reading as the weaker fall-back. The identification chain is ordered by dependency and breaks at the objective link, where the threshold for "bound" lacks an instrument; pre-registration is the remedy, not a better dataset. Biological names enter only as provenanced imports with a shadow test, and each candidate owes a refusal condition.

No result here is `grounded`. The molecule/atom relation is structural; whether any particular culture pair is an isomer over a fixed atom set is a ground question for the discriminator.
