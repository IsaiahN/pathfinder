# Expert verification packet: biological limits on cultural variation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an evolutionary anthropologist or human-universals researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` per domain and on the identification protocol, `gap` on the list of particular limits. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of three conditions with different anchors: universal presence, a bounded form-range, and discrimination of a biological bound from a habitat or historical bound. Verdicts must be kept per trait and never pooled into a percentage. Any biological limit, if one exists, is a property of shared machinery in habitat contact, not a standing list of genes; over a fixed atom set, cultural forms stand as isomers (same atoms, different bond, different substance), with the functional group as the weaker reading. The figures return this shape and a discriminator protocol; they do not return the names of particular limits, so the list is a `gap`, stated plainly. Identification requires a discriminator that can fail, a threshold fixed before the run, low-overlap frames ranging against a non-updating anchor, and a record of which conclusions were convergent versus imported.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 11

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound; limits are per trait; a limit is agent-and-habitat, not a gene list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :39: *"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is the split into presence, form-range and source genuine, or is one of them reducible to the other two? Is there a trait whose form-bound is a property of the gene alone, with no reaction range?
- **Refuter:** one instrument settles all three conditions for every trait in one measurement; or a trait's bound is invariant across all habitats with no reaction norm; or a law returns the biological constraint for an arbitrary trait without per-slot measurement and reproduces every per-slot verdict.

### C2 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 6 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** identification is an instrument question. A discriminator must be sharpened from something already returning a reading, must have its threshold fixed before the run, and must be able to refuse.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- **What to check:** do cross-cultural measurement invariance, reaction norms, common-garden designs and the comparative method actually return readings that a cultural discriminator could sharpen? Is there any trait for which nothing at any resolution is returning a reading, so the honest report is `unreachable`?
- **Refuter:** a working discriminator produced from a definition alone with no prior instrument; or a pre-registered threshold changed after data inspection without record; or a discriminator that cannot return "no bound".

### C3 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 13 and 8

- **Statement:** the list of particular biological limits is a `gap`; the figures return a shape, not a name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*
- **What to check:** does any figure line actually name a trait or a gene as a biological limit? If one does, the `gap` is false and should be closed. If none does, is `gap` being reported as a verdict rather than as an excuse to stop?
- **Refuter:** derive a specific biological limit for a specific trait from the six-term set and the operators alone, with no import and no measurement. Failing that, show that the `gap` returned is the underivable kind rather than the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, and state which biological frame holds the name.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of human universals and not a percentage of culture attributable to biology.
- Not that cross-cultural recurrence proves a common mechanism; independent invention and diffusion give the same reading.
- Not that a named gene, receptor or faculty is *the* bound for a named trait. Candidate bindings are provenance, not derivations.
- Not that a convention agreed by specialists decides the outcome. Fig 10 :47 applies.
- Not that this is `grounded`. No measurement was taken.
- Not that a standing list of "biological constraints on culture" is admissible (Fig 11 :51).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not further argument:

1. A reaction norm or common-garden result showing a form-bound invariant across habitats for a named trait.
2. A measurement-invariance result showing the same construct is measured in both populations, so the comparison is admissible.
3. Two low-overlap frames converging with independent provenance, not one corpus quoted twice.
4. A completed census with a closed trait-identity criterion for a presence claim, with the openness condition still declared.
