## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a shape here is the form any answer must have before we can name the physical substrate. For this question the shape is: a bounded, catalytically closed, heritable compound in contact with a habitat. "RNA," "autocatalytic set" and "lipid vesicle" are names, not shapes.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." This is a bootstrapping convergence, not a single guess.
- Fig 9 :30: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" and :31: "rather than the cause." The effect here is heritable differential persistence; the causes are the candidate substrates.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place," because the answer has been composed through the process of locating it.

## 3. The bundle diagnostic, which governs this question directly

The most important line in the essays for ORI-0005 is not about shapes at all. *The Library of Babel* asks: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

That is exactly the defect in "molecules, networks, or compartments." The question bundles three conditions and presents them as alternatives. Fig 9 :55 says the same thing in the framework's terms: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The essay is a low-overlap witness for the figure verdict, not its source. The figures decide; the essay corroborates.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical rule says the framework stops "at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8).

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the first. An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," so one reading is compatible with several sources. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

Applied here: observing that a protocell-like compartment evolves does not tell us which of the three conditions came first, because several histories could give the same present reading. What is owed is a source-discriminating ground, not a name.

## 5. The specific answer for ORI-0005
