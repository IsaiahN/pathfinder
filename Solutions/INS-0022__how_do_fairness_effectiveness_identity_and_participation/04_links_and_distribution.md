## 2. Wider literature that bears on the four-condition claim

**Fairness and legitimacy (one evidence pool: procedural-justice research).** Tyler, "Governing amid Diversity: The Effect of Fair Decisionmaking Procedures on the Legitimacy of Government", *Law & Society Review*, 2024 (Cambridge Core), reports that "procedures provide a viable basis for maintaining public support in the face of differences in individuals' policy positions and background characteristics." Somuano Ventura and Takahashi, "Procedural Justice, Representation, and Institutional Trust in Mexico", 2025, state that "citizens trust processes and institutions that they believe are honest and unbiased, even when they produce outcomes they do not agree with, as well as those with which they perceive themselves to have a voice." These predict that fairness operates through process, not only through outcomes, and that voice and fairness travel together. They corroborate the bond reading of F4 but share a literature, so they count as one witness.

**Effectiveness, identity and group-varying weights.** "Fairness, function, and belonging: what shapes police legitimacy in Aotearoa New Zealand?", *Policing: An International Journal*, 2026, reports that "procedural fairness is foundational to police legitimacy in Aotearoa New Zealand, it may be insufficient for all communities" and that for Maori and Pacific respondents effectiveness was more strongly associated with duty to obey. Schnaudt, Hahn and Heppner, "Distributive and Procedural Justice and Political Trust in Europe", *Frontiers in Political Science*, 2021, find that "different facets of distributive and procedural justice evaluations exert varying effects on citizens' trust in representative as compared to regulative authorities and institutions." Both predict that the same factor has different valence in different slots. They corroborate F2 and F5 and are the strongest world support for the union claim, but they are still one broad social-science pool.

**The anchor and the ground.** Teorell, "The Impact of Quality of Government as Impartiality: Theory and Evidence", QoG Working Paper 2009:25, states prediction H2: "Countries with impartial government institutions have higher levels of institutional trust." He also records that a version of the predictions was published three years before data collection, which is a real pre-registration. Rothstein, "What Is Quality of Government? A Theory of Impartial Government Institutions", *Governance*, 2008, argues that "Social capital, defined as access to social networks and generalized trust in other people, seems to be determined by the quality of government institutions rather than the other way around." These predict that independently measured institutional quality, not the average survey response, is the candidate ground. They corroborate F3 but the direction-of-cause caveat in the *Oxford Handbook of Social and Political Trust* must stay visible: the handbook notes "Perhaps the main problem with trust research is isolating clear causes and effects."

**Low-overlap field: engineering and human factors.** Lee and See, "Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance", *Human Factors* 46(1), 2004, open with "Automation is often problematic because people fail to rely upon it appropriately. Because people respond to technology socially, trust influences reliance on automation." Hoff and Bashir, 2015, report the same group's finding that "Accidents can occur when operators misuse automation by overtrusting it, or disuse automation as a result of undertrusting it." These are a genuinely different evidence pool. They predict that trust must be calibrated against actual capability, which is the engineering form of Fig 2's anchor rule, and that overtrust and undertrust are both failures. They corroborate F3.

**Low-overlap field: business and organisations.** Mayer, Davis and Schoorman, "An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust", *Academy of Management Review* 20(3), 1995, define trust as "the willingness of a party to be vulnerable to the actions of another party based on the expectation that the other will perform a particular action important to the trustor, irrespective of the ability to monitor or control that other party", and note that "trust is domain specific". Ability, benevolence and integrity map roughly onto effectiveness, identity and fairness. This predicts that trust is behavioural and domain indexed, corroborating F2 and F3. It is a separate field, so it carries more independent weight than a fifth political-science survey.

**Low-overlap field: public administration and design.** Fung, "Varieties of Participation in Complex Governance", *Public Administration Review*, 2006, states that "Different regions of this institutional design space are more and less suited to addressing important problems of democratic governance such as legitimacy, justice, and effective administration." Escher, "Effects of online citizen participation on legitimacy beliefs in local government", *Policy & Internet*, 2024, finds that "for some engagement with the process leads not to more, but in fact to less legitimacy." These predict that participation is not a single lever and that its valence flips by context, corroborating F1 and F5.

**Method venue, not evidence.** The *Journal of Experimental Political Science* reporting standards and registered-report track were opened for the distribution rule below: it "welcomes new submissions" that include "informative null findings" and requires pre-registered results to be reported. This is a public convention a checker can use, not evidence about institutions.

## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the union claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending a recording of the conclusion before anyone has described a residual sends a description of one occasion, not a generator.
- **Fig 2 :40** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Later agreement from a recipient who read the framework is not corroboration.

Recommended order in a reply: (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the three framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. One to one, never bulk. Use the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, not personal addresses.

## 4. Honest limit on this literature

All the political-science and public-administration sources above agree that fairness, effectiveness, identity and voice matter for institutional trust. That agreement is expected because they share a question, methods and often a citation pool. "Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). The engineering and business sources are lower overlap and corroborate the *shape* of the claim, behavioural anchor, domain indexing, calibration, not the specific finding that this union closes R in a named institution. That remains a `gap` in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F8. No world source was found that conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.
