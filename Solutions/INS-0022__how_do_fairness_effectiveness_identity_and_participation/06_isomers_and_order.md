# Union, bonds, isomers and order in the four-condition claim

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how four named conditions combine to shape institutional trust. Two sub-derivations are owed: (1) is the combination a union of overlapping conditions or a partition of independent causes, and (2) what is the bond-level structure and dependency order of the combination. A falsifier is written for each before the result.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the institutional-trust frame: fairness as a comparison, effectiveness as a production plus threshold, identity as an identity relation, participation as conjunction plus production. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. The anchor is behavioural reliance ranged against independently measured performance. No new primitive is available without a source gate. The four conditions overlap, so the split is a candidate union.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

The four conditions are not mutually exclusive. An institution can be fair and ineffective, effective and identity-excluding, participatory with no decision linkage. Those are intersections, and each is a specific compound with its own loop. So the split is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54-57 governs directly. World corroboration: Fung (2006) states that "Different regions of this institutional design space are more and less suited to addressing important problems of democratic governance such as legitimacy, justice, and effective administration"; Escher (2024) finds that "for some engagement with the process leads not to more, but in fact to less legitimacy." Both predict that a lever's effect depends on which other condition binds.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a single intervention that closes R in every intersection across several institution and group slots with no categorically different residue, or a replicated model in which four independent factor scores predict cooperation with no interaction terms in any slot. If either holds, union fails and partition stands.

### Q2: What bond does each condition carry?

- Fig 12 :47: "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

| condition | bond | figure definition (verbatim) | what the bond adds beyond the noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| fairness | `⋛` comparison | "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87) | it asks which is larger: treatment received versus treatment owed |
| effectiveness | `⇒` production then `⋛` | "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77) | it asks whether the promised outcome came into being at all |
| identity | `≡` identity | "two names, one referent" (Fig 12 :67) | it asks whether "the institution" and "us" name the same group |
| participation | `+` conjunction and `⇒` production | "both hold at once" (Fig 12 :57) | it makes the citizen a held operand and creates an input where none existed |

The binding is from the institutional-trust frame onto operators already held. It is provenance, not a derivation and not an import that moves a wall. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The framework returns the *kind* of relation for each condition; it does not derive that a particular reform instantiates it. That needs the ground.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** two institutions with identical formal elements but different bond arrangements showing identical trust trajectories in every slot; or a demonstration that fairness is a plain symmetric conjunction that gains no predictive power from being a comparison. If so, arrangement is free and the bond reading fails.

### Q3: Are two institutions with the same four elements isomers?

- Fig 12 :117, quoted above, defines isomer: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two institutions that both have a fair procedure, an effective output, an identity narrative and a participation channel are not automatically the same trust object. If the bond arrangement differs (for example identity is `≡` in one and only a rhetorical label in the other, or participation is `⇒` in one and `∥` in the other), they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different trust behaviour. If one of the four is absent, they are not isomers; they share only a functional group, the `state -> structure -> state` shape of an institution that affords something to someone. The stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing.

**Falsifier:** an institution missing one of the four conditions shows the same trust trajectory, with adequate power and a measured anchor. Then the functional-group claim fails as well and only the bare presence of an institution matters.

### Q4: What order governs the combination?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"

Dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception:** can the citizen tell what the institution actually does, and tell it apart from what it did before?
2. **Vocabulary:** does the field have terms for what the institution affords (fair, effective, of my group, open to my voice)?
3. **Objective:** can the institution say, in its own terms, what would count as progress on each condition? This is the middle link, the one with neither obvious failures nor obvious tests.
4. **Planning:** which lever follows, given which intersection binds?
5. **Learn and carry:** does the result settle against behaviour and carry to the next institution?

Effort lands above the break, where the surveys already exist. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The order is dependency, not importance, so an institution cannot be told to improve participation before the vocabulary-to-objective link is measured.

**Falsifier:** a validated instrument at the vocabulary-to-objective link that shows the link was already working and that the break is elsewhere, with a measured residual. Then the ordering claim is relocated, not abandoned.

### Q5: Where does the arrangement stop being composable?

- Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Compositions of the four conditions do not run out. More surveys, more indexes, more subscales add arrangements without adding a ground. The specific configuration that closes R in a named institution cannot be named from inside the frame. It is `unreachable at this budget` for a source-discriminating instrument, not `underivable in principle`, and importing a favourite theory against that limit adds an atom that was not needed. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions form a **union** with typed **bonds**. Their arrangement, not their ingredient list, is the trust-relevant substance: institutions with the same four elements under different bonds are isomers, and institutions missing one share only a functional group. The order is Fig 3's dependency chain, with the vocabulary-to-objective link the unmeasured middle. The winning configuration in a named institution is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. The falsifiers above were written before the run.
