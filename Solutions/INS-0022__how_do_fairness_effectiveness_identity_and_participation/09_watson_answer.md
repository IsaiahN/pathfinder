# WATSON answer: how do fairness, effectiveness, identity and participation combine to shape trust in public institutions?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is a simulacrum and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the claim it reports on. No behavioural measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the combination of fairness, effectiveness, identity and participation in shaping institutional trust. Option A: treat them as four additive inputs to one national trust score and optimise the score. Option B: treat them as a union of overlapping conditions indexed per institution and group slot, range each against a behavioural anchor, compose them through typed bonds, and leave the winning configuration open. What each leads to: A applies one lever to a compound, makes the survey reading its own anchor, and selects checkers likely to agree; B returns an unbundled shape, per-slot verdicts, and pre-registered refuters.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** For an institution I and group slot s at time t, the gap between trust predicted from the four conditions and observed behavioural reliance on I. Arity: four operands combined into one slot outcome. Symmetry: the operands are not interchangeable; identity behaves like `≡`, participation like `⇒`, fairness like `⋛`, effectiveness like `⇒` plus `⋛`. Scale: person slot, institution slot, population aggregate, and the time channel. R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), never averaged.
- **D1 Institutional performance.** Actors: institution, procedures, officials, audit. Currency: impartiality, competence, delivered outcomes. Anchor: administrative records and observed outcomes, which do not update on frames. Admitted.
- **D2 Behavioural reliance.** Actors: citizens and groups as cooperators. Currency: compliance, cooperation, take-up, complaint, retention. Anchor: observed behaviour, the "market" of Fig 2 :29. Admitted.
- **D3 Perception frame.** Actors: respondents, media, elites, question wording. Currency: self-reported trust. Anchor: none that does not update on frames. Admitted as a frame and **refused as the anchor**.
- **D4 Participation and procedure.** Actors: participants, forums, rules, decision linkage. Currency: voice and whether input changed a decision. Anchor: procedural records and decision traces. Admitted.
- **D5 Identity and belonging.** Actors: social groups, narratives, membership signals. Currency: belonging, "on the same side". Anchor: observed cross-group cooperation and membership signals. Admitted.
- **U The compound.** Actors: the four conditions and their intersections. Currency: configuration of conditions and bonds. Anchor: behavioural reliance ranged against D1. **Union, not partition** (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **X1 One national trust score as decision variable.** Actors: survey designers and aggregate reporters. Currency: one pooled number. Anchor: none nameable; it is a pooled frame reading. **Not a domain yet**; refused as anchor and used only as a budget filter (Fig 9 :60).

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20): perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The vocabulary-to-objective link is the unmeasured middle (Fig 3 :27). Strain test: Fig 9 governs the problem split (conditions and intersections), Fig 12 governs the composition grammar (bonds between operands). Different objects, so no strain after re-census.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates / conflicts)

**Evidence pool A, procedural justice and institutional trust (one witness, not several).**

- **Political science, procedural justice.** Tom R. Tyler, "Governing amid Diversity: The Effect of Fair Decisionmaking Procedures on the Legitimacy of Government", *Law & Society Review*, 2024, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "procedures provide a viable basis for maintaining public support in the face of differences in individuals' policy positions and background characteristics." Prediction: fair process sustains legitimacy independently of outcome agreement. **Corroborates** the fairness-as-comparison bond (C2), but shares the procedural-justice pool with the next three sources.
- **Political science, Mexico.** Maria Fernanda Somuano Ventura and Yuriko Takahashi, "Procedural Justice, Representation, and Institutional Trust in Mexico", 2025, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "citizens trust processes and institutions that they believe are honest and unbiased, even when they produce outcomes they do not agree with, as well as those with which they perceive themselves to have a voice." Prediction: fairness and voice travel together. **Corroborates** the union reading, one pool.
- **Political science, regulatory compliance.** K. Murphy, "Nurturing regulatory compliance: Is procedural justice effective when people question the legitimacy of the law?", *Regulation & Governance*, 2009, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "procedural justice is more important for shaping compliance behaviors when people question the legitimacy of the laws than when they accept them as legitimate." Prediction: the effect of fairness depends on another condition, the legitimacy of the law. **Corroborates** the union and valence claims, one pool.
- **Police legitimacy, cross-cultural.** "Fairness, function, and belonging: what shapes police legitimacy in Aotearoa New Zealand?", *Policing: An International Journal*, 2026, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "procedural fairness is foundational to police legitimacy in Aotearoa New Zealand, it may be insufficient for all communities". Prediction: the weight of fairness versus effectiveness changes by group. **Corroborates** per-slot indexing and F5, same broad pool.
- **Political science, Europe.** Christian Schnaudt, Caroline Hahn and Elias Heppner, "Distributive and Procedural Justice and Political Trust in Europe", *Frontiers in Political Science*, 2021, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "different facets of distributive and procedural justice evaluations exert varying effects on citizens' trust in representative as compared to regulative authorities and institutions." Prediction: one aggregate is inadequate. **Corroborates** Fig 1 per-slot indexing, one pool.

**Evidence pool B, institutional quality and measurement (a partly separate political-science pool).**

- **Political science, impartiality.** Jan Teorell, "The Impact of Quality of Government as Impartiality: Theory and Evidence", QoG Working Paper 2009:25, University of Gothenburg, 2009, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Countries with impartial government institutions have higher levels of institutional trust". Prediction: independently measured impartiality raises institutional trust. **Corroborates** the anchor reading; its predictions were published before data collection, which is a real pre-registration.
- **Political science, quality of government.** Bo Rothstein, "What Is Quality of Government? A Theory of Impartial Government Institutions", *Governance*, 2008, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Social capital, defined as access to social networks and generalized trust in other people, seems to be determined by the quality of government institutions rather than the other way around." Prediction: institutions shape trust, not only the reverse. **Corroborates** the ground direction; the *Oxford Handbook*'s cause-and-effect caveat is kept visible.
- **Political science, handbook.** Kenneth Newton, Dietlind Stolle and Sonja Zmerli, "The Oxford Handbook of Social and Political Trust", Oxford University Press, 2021, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Perhaps the main problem with trust research is isolating clear causes and effects." Also "two overlapping clusters" inside political trust. Prediction: measurement, not just theory, is the binding constraint. **Corroborates** the gap and the middle-link finding.

**Low-overlap fields (more independent witnesses for the mechanism shape).**

- **Engineering, human factors.** John D. Lee and Katrina A. See, "Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance", *Human Factors* 46(1), 2004, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Automation is often problematic because people fail to rely upon it appropriately. Because people respond to technology socially, trust influences reliance on automation." Prediction: trust must be calibrated against actual capability, not maximised. **Corroborates** the anchor and calibration reading (C3), low overlap.
- **Engineering, review.** Kevin Anthony Hoff and Masooda Bashir, "Trust in Automation", *Human Factors*, 2015, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Accidents can occur when operators misuse automation by overtrusting it, or disuse automation as a result of undertrusting it". Prediction: both overtrust and undertrust are failures. **Corroborates** C3, same engineering pool as Lee and See.
- **Business, organisations.** Roger C. Mayer, James H. Davis and F. David Schoorman, "An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust", *Academy of Management Review* 20(3), 1995, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "the willingness of a party to be vulnerable to the actions of another party based on the expectation that the other will perform a particular action important to the trustor, irrespective of the ability to monitor or control that other party"; also "trust is domain specific". Prediction: trust is behavioural and domain indexed. **Corroborates** per-slot indexing and the anchor reading, low overlap.
- **Public administration, design.** Archon Fung, "Varieties of Participation in Complex Governance", *Public Administration Review*, 2006, read 2026-11-23. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the winning configuration does not exist yet. Fairness, effectiveness, identity and participation combine as a union of overlapping conditions indexed per institution and group slot; they carry distinct bonds whose arrangement is the trust-relevant substance; their valence is relative to the residual and the habitat; the anchor is behaviour and independent performance, not the survey reading; the field's unmeasured link is vocabulary-to-objective; and which configuration closes R in a named institution is a declared `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument settles it. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. The world pools corroborate the mechanism shape after the anchor is separated from the reading; they do not supply the missing configuration. A `⋛` operand-type strain at cross-slot comparisons is named, not hidden.

- **cost if wrong:** a single lever is applied to a union and the intersections remain, or a survey score is promoted to an anchor and the measure drifts coherently while every internal instrument reports green. The cost is bounded and locatable because the packet states refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and by not selecting checkers for agreement.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because "something was added rather than because anything was deleted" (Fig 6 amendment). The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance, so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A pre-registered, well-identified experiment that closes R in every intersection across several institution and group slots; a self-report measure invariant to framing that fully predicts behaviour with no behavioural measure adding anything; an instrument at the vocabulary-to-objective link showing the link was already working; two institutions with identical elements under different bonds showing identical trust trajectories; or a verbatim figure line naming the winning configuration. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
