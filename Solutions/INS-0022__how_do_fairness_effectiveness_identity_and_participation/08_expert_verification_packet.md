# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutional trust

**What this is:** a claim in a form a trusted-research reader can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No behavioural measurement is claimed to settle the binding. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Fairness, effectiveness, identity and participation do not combine as four additive inputs to one trust score. They behave as a union of overlapping conditions indexed per institution and group slot. A lever that clears a slot where one condition is missing leaves standing the slots where two conditions bind. The four carry different bond types, so their arrangement, not their ingredient list, is the trust-relevant substance, and two institutions with the same elements under different arrangements are isomers. No standing list of trust builders survives a change of group. The anchor is behavioural cooperation and independently measured institutional performance, not the self-reported trust number that moves with framing. The specific configuration that closes the gap in a named institution is not named by the framework.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** the four conditions form a union with live intersections, and the residual is indexed per slot rather than aggregated.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Also Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** one lever closes R in every intersection across several institution and group slots with no categorically different residue; or one scalar trust score predicts cooperation equally well in every slot with no live residual anywhere, under a design with power to detect one.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 11

- **Statement:** the four carry distinct bond types, arrangement is the substance, and valence is relative to the residual and habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :47 "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** do the proposed bonds match the figure definitions? Fairness as "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87); identity as "two names, one referent" (Fig 12 :67); effectiveness as "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77). These are bindings from the institutional-trust frame onto held operators, not derivations.
- **Refuter:** two institutions with identical formal elements but different arrangements show identical trust trajectories in every slot; or a factor raises behavioural trust by the same amount in every group across a documented change of residual.

### C3: framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the cited studies

- **Statement:** the anchor is behaviour and independent performance, not the survey reading; the survey is a frame to be ranged against it separately.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **What to check:** Lee and See (2004), *Human Factors* 46(1), "Automation is often problematic because people fail to rely upon it appropriately. Because people respond to technology socially, trust influences reliance on automation." Mayer, Davis and Schoorman (1995), *Academy of Management Review* 20(3), define trust behaviourally and note that "trust is domain specific". The 2026 Aotearoa New Zealand police study reports that "procedural fairness is foundational to police legitimacy in Aotearoa New Zealand, it may be insufficient for all communities".
- **Refuter:** a self-report measure invariant to question framing and to respondent identity that fully predicts cooperation, with no behavioural measure adding anything. Then self-report qualifies as an anchor and C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that one of the four is the main driver, and not that they add linearly.
- Not that a national trust average is a verdict. It is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60), never a witness for the verdict.
- Not that survey trust is useless. It is a frame reading to be ranged separately against behaviour.
- Not that the framework derives fairness, effectiveness, identity or participation. They are bindings, provenanced as such.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a trust engineering recommendation. No lever is named, because the framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. Does behavioural cooperation move when only framing changes and behaviour is held fixed?
2. Do the four conditions and their intersections separate experimentally, leaving different residues?
3. Can an institution measure, in its own terms, what counts as progress on each condition, and does that reading predict cooperation?
4. Do two institutions with identical elements under different bond arrangements have different trust trajectories?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :47 and :117, Fig 11 :51, Fig 2 :29. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against what you know of combined institutional-reform programmes: does a single lever leave group-specific residues?
3. Check C3 against your own work: is the survey score you use a ground or a frame? What would make it a ground?
4. Name one behavioural instrument that would separate fairness from identity in a named institution, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
