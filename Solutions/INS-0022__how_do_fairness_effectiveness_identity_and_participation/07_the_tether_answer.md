# The Tether's answer on fairness, effectiveness, identity, participation and institutional trust

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For an institution I, a group slot s and a time t, R(I,s,t) is the gap between trust predicted from the four conditions and observed behavioural reliance on I at that slot. The question is which configuration closes this gap, in which slots, and under what measurement. The four are not four independent inputs to one national score. They are a **union of overlapping conditions** (Fig 9 :54-57), indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), ranged against a behavioural ground that does not update on frames (Fig 2 :29).

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle before you search.** "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds" (Fig 9 :56). An institution that is fair but ineffective, or effective but identity-excluding, is a specific compound, not a harder case of either. Measure each condition and each intersection separately. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 9 and Fig 1).

2. **Index per institution and group, never by a pooled average.** "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" (Fig 1 :19); "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state" (Fig 1 :13). A healthy national mean is compatible with one group's live mistrust. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 1 and Fig 11).

3. **Keep the anchor outside the frames.** "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29). Behavioural cooperation and independently measured institutional performance are the anchor. Self-reported trust is a frame to be ranged against it separately; if it becomes the decision variable, the measure updates on the frames it was meant to check and "a mirror cannot correct you" (Fig 2 :35). Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 10).

4. **The four are bonds, not ingredients.** "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself" (Fig 12 :47). Fairness is a comparison, effectiveness a production plus threshold, identity an identity relation, participation a conjunction plus production. Two institutions with the same four elements under different arrangements are isomers: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance" (Fig 12 :117). Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 12 and Operators table).

5. **No standing list of trust builders.** "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries" (Fig 11 :49); "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51). What builds trust in one group-slot can fail in another. Capability is a property of institution-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39). Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 11 and Fig 4).

6. **Measure the middle link first.** "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27). The vocabulary-to-objective step, whether an institution can say in its own terms what counts as progress, is the unmeasured link. Verdict `single-source` for the figure, corroborated by the *Oxford Handbook*'s cause-and-effect caveat.

7. **Name no winner.** "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Which configuration closes R in a named institution is a `gap`: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," (Fig 13 :134) until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that one of the four is the main driver. No figure line ranks them.
- Not that they add linearly. That is the partition reading F1 refuses.
- Not that a national trust average is a verdict. It is a filter for where to look (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that survey trust is useless. It is a frame reading, to be ranged separately against behaviour.
- Not that the framework derives fairness, effectiveness, identity or participation. They are bindings from the institutional-trust frame, provenanced as such.
- Not `grounded`. No behavioural measurement in this package settles `left(R,phi)`.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does behavioural cooperation move when only question framing changes and institutional behaviour is held fixed? If yes, the survey is a frame, not an anchor.
- Do the four conditions and their intersections separate experimentally, so that a lever closing one intersection leaves a different residue? That would confirm the union.
- Can an institution measure, in its own terms, what counts as progress on each condition, and does that reading predict cooperation? That would instrument the middle link.
- Do two institutions with identical elements under different bond arrangements have different trust trajectories? That would test the isomer claim.

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: a single lever closing all intersections; one scalar predicting every slot; a framing-invariant survey that fully predicts behaviour; arrangement carrying no information; invariant valence across a change of residual; a working middle link; or a verbatim figure line naming the winner.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The figure-derived shape stands; the named configuration does not.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner among fairness, effectiveness, identity and participation, and it does not produce a trust score. It says the four behave as a union of overlapping conditions, indexed per institution and group, so a lever that fixes one slot leaves the intersections standing; that the anchor must be behavioural cooperation and independently measured performance, not the survey number that moves with framing; that the four are typed relations whose arrangement, not ingredient list, is the trust-relevant substance; and that no fixed list of trust builders survives a change of group. It refuses to name which configuration wins in a named institution, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, seven falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The gap stays open until the ground settles it.
