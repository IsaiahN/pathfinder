## 0. The residual in effect terms

For a public institution I, a citizen or group slot s, and a time t, write

```
R(I,s,t) = | trust predicted from {fairness, effectiveness, identity, participation}
            - observed behavioural reliance on I at slot s and time t |
```

The question asks which configuration of the four conditions closes this residual, in which slots, and under what measurement. Three things follow before any figure is chosen.

1. **Trust is a reading, not automatically an anchor.** Self-reported trust is produced by frames (respondents, media, elites, question wording). If we make it the thing that settles the question, we have put a frame in the anchor's seat. Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor as "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Behavioural reliance (compliance, cooperation, take-up, complaint, retention) and independently measured institutional performance (impartiality, audit data, service outcomes) are the candidates that do not update on what a frame thinks.
2. **"Combine" is the load-bearing word.** The four are not a list to be averaged. They may be a union of overlapping conditions, or they may be bonds in one composition. Both readings are tested below, and the strain test decides between them.
3. **The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term here.** The shape I am looking for is: a union of overlapping conditions, indexed per slot, ranged against a behavioural anchor, composed by typed bonds, with the winning configuration left open. Binding it now means it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else (formula step "Direction").

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** four operands combined into one institution-group-time outcome. A four-place composition, not one input to one score.
- **Symmetry:** not all swaps preserve meaning. Identity behaves like `≡` (two names, one referent); the fairness to legitimacy to cooperation path behaves like `→` (ordered); participation behaves like `⇒` (voice produces an input that did not exist); effectiveness behaves like `⋛` (a threshold comparison). So the operands are not interchangeable and a flat additive score would erase the bond information.
- **Scale:** person slot, institution slot, population aggregate, and the time channel across events. The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Institutional performance | institution, procedures, officials, audit | impartiality, competence, delivered outcomes | administrative records, audits, observed outcomes (does not update on frames) | admitted |
| D2 Behavioural reliance | citizens and groups as cooperators | compliance, cooperation, take-up, complaint, retention | observed behaviour, the "market" of Fig 2 :29 | admitted |
| D3 Perception frame | respondents, media, elites, question wording | self-reported trust, perceived fairness | none that does not update on frames | admitted as a frame, **refused as the anchor** |
| D4 Participation and procedure | participants, forums, rules, decision linkage | voice, inclusion, whether input changed a decision | procedural records, decision traces | admitted |
| D5 Identity and belonging | social groups, narratives, membership signals | "on the same side", recognition | observed cross-group cooperation and membership signals | admitted |
| U The compound (this question) | the four conditions and their intersections | configuration of conditions and bonds | behavioural reliance ranged against D1 | admitted as a **union**, not a partition |
| X1 One national trust score as the decision variable | survey designers, aggregate reporters | a single pooled number | none nameable; it is a pooled frame reading | **not a domain yet**; refused as anchor, used only as a budget filter |

### Partition or union

Union. D2 and D1 form the ground compound (behaviour ranged against performance). D3, D4 and D5 are frames that must be ranged against it separately. The four named conditions overlap: a procedurally fair institution can be ineffective; an effective one can exclude a group's identity; participation without decision linkage can be symbolic. An intersection is a specific compound, not a harder case of either condition.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20)

"Perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry." For this question: can the citizen perceive what the institution does; does the field have terms for what an institution affords (fair, effective, of my group, open to my voice); can an institution say what would count as progress in its own terms; which lever follows; can the result be settled and carried to the next institution. The middle link (vocabulary to objective) is the one this field least often instruments.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The four conditions form a union, not an additive list (decisive shape)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

A trust campaign that improves only fairness clears the slots where fairness alone was missing. It leaves standing the slots where fairness and identity both bind. Those are categorically different compounds and need their own loop. The four do not sum; they overlap.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 9 (union and partition) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual) are two figures with low overlap that converge on "unbundle, do not aggregate."

**Refuter:** an intervention that raises all four condition measures and closes R in every intersection at once, with no categorically different residue left in any slot; or a replicated linear model in which four independent factor scores fully predict cooperation in all tested slots with no interaction terms.

### F2: R is indexed per institution-slot and group-slot, never aggregated

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A national score near zero is compatible with one group's live mistrust. Reporting the mean is how that signal is thrown away. The world literature concurs: Schnaudt, Hahn and Heppner (2021) find that "different facets of distributive and procedural justice evaluations exert varying effects on citizens' trust in representative as compared to regulative authorities and institutions," and the *Oxford Handbook of Social and Political Trust* records "two overlapping clusters" inside political trust (Newton, Stolle and Zmerli 2021).

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 1 (per-slot) and Fig 11 :49-51 (valence relative to residual) have low overlap and agree.

**Refuter:** one scalar trust score that predicts cooperation equally well across representative and regulative institutions and across all groups, with no live residual in any slot, under a design with power to detect one.

### F3: The anchor is behaviour and independently measured performance, not the survey reading

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :44: **"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

If the measured quantity is itself produced by the frames being checked, raising it is coherent drift (Fig 2 panels 1 and 2), not correction. The channel to behaviour decays: a metric saturates, a group is pooled into an average, a question is reframed. The seat's job is to keep the anchor reachable, not to adjudicate what the survey would have said. Lee and See (2004) make the same distinction from engineering: "Automation is often problematic because people fail to rely upon it appropriately. Because people respond to technology socially, trust influences reliance on automation." Mayer, Davis and Schoorman (1995) define trust behaviourally as "the willingness of a party to be vulnerable to the actions of another party based on the expectation that the other will perform a particular action important to the trustor, irrespective of the ability to monitor or control that other party."

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 2 (anchor rule) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are distinct figures with low overlap.

**Refuter:** an institution whose behavioural cooperation is fully predicted by its survey trust score with no behavioural ground measure adding anything, where the survey score is invariant to question framing and invariant to who answers. Then self-report would qualify as an anchor and this finding fails.

### F4: The four conditions are bonds, not ingredients; arrangement is the substance

Fig 12 :47: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

Bond-level reading, binding from the institutional-trust frame onto operators already held by the framework (provenance recorded, not a derivation):

- **Fairness** is a comparison `⋛`: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87). It asks which is larger, treatment received versus treatment owed.
- **Effectiveness** is a threshold plus a production chain (`⋛` over an `⇒`): "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77). It asks whether the promised outcome came into being.
- **Identity** is `≡`. The figure definition is "two names, one referent" (Fig 12 :67). The bond asks whether the institution and the citizenry name one referent, which is the identity condition.
- **Participation** is `+` and `⇒`: "both hold at once" (Fig 12 :57) plus voice that produces an input. It asks whether the citizen is a held operand, not an observer.

Institutions with the same four elements arranged under different bonds are isomers. Fung (2006) reaches the same structural point from public administration: "Different regions of this institutional design space are more and less suited to addressing important problems of democratic governance such as legitimacy, justice, and effective administration." No single design serves all three values at once.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 12 and the Operators table are distinct sources with low overlap (figure definition versus operator table).

**Refuter:** two institutions with identical formal elements but different bond arrangements showing identical trust trajectories in every slot; or a demonstration that fairness is a plain symmetric conjunction that gains nothing from being a comparison.

### F5: No standing list of trust builders; valence is relative to the residual and the habitat

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
Fig 4 :20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

The same fairness reform is a benefactor to one group's residual and a malfactor to another's. The 2026 Aotearoa New Zealand police study reports that "procedural fairness is foundational to police legitimacy in Aotearoa New Zealand, it may be insufficient for all communities" and that its effects were not invariant across Māori and Pacific respondents. Murphy (2009) finds that "procedural justice is more important for shaping compliance behaviors when people question the legitimacy of the laws than when they accept them as legitimate." These are not noise around a common effect; they are the valence rule.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 11 (habitat and valence) and Fig 4 (actors, currency, anchor change with size) are distinct figures with low overlap.

**Refuter:** a factor that raises behavioural trust by the same amount in every group and every institution regardless of the residual, across a documented change of residual with adequate power.

### F6: The vocabulary-to-objective link is unmeasured

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The middle link in this field is "having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress." A public institution can be scored on fairness, effectiveness, identity and participation inputs while nobody has measured whether it can say, in its own terms, what would count as progress. Effort lands above the break, where the surveys already exist. The *Oxford Handbook* records the matching methodological complaint: "Perhaps the main problem with trust research is isolating clear causes and effects."

**Status: single-source for the figure (Fig 3), corroborated by world sources, not by a second figure.**

**Refuter:** a validated instrument that measures, in an institution's own terms, what counts as progress on each of the four conditions, and whose readings settle behavioural cooperation across a change of institution.

### F7: The winning configuration is a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

The Tether can say that a configuration must unbundle, must range against a behavioural anchor, must compose by bonds, and must be re-censused when the residual changes. It cannot say "participation is the lever" without naming what the framework withholds. Selecting a favoured factor first and mapping the literature onto it is the inversion Fig 9 warns against.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 9 (shape, not a name) and the canonical figures README ("a shape, not a name") are two low-overlap routes inside the framework, though the README shares the author and evidence pool.

**Refuter:** a figure line that directly names which of the four closes R for public institutions, found on re-census of the verbatim text.

### F8: Which configuration closes R in a named institution is a gap

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

After a genuine census no figure line names the winning configuration for a given institution. This is stated as `gap`, not hidden. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument: a design that separates the four conditions and their intersections against the behavioural anchor, described in effect terms before a frame is chosen. Searching more trust surveys at the same reach is "unreachable at this budget", not "underivable in principle", and importing a favourite theory against that limit adds an atom that was not needed.

**Status: gap confirmed after re-census.** Verdict, not silence.

**Refuter:** a well-identified, pre-registered experiment that closes R in the intersections across several institution and group slots and names the configuration with a source gate and shadow test.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: the seat map says "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuted:** a single lever closes R in every intersection at once across several institution/group slots, with no categorically different residue.
2. **Per-slot refuted:** one scalar trust score predicts behavioural cooperation equally well in every institution and group slot, with no live residual anywhere.
3. **Anchor refuted:** a self-report measure is invariant to framing and to respondent identity and fully predicts cooperation with no behavioural measure adding anything; behaviour then proves to be a frame that updates on opinion.
4. **Bond refuted:** arrangement carries no information, institutions with the same elements under different bonds have identical trust trajectories, and a flat additive score is sufficient.
5. **Valence refuted:** a factor raises trust by the same amount in every group and institution across a documented change of residual.
6. **Link refuted:** a validated instrument at the vocabulary-to-objective link exists and shows the link was already working.
7. **Gap refuted:** a verbatim figure line, or a pre-registered source-discriminating experiment, names the winning configuration and closes R in the intersections.

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn and the reach falls because something was added (the refusal), not because text was deleted (Fig 6 amendment). Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union and the refuter, not with a trust score (F1, F2). Unbundle the four conditions and measure the intersections.
2. Protect the anchor. Range survey readings against behavioural cooperation and independent performance, separately, never average them into one verdict (F3).
3. Treat the four as bonds. Report arrangements, not ingredient lists (F4).
4. Re-census when the group or institution changes; carry no standing list of trust builders (F5).
5. Instrument the middle link before recommending a lever (F6).
6. State the `gap` out loud and ask for a refutation, not belief (F7, F8).
7. Use the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, one to one. "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." (Fig 9 :60)

## 7. Updated verdict after canonical check

The shape survives: the four conditions are a union of overlapping conditions indexed per institution and group slot; they compose through typed bonds whose arrangement is the substance; their valence is relative to the residual and the habitat; the anchor is behaviour and independent performance, not the survey reading; the field's unmeasured middle link is the vocabulary-to-objective step; and the winning configuration in a named institution is a `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument settles it. Strains after re-census: one, named above. No figure quote was found to have drifted from `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
