## Three findings that bear directly on the residual

1. **The form/meaning separator is already a published position.** Bender and Koller 2020 argue that *"a system trained only on form has a priori no way to learn meaning."* This corroborates F1 to F3. It comes from NLP, a field with genuine contact with the claim, and is not part of the framework's evidence pool. It does not by itself settle the question, because the paper's target is the inference from form to meaning, while the figures ask a sharper question about what an anchor is.
2. **The missing pretext is a structural, not a volume, problem.** The tacit knowledge essay says writing carries context well, subtext partly and pretext almost never, and that a model trained on the record *"learns a statistical stand-in for the history and sounds fluent."* It adds the two-way correction loop from Clark and Brennan 1991: a puzzled look is the correction. This corroborates F10 and gives the social option its mechanism. Overlap with the figures is maximal (same author), so it is canonical reading, not an independent witness.
3. **The text-alone side has a serious classical defence.** Firth's distributional hypothesis says a word is known by the company it keeps, and distributional semantics has had considerable success. This corroborates F4's convention half and is logged in `09` as a source that *corroborates one domain and conflicts with the naive reading of another*. The conflict was returned to the census and dissolved by splitting convention from verdict (Fig 2 :39-41); it did not flip the answer.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the residual and its refuter first. The framework's interiors come later, and only if asked for.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 8 :53** *"Residual first, frame second."* Describe the effect before naming a field, otherwise any field will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).
- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so it may be given away. The conclusion is not a generator.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and **Fig 2 :41** *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Do not select recipients for receptiveness, and do not pool their later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :60** *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A journal's impact factor or a venue's prestige is a filter for where to spend the attempt.

**Recommended order in a reply.** (1) The three-condition shape and the pre-registered refuter. (2) Ask what the recipient would count as a failure, and what instrument they would use. (3) Record that verdict. (4) Only then offer the links, noting that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. (5) If asked, offer the formula as a method with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1:** the residual and the refuter, with no theory. The recipient checks the separator without holding the framework's interior.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, the three source links. Their agreement at this point is never pooled as evidence, because they now hold the frame.

**Stage 3, method only:** the formula for reuse, with the standing instruction that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question (Formula step "Direction") and that each user must state a mode and a refuter.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 separator survives. If it does not, the correct outcome is a recorded refutation in `11_audit.md`, not a better pitch.
