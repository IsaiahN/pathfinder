# The Tether's answer: text, convention and grounding

**Mode: `specified` on the domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the discriminator. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a language model can develop grounded meanings through text alone, or whether sensory and social experience is necessary. In effect terms the residual is: a term can be fluent, useful and widely agreed without anything in the arrangement registering whether the term is settled by a ground the frame cannot move. The question bundles two quantities under one word, "meaning": shared conventional meaning and anchored meaning. Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

## 2. The shape

1. **A declared meaning is not a grounded one.** Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :19: *"→ install what can be violated"*. Grounding is an anchor property, not a property of form or fluency.
2. **Text is an import source, not a meaning anchor.** Fig 6 :10 lists *"nature · corpus · peer frame"* as external sources, so a corpus imports atoms. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* A corpus is an anchor for which strings were written, and only an instrument for what a term means in a new situation. The first-pass claim that text adds no atom is retracted in `01` §7 R1.
3. **Convention and grounding are two quantities with different anchors.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Text-built systems can be excellent at the first and have no standing on the second. Both verdicts are kept; neither is averaged into "understanding."
4. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Scaling a text-only model changes the agent, not the contact. A text-only benchmark is a substituted habitat: Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
5. **What grounding needs is a shape, not a sensor.** The shape has three parts: a commitment that can be violated, an anchor that is constitutively about meaning and does not update on the frame, and contact with a closure that differs on that question so a correction can cross (Fig 10 :19, Fig 2 :29, Fig 6 :23). Sensory and social experience are candidate bindings for those effects. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
6. **Social contact only works when the closures differ.** Fig 8 :49: *"completely are one frame wearing two names."* A partner who shares the model's corpus supplies a convention, not a correction. Fig 10 :80: *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained"*; Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that language models produce nothing. They import atoms, compose bonds and sustain conventions.
- Not that scale can never help. It cannot supply an anchor on its own (Fig 11 :40). An agent-only change that moved a ground reading would refute that claim.
- Not that a benchmark score, a fluency judgement or user agreement is a grounding test. Those are frame-internal or conventional quantities (Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :40).
- Not that sensory and social experience is the unique necessary condition. That is a name where the figure returns a shape, and no verbatim line licenses it.
- Not that the missing discriminator might not exist. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The `gap` is an honest stop, not an absence claim.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it, and the standing refuter

The figures answer only the shape. A concrete model needs a ground reading. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, the needed thing is a discriminator: an instrument that reads the same output and can tell whether several sources produced it. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* So the search is for a proto-instrument already failing to resolve, not for a sensor specified in advance.

The shape claim is withdrawn if any pre-registered refuter in `01` §5 fires: a text-only term settled by a constitutively meaningful non-updating anchor; a text-immanent meaning anchor that is not form or agreement; an agent-only improvement moving a ground reading; convention shown inseparable from verdict; identical-evidence frames producing a genuine ground correction; or a verbatim figure line naming a required sensorium.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and it cannot name a missing primitive. The six-term substrate and its figures are a careful guess at compression axes, not nature's own list. The answer stops where the available instruments stop discriminating, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that text is empty or that language models understand nothing. It says that text is an import channel and a convention generator: by Fig 6 :10 a corpus supplies atoms, and by Fig 2 :41 agreement about a shared meaning is the product for a convention. It also says that a declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :49), because grounding needs an anchor that is about meaning and does not move when the model speaks (Fig 2 :29, :33). Convention and grounding are therefore two quantities with different anchors, so the question's either/or is a union, not a partition. What grounding requires is a shape: a commitment that can be violated, an unmoved anchor, and a differing closure through which a correction can cross. Sensory and social contact are candidate bindings for that shape, not names the framework derives. Scaling the agent alone does not change contact (Fig 11 :40), and a text-only benchmark proves wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70). No instrument here separates a grounded term from a fluent one, so that part is reported as a gap, not as silence and not as an answer. The shape, its refuters and the missing instrument are what we offer; a ground reading is what would close it.
