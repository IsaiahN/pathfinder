# Expert verification packet: the Tether on grounded meaning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in language, cognition or AI can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the discriminator. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Text is an import channel and a convention generator, not a meaning anchor. A corpus imports atoms by Fig 6 :10, and shared agreement about a word is the product for a convention by Fig 2 :41. But a declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :49): grounding needs a commitment that can be violated, an anchor that is constitutively about meaning and does not update on what the model outputs, and contact with a closure that differs on that question so a correction can cross. Convention and grounding are therefore two quantities with different anchors, and the question's either/or is a union, not a partition. Sensory and social experience are candidate bindings for those effects; the framework returns the shape, not the name. Scaling the agent alone does not change contact (Fig 11 :40), and a text-only benchmark proves wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70). No instrument here separates a grounded term from a fluent one, so that part is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10 and 2

- **Statement:** a term declared from inside a frame is not thereby grounded. Grounding is an anchor property: the anchor must not change its answer because of what the frame thinks, and the question must be constitutively about it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :19 *"→ install what can be violated"*; Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when"*; Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*; Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** is there any reading of these lines on which a term could be grounded without a non-updating anchor? The canonical figures README adds that the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* for a missing thing, which reinforces the refusal to name a substitute.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a term settled by a constitutively meaningful anchor that does not update on the frame and has actually refused at least one use, with no sensorimotor or social channel. If shown, C1 fails.

### C2. Framework-internal with a physics-of-language check; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 2

- **Statement:** a corpus is a listed import source, so text can add atoms; but corpus agreement anchors form, not meaning. Agreement about a shared meaning is efficient for a convention and disqualifying as evidence for a verdict about the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :10 *"nature · corpus · peer frame"*; Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*; Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*
- **What to check:** Fig 8 :32 governs composition inside a fixed closure, not training. If a reader uses it to claim that text adds no content, they are applying it to the wrong object; the re-census is recorded in `01` §7 R1. Also check that the two clauses of Fig 2 are opposite valences of one independence parameter, not a contradiction.
- **Refuter:** show that convention agreement settles a world-facing question without any independent anchor and that the two quantities are inseparable by any instrument. Or show that Fig 6 does not list corpus as a source. Either breaks C2's separator.

### C3. Capability and habitat; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 6

- **Statement:** scaling a text-only model changes the agent, not the contact, so it cannot by itself supply grounding. A text-only benchmark is a substituted habitat. Social contact helps only when the partner's closure differs and independence is renewed; otherwise it is a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*; Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*; Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*; Fig 10 :80 *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained"*; Fig 8 :49 *"completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- **What to check:** Fig 11 :71 says the isolation law and the capability claim *"fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they"* can come back wrong. That caveat is part of C3, not an omission. Ask for an agent-only improvement that moved a ground reading; that is the named refuter.
- **Refuter:** an agent-only change with contact held fixed that changes a ground-verified reading; or two frames with demonstrably identical evidence producing a correction neither could produce alone with no third anchor.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that language models produce nothing, or that text cannot import atoms (Fig 6 :10 says it can).
- Not that sensorimotor or social contact is the unique necessary condition. No verbatim figure line names a sensorium; the figures return a shape (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that scale can never help. Only that an agent-only improvement does not change contact (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that a benchmark, a fluency rating or user agreement is a grounding test. Those are produced by the frame or shared by the agreeing frames (Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the missing discriminator is impossible. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* It is unreached, reported as `gap`.
- Not that this packet is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation.
- Not a theory of everything. The question stays a compound with its own loop.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Is there a reading that separates a term whose standing is settled by a non-updating ground from one whose standing is settled by form or user agreement, on the *same* output? If not, the F4 and F11 gaps stand.
2. Does an agent-only change, with contact held fixed, move any ground-verified reading? If yes, C3 is refuted.
3. Is there any text-immanent anchor that is neither a form distribution nor user agreement nor another text source? If yes, C1 and C2 are refuted.
4. Can convention agreement and ground verdict be separated by an instrument? If not, the union reading in Q1 of `06` is refuted.
