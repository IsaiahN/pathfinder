# Outreach draft  -  check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. Nothing here asks for trust, agreement, or endorsement.

---

**Subject:** One claim to check: does "adapt without concentrating" split into two conditions?

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An economy faces a technological change. Two things can fail: it cannot absorb the change, or it absorbs it while the gain is captured by one group. The question asks for institutional conditions that close both. The output claims these are **two conditions whose intersection is a different case**, not one problem with a qualifier attached. A lever that raises adoption while allowing concentration clears the first condition and leaves the second binding.
>
> **The shape claim (`general`, not `grounded`).** Five things follow before any institution is named:
>
> 1. Gains have to be read per slot. An aggregate such as total output growth cannot register concentration, because aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.
> 2. The measurement channel is the vulnerable part. It can pool readings, saturate at the top, or be captured by someone who benefits from the verdict. The ground does not decay; the channel does.
> 3. Capability is a property of the economy and its habitat, not of firms in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing.
> 4. A technology's distributional effects cascade beyond first contact, so an institution is permanent work, not a solved design.
> 5. Arrangement carries what a list of institutions omits. Two economies with the same formal rules can be different substances.
>
> This is a shape, not a list. I am explicitly **not** claiming to know which named institutions close both conditions. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before contacting you.**
>
> 1. Show that the two conditions are separable over a stated institutional set, so one lever clears both.
> 2. Show that a per-slot gain distribution is a deterministic function of the aggregate, so slot indexing adds nothing.
> 3. Exhibit an economy where an agent-only improvement moved the ground.
> 4. Show that all distributional effects of a technology are first order and the habitat needs no maintenance.
> 5. Exhibit two economies with identical formal rules and identical outcomes under every arrangement.
>
> If any holds, the corresponding part of the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not asking:** I am not asking you to trust me, to endorse the framework, or to say you agree. I am asking what you would count as a failure. Your verdict on this output is recorded before any theory is shown, and I will not treat later agreement as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the third link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution rules

- One to one. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line.
- Public institutional or opt-in channels only (see `03_public_contact_points.csv`). No personal emails harvested from private posts.
- Record the recipient's verdict *before* showing the source links. Never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source (Fig 9 :51: *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*).
