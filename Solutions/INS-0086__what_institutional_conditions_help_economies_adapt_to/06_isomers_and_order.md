## RESIDUAL

The question asks for institutional conditions such that two things hold at once: an economy absorbs a technological change, and the gain is not captured by one slot. Written as an ordinary term before the run (formula, "Direction"): a set of conditions C such that, under C, `R_adapt` trends down and the per-slot residual of `R_dist` does not concentrate in one slot. That binding is written down so it can accrue a residual and be refused like anything else. It is not itself a figure line.

Describe R before choosing any figure line (Fig 9 :21 to :22): arity two, symmetry broken because `R_dist` is indexed by slot and any sum over slots destroys the signal (Fig 1 :19), scale local to economy and back to slot, anchor an instrument that reads both components and does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29, :33). Option A treats the question as one lever and reports success when aggregate output rises. Option B treats it as a union compound and requires both components to close against an anchor. A yields a metric that hides the thing asked about; B yields a shape with a stated refuter and an open name.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Institutions are bonded arrangements, not ingredient lists (mode: `general`)

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :14: **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**. Fig 12 :15 to :16: **"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost"**.

The usual answer to the question is an ingredient list: property rights, education, competition policy, social protection, active labour-market programmes. Under Fig 12 that list records half of itself. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :95 to :96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."** This is not an analogy imported from chemistry for decoration. Fig 12 :97 to :98: **"This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."**

Apply to economies (`specified`): two economies with the same formal rules can be different substances, because the bond that joins them is not in the list. The bonds the framework names include sequence (→), production (⇒), subtraction (−), comparison (⋛), conjunction (+), disjunction (∥) and identity (≡), and the Operators table adds negation (¬) as a unary operator that is not a bond. The relevant institutional differences are therefore things like: does training precede the technology or follow it (sequence); does the rule create a new capability or merely fund an existing one (production); is the gain defined as a threshold comparison between slots (⋛); is the protective floor a subtraction from a captured baseline (−). None of these is recoverable from the list of instruments.

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two economies that differ only in bond are isomers in the figure's sense. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** The question asks for a route (conditions that produce adaptation without concentration), so an ingredient-only vocabulary cannot even write the answer.

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** An institution such as an independent statistical office is a catalyst-like part if it survives the political use it is put to; if it is spent, it behaves differently across uses. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Gains that fund the channel that measures the gains are a cycle; the sign decides whether it closes or captures. Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Social protection is a functional-group-like substructure, not a named institution; the qualifier says which member. These four structures are the figure's own list; applying them to economies is `specified`.

**Price of the arrangement.** Fig 12 :100 to :101: **"The price of an arrangement is the choice it records, so it is logarithmic in the number of bonds available. With one bond type the term is zero, and it rises as the vocabulary of bonds grows, and only then. A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."** Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."** An institutional description priced only in bits (the list) and not in actions (the cost of finding out whether the bond holds) will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. `specified` on the economics reading; the two-currency claim is `general`.

### Q2. Order by dependency, and why the first condition is a term and an instrument (mode: `general`)

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :24: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** Fig 3 :25 to :26: **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Most adaptation policy lands above the break: skills, R&D, infrastructure, all with existing measurements (Fig 3 :29 to :30). Those improve links that already work. The link that lacks an instrument is the one that states what a technology affords to whom and what would count as a broadly shared gain. By Q1 and by F2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` that is the per-slot reading. Fig 3 :7: **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"**; Fig 3 :10: **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"**.

The order, stated so it can be refused:

1. Build or name a per-slot gain instrument that can resolve the top of the distribution (D2, Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 10 :24 to :29).
2. State terms for what the technology affords and what would count as broad gain (Fig 3 :7, :10).
3. Bind the objective as an ordinary term, both conditions, so it accrues a residual (formula, "Direction").
4. Only then choose rules and planning: habitat enumeration (Fig 11 :43 to :44), differing rooms (Fig 7 :35 to :36), import from a differing closure (Fig 6 :23 to :24).
5. Verify against the ground, never against agreement (Fig 2 :35, :40; Fig 10 :42).

Fig 7 :12 to :13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**. A chain of agencies that applies one policy uniformly gets motion without progress. Fig 3 :29 to :30: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

### Q3. The union is unbundled before it is searched (mode: `general`)

Fig 9 :55 to :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** Fig 9 :57 to :58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

So "adapt without concentrating" is not adaptation with a soft qualifier. Institutions that raise adoption while allowing incumbents to capture the gain clear the first condition and leave the second binding. The union must be unbundled before a frame is chosen (Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**), because Fig 9 :27 to :28 warns: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. The residue after unbundling is smaller and describable; that is its second-order value (Fig 9 :59).

### Q4. Analogues and where they stop (mode: `specified` for each mapping)

- **Chemistry / isomerism.** Fig 12 :97 to :98 above is literal chemistry. Mapping it to economies is an analogue. What carries over is the structure: same atoms, different bond, different substance. What does not carry over is any claim that a named economy is an isomer of another named economy without a stated atom set and a stated bond. `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Ecology / habitat.** Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Applied to economies: capability belongs to the economy and its habitat, not to firms in isolation. The Lundvall (2022) innovation-systems entry and the Resilience Alliance page corroborate this from outside the framework (see `09_watson_answer.md`, WORLD). `specified`.
- **Rooms / selection.** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :42: **"The next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."** A room is a frame, not a policy jurisdiction. The institutional reading is `specified`; the figure governs selection between differing frames.
- **Membrane / scale.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** A local adaptation becomes an economy-wide capability only as a reapplicable method; a recording of one firm's success is not that. `specified`.
- **Mechanisms / import.** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence."** Openness expands what is reachable; copying a live peer's exact arrangement spends the witness that made the comparison meaningful. `specified`.

**Retraction kept visible.** The earlier mapping "technology is the Gradient, institutions are the Network" is withdrawn as figure-derived. Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Fig 13 governs the six primitives, not economic institutions; it constrains any composition claim but names no institution.

## FALSIFIER

Written before the conclusion (a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument):

1. Exhibit two economies with identical formal ingredients and identical outcomes under every arrangement. Then the bond carries nothing and Q1 is withdrawn.
2. Exhibit a case where the binding constraint is demonstrably planning and that constraint has a working instrument while vocabulary and objective are already in place. Then Q2's middle-link priority is withdrawn.
3. Show that over a stated institutional set the adaptation and non-concentration conditions are separable, so one lever clears both and the intersection is empty. Then Q3 and the union reading in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 are withdrawn.
4. Show that every mapping in Q4 is not an analogue but a figure-derived name for an economic institution. Then the `specified` labels are wrong and the derivation has overreached.
5. Exhibit an agent-only improvement that moves the ground for an economy. Fig 11 :72 states this falsifier itself: **"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."**

If any fires, the corresponding sub-claim is withdrawn. Until an instrument closes both components against an anchor, no application here may be labelled `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

What survives is structure: institutions are bonded arrangements rather than ingredient lists; the first institutional condition is a term and an instrument for the objective, ordered by dependency before spending programmes; the question is a union compound whose intersection must be unbundled; capability belongs to the economy and its habitat; and contact with differing closures is the only operator that moves what is reachable. What does not survive is any list of named institutions presented as the figure's answer. The figures return a kind of thing, and the named list is a binding from the economics frame, `specified`, unmeasured, and owed the refuter above.
