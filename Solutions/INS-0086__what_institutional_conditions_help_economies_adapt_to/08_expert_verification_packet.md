## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which institutional conditions help economies adapt to technological change without concentrating its gains" is a union compound, not one problem with a qualifier attached. Two conditions must close at once: the economy absorbs the technique, and the gain does not concentrate in one slot. A lever that raises adoption while allowing incumbents to capture the gain clears the first condition and leaves the second binding (Fig 9 :55 to :56). Three things follow before any institution is named: gains must be read per slot because an aggregate discards the live signal (Fig 1 :19, :22); the measurement channel decays and must be kept reachable and unowned (Fig 10 :24, :29, :93; Fig 2 :33); and capability belongs to the economy and its habitat, with arrangement carrying what an ingredient list omits (Fig 11 :39 to :40; Fig 12 :5, :117). The first institutional condition is therefore a term and an instrument for the objective, ordered ahead of spending programmes (Fig 3 :24 to :27). The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32), so no named institution is claimed as the answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The question is a union compound with a per-slot distribution condition (`general`)

- **Statement:** adaptation and non-concentration are two conditions whose intersection is a different case; the distribution condition needs a per-slot instrument, not an aggregate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 to :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** Fig 9 :57 to :58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- **What to check:** take a technology and an institutional set you know. Is there a lever that raises adoption but leaves the gain concentrated? If yes, the two conditions do not close together. Does the available gain statistic resolve the top of the distribution, or is it an average?
- **Refuter:** show that over a stated institutional set the two conditions are separable, so one lever clears both and the intersection is empty; or exhibit a case in which the per-slot gain distribution is a deterministic function of the aggregate, so slot indexing adds no information. If either holds, C1 is withdrawn.

### C2. The measurement channel is the vulnerable institutional quantity (`general`)

- **Statement:** the ground does not decay, but the channel that reads it does; an institution whose reputation depends on a favourable verdict is disqualified as an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 10 :25 to :26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**. Fig 10 :29: **"keep the anchor reachable"**. Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**
- **What to check:** can the gain instrument pool readings or saturate at the top? Does the body that publishes it gain from a favourable reading? The Our World in Data page (https://ourworldindata.org/economic-inequality, accessed 2026-11-23) records that survey measures *"likely understate the true level of income concentration at the top"* and that methodology changes create breaks in comparability, a channel failure in this sense.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a channel whose readings cannot degrade, cannot be pooled, cannot saturate, and cannot be captured; or show that the economic ground itself decays, which contradicts Fig 10 :24. If either holds, C2 is withdrawn.

### C3. Capability is a property of the economy and its habitat, and arrangement carries information the list omits (`general`; the mappings are `specified`)

- **Statement:** there is no standing list of good institutions; enumerate the habitat in contact with this residual, and note that two economies with the same formal rules can be different substances because the bond is not in the ingredient list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 11 :72 states the falsifier: **"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."**
- **What to check:** is there an institutional list that keeps both conditions closed across a change of residual? If not, the list form fails. Find two economies with the same formal rules but different arrangements and different outcomes; if they behave identically under every arrangement, the bond carries nothing.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two economies with identical formal ingredients and identical outcomes under every arrangement; or produce an agent-only improvement that moves the ground for an economy. If either holds, C3 is withdrawn.

**Ordering claim, checked with C1 to C3:** the middle link comes first. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Refuter: show a case where the binding constraint is demonstrably planning and that constraint has a working instrument while vocabulary and objective are already in place.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot gain distribution with top-resolution, run on a named technology in a named habitat, with methodology breaks declared.
2. An adaptation reading that is not frame-internal: Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**
