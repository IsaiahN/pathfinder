## 0. The residual in effect terms

> Does public borrowing crowd out private investment, or can it support private activity by raising demand and income?

Restated as an effect: the quantity in dispute is the response of private investment `I` to deficit-financed public borrowing `G`. Frame A (loanable funds, real resource competition) predicts a negative response through the interest rate and the cost of capital. Frame B (aggregate demand, multiplier and accelerator) predicts a positive response through income and capacity utilisation. A third condition, forward-looking saving (Ricardian equivalence and the Lucas regime question), predicts that the response depends on expectations and on the policy regime itself.

**R:** the sign and magnitude of `dI/dG`, indexed by the economic state in which `G` occurs.

- **Arity:** two named conditions about one quantity, plus an expectations modifier. Not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The two channels run in different currencies and in different directions. Their comparison is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join.
- **Scale:** micro (firm and household decisions), meso (the market for loanable funds, aggregate demand), macro (general equilibrium and the business cycle), intertemporal (expectations of future taxes and policy rules).

**Sought-for shape, bound before the run:** an answer is a function from regime slots to a sign and magnitude, not a single number. This is the ordinary term that the loop can let accrue a residual (formula step "Direction").

## 2b. Findings

### F1 - The question as posed aggregates across slots (decisive for the shape)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

The object slots here are the economic states: full employment, slack, zero lower bound, normal monetary policy, open economy with floating rates, high-debt economy. A single "does it crowd out or in" averages over them. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." So an average effect near zero, or an average that looks like crowding out, does not kill a live crowding-in effect in the slack slot.

**Verdict per domain:** D4 (regime index) is `triangulated` across Fig 1 (slot indexing) and Fig 9 (union unbundling, below), two figures with low overlap. The universal-sign reading is REFUTED as a figure-licensed shape.

### F2 - This is a union compound, so unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The crowding-out condition and the crowding-in condition are simultaneously present in most actual borrowings. They do not form a list of separate questions, and a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds while leaving the intersection standing. The intersection, an economy with slack where the central bank still leans against the expansion, is the specific compound worth studying, not a harder version of either channel.

### F3 - The two channels are bonds (routes), not competing ingredients

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Both frames name the same ingredients: G, I, the interest rate, income. They differ in the bond. The crowding-out route is a production-like chain from borrowing through the price of credit to investment. The crowding-in route is a chain from spending through income and capacity utilisation to investment. A model that records only the ingredients cannot tell the two apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost. This is why the question cannot be settled by a regression containing both `G` and `r` as right-hand-side terms and calling the coefficient "the effect".

**Verdict per domain:** D1 and D2 are `triangulated` for the route requirement (Fig 12 bond logic plus Fig 11 valence logic, below). The magnitude in each slot is `single-source` for the figure derivation and stays ungrounded until data settles it.

### F4 - The same borrowing changes valence with the regime

Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Public borrowing is not intrinsically expansive or intrinsically contractionary for private investment. Its valence is relative to the residual and to the regime. At capacity, or when monetary policy offsets the fiscal impulse, it is a malfactor to private investment. With slack and an accommodative or constrained central bank, it is a benefactor. This is a structural claim from the figures and it predicts the sign flip that the world literature reports.

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The relevant effect on investment can be several links out: deficit, rate, credit conditions, balance sheets, then capital expenditure. A first-order-only study is a scoped habitat, not a refutation.

### F5 - Two frames with differing closures are needed; agreement inside one is one witness

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."
Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

The loanable-funds frame and the demand frame have different closures. Running more regressions inside either one cannot manufacture the other's atom. The synthesis requires their low-overlap union, aimed at a described residual: Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."

A caution that matters here. Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Both schools now accept that a spending impulse has a larger output effect in slack with a bound policy rate. If they reached that through different mechanisms and different data, that is convergent derivation and genuinely counts. If they quote the same meta-analysis, it is one evidence pool.

### F6 - The ground is the anchor, not the school

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

For this question the anchor is an identified empirical response, not an authoritative economist. Fiscal errors affect employment and capital formation, so the cost of being wrong is high and independence must be bought in proportion. A consensus among readers of the same textbook is a shared pool talking.

### F7 - A universal sign fails the acceptance test

Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."
Fig 5 :44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."

A term saying "public borrowing always crowds out" explains the full-employment occasion and leaves the slack occasion unexplained. A term saying "it depends" explains everything by saying nothing unless it is bound to the regime index and to a direction of effect. The state-contingent function pays the bargain: it states the sign per slot and leaves less unexplained than either universal. This is MDL applied to the policy question, and it is a `specified` claim, not a measurement.

### F8 - Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

School affiliation, prestige, tier, citation count and receptiveness are filters for which work to read first. They cannot decide whether crowding out dominates. This is the rule that keeps the outreach artifacts in this folder honest: the verdict comes from the identified ground reading, never from who is approached.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is: the effect of public borrowing on private investment is a regime-indexed sign and magnitude, and no single sign governs all slots. It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An identified ground reading returns the same sign and a statistically indistinguishable magnitude across all enumerated regime slots (full employment, slack, zero lower bound, normal policy, open and closed, high and low debt).
2. A derivation from the figures shows that aggregating across slots preserves the live signal that Fig 1 :22 says is lost, that is, the slot indexing in Fig 1 is inapplicable to economic regimes.
3. The union reading in Fig 9 :54 is shown to be wrong for this case: the two conditions form a partition, not overlapping conditions, so no compound residue exists.
4. A ground reading shows the interest-rate route and the income route are the same bond, so Fig 12 :111 does not distinguish them here.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and its refuter, not with a school. Ask which regime slot a proposed estimate belongs to.
2. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53), so a picked frame cannot be made to seem to fit.
3. Treat the two channels as a low-overlap union and keep their verdicts separate. Do not average a crowding-out estimate and a crowding-in estimate into a "net effect".
4. Bind the regime index explicitly. An estimate without its slot is a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).
5. Send the generator, the residual, the refuter and the state-contingent shape, not a single conclusion.
