# The Tether's answer on public borrowing and private investment

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rule. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the sign of `dI/dG`: does deficit-financed public borrowing reduce private investment, or raise it through demand and income? The two predictions come from frames with different closures, and the question bundles them into one either/or. The honest residual is the sign and magnitude of that response **indexed by the economic state**, not a single coefficient. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

## 2. The shape

1. **The residual lives in slots, not in an average.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The slots here are full employment, slack, the zero lower bound or a bound policy rate, normal policy, open versus closed economy, and high versus low debt. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." So an average showing little effect is compatible with a strong effect in one slot.
2. **The channels are distinct routes, not ingredients.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The credit route (`G → r → I`) and the income route (`G ⇒ Y → I`) share atoms but differ in the bond. An ingredient-only regression cannot distinguish them (Fig 12 :117 isomer).
3. **The borrowing changes valence with the regime.** Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." At capacity or with an offsetting central bank the response is negative; with slack and an accommodative or bound policy rate it is positive. There is no standing list of expansive or contractionary borrowings (Fig 11 :51).
4. **The regime crossing is a threshold, not a smooth average.** Operators table: ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities". Whether spare capacity exceeds the impulse, and whether the policy constraint binds, decides which route governs. This is a comparison, ordered, not a conjunction.
5. **No single sign governs.** A universal "always crowds out" or "always crowds in" fails the acceptance test (Fig 5 :44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly"). The state-contingent function is the shape; a universal sign is REFUTED as written.
6. **The verdict comes from a ground, not a school.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fiscal mistakes are costly, so independence is bought with identified readings, not with consensus.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single number, a net effect, or an average across regimes.
- Not that "it depends" is an answer on its own. The slots and the routes must be stated.
- Not that a particular empirical study is correct. No estimate was recomputed and no dataset was opened here.
- Not that the 13-figure substrate literally maps onto macro quantities. That analogue is recorded and strained (F10 in `01`).
- Not that agreement among economists settles the question. A shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. Every substantive claim above is `specified` or `general`.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

The world literature already points to the discriminating instrument, and it is a reading, not a further argument:

- **Per-slot identified responses.** The IMF admission quoted in the opened Fiscal multiplier page reports multipliers in the 0.9 to 1.7 range after the Great Recession, well above the 0.5 previously used, in an environment of "substantial economic slack, monetary policy constrained by the zero lower bound". That is a ground reading inside a slot, not a universal law.
- **Route discrimination.** Does the response move with the cost of credit when the policy rate is free, and with income when it is bound? An instrument that separates those two would name the route. Until then, a single coefficient is a projection that cannot comment on its source (from the opened essay "The Hard Problem is About Source").
- **Expectations.** Lucas (1976): "any change in policy will systematically alter the structure of econometric models." A stable coefficient across a change in the policy rule would refute the regime-indexed shape.
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** same sign and indistinguishable magnitude in every slot; proof that slot aggregation preserves the signal; proof that the two routes are one bond; proof that the split is a partition, not a union.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the magnitude.** The per-slot constraint and the union reading are figure-derived and corroborated by low-overlap fields. The sign in any particular slot, and the size of the effect, belong to an identified ground reading that was not taken here. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence": a single universal sign is a `gap` after a genuine re-census, and that is the report, not an omission. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in the crowding-out debate. It says the question is bundled: the response of private investment to public borrowing has to be read in regime slots, because the framework indexes residuals per object and says that averaging across slots throws away the signal. Two distinct routes share almost the same ingredients, a cost-of-credit route and an income-and-demand route, and they are isomers in the figure's sense, invisible in an ingredient-only regression. The same borrowing helps investment in a slump with a bound policy rate and hurts it at capacity or when the central bank leans against the impulse, because valence is relative to the regime, not a property of the borrowing. The regime crossing is a threshold, not a smooth average. What the framework offers is that shape, the three refuters written in advance, and the reading that would close it. The magnitude stays open until a ground settles it.
