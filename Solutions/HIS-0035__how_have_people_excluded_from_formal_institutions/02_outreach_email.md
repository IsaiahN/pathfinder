**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the strained readings in F7 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are understood and the distinction between a frame crossing (creation) and a scale crossing (preservation) is kept. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One claim about excluded knowledge and its refuter, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: how people excluded from formal institutions created and preserved knowledge. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I have one output and one failure condition. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** Take the institution as a frame with a library of terms it can express. Two things follow from the figures I am using.
>
> 1. A closed frame cannot manufacture a term it does not already hold. New atoms arrive only through contact with a frame whose closure differs. So the knowledge an excluded group holds is an **import** into the institution, not a derivation from inside it, and what separates an import from an independent rediscovery is the record of where it came from.
> 2. Preservation is not a property of an isolated person. It is maintained contact with a ground that does not decay, carried by a lineage and a habitat. What crosses between generations is a **method that can be reapplied**, not the recording of one success. The institution's channel decays; the ground does not.
>
> This is the **shape** of an answer, not a name. It does not say who, when, or where. Those are historical questions and this output does not settle them.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that any particular recovered figure proves the shape; that a list of famous names measures preserved knowledge; that absence of a record proves absence of knowledge; or that the institution was merely inattentive. Inattention is not the mechanism. The mechanism is an import across a boundary, with provenance.
>
> **What would refute the shape (written before this message):**
> 1. The knowledge was fully derivable inside the institution's own library, with no contact with a differing frame.
> 2. Preservation is a property of the isolated knower, shown by an agent-only change that moved the ground.
> 3. Provenance is not needed, because convergent derivation and adopted import can be told apart from their contents alone.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a result contrived to fit the formula is no test of it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
