# Solution HIS-0035 - figure-grounded package

**Question:** How have people excluded from formal institutions created and preserved knowledge?

**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 44 (question 35), section 4, Knowledge, power, and exclusion.

**Field for public contact points:** marginalized knowledge research.

**Mode:** `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural rules (import is the only operator that moves a frame's wall; capability belongs to agent-and-habitat, never to the isolated agent). No historical case is made `grounded` by the figures. World sources are secondary corroboration only.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry pass, not by this folder).

## What this folder does and does not claim

The figures are primary and decide. They do not tell a historical narrative and they do not name a single person or event. What they govern is the **shape** of the answer: creation is an import from a frame whose closure differs, and preservation is the maintenance of contact with a non-decaying ground through a lineage and a habitat while the institution's own channel decays. The named cases in `09_watson_answer.md` come from world sources and are witnesses only, never the verdict.

This is a **simulated derivation**, checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figure text. It is not a ground reading and it cannot settle the question it reports on. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the framework settles a historical dispute. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Three figure-grounded reasons for the refusals above:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
2. **The verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So stage 1 carries the residual and the refuter, not the theory.
3. **A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.** Fig 2 :43. The public venues in `03` are routes for a claim to be tried, not committees to endorse it.

Legally: public opt-in channels avoid the failure mode of scraping personal addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach (GDPR/CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions), which is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
