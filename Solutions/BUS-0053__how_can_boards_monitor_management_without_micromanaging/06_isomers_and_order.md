# Structure and order: the four seats, the two failure conditions, and what may cross

**Mode: `specified` on the board instance, `general` on the structural claims.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The board question is often written as a flat list of good practices (independent directors, audit committee, whistleblower line, KPI review). The derivation owes an account of **structure and order**: which relations hold between the parts, which condition fails first, and what may legitimately cross between the parts. Three questions are owed: (1) is this a sequence or a set; (2) are "information capture" and "micromanagement" isomers or a union; (3) what crosses the membrane, and in which direction?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: management as maker (Seat 1), the audit function as interior auditor (Seat 2), the board as ground maintainer (Seat 3), external frames as outside witnesses (Seat 4), the ground of realized outcomes, and the board pack as Γ. Bonds held: the seven, including `→` sequence and `⋛` comparison, plus negation. No new atom is available without import. The anchor is the realized outcome, which does not move on being pushed (Fig 2 :29-30).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the arrangement a sequence or a set?

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** (the sequence bond).
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Seat Map :45 to :145 sets out the four seats in a fixed dependency: the maker produces the artifact; the auditor reads the trail; the maintainer keeps the anchor reachable and routes; the outside frame reads reports without seeing what they describe.
- Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

The arrangement is a **sequence with a branch**, not a set. The order is: describe the residual → install a reading of the ground → range management's report against it → rule with provenance → refresh the checking frame. A board that buys a new dashboard before it can describe the residual has spent budget above the break (Fig 3 :29-30). Seat Map :93 gives the maintainer's first job: keep the anchor reachable, and report *"contact has been lost"* rather than a verdict about what the lost contact would have said.

The order matters because soundness is asymmetric. The checker must be unable to produce what it checks (Fig 10 :43). Swapping Seat 1 and Seat 3 turns the arrangement into one frame checking itself through intermediaries. The order is not a preference; it is the difference between verification and self-confirmation.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a board can rule independently on a decision it authored, repeatedly and against a ground, with no loss of outside view, the sequence is not load-bearing and Seat 3 collapses into Seat 1. Q1 falls.

### Q2: Are information capture and micromanagement isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

A board captured by management's information and a board that micromanages can look identical from outside: long meetings, detailed packs, many questions, a seemingly engaged chair. The symptoms are the same; the bonds are different. In one case the anchor updates on management's report (Fig 2 :33-34), so the board is accurately tracking a moved ground. In the other case the checker authors the atom it grades (Fig 10 :43), so the board's report is a description of its own plan.

By Fig 12 :117 these are **not isomers** in the strict sense, because they do not share the same atom set arranged differently; they fail at different links. They are two Members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124: a broken checking arrangement) with different qualifiers. More precisely, Fig 9 :57 makes the pair a **union compound**, not a harder case of either failure. A single lever, such as "add more independent directors", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

**Falsifier:** a single intervention that restores both an unmoved anchor and an outside seat in both failure types, with no categorically different residue, would collapse the union into a partition. Q2 falls.

### Q3: Two analogues, both as corroboration only

- **Control engineering (observability).** Åström and Murray, *Feedback Systems*: *"if a system is observable, it is possible to recover the state from measurements of the inputs and outputs to the system"*; *"If a system is observable, then there are no 'hidden' dynamics inside it"*. In board terms: if the board's sensor set observes only management's reports, the unobservable subspace contains exactly the divergence between report and outcome. More of the same report cannot reduce it. This predicts Fig 2 :31-32 from a different closure.
- **Cryptography (zero-knowledge).** Wikipedia: soundness means *"if the statement is false, then no cheating prover can convince an honest verifier"*, and the verifier learns nothing *"beyond the mere fact of that statement's truth"*. A board that demands full access to management's interiors has not strengthened verification; it has destroyed the soundness condition. Corroborates Fig 10 :42-44. Overlap is declared high: Fig 10 :84 already cites zero-knowledge proof systems.

**Falsifier:** an engineering demonstration that adding correlated copies of the same sensor output reduces state-estimation error, or a proof system whose soundness improves when the verifier can reconstruct the witness. Either would break the analogue, though not the figure line it is used to corroborate.

### Q4: What crosses the membrane, and in which direction?

- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
- Fig 4 :21: the membrane is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*
- Seat Map :173-178: upward, **generators only** (claims, receipts, named failure classes); downward, **priors only** (rulings arrive as decisions, not deliberations); sideways, mediated only.
- Fig 10 :45: **"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."**
- Fig 10 :46: **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."**

From management to the board should cross: named failure classes, receipts, the arrangement of the evidence (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information"*), and the residual itself. What should not cross as a substitute is the full interior, because a checker that reconstructs the claim cannot check it (Fig 10 :42). From the board to management should cross: conventions and rulings as decisions, not the board's deliberative model of the CEO. A board pack that contains only numbers and no arrangement is a flat formula; it records the ingredients and not the bonds, and nothing downstream can tell the changes apart (Fig 12 :14-17).

The transform is lossy, so the round trip has a gap: R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)) (Symbols table, `R_T`; Formula step 6). A board that treats its reporting template as a lossless copy of the firm is navigating by dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).

**Falsifier:** a report format that preserves every bond as well as every ingredient across the board/management seam, with R_T measured at zero. Under Fig 4 :27-29 the transform loses information by construction; if one did not, Q4 would need re-census.

## CONCLUSION

The checking arrangement is a **directed sequence of four seats**, not a flat list of practices. Its two failure conditions (anchor moving, checker authoring) are a **union compound** with the same outward symptoms and categorically different bonds, not isomers and not degrees of one failure. Control theory and zero-knowledge proofs corroborate the ordering from low-overlap fields. What crosses between management and the board is restricted by direction: generators up, priors down, conventions authored by the seat, never verdicts or interiors. Four falsifiers are stated above; none has been run, so none of this is `grounded`.
