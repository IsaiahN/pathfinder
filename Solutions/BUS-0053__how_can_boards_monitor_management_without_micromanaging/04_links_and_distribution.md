## 3. Wider literature from low-overlap fields

- **Control engineering, observability and state estimation.** Åström and Murray, *Feedback Systems*, chapter 8: *"if a system is observable, it is possible to recover the state from measurements of the inputs and outputs to the system"* and *"If a system is observable, then there are no 'hidden' dynamics inside it"* (https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/fbs-outputfbk_24Jul2020.pdf). This is the engineering shape of C1: a checker cannot estimate a state it has no sensors for, and asking the plant to report its own state is not an independent measurement. Stanford EE263, *Observability and state estimation*: *"null(O) gives ambiguity in determining state from input and output"* (https://ee263.stanford.edu/lectures/observ.pdf). Prediction: more reports from the same sensor do not reduce the unobservable subspace. Corroborates Fig 2 :31-32.
- **Economics and social measurement, Goodhart and Campbell.** Goodhart's original regularity and Strathern's paraphrase both predict that a monitoring metric loses information when it becomes a target (CNA report; Rodamar 2018 in *Significance*: *"The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures"*). Corroborates Fig 10 :47.
- **Auditing standards.** ISA 500 A6 (*"enquiry alone ordinarily does not provide sufficient audit evidence"*) is a professional version of the same refusal to accept management's account. Corroborates F1 and F2.
- **Cryptography.** Zero-knowledge proofs separate verification from access to the interior, corroborating Fig 10 :42-44. Overlap is declared high because the figure already names it.
- **Board scholarship.** The monitoring/advising tradeoff papers corroborate F10, but from the same pool and with the opposite emphasis: they worry about voluntary disclosure, while the figure worries about the checker's soundness. Both are recorded, neither is used to flip the other.

No world source conflicts with a figure verdict. Had one, the rule from the README would send the derivation back to the census, not to a softer reading.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends the interior to a potential verifier and spends the only thing they contribute.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A board that has absorbed the framework before giving its verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the article.
- **Fig 2 :35**, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Seat Map :163**, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A list selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two conditions and the refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own setting; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer, plus the reuse stage

1. **Stage 1:** check the shape against a ground. The recipient may use a board they know, an audit committee's records, or a public case. No framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** if curious, read the figures and formula. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode and names their own ground, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (Formula, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output can actually fail against a ground. The pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is what makes that possible. If a recipient cannot name a ground, do not send stages 2 or 3.
