# Expert verification packet: the Tether on board monitoring

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate-governance researcher, auditor or experienced director can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the board-design claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No board was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A board monitors management without micromanaging or being captured by management's reporting when it keeps a reading of realized outcomes that management cannot move, draws that reading from frames with low evidence overlap with management's own, refuses to author the decisions it grades, authors conventions (metric definitions, thresholds, evidence requirements) rather than verdicts, tracks the residual per decision area, admits evidence about the report's match to reality and not about its maker's standing, declares its own stakes and records rulings with provenance. Anchor-reachability and checker-independence are two conditions, not one score, and more of the first can destroy the second. The framework does not name a specific board structure; it returns the shape those choices must satisfy.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and ground-facing; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** management's report cannot be the anchor, and the board's reading of what happened must not move when management disputes it. Independence is low evidence overlap, not headcount.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :32 *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, identify the outcome the board actually used as its anchor. Did it change when management challenged it? Are the board's "independent" readings drawn from the same management-produced dataset?
- **Refuter:** the anchor moved on being pushed and the change was accepted as legitimate; or two channels sharing one management dataset produced independent information about the realized outcome.

### C2 - seat rule; checkable by Figure 10 and the Seat Map

- **Statement:** the board must stay outside the decisions it grades. Its legitimate authorship is conventions, not verdicts. Evidence about the maker (reputation, tenure, standing) is inadmissible.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43 *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom."* Fig 10 :45 *"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."* Fig 9 :51 *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*
- **What to check:** can the board produce a ground-checked grade of a decision it authored, with no loss of outside view? Does the escalation threshold still carry news after it becomes a target? Is the board grading reports against its own model of the CEO or against outcomes?
- **Refuter:** a board that routinely authors decisions and still grades them independently against a ground; or a threshold that remains informative after becoming the target; or maker-level evidence that reliably beats low-overlap instruments at early detection.

### C3 - union and method; checkable by Figure 9 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** information capture and micromanagement are two conditions of a union compound, not degrees of one failure. A single blended governance score destroys the diagnostic; R is per decision area.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** do the two failures require different remedies in a real case? Can a single composite score predict, prospectively and per named area, which one will fail next? Does the board pack preserve the arrangement of the evidence, or only its ingredients?
- **Refuter:** one lever restores both conditions with no categorically different residue; or a composite score with genuine per-slot predictive power.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a governance code, compliance checklist or legal advice.
- Not that a particular number of independent directors, committee structure or reporting cadence is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that management is untrustworthy, or that more monitoring is always better. The board-literature sources warn that intense monitoring can reduce voluntary information sharing.
- Not that the board should never decide. It authors conventions and rulings; it does not author the decision it later grades.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement, and no refuter has been run.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Anchor test:** can management change the outcome the board calls its anchor by disputing the report?
2. **Independence test:** do the board's channels share an underlying evidence pool? ISA (NZ) 500 A12 and paragraph 11 give the auditing profession's version of this test.
3. **Seat test:** can a board that authored a decision still grade it independently against a ground?
4. **Threshold test:** does an escalation metric remain informative after it becomes a target? Goodhart and Campbell predict it will not.
5. **Slot test:** does a blended score retain per-slot predictive power?
6. **Barrier test:** is a supposed new monitoring insight reachable from the board's existing closure by composition alone, or did it require contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-32, :40 and Fig 10 :42-47, :51, :59-61. Eight short passages.
2. Pick one board or case you know. Name the anchor actually used and whether it moves when management pushes.
3. Name one pair of "independent" readings and say whether they share an evidence pool.
4. Say whether the board authored any decision it later graded, and what happened to its outside view.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
