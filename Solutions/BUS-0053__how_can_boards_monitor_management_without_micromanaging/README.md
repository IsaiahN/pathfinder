## The question restated as an effect

A board must decide whether what management reports (Γ(b,a), the prediction) matches what actually happens (o', the ground), per decision area and per period (R indexed by slot s). Two failures are named in the question itself:

1. **Micromanaging.** The board takes over the action a and grades its own work.
2. **Information capture.** The board's only channel to o' runs through the party being checked, so the reading moves when that party moves.

An answer would look like: a set of conditions on the board's checking arrangement, each with an instrument that can fail, plus a statement of what cannot be answered without a ground reading. That shape was bound before the derivation began (Formula, "Direction") so the refuter in `01` can fire on it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that any particular board structure is the answer. The figures return a shape, not a name.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **Agreement is not evidence.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*
3. **A verifier that holds the interiors cannot verify.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Sending a board the whole framework before asking for its verdict would convert an outside frame into a participant.

Legally: public opt-in institutional channels are used instead of scraped personal addresses. Unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, which is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
