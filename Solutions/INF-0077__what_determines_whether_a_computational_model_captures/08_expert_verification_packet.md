# Expert verification packet: what determines causal capture in a computational model

**What this is:** a claim in a form a modeler, statistician or methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` shape, `specified` checks, NOT `grounded`.** No measurement, fit or intervention is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures; it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A computational model's own predictive score is produced by the frame being graded, so fit cannot witness causal fidelity (Fig 1 :27). Causal structure is the directed arrangement between variables, and the arrangement is not recoverable from the observational ingredients (Fig 12 :46, :94), so observationally equivalent models are isomers that come apart only under intervention. What settles which arrangement is right is a non-updating anchor reached from a closure that differs from the model's (Fig 2 :29; Fig 6 :23). The failure must be read per variable and per link (Fig 1 :22), the model's vocabulary must be able to write direction and refuse a wrong direction (Fig 12 :111; Operators table :58), and the checker must not be able to reconstruct the claim (Fig 10 :42). The framework returns this as a shape, not a named estimator.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12
- **Statement:** predictive fit is a filter for the budget, not a witness for causal structure. Same-observation models with different directed edges are different substances.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Refuter:** show that a frame's own fit settles causal fidelity without external contact, or that two observationally equivalent graphs agree under every intervention. If either holds, C1 falls.

### C2. Modeling-facing, checkable against any concrete model and dataset
- **Statement:** a model whose vocabulary has no ordered bond and no refusal operator cannot represent or carry a corrected causal direction. The residual is on a specific variable or link, not in an aggregate score.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Operators table :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** does the model's representation distinguish `A → B` from `B → A`? Can it record that a direction it held is false? Does its diagnostic report identify which variable or link fails under intervention, or only overall error?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a model that writes, tests and reverses a directed claim with symmetric co-occurrence terms and no negation, or a fidelity failure visible only in aggregate. Either refutes C2.

### C3. Verification-facing, checkable against the checking arrangement
- **Statement:** the settling anchor must not update when the frame changes, must come from a differing closure, and must be read by a checker who cannot reconstruct the claim. A synthetic held-out set is a substituted habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **Refuter:** show an internal, non-updating anchor that settles direction with no external contact; or show that a substituted habitat transfers to the real causal ground with measured, not assumed, success; or show that a checker who can reconstruct the claim still verifies it. Any one refutes C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named estimator. The framework returns *"a shape, not a name."* Do-calculus, RCTs, IVs and score-based discovery are bindings, not figure derivations.
- Not that fitting is worthless. It is legitimate for prediction and valid as a budget filter.
- Not that correlation is always misleading. It cannot by itself carry directed arrangement.
- Not that every causal effect is identifiable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not a theory of everything, and not the only true framework. A frame cannot certify its own limit.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Intervention test:** on each variable and link, does the model's response to a change track the system's response? A discriminator, not another argument.
2. **Isomer test:** do two observationally equivalent models come apart under that change? If yes, the bond carries real information. If no, C1 falls.
3. **Reachability test:** is the effect identifiable from the stated graph and data at all? This can be checked before collecting more data (Fig 13 :133).
4. **Independence test:** can the checker return a verdict without being shown the interiors of the model? Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 12 :46 and :94, Fig 2 :29, Fig 6 :23, Fig 10 :42. Five short passages.
2. Pick one model you know. Does its representation distinguish edge direction, and can it refuse a direction it held? (C2)
3. Name one instrument that would separate two observationally equivalent models, or say why none is possible. (C1, C3)
4. Say whether the effect is identifiable from the graph before more data are collected. (reachability)
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
