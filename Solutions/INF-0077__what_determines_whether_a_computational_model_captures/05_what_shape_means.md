# What "shape" means for this question

**Mode: `general`.** This is a reading of the canonical usage rule and the source-discrimination material, not a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` header).

## 1. The rule itself

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :133: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*

So a **shape** is the constraint on an answer that can be stated before the answer can be named. It narrows; it does not fill.

Applied here:

> A shape: a model has causal structure to the extent that the arrangement it holds has been settled against a non-updating anchor reached from a differing closure, checked per variable and per link, by a checker that did not author it.

> A name (not delivered): do-calculus, randomised controlled trial, instrumental variable, Granger causality, structural causal model, the additive-noise model. These are bindings from the world frame.

The distinction matters because naming one estimator as *the* determinate turns a visible choice into an invisible one. *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible,"* as recorded in the completed QMGR-0037 exemplar's `05_what_shape_means.md` from the framework essay *They're Scanning Books When They Should*. The present folder does not re-fetch that essay; it relies on the canonical local statement of the same rule.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

The residual for this question was described in effect terms before any field was searched: a gap on a variable or link between prediction and response under change, with directed asymmetry. Only after that were causal inference, medicine, engineering and cognition searched. Had "causal inference" been named first, any source found would have seemed to fit.

The framework essay *The Library of Babel*, as quoted in the completed exemplar's `05_what_shape_means.md` (read this session, essay itself not re-fetched), gives the search version: the space is cut short when the returned set is *"small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* A shape is that narrowed set.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, a specific estimator earns its name when a measurement shows the model's intervention responses track the system's, not when the estimator is elegant.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45-46), with a source gate and a shadow test. The world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` are such imports. They are recorded with URL, author, venue, year, accessed date and quote precisely so the record shows where each came from.

Until one of those, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

## 4. The source-discrimination limit, and why it governs this question

The framework essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism, recorded in the exemplar's `05_what_shape_means.md`: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* An instrument that cannot distinguish these cases *"demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

This is exactly the present question. A model's fit is a reading of the observed behavior. It cannot comment on the source, that is, on which directed arrangement produced that behavior. Observationally equivalent graphs are the multiple paths to the same green. What is owed is a discriminator (an intervention, an instrument, a hard endpoint), not another argument about which graph is prettier.

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* Declaring a variable "the cause" in a model's documentation does not ground it. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* A stale or pooled reading is a channel failure, not a correction of the ground.

## 5. Consequence for the answer's mode

- The **shape** is `general`: the figures are metalaws and the same loop applies to a brain, a market or a model (Fig 4 kernel rule: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*).
- The **checks** are `specified`: they say what would have to be present (ordered bond, refusal operator, non-updating anchor, per-slot residual, independent checker) and each has a refuter.
- Nothing is `grounded`: no model was fitted, no intervention run, no source-discriminating instrument applied.

## 7. One-paragraph version

A shape is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :133). Here the shape is contact with a non-updating ground from a differing closure, read per variable and per link, with an ordered bond and a refusal operator, checked by someone who did not author it. Naming do-calculus or a randomised trial as the answer is a binding from another frame, licensed only by a ground measurement or a provenanced import. Fit is the reading that cannot comment on the source; the intervention is the discriminator. Nothing here is measured, so the mode is `general` plus `specified`, never `grounded`.
