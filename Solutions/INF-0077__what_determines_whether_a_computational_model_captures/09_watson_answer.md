## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what determines whether a computational model captures the causal structure of the system it describes. Option A: fit, measured by predictive accuracy, likelihood or compression. Option B: contact with a non-updating ground from a differing closure, with the arrangement held in ordered bonds, the failure read per variable and link, and the check made by a verifier who did not author the claim. What each leads to: A scores a frame with a quantity the frame produces and cannot separate observationally equivalent structures; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap, on a variable or link, between what the model represents or predicts and what the system returns when that variable or link is changed. Arity: per slot, not one global number. Symmetry: asymmetric, because causal direction is not interchangeable with its reverse. Scale: fitting inside the model's closure, settling at the ground. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 names it a union compound: two conditions overlap and one lever does not clear both.
- **Domain 1, the model frame.** Actors: variables, parameters, candidate graphs, fitted weights. Currency: accuracy, likelihood, description length. Anchor: none internal that can settle direction, because a quantity the frame produces is frame-internal. This domain settles predictive adequacy, not causal fidelity. Anchor test fails for the target quantity, so it is not yet a domain that can decide the question.
- **Domain 2, the system and its interventions.** Actors: mechanisms, the intervention, instruments, the experimenter's act. Currency: response under change, a counter, a patient outcome. Anchor: the ground, whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. This is the nameable anchor and the deciding domain.
- **Domain 3, the verification frame.** Actors: outside checkers, independent instruments, other closures. Currency: residuals and refutations. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is not corroboration.
- **Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. Fit and causal fidelity overlap; the intersection is the model that predicts well with the wrong edges. One lever clears the fit-only region and leaves the intersection standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, then vocabulary (ordered bonds, intervention, refusal), then objective (what counts as progress), then planning (design the intervention), then learn and carry. The likely break is at vocabulary/objective, because that link has no standard instrument.

Strain test applied: the bond analogy maps onto a directed graph edge by binding from the statistics frame, not by derivation from the figure. The figure derives the need for an arrangement the ingredients do not carry; the world frame names the arrangement. No figure line was strained. One open item, the unpriced bond, is named in section 11 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

What determines causal capture is not fit. It is contact with a non-updating ground from a closure that differs from the model's, read per variable and per link, held in a vocabulary with ordered bonds and a refusal operator, and checked by a verifier who cannot reconstruct the claim. Observational equivalence gives the mechanism of failure: same ingredients, different arrangement, identical fit, divergent behavior under intervention. Non-identifiability is a verdict, not silence. The answer is a `general` shape with `specified` checks, ungrounded and candidate. No estimator is named.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded but real. The shape is mode-labeled and carries four pre-registered refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording or a named protocol before they have checked the output; Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no model is committed. If a refuter fires, the shape is narrowed: an internal anchor would remove the contact requirement, recoverability of direction from ingredients would remove the bond requirement, and a transferable synthetic habitat would remove the substitution limit. The files already state the narrower forms.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that separates two observationally equivalent models on their intervention responses would settle which bond is right; a demonstration that direction is recoverable from observational ingredients alone would break the bond claim; a measured transfer from a substituted habitat to the real system would break Fig 11; a frame that checks its own direction without binding the sought-for shape would break the direction limit. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
