## Restating the question as an effect

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A: a model captures causal structure when it predicts well.** This leads to a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces, and to treating observationally equivalent structures as the same structure. It spends the independence of the people asked to check the claim.
- **Option B: a model captures causal structure when its arrangement is settled against a non-updating anchor from a closure that differs from its own, checked per variable and per link, by a checker that did not author it.** This leads to a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

What an answer would look like is bound as an ordinary term before the run: *contact with a non-updating ground through an intervention (or other import from a differing closure), with ordered bonds, a per-slot residual, and an independent verifier.* That binding is the sought-for shape made refusable (Fig 5 :62-66).

## Verdict in one line

The shape survives; no specific algorithm is named. Predictive fit, compression and agreement among models trained on the same data are frame-internal filters, not witnesses. Causal structure lives in the directed bonds, which are not recoverable from the observational ingredients. What settles it is a ground anchor the model did not author and cannot move, reached from a closure that differs from the model's own. `general` + `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
