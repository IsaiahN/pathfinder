## Independent domain census

I redid the census using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity, symmetry, scale).** Three-place, (trait configuration, species-slot, changing environment), plus a threshold comparison against a persistence quantity. The environment term cannot be removed without the residual vanishing, so this is not a two-place trait-to-extinction question. Symmetry: the ordinary word "trait" suggests a symmetric property, but Fig 11 :49 makes the sign residual-relative, so the apparent symmetry is a misreading. Scale: trait at organism scale, extinction at lineage scale, the causal path crossing the membrane (Fig 4) and cascading through habitat contact (Fig 11 :52-54).
- **D1 Library and closure.** Actors: alleles, life-history traits, genetic variance. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ_s), reach. Anchor: settlement by the ground. Admitted.
- **D2 Habitat and contact.** Actors: conditions and relations, other species. Currency: contact, enumerated. Anchor: the environment as selector when no seat maintains it. Admitted.
- **D3 The ground.** Actors: rate and severity of change, instruments. Currency: survival or extinction. Anchor: measured persistence, non-updating. Admitted.
- **D4 Generational channel.** Actors: generations, dispersal, gene flow. Currency: tracking of a moving optimum. Anchor: the ground. Admitted.
- **X1 Standing list.** No anchor that does not move with the residual. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 Trait in isolation.** Absorbed into D2.

Union, not partition: the four conditions overlap, so a single lever leaves intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). Dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The objective link lacks an instrument in most studies, so work above it cannot rescue it.

My census matches the primary's four domains, its union reading, and its refusal of X1. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39, :43, :49, :51, :52-54 (habitat and valence), Fig 6 :21 and :23 (closure), Fig 2 :29 (anchor), Fig 7 :36 (channel).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, that vulnerability is species-and-habitat, valence flips with the residual, the habitat is enumerated, small closure leaves no reachable term, persistence is the anchor, and the generational chain must differ.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete macroecological claim, that large body size, long generation, narrow range and high specialization are the vulnerable traits, with a pooled coefficient as the verdict.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** of the residual (Fig 1 :19), which a pooled coefficient discards;
- the **residual-relative valence** (Fig 11 :49), which a fixed trait ranking freezes;
- the **frame-versus-ground distinction** (Fig 2 :29), which a Red List status as outcome collapses;
- the **bond information** (Fig 12 :5), which an additive risk list does not record.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a species' relation to its habitat and the length of its contact cascade are what decide whether the ground settles it. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` states.
