**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation biologist or evolutionary ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal standing list, not `grounded`.** No per-species persistence measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a separator, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation, not a ground reading.** It is checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question asks for a standing list of traits that make species vulnerable to extinction in rapidly changing environments. The standing list is not the right object: trait valence is relative to the residual, so the same trait helps or hurts as the environment changes what the ground pays for. What survives is a four-domain separator. Vulnerability is a property of species-and-habitat, not of the species alone (D1/D2). The habitat is enumerated, not composed, and contact cascades outward, so range, dispersal, diet and dependency matter through the substitutes they leave (D2). A small library and narrow closure give few reachable adaptive terms (D1). The anchor is measured persistence, not a Red List category, and the ground does not decay while the channel does (D3). A long generational chain runs in place when the optimum moves faster than adaptation (D4). These are four conditions in a union; their verdicts are kept separate and never averaged into one score. The concrete trait list and its effect sizes remain a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and ecology-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and a species' natural history

- **Statement:** a standing list is structurally refused. Trait valence is residual-relative, and capability is agent-and-habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **What to check:** can the checker name a trait whose sign for extinction risk is invariant across a documented change of environment? Cardillo et al. 2005 (Science, abstract) is the relevant counter-test: the same body-size variable changes its driver composition above about 3 kg.
- **Refuter:** an invariant trait sign across a documented change of residual; or extinction probability unchanged under a habitat substitution that removes all contact with the relevant condition. Either breaks C1.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against a species' habitat enumeration

- **Statement:** the habitat is enumerated, contact cascades, and a narrow closure leaves no internal route to a fitting adaptive term.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* Fig 11 :52-54 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- **What to check:** enumerate actors, conditions and relations for one species. Does a second-order or third-order contact effect reach it? Does a specialist have any reachable term if the changed residual falls outside its closure? Zhang et al. 2026 (Science Advances) and Rivas-Salvador and Reif 2023 (Naturwissenschaften) report associations with small range and habitat specialization, but those are pooled filters, not per-species witnesses.
- **Refuter:** a predicted cascade that fails to arrive; or a specialist reaching a fitting term by internal composition alone with no import. Either breaks C2.

### C3 — framework-internal and measurement-facing; checkable against an anchor test

- **Statement:** the ground is persistence, not a category; the ground does not decay but the channel does; the generational chain must differ and match the residual or it runs in place.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **What to check:** is the study outcome a census or a demographic rate, or is it an IUCN category? Does the channel (dispersal, plasticity, genetic rescue) change while the ground does not? Is the lag of the mean trait behind the moving optimum zero when the environment changes faster than generation time? Bell and Gonzalez 2009 and Alexander et al. 2014 give the rescue conditions and the expected lag.
- **Refuter:** a Red List category that changes the measured trajectory rather than reporting it; or a long-generation lineage tracking a moving optimum with zero lag without import. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal trait list, ranking, or percentage.
- Not that large body size, narrow range, long generation, short dispersal or specialization is intrinsically bad.
- Not that a pooled macroecological coefficient settles a species-slot (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a Red List category is a ground measurement (Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*).
- Not that conservation can subtract a habitat; introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the isomer claim in `06` is triangulated. It carries one live fixed-atom condition and a stated falsifier.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Sign-flip test:** does a named trait keep its sign across a documented change of residual?
2. **Habitat-substitution test:** does extinction probability move when contact is removed with no silent failure?
3. **Cascade test:** does a predicted second-order or third-order contact effect reach the species?
4. **Closure test:** can a specialist reach a fitting term by internal composition alone?
5. **Lag test:** does a long-generation lineage track a moving optimum with zero lag and no import?
6. **Anchor test:** does a category change the trajectory rather than reporting it?

A source-discriminating instrument is needed, not a further argument: one that separates area requirement, low reproductive rate, trophic position and slow adaptation when they produce the same pooled correlation.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39, :43, :47, :49, :51, :52-54 and Fig 6 :21, Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24, Fig 7 :36. Ten short passages.
2. Check C1 against one trait you know well: does its sign really flip with the environment?
3. Check C2 against one species: enumerate its actors, conditions and relations, and name one second-order effect.
4. Check C3 against one study you know: is the outcome a measurement or a category?
5. Name one instrument that would discriminate the sources of a body-size correlation, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
