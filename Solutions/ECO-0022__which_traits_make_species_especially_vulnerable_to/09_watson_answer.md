## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures say about a standing list of traits that make species vulnerable in rapidly changing environments. Option A: answer with the roster that the macroecological literature suggests (large body, long generation, narrow range, high specialization, low dispersal). Option B: refuse the roster as a category error and return a four-domain separator with per-domain verdicts and a declared `gap`. What each leads to: A gives a pooled filter the standing of a per-species verdict, freezes trait valence across a change of residual, and treats a Red List category as a ground; B gives a checkable shape, seven written refuters, and an open list until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** For species-slot s, the gap between predicted persistence from the species' library and habitat and observed persistence when the environment moves. Arity: three-place (trait configuration, species-slot, changing environment) plus a threshold comparison; the environment term cannot be dropped. Symmetry: appears symmetric in the ordinary word "trait," but the figure reading makes it ordered and residual-relative (Fig 11 :49). Scale: trait at organism or lineage scale, extinction at population-to-lineage scale, causal path across the membrane and outward through the habitat.
- **D1 Species library and closure.** Actors: alleles, life-history parameters, body size, niche breadth, dispersal, genetic variance. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ_s), reach, cost. Anchor: settlement by the ground, does the trait pay in persistence. Admitted.
- **D2 Habitat and contact field.** Actors: conditions and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency), other species. Currency: contact, enumerated not composed. Anchor: the environment as selector in the unmaintained mode. Admitted.
- **D3 The changing environment as ground.** Actors: rate and severity of change, instruments. Currency: what the ground pays. Anchor: actual persistence, a non-updating reading, not a category. Admitted.
- **D4 Generational and dispersal channel.** Actors: generations, plastic responses, dispersal events, gene flow. Currency: tracking of a moving optimum. Anchor: the ground. Admitted.
- **X1 A universal standing list of vulnerable traits.** Actors: a fixed roster. Currency: category membership. Anchor: none that does not move with the residual. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 Trait as free-floating property of the species alone.** Absorbed into D2 by the agent-and-habitat claim.

Union, not partition: D1 to D4 overlap, so one lever leaves the intersections standing. Order by dependency: perception of change, vocabulary of what it affords, objective (what counts as persistence), planning (plasticity, dispersal, rescue), learn and carry. The objective link usually lacks an instrument, so effort at the planning link cannot rescue it.

Strain test applied: the macroecological correlations versus Fig 11 :51 was returned to census and re-mapped as a budget filter (Fig 9 :60), not used to flip the verdict. Two Fig 11 modes (unmaintained and maintained) govern different objects. One live type condition remains: whether traits compose as typed bonds (the isomer claim in `06`), named and not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: the question as posed asks for the wrong object. A standing trait list cannot survive a change of residual (Fig 11 :51), so the figures return a four-domain separator with verdicts kept separate and never averaged. Vulnerability is species-and-habitat; valence is residual-relative; the habitat is enumerated and contact cascades; a small library and narrow closure leave few reachable terms; the anchor is measured persistence and the channel decays while the ground does not; and the generational loop runs in place when the optimum moves faster than adaptation. The macroecological trait correlations are filters for the budget, not witnesses for a per-species verdict. The concrete trait list and its effect sizes are a declared `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, with a source-discriminating instrument owed. One live type condition, the fixed-atom isomer claim in `06`, is named rather than hidden. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected conservation effort. A species failing D1 would be given more dispersal corridors, or a species failing D3 would be given more genetic rescue, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs several. In verification terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a roster that was never the right object. The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible, because the packet leads with the refuter and the separator rather than a ranking. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refusal is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out. The seven refuters and the retraction log are in a provenance record so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."*). The four domain verdicts can be corrected independently without withdrawing the shape.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A named trait holding its sign across a documented change of residual, extinction probability invariant under a habitat substitution with no silent failure, a predicted cascade that fails to arrive, a specialist reaching a fitting term by internal composition alone, zero lag in a long-generation lineage without import, a Red List category that changes the measured trajectory, or one lever clearing all four conditions with no categorically different residue, any one of which fires a pre-registered refuter. A per-species source-discriminating measurement would move the concrete list from `gap` to `specified` or `grounded`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
