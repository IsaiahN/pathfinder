# The Tether's answer on extinction vulnerability traits

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal standing list, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each species-slot s, R_s is the gap between predicted persistence given the species' library and habitat and observed persistence when the environment moves. The question asks which traits enlarge R_s. The answer refuses the form of the question: there is no standing list, because valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51). What is returned is a four-domain separator with per-domain verdicts, kept separate and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :55, :60).

## 2. The shape

1. **Vulnerability is agent-and-habitat, not a trait carried in isolation.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A trait that does not alter contact with the ground cannot settle persistence.

2. **Trait valence flips with the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Large body size, long generation, narrow diet, and short dispersal help or hurt depending on what the environment pays for. Cardillo et al. 2005 corroborate the sign change with a threshold around 3 kg and a shift in drivers.

3. **The habitat must be enumerated and contact cascades.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*; Fig 11 :52-54 gives the DDT-to-eagles cascade, *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Narrow range, short dispersal, and high specialization matter because they shorten the cascade and remove substitutes when a relation is severed.

4. **Small library, narrow closure, low support.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* A small population with low genetic variance has few reachable adaptive terms and low positive support per slot (Fig 1 :20). Bell and Gonzalez 2009 and Alexander et al. 2014 corroborate the population-size and genetic-variation conditions experimentally and theoretically.

5. **The ground is persistence, not a category.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A Red List category is a frame reading; a census is an anchor. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*

6. **The generational chain must differ and match the residual.** Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Long generation time makes the chain run in place when the environment moves faster than adaptation; evolutionary-rescue theory predicts a stable lag behind a moving optimum.

7. **The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* One lever leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are per domain.

8. **Conservation is the maintained mode.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* A substituted habitat carries two silent failures: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69).

Correlations across species (body size, generation length, range size, specialization) are filters for which species to study first, not witnesses for a per-species verdict (Fig 9 :60). They do not enter the verdicts above.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal trait list, and not a ranking of traits by importance.
- Not that large body size, narrow range, long generation, or specialization is intrinsically bad. Valence is residual-relative.
- Not that a Red List category is a measurement. It is a convention and a frame reading.
- Not that a pooled macroecological coefficient settles a species-slot.
- Not that conservation can subtract a habitat. Introduction is available, exclusion is not.
- Not `grounded`. No per-species persistence measurement under a habitat substitution was taken here.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating, per-species instrument, not a further argument. The missing discriminator must separate at least these sources of one reading: area requirement, low reproductive rate, trophic position, and slow adaptation behind a moving optimum. Specific tests, each tied to a refuter in `01` §3:

1. **Sign-flip test:** does a named trait keep its sign under a documented change of residual? If yes, refuter 1 fires.
2. **Habitat-substitution test:** does extinction probability move when contact with a condition is removed with no silent failure? If not, refuter 2 fires.
3. **Cascade test:** does a predicted second-order or third-order contact effect reach the species whose enumeration predicted it? If not, refuter 3 fires.
4. **Closure test:** can a specialist reach a fitting term by internal composition alone? If yes, refuter 4 fires.
5. **Lag test:** does a long-generation lineage track a moving optimum with zero lag without import? If yes, refuter 5 fires.
6. **Anchor test:** does a Red List category change the measured trajectory rather than reporting it? If yes, refuter 6 fires.

Alexander et al. 2014 note the practical difficulty: there are *"few clear-cut empirical examples"* and *"the difficulty of ascertaining whether phenotypic change has a genetic basis."* That is why the stop is honest rather than premature.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four-domain separator is as far as the figures carry this question. The concrete trait list and its effect sizes are a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind; the missing thing is an instrument, characterized in effect terms but not held. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* The canonical limit says the framework stops at what the instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

One live strain remains: whether ecological traits compose as typed bonds (the isomer claim in `06` Q1) or merely co-vary as an ingredient list. It is named, not hidden, and has a falsifier.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a list of vulnerable traits, and it does not claim a universal ranking. It says that vulnerability is a relation between a species and its habitat, so the same trait can help or hurt when the environment changes what the ground pays for. What it returns is a four-part separator: the species' library and closure, the habitat's enumerated contact and cascades, a persistence ground that does not update on what anyone thinks, and a generational channel that must track a moving optimum. Small populations and low genetic variance matter because they leave few reachable adaptive terms; narrow ranges, short dispersal and specialization matter because they remove substitutes when a relation is severed; long generation matters because the chain runs in place. Pooled correlations filter which species to study first, but they do not settle a species-slot. The specific trait list and its effect sizes stay open until a per-species, source-discriminating measurement is run under a documented habitat substitution. The falsifiers were written before this paragraph.
