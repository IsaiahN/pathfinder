# Outreach draft: check the separator before you ask for the list

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four domain verdicts and the seven refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are intact in the text you actually send. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would refute a trait-based account of extinction vulnerability?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in extinction vulnerability research: which traits make species especially vulnerable in rapidly changing environments? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the applications, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`):** the question as posed asks for a standing list of vulnerable traits. I think that list is not the right object. Trait valence is relative to the residual: the same body size, dispersal capacity, or generation time can help or hurt depending on what the environment is paying for. What I get instead is a four-part separator, with verdicts kept separate and never averaged:
>
> 1. **The species' library and closure.** Small populations and low genetic variance are not just few individuals; they are few reachable adaptive terms. Composition inside a closure cannot add an atom, so a specialist has no internal route to a term that fits a changed environment.
> 2. **The habitat and its contact field.** The habitat must be enumerated, not composed: actors, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Contact cascades outward. Scoping to direct contact is how a contaminant three steps out reaches a top predator. Large range and broad relations leave more substitutes when one relation is severed.
> 3. **The ground.** The thing that settles whether the species persists is the actual persistence measurement, not a Red List category. A category is a classification produced by a frame; a census is an anchor. The ground does not decay, but the channel to it does.
> 4. **The generational channel.** Successive generations must differ and match the residual the last ones surfaced. Long generation time makes that chain run in place when the environment moves faster than adaptation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any concrete trait is intrinsically bad; that a pooled correlation gives a per-species verdict; that a Red List status is a measurement; or that this is a grounded result. The framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself, so the specific trait list and its effect sizes remain openly unstated. Correlations are filters for which species to study first, not witnesses for why a species crosses the threshold.
>
> **What would refute this, written before the run:** (1) a named trait keeps its sign across a documented change of residual; (2) extinction probability is unchanged by a habitat substitution that removes all contact with the relevant condition; (3) a predicted second-order or third-order contact cascade fails to arrive; (4) a specialist reaches a fitting term by internal composition alone, with no import; (5) a long-generation lineage tracks a moving optimum with zero lag; (6) a Red List category changes the population trajectory rather than reporting it; (7) one lever clears all four conditions with no categorically different residue. Any one of those breaks the separator and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a species you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## A note on stage 1 for this particular question

A checker in conservation biology may object that the macroecological literature already has the list (body size, generation time, range size, specialization, migratory strategy, diet breadth). The correct stage-1 reply is not to deny those findings. It is to point out the object mismatch: those are averaged correlates across many species, while the residual is indexed per species (Fig 1 :19). The checker should be asked what they would expect to see if valence were residual-relative, and whether a single coefficient could reorder species under a habitat substitution. Their answer to that is the stage-1 verdict, and it must be recorded before any framework is shown.
