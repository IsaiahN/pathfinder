## The world literature that bears on the claim

1. **Conservation biology, selectivity.** Cardillo et al. 2005 report two patterns in the size selectivity of extinction risk: impacts rise sharply above about 3 kg, and the drivers shift from environmental factors in smaller species to a combination of environmental and intrinsic traits in larger ones. This is the empirical face of Fig 11 :49. It is the strongest world corroborator for the refusal of a standing list. Only the abstract was opened; the full text is subscription only, and the row is marked as such in the ledger.
2. **Evolutionary ecology, rescue.** Bell and Gonzalez 2009 show experimentally that rescue depends on population size, genetic variation supply, and maladaptation, with a clear population-size threshold. Alexander et al. 2014 link conservation and medicine under the same model and describe a race between adaptation and extinction. Together they corroborate the small-library and loop-rate domains.
3. **Macroecology, range and specialization.** Zhang et al. 2026, Rivas-Salvador and Reif 2023, and Mi et al. 2025 all associate narrow ranges, large body size, long generation time, and habitat specialization with higher vulnerability. They are one broad evidence pool rather than three independent witnesses, and the ledger says so.
4. **Microbiome and dependency.** Greenspan et al. 2022 report lower skin bacterial diversity in threatened amphibians and name habitat specialization and narrow environmental tolerances as homogenizing factors. This is the genuinely low-overlap witness for the dependency relation in Fig 11 :47.
5. **Reference syntheses.** The Wikipedia pages on evolutionary rescue, rarity, and biomagnification were read for mechanism descriptions, not for verdicts. They are secondary and marked as such.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Where a source appeared to strain the verdict (the pooled trait list versus Fig 11 :51), it was returned to the census and re-mapped as a budget filter, not used to flip the verdict (see §5 S2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-part separator, and the seven refuters. Ask what the checker would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory or essay.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local framework links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction because a recipient who has absorbed the theory stops being an outside frame. Stage 3 sends a generator, which Fig 4 :22 permits. Every stage still needs a ground at the species level; a system that cannot settle a species' persistence has one level however large its library.
