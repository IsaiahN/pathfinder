**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a question in generalization research: how to tell a representation that will work in a new habitat from one that only fits the corpus it was trained on. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a separator with seven parts. (1) Read the gap between prediction and observation per object slot, not as one aggregate accuracy; an average hides the only signal that says where to look. (2) Use a held reading whose anchor does not change when the model's own prediction changes; a split from the same corpus is a mirror, not an anchor. (3) Ask whether a reapplicable method crossed into the new habitat or a recording of one occasion was replayed; a synthetic harness proves wiring and never capability. (4) Check whether the representation carries typed relations between features, not just the feature list; same ingredients under a different arrangement are a different substance. (5) Check reach, not library size: fitting inside a closed set cannot add a primitive, so ask whether the needed structure was already in reach and unfound, whether the instrument can be sharpened, or whether a frame with a different closure must be imported. (6) State how independent the evaluator is from the fitting evidence; agreement from one shared pool is a convention, not a verdict. (7) Keep the lineage: where each atom and bond came from, because convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a result and not a universal recipe. I am not claiming that any named architecture, sample count or regularizer is the answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a zero training/test gap proves generality, that every perfect fit is memorization (benign overfitting is a real conflict and I have recorded it), that a new atom is needed for generalization, or that any of this is settled by experiment. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A verbatim recording of one training occasion, with a lineage showing a lookup and no carried method, predicts the shape of residuals at new slots in a new habitat as well as a method-carrying representation.
> 2. A metric produced by the model itself (training loss, compression achieved, or pooled accuracy) is shown to be a non-updating anchor that settles the cross-habitat residual at every slot.
> 3. Two evaluations sharing one evidence pool predict external-validity failures as well as two blinded evaluations from independent instruments.
> 4. A flat feature-only representation, with no typed relations, distinguishes two arrangements with identical ingredients at a new slot without spending extra actions on order.
> 5. One scalar computed inside the fitted corpus settles every per-slot failure in a new habitat without a measurement on a non-updating ground.
> 6. A figure line names the architecture or sample count that guarantees generalization.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the claim in that domain. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
