## 0. The residual in effect terms

**Question (source line 82):** "What makes a ritual feel meaningful or effective to participants?"

**Effect statement.** The same outwardly similar performance sometimes lands as meaningful and efficacious and sometimes as hollow. Across rituals and across participants in one ritual, there is a differential: felt meaning arrives in some slots and not in others. We owe the condition under which it arrives and a discriminator that can see its absence. This is a residual `R`, not a topic.

**Bound sought-for shape (Formula "Direction").** Write down what an answer would look like and let it accrue a residual like any other term: *"For participant s, felt meaning is present iff X is present; when X is removed the feeling fails; the instrument that reads X is named."* That sentence is a claim, not a preference, so the refuter can reach it.

**Options and their consequences.**

| option | content | what it leads to |
|---|---|---|
| A, form only | sequence, repetition, arousal, cost, symbol | an ingredient checklist; cannot explain why the same form lands differently in different people |
| B, participant only | belief, expectation, cultural competence | an agent-only account; contradicted by co-presence findings and by Fig 11 :39 |
| C, habitat only | community, transmission, co-presence | a group-only account; erases the per-person verdict |
| A and B and C | union compound | unbundle first, then one loop per condition (Fig 9 :54) |

**Arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least three conditions, so a union, not one hard question. Symmetry: the felt verdict is first-personal and per-slot; agreement between participants is a symmetric correlation, a different quantity. Scale: first order is one person in one performance; second order is a practice-lineage that survives across performances and generations.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The ground of this question is the participant's own verdict (decisive)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** The same line continues: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

The question asks what *feels* meaningful *to participants*. It is constitutively about people's own experience, so human judgment is the legitimate anchor, not an instrument standing in for it. An EEG signature, an anthropologist's interpretation, or a count of formal features is an instrument. Cho et al. (2018) found that alpha power suppression tracked efficacy ratings, but an instrument that registers arousal need not register meaning; it can be wrong on the axis it does not measure.

**Mode: `general` (structural). Triangulated:** Fig 2 plus the Seat Map anchor section, low overlap with the ritual-science finding that ratings of efficacy are self-reported first-person judgments.
**Refuter:** show that "feels meaningful to participants" is not constitutively about participants' own verdict, or produce an instrument that tracks felt meaning on every axis the participant report tracks and more, so that it can serve as the anchor rather than an instrument. Until then every non-participant measure is demoted to corroboration, never promoted to the ground.

### F2 - The residual is indexed per participant, not per ritual average

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

"Did the ritual work?" asked of a congregation collapses live slots into a mean. Cho et al. (2018) report that between-subject variance of efficacy ratings fell from SD = 1.99 (individual) to SD = 1.49 (group), about 60 percent. That is a change in the *agreement statistic*, not proof that the ritual worked for each person. Irving et al. (2024) likewise find that effects "vary depending on the specific social context" and by role (dancer, observer, control), so a single group mean hides the structure.

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated:** Fig 1 plus two independent ritual-science findings with different methods (EEG lab paradigm; field quasi-experiment).
**Refuter:** exhibit a ritual for which the group mean tracks each participant's verdict with no slot-level residual (every participant at the mean and stable under repetition). Then aggregation is harmless for this case and F2 is withdrawn.

### F3 - A convention and a verdict are different quantities (apparent strain, resolved by re-census)

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :41: **"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
Fig 2 :43: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Read together these look like a contradiction about agreement. Re-census: they govern different quantities. :40 governs a **verdict**, a claim about the world ("this ritual cures illness", "this ritual is objectively efficacious"), where agreement among believers is disqualifying. :41 governs a **convention**, a shared meaning ("we are doing the same thing together"), where convergence is the product, not corruption. Durkheim's collective effervescence, as reported in the source opened this session, "excites individuals and serves to unify the group"; Legare and Nielsen (2020) define rituals as "socially stipulated, conventional behaviours". Both describe the convention half. The moment the convention decides an outcome, Fig 2 :43 says it has stopped being a convention and become a verdict with no ground.

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated:** Fig 2 :40-43 plus Durkheim (sociology, 1912) and Legare and Nielsen (cultural psychology, 2020), three low-overlap frames.
**Refuter:** exhibit a ritual whose world-outcome claim is settled by agreement alone with no independent ground (breaks :40), or a shared meaning produced without convergent alignment (breaks :41). Either fires independently.

### F4 - Felt meaning is a property of participant-and-habitat, not of the form alone

Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."**
Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

"Meaningful" is a label applied afterwards to a practice-lineage that met its ground. It is not a substance inside the action sequence. Form-only accounts (formalism, invariance, costly display) enumerate part of the habitat and stop. Irving et al. (2024) find interaction effects between belief and participation that depend on "the specific social context", and Cho et al. (2018) find that co-presence changes agreement even when the stimulus is identical. A change of form that does not change contact changes nothing.

Fig 11 :72 states the standing falsifier: **"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."** Recorded, not hidden.
**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated:** Fig 11 plus two world sources with different designs.
**Refuter:** an agent-only or form-only manipulation that moves the participant's ground verdict while contact is held constant.

### F5 - The arrangement carries the meaning, not the ingredient list

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Bell's six characteristics (formalism, traditionalism, invariance, rule-governance, sacral symbolism, performance, as reported in the Wikipedia "Ritual" page opened this session) are an ingredient list. Legare and Nielsen (2020) add the bond: rituals are "predefined sequences of action characterized by rigidity, formality and repetition, ... embedded in systems of meaning and symbolism". Myerhoff's line, quoted on that page, "not only is seeing believing, doing is believing", is a claim about the route, not the list. Under Fig 12 :117, **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two rites with the same actions in a different order are different substances, and a flat list cannot tell them apart.

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated:** Fig 12 plus the ritual-science definition of ritual as a *sequence*.
**Refuter:** show that felt meaning is invariant under permutation of the action order with atoms and participants fixed, or exhibit two rites with identical atoms and bonds that differ in felt meaning for the same participants on repeated trials.

### F6 - The framework returns a shape; a single cause is not nameable from here

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 9 :27: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Legare and Nielsen (2020) write that practitioners "can't tell you (from a causal mechanistic perspective) how they work" and ask *"how should the perceived efficacy of ritual action be evaluated in the absence of causal information?"* That is the shape limit in the field's own terms. The Tether therefore gives the four constraints any answer must satisfy (F1 to F4) and does not fill the cause slot. Picking arousal, or synchrony, or belief first would invert Fig 9 :27 and make any picked frame seem to fit.

**Mode: `general` (limit). Single-source for the rule:** Fig 9 plus the canonical figures README; world sources corroborate the absence of a single causal mechanism but are not the authority.
**Refuter:** produce a candidate mechanism that satisfies all four constraints and is settled at the ground for every participant slot. Then the slot is filled for this case and the limit is lifted here, not generally.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map :287).

The shape claim is refuted, constraint by constraint, if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor fails:** "feels meaningful to participants" is not constitutively about participants' own verdict, or an instrument outperforms the first-person verdict on every axis and becomes the legitimate anchor.
2. **Slot fails:** averaging across participants leaves no slot-level residual under repetition.
3. **Convention/verdict fails:** a ritual's world-outcome claim is settled by agreement alone, or shared meaning is produced without convergent alignment.
4. **Habitat fails:** an agent-only or form-only change moves the ground with contact held constant (the falsifier Fig 11 :72 already names).
5. **Bond fails:** felt meaning is invariant under permutation of the action sequence with atoms fixed.

If any fires, the corresponding constraint is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused. Nothing is silently softened. Until one is run, no verdict here is `grounded`.
