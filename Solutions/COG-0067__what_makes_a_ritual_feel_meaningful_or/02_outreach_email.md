# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `08_expert_verification_packet.md` has been checked by someone in the field and the three sub-claims are either confirmed or corrected. An output that does not survive validation collapses the two-stage offer on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about ritual experience before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about what makes a ritual feel meaningful to participants. I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** The same outwardly similar rite lands as meaningful and efficacious for some people and as hollow for others, sometimes within the same gathering. I owe the condition under which it lands and an instrument that can see its absence. I am treating this as a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question, so I have not bundled the answers.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The framework returns a shape with four constraints, not a single cause:
>
> 1. The anchor is the participant's own verdict about whether the rite felt meaningful. The question is constitutively about people, so an outside measure (arousal, neural synchrony, an observer's reading) is an instrument, not the ground. It can be right on the axis it measures and wrong on the one it does not.
> 2. That verdict is indexed per person. A group average can hide the live signal; convergence under co-presence is a result about agreement, not proof that the rite worked for each participant.
> 3. Shared meaning is a convention, and agreement is its product, not a corruption of it. But a claim that the rite produced a world outcome is a verdict, and for that, agreement among believers is disqualifying. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
> 4. Felt meaning is a property of participant and habitat together, and it lives in the arrangement of acts (order, relation, sequence), not in the ingredient list. Changing the form without changing contact changes nothing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the cause is "collective effervescence", or arousal, or synchrony, or belief, or cost. Those are candidate conditions from the literature, not names the framework licenses. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming any ritual is objectively efficacious because people agree that it is. That would let a convention decide an outcome.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. "Feels meaningful to participants" turns out not to be constitutively about participants' own verdict, or an outside instrument outperforms that verdict on every axis and becomes the anchor.
> 2. Averaging across participants leaves no person-level residual under repetition.
> 3. A rite's world-outcome claim is settled by agreement alone, or shared meaning is produced with no convergent alignment.
> 4. An agent-only or form-only change moves the ground while contact is held constant.
> 5. Felt meaning is invariant under permutation of the action order with the acts and participants fixed.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding constraint. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **What I would actually value from you:** which of the five refuters you think is most likely to fire in your own data, and what you would count as a failure of the shape claim. Please send that verdict before reading the framework, so it stays independent of it.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
