# Union, bond and order: the supporting derivation for COG-0067

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that the question is a union of three conditions and that the felt verdict is per participant. Two further questions are owed: (1) if the conditions are unbundled, what is their dependency order and where does a single lever leave an intersection standing; (2) if the ritual form is a molecule, what does the bond, rather than the ingredient list, contribute to the felt result.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the dependency order of the three conditions?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Order by dependency, not importance: **D1 anchor** (what counts as felt meaning, settled by the participant) -> **D2 vocabulary** (terms for what the rite affords the participant) -> **D3 objective** (what would count as progress in that rite) -> **D4 habitat** (co-presence, transmission, maintenance). D4 consumes what D1 to D3 produce.
- Effort spent on D2 (for example, adding symbols to a rite) before D1 is fixed arrives at a step that cannot use it. This is Fig 3's broken-middle failure: the link with no instrument is the one nobody has looked at.
- **Falsifier:** show that a habitat manipulation (co-presence) changes the participant's verdict with the anchor and form untouched and with no vocabulary or objective link in between. Then the order is wrong and the dependency claim falls.

### Q2 - Why a single lever leaves a compound standing

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**; it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound.
- Concretely: increasing arousal (a form lever) may clear the case where only form bound, while leaving the intersection where the participant did not hold the relevant belief and the habitat supplied no co-presence. Irving et al. (2024) report exactly this kind of interaction: belief and participation effects vary by social context and by role. A single lever does not clear the intersection.
- **Falsifier:** a single manipulation that clears every participant's negative verdict across all three conditions with no residual compound. Then the union reading is unnecessary.

### Q3 - Are two rites isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- A flat ingredient list (actions, music, symbols, repetition, cost) cannot distinguish two rites that use all of them in a different order. Under `→` (sequence), swapping two operands changes the thing: Fig 12's test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* So two rites with the same atoms but a different order are **isomers** by the figure's definition.
- The weaker claim: if two rites share the substructure `action → repetition → symbol` but differ in the surrounding atoms, they are members of the same **functional group**. The group says what kind of rite; the qualifier says which member. Legare and Nielsen (2020) describe rituals as predefined sequences embedded in systems of meaning, which is this substructure in the field's own vocabulary.
- The Myerhoff line from the "Ritual" page, *"not only is seeing believing, doing is believing"*, is a claim about the route (`→`), not about the ingredients. A vocabulary that lists only ingredients *"can name a state and cannot name a route to one"* (Fig 12 :111).
- **Falsifier:** show that felt meaning is invariant under permutation of the ordered actions with atoms and participants held fixed, or that two rites with identical atoms and identical bond structure still differ systematically in felt meaning for the same participants. Either result kills the isomer claim; the functional-group claim can still stand.

### Q4 - What is second order here?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- A rite that has repeatedly settled for its participants becomes an atom for the tradition: the repeated rite is now something the community composes over (calendar, identity, obligation). This is why Irving et al. (2024) can study a *naturally occurring* annual festival rather than a laboratory action: the ritual is already a settled molecule at the community level.
- At second order the comparison `⋛` enters: the participant compares the performed rite against the remembered or expected one (Fig 12 :85, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). Whether a felt comparison counts as a quantity at that level is a type question, like the operand type of `⋛` in the QM/GR exemplar. It needs an instrument, not an argument. Marked as a strain, not smoothed.
- **Falsifier:** show that a settled rite cannot enter a tradition as an atom, or that the felt comparison cannot be written as a threshold relation at any operationalisation. Then Q4 falls while Q1 to Q3 may stand.

### Q5 - Which analogues actually carry over?

| Fig 12 structure | ritual analogue | does it carry? |
|---|---|---|
| isomer | same acts, different order, different felt result | yes, with the permutation falsifier in Q3 |
| functional group | `action → repetition → symbol` shared across rites | yes, with the qualifier distinguishing members |
| catalyst | an element that survives its own use (the officiant, the object that is consecrated and stays consecrated) | partial; Durkheim's totem is described as a collective representation produced by the gathering, which is closer to `⇒` production than to a catalyst; needs a longitudinal test |
| cycle | the output re-enters as input (annual repetition, the rite renewing the group that performs it) | yes, and the sign matters: a rite can bind or can exhaust |

The catalyst analogue is the weakest and is recorded as such rather than dropped.

## CONCLUSION

The union is ordered anchor -> vocabulary -> objective -> habitat, and a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds, leaving a compound. The ritual form is a molecule whose bond, not its ingredient list, carries the felt difference: rites sharing atoms in different orders are isomers under Fig 12, and rites sharing the core substructure are a functional group. A settled rite becomes an atom for the tradition at second order. The type of the felt comparison `⋛` remains an open strain requiring an instrument.
