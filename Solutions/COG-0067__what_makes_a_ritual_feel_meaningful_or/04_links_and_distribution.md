## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** - **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Send the residual and refuter first. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 4 :22** - **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A recording of a conclusion sent before anyone has described a residual is an instance trying to cross upward. Send the generator (the derivation and the refuter), not the conclusion.
- **Fig 9 :27** - **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :35** - **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Do not ask for endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-constraint shape and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three public links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 - **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits this: a generator can be given away without giving away an answer.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is a simulated derivation, so a real check is required before this is used as more than a prompt for refutation.
