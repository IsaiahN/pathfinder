# Expert verification packet - the Tether on ritual meaning (COG-0067)

**What this is:** a claim in a form a ritual-studies scholar or cultural psychologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural limits, not `grounded`.** No participant-level measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The participant's own verdict is the anchor for "felt meaningful", because the question is constitutively about people; arousal, neural synchrony and observer readings are instruments that can be wrong on axes they do not measure. That verdict is per participant, not a group average: convergence under co-presence is a result about agreement, not proof the rite worked for each person. Shared felt meaning is a convention in which agreement is the product, while a world-outcome claim needs a ground where agreement is disqualifying. Felt meaning is a property of participant-and-habitat together and is carried by the arrangement of acts, so the same acts in a different order are a different substance. The framework returns this shape; it does not name a single cause.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2
- **Statement:** the legitimate anchor for a question about people's felt experience is those people's own verdict. Non-participant measures are instruments, not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* The line continues: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Refuter:** show that "feels meaningful to participants" is not constitutively about participants' own verdict, or produce an outside instrument that tracks felt meaning on every axis the participant report tracks and more, so that it becomes the legitimate anchor. Until then, no EEG or observer measure may settle the claim.

### C2 - cross-domain; checkable against ritual studies and cultural psychology
- **Statement:** agreement plays two different roles that must not be merged. Agreement about shared meaning is the product of a convention. Agreement about a world outcome is disqualifying as evidence, because the agreeing frames share an evidence pool. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 - *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :43 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** Durkheim's collective effervescence (Wikipedia entry opened 2026-11-22) describes the unification of the group, and Legare and Nielsen (2020) call rituals *"socially stipulated, conventional behaviours"*. Do these describe the convention half rather than a world-outcome claim? Cho et al. (2018) report convergence of efficacy ratings in groups; is that a change in an agreement statistic, not a ground reading?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a rite whose world-outcome claim is settled by agreement alone with no independent ground, or a shared meaning produced without convergent alignment. Either fires independently.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a world-facing check
- **Statement:** felt meaning is participant-and-habitat and is carried by the arrangement of acts, not the ingredient list; the verdict is per participant.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 12 :94 - *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** Irving et al. (2024) find that belief and participation effects depend on social context and role, and Cho et al. (2018) find that co-presence changes agreement with an identical stimulus. Does a permutation of the ordered acts, with atoms and participants fixed, change the felt verdict in your data? That is the Fig 12 isomer test.
- **Refuter:** show that felt meaning is invariant under action-order permutation with atoms fixed, or that an agent-only or form-only change moves the ground with contact held constant. Fig 11 :72 already names the second: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the cause is effervescence, arousal, synchrony, belief, cost or any single variable. Those are provenanced imports, not framework names (Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that a ritual is objectively efficacious because participants agree.
- Not that a group mean settles the per-person question.
- Not that the six-term substrate names a ritual mechanism; it governs composition legality only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by participant measurement in this derivation.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a final answer. The cause slot stays open.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A participant-level variation that changes the verdict while the other conditions are held fixed: order permutation (isomer test), co-presence change with identical stimulus, or belief change with identical form and habitat.
2. An instrument that discriminates among the candidate sources of a convergent rating, rather than merely registering arousal or agreement.
3. A ground reading in which the participant's own verdict moves while contact is held constant (which would fire Fig 11's own falsifier).
