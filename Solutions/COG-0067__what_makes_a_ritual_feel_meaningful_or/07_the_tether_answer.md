# The Tether's answer on ritual meaning

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural limits, not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement settles the binding, so every verdict is `candidate`.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same rite lands as meaningful for some people and hollow for others, sometimes in the same gathering. The question bundles at least three conditions: the participant, the form, and the habitat (co-presence, repetition, transmission). Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** So it is unbundled before it is searched, and the answer is kept as a shape.

## 2. The shape (four constraints, kept separate and never averaged)

1. **The anchor is the participant's own verdict.** Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** The question asks what *feels* meaningful *to participants*, so an outside measure (arousal, neural synchrony, an observer's interpretation) is an instrument, not the ground. Instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (same line).
2. **The verdict is per person, not a group average.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Cho et al. (2018) found that group efficacy ratings converged (variance down about 60 percent). That is a fact about agreement, not proof the rite worked for each participant.
3. **Convention and verdict are different quantities.** Fig 2 :41: **"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**, while Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**, and Fig 2 :43: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. Shared felt meaning is a convention and convergence is its product. A claim that the rite produces a world outcome needs a ground where agreement is disqualifying.
4. **Felt meaning is participant-and-habitat, carried by the arrangement.** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** So form matters as a bond, not as an ingredient list; two rites with the same acts in a different order are Fig 12 isomers.

**No single cause is named.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. The single-cause slot is a `gap` at this budget with a pre-registered refuter, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the cause is "collective effervescence", arousal, synchrony, belief or cost. These are imports from the ritual-science frame, provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`, not framework derivations.
- Not that a ritual is objectively efficacious because participants agree it is. That would let a convention decide an outcome (Fig 2 :43).
- Not that averaging a congregation's ratings settles the question. Fig 1 :22.
- Not that changing a rite's form in isolation will change anything while contact is held fixed. Fig 11 :40.
- Not `grounded`. No participant-level measurement at a discriminating instrument was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

Ground means a participant's own verdict under a controlled variation, not further argument. The discriminating questions are:

- Does the verdict move when the **order** of the same acts changes (Fig 12 `→`, the isomer test)?
- Does it move when **co-presence** changes with the same stimulus (Cho et al. 2018 already show agreement moves; the per-slot question is still open)?
- Does it move when **belief** or expectation changes with the same form and habitat (Irving et al. 2024 report context-dependent interactions)?
- Standing falsifiers: an outside instrument that outperforms the first-person verdict on every axis; a union lever that clears all conditions at once; an agent-only change that moves the ground with contact fixed.

A search that finds no single cause is not a proof of absence: Fig 8 :39 **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four constraints are checkable now; the cause slot is not filled. The six-term substrate and the operator set are a visible guess compressed from many frames, not nature's own list. The canonical README: the framework stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is honest. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Prestige or agreement is not an instrument: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, 2026-11-19).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the single thing that makes a ritual feel meaningful. It returns a shape with four constraints. The anchor is the participant's own verdict, because the question is constitutively about people; outside measures are instruments that can be wrong on the axes they do not measure. That verdict is per person, so a group average hides the live signal. Shared felt meaning is a convention where agreement is the product, but a claim about a world outcome needs a ground where agreement is disqualifying. And felt meaning is a property of participant and habitat together, carried by the arrangement of acts, so the same acts in a different order are a different thing. What the package offers is that shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the variation that would close it. The cause slot stays open until the ground settles it.
