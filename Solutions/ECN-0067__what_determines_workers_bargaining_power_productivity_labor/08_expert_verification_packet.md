# Expert verification packet: the Tether on workers' bargaining power

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labor economist or industrial-relations researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural shape, `general` on the cross-kind and anchor claims, `gap` on the empirical ranking. Not `grounded`.** No wage or employment measurement is claimed by this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The five listed determinants are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition, so "which one?" is the wrong form of question (Fig 9 :55). Productivity is a support condition: it sets the size of the surplus, not the worker's claim on it (Fig 13 :22, Fig 11 :40). Competition and employer concentration are two readings of one buyer network, at least a functional group and candidates for Fig 12's isomers. Unions introduce an actor into the habitat and persist across uses; law arrives as a provenanced import from the polity, and a declared rule is not its own ground (Fig 6 :14, Fig 10 :18). The figures deliver this shape and its refuters; they do not deliver a coefficient, so the empirical ranking is recorded as `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 11

- **Statement:** the five conditions overlap, and the residual must be tracked per slot rather than aggregated into one winner. Productivity is a support gate, not a lever; capability is worker-and-habitat, not worker alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :40 - "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **Refuter:** show that the five conditions are independent (unions never change effective competition, law never changes union viability or floors, concentration is not a reading of the same network as competition), or show a controlled productivity rise with all contact terms fixed that moves the worker's share in the same proportion.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against the labor literature

- **Statement:** competition and employer concentration are two readings of one buyer-network condition, not two independent determinants. At minimum they form a functional group; the stronger isomer claim is falsifiable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :24 - "that entities relate, and influence travels". Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123 - "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **What to check:** does the concentration measure and the competition measure in a given study move together, or can they be shown to move independently? Note the conflict recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md`: Wikipedia, Monopsony, says "Empirical evidence of monopsony power has been relatively limited" and cites a 2020 review in which most economists do not ascribe notable monopsony effects to labor markets. That conflict was sent back to the census and produced the open-magnitude verdict; it was not smoothed away.
- **Refuter:** repeated measures showing concentration falling while workers' outside options worsen, or the reverse. That kills the one-network reading and leaves two independent determinants.

### C3 - framework-internal plus provenance; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 10 and a statute

- **Statement:** law is an import from a frame whose closure differs from the firm-worker frame. A statute has provenance (date, jurisdiction, text) and owes a shadow test (what gap does it explain?). Its declaration is not a measurement of its effect, and a rule that directly fixes the outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14 - "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 10 :18 - "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 10 :47 - "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** is the legal change being cited with its date and jurisdiction, and is the measured outcome reported separately from the text of the rule? The NLRA 1935 entry in Wikipedia, Collective bargaining, is an example of a provenanced import; the wage effect is a separate measurement.
- **Refuter:** show that the legal rule changing the claim is derived entirely inside the firm-worker frame as a new composition of the same atoms, with no contact with a differing closure. Then it is a mint, not an import, and C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a league table. No determinant is ranked first, and no coefficient is asserted.
- Not that productivity is irrelevant. It is the support condition; Fig 11 :40 bars treating it as an agent-only lever, not as a cause of surplus.
- Not that unions are always beneficial. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A 5 to 10 percent wage markup and a 0.2 to 0.5 percent of GDP deadweight loss are both reported in Wikipedia, Collective bargaining.
- Not that a law's text is its measured effect, and not that law is a seventh bond between worker and firm (Operators table :68).
- Not `grounded`. This packet reports a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the labor-economics question it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A separating instrument, not further agreement. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Here:

1. A design that moves productivity with alternatives, union status and law held fixed.
2. Repeated measures that separate employer concentration from workers' outside options.
3. A legal change with its provenance and its measured wage and employment outcomes reported separately.
4. A figure line that directly governs the ranking, which would close the `gap` in F6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 11 :40, Fig 13 :24, Fig 12 :117 and :123, Fig 6 :23, Fig 10 :18. Nine short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own reading of the monopsony and union literature: are concentration and competition two measures of one buyer network, or independent conditions?
3. Name one design that would separate two of the five slots, or say why the overlap makes it impossible.
4. Send the refutation, not an endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement.
