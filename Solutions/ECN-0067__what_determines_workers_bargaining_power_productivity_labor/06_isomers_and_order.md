# Isomers, catalysts and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the cross-kind rule. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).**

## RESIDUAL

The question lists five determinants as if they were one flat set of alternatives. Three derivations are owed: (1) are competition and employer concentration two determinants or two arrangements of one? (2) what structural role do unions and law play? (3) in what dependency order must the five be characterized? A fourth is implicit: does the flat list itself commit a notation error?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the figures: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. In the labor-economics frame, the five determinants have nameable anchors (see `01`, section 1). No new figure primitive is available without import. The five are bindings from the world frame onto held atoms, not new members of the six.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the five-item list a union or a partition?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- The overlaps are specific: unions change effective competition; law changes union viability and sets wage floors; concentration is a reading of the same buyer network that competition describes.
- ⇒ **union compound.** A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. `general`, triangulated with Fig 1 :19 and :22.

### Q2 - Are competition and concentration isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 13 :24 names Network as "that entities relate, and influence travels". Both "labor-market competition" and "employer concentration" read the same buyer network. The stronger claim is that they are **isomers**: same network atoms, different arrangement of the measurement, different behavior in the model. The weaker claim, which survives without a figure re-census, is that they are a **functional group** with different qualifiers.
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." So which of the two is used in a study is not a cosmetic choice: it changes the substance of the claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if repeated measures show employer concentration falling while workers' outside options worsen, or the reverse, the two are independent determinants, not two readings of one network. The isomer claim fails and the single-network reading must be withdrawn. Recorded as the live empirical strain in `01`, F3.

### Q3 - What are unions and law structurally?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A union and a statute both persist across bargaining rounds and are not consumed by one use. They are catalyst-like parts, not one-off events.
- Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A union is an actor introduced into the habitat; the habitat is not subtracted by removing it.
- Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A union is a benefactor relative to a wage residual and a malfactor relative to an employment residual. The collective-bargaining source records both: a 5 to 10 percent wage markup and a 0.2 to 0.5 percent of GDP deadweight loss.
- Law is different in kind. Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall" is import, and Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." A statute comes from the polity frame. Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." A catalyst-like persistence is not the same as a figure-internal bond; neither unions nor law are a seventh operator. Operators table :68: "A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."

### Q4 - Is the flat list a notation error?

- Fig 12 :94 (above) and Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
- Writing "productivity OR competition OR unions OR law OR concentration" uses a flat disjunction. Fig 12 :90-91 warns that a relation failing all six bond tests is not a missing bond but a claim not yet made. The relation among these five is not disjunction; it is overlap and dependency.
- ⇒ The question's grammar is a **flat formula** in the sense of Fig 12 collapse 6: it records ingredients and not arrangement. `general`, single-source for the notation claim (Fig 12 with Operators table corroborating).

### Q5 - What order do the five come in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- Fig 5 :20: "is there anything to explain?" is the support gate before a search.
- Dependency order: **D1 surplus** (a difference must exist to be spent) before **D2 contact structure** (who can spend it against whom) before **D3 actor configuration** (one worker or many acting together) before **D4 legal frame** (rules imported from the polity that license or forbid the earlier moves) before **D5 settlement** (measured outcomes).
- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
- The five do not all have the same kind. A candidate binding, marked as a binding and not a derivation:
  - productivity sets a spendable difference, Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent" (Gradient, a structure);
  - the wage ordering is a ranking, Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said" (Spectrum, a structure);
  - competition and concentration are relations among buyers, Fig 13 :24 (Network, a structure);
  - the match sits in a state (Time or Energy); law is an import, not one of the six.
- Three structures cannot compose directly with one another under Fig 13 :75; each needs a state between. This is a live structural constraint on any attempt to compose the determinants into one term. It is recorded as a **strain, not smoothed**: the binding is `specified` and single-source for the figure mapping, and the empirical literature is the ground that would settle which athlete occupies which slot. Re-census does not remove the strain because the objects differ (Fig 13 governs the six primitives; the labor frame governs measured determinants). No figure line is forced.

### Q6 - Does any determinant qualify as a seventh primitive?

- Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."
- Fig 13 :43-44: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."
- None of the five determinants is indivisible in this sense: each is a composition of measured quantities in the labor frame (output, counts of employers, coverage, statutes). So none is a seventh figure primitive. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." If someone proposes a new determinant, the question is whether it is a new arrangement of held atoms or a new kind; only the latter would move the wall (Fig 6 :14), and that needs an import with provenance.

## CONCLUSION

The five determinants are a union compound in a flat notation. Competition and concentration are at least a functional group and are candidates for Fig 12's isomers, with a stated falsifier. Unions and law are catalyst-like persistent parts; law additionally arrives as a provenanced import from a differing closure. The dependency order is surplus, contact, actor, legal frame, settlement. The three structure-like determinants cannot compose directly with one another under Fig 13 :75; that is a named strain, not a hidden one. No determinant is a seventh primitive. This rescues the defensible content of the question, which is a checkable structural shape, without the league table the figures do not license.

## FALSIFIER FOR THE WHOLE DERIVATION

If a single controlled manipulation is found that changes all five slots independently, with measured outcomes that cleanly separate them, the union-compound claim loses its main empirical motivation and the flat list may be closer to right than this derivation allows. If, in addition, a figure re-census admits a direct structure-structure composition among the three structure-like determinants, Q5's strain dissolves and the five could compose into one term. Both would be recorded, not ignored.
