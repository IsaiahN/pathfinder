# The Tether's answer: when are species interchangeable?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the ecology-facing claims, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a decision procedure, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a boundary: when may one species stand in for another without loss of function, and when is that claim a misleading reading? The residual is per species-slot and per function-slot, not one community-wide number. It is a **union compound** of at least three conditions (trait equivalence, bond equivalence, instrument equivalence), so it must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). It has its own ground: a substitution experiment with per-slot measurements.

## 2. The shape

1. **Interchangeability is the identity bond, settled by substitution.** Fig 12 :66 "two names, one referent"; Fig 12 :69 "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." Trait resemblance is the hypothesis; the substitution reading is the settlement. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" means the framework returns the shape of the test, not the name of the pair.

2. **The residual lives per slot.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". A flat community curve is compatible with one species-slot carrying a live residual. That is the formal core of misleading redundancy. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

3. **Same traits, different arrangement, different substance.** Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." A functional-group label records ingredients. It cannot distinguish a genuine substitute from an isomer.

4. **Redundancy is relative to a residual in a habitat, and moves with conditions.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fetzer et al. 2015 is the empirical counterpart: *"Species that are functionally redundant in one environment may become pivotal in another."*

5. **Scoping stops too early at direct contact.** Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A pair interchangeable at first order can fail at third order.

6. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The anchor here is the substitution measurement, not a consensus of functional-group lists.

7. **The answer stops at the instruments.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". No species pair is named. The lineage of conditions and slots is the term.

Per-domain verdicts, kept separate and never averaged:

| domain | verdict | status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 trait and function | bond settled by substitution; traits only hypothesize | `triangulated` (Fig 12, Fig 8, convergence literature) |

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named pair of species is interchangeable. No substitution experiment was read or run for a specific pair.
- Not that functional groups are useless. They are filters for where to measure (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not that redundancy never exists. If the pre-registered settlement refuter fires, the pair is genuinely interchangeable under the tested conditions.
- Not that the figure's habitat is an ecological niche. The source essay declines that mapping; see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a field or laboratory reading.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A substitution assay, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, read this session) states that where several paths give one reading, *"behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and that the case was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the better instrument has five properties:

1. **Per slot.** Measure each species-slot separately, never only the community total.
2. **Reciprocal.** Substitute A for B and B for A; the identity test is symmetric.
3. **Two conditions at minimum.** The Fetzer result shows verdicts flip with environment.
4. **Cascade depth.** Include at least a third-order effect, or state the horizon explicitly and accept the limitation.
5. **Independent frame.** The tester must not share the evidence pool that produced the grouping (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").

Standing falsifiers from `01` §1: a zero-residual substitution across slots and conditions; a trait-only method that licenses the bond; an aggregate curve that predicts substitution outcomes as well as per-slot readings; a bond case the Operators table cannot represent.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is closed by the figures; the species names are not. The canonical README says the framework returns a shape, not a name, and stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* That is where this answer stops. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 13 :128-129 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." The record of conditions, slots and refruters is what makes the stop checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that functional redundancy is real or that it is a myth. It says that "interchangeable" is a symmetric test, not a resemblance: put one species where the other was and see whether anything is lost at each measured slot. A species list can tell you what ingredients are present and cannot tell you the arrangement, so two species in one functional group may be isomers rather than copies. A community-wide curve can sit near zero while one species-slot carries a live residual, which is the formal reason apparent redundancy misleads. The verdict also depends on the habitat and on indirect effects: a species substitutable in one environment can become pivotal in another, and a first-order substitute can fail three steps out. Agreement among ecologists who read the same literature is a convention until an independent substitution measurement settles it. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. It names no species pair, and this is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.
