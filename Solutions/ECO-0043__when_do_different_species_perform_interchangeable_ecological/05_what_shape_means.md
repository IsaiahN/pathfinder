## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the case

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, and Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*: the honest report is a shape plus what is owed, not an invented species name.

From *The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And on method: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*

For this question the shape is: **substitutability is a symmetric relation settled by a ground measurement at a particular slot, in a particular habitat, over a horizon that includes indirect effects; resemblance supplies only a hypothesis.** That is a shape. "Species A and species B are interchangeable" is a name, and it requires a measurement this session did not take.

The same essay's stopping rule applies: you know you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, because *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here that means the vocabulary of slot, bond, condition and cascade is the answer-shape; there is no further sentence in which a particular pair is named by the framework itself.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: describe the effect, then pick a frame

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge: Why Context, Subtext and Pretext Matter*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

Applied here: describe the effect (substitution leaves the measured function unchanged at this slot under this condition), then go looking for the frame that predicts it. If instead you start with "functional redundancy" and map species onto it, any group you pick will seem to fit. That is the inversion the exemplar calls apophenia.

*Library of Babel* on proximity: use the vocabulary you already hold that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. For this question the starting vocabulary is "same role", and the new terms acquired are per-slot residual, identity bond, isomer, condition dependence, cascade depth, and independent anchor.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For this question, a controlled reciprocal substitution with per-slot measurements under contrasting conditions is the instrument.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50). The Fetzer et al. 2015 experiment is such an import: it arrived independently in microbial ecology and predicts the shape of our residual, namely that redundancy verdicts flip with environment.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* sharpens the limit: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* A functional-group list is likewise a visible guess, not nature's own list. Turning it into an identity claim makes the guess invisible. The same essay's central distinction, *"Outputs are not generators"*, is the reason a list of species traits cannot by itself generate the substitution verdict.

## 4. Why "indiscernible" is not "interchangeable"

*How Intelligence Actually Works*, which I read in full this session, states the rule most directly relevant to ecology:

> *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity. The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect."*

And, in the same essay's Rule 4: *"you can find instances where similar paths of varying derivatives could create similar or potentially identical derivations. Only their provenance chains/lineage would be able to distinguish them, and those get lost through transformation over time. This would also explain why and how divergence and convergence can co-occur in any lineage or system."*

For ecology: a flat reading of function F may be indiscernible between species A and B under the instrument currently used. That does not make A and B identical, and it does not license substituting them for other functions, in other habitats, or under other conditions. The lineage (which function, which slot, which condition, which horizon) is part of the term. Dropping it produces the misleading redundancy the question asks about.

The same essay's operational stopping rule: *"It stops when nothing you have registers a difference from it. That's operational, and it's the only version that doesn't require knowing the answer in advance."* So "interchangeable" means: no registered difference at the slots and instruments under discussion. It is not a metaphysical identity claim.

This is also the metamerism point of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* An aggregate function curve can be produced by several different species-level arrangements. The essay's example is resolved by an instrument: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* In ecology, the better instrument is a per-slot substitution assay, not a further argument about group membership.

## 5. Contact, independence and why the reviewer cannot be a believer

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* again: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are 'one frame wearing two names'"* and *"A shared mindset lets two people drift together without either one noticing."* Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* carries the same rule into a different setting: *"Selection between rooms is where the ground votes"*, and it names the failure of averaging with Fig 1's line, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* It also restates Fig 11 :50 in ecological language: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another."*

The practical consequence for outreach: the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the theory. A reply is a correction on shape, not a vote of confidence.

## 6. Retraction: the habitat and the ecological niche are not the same thing

The *Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture* essay (2026-08-19), in its section "Three mappings offered and declined", says:

> *"The habitat as an ecological niche. A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes. The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align, which is why the figure says enumerated, never composed rather than borrowing the ecological term outright."*

**Earlier reading retracted.** An early draft of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` treated ecological niche as a synonym for the figure's habitat. That is not available. The figure's habitat is enumerated from contact with a specific residual; an ecological niche is a relation to an environment. They overlap in vocabulary and not in definition. All uses of Fig 11 in this folder are structural: actors, conditions, relations, cascade. No claim is made that the figure proves anything about niche theory. The same essay's "A note on what is not cited" lists *"an actor's valence is relative to the residual rather than a property it carries"* as a claim for which no prior source was found. The ecology literature fetched this session (Fetzer 2015, the keystone article) is therefore genuine external corroboration of that structural rule, while still not establishing the figure's definition.

## 7. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of well-known ecologists who agree that a guild is redundant is the named collapse. A single substitution assay that fails is worth more.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *Arrival of the Fittest* adds the warning that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So every claim here is labelled `general` or `specified`, and none is `grounded`. `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
