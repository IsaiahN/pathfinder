## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, when two species may be treated as performing interchangeable roles and when that reading misleads. Option A: treat membership in a shared functional group as interchangeability. Option B: treat interchangeability as an identity bond settled by substitution at each species-slot, in a stated habitat, over a horizon that includes indirect effects, and checked independently. What each leads to: A clears a region fast but leaves a flat formula that cannot tell a genuine substitute from an isomer, hides live slot residuals in an aggregate, and borrows a verdict from one environment; B is slower, states its conditions, and produces a refusable term. The figures decide between them.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two indices, species-slot and function-slot. Symmetry: substitutability is the symmetric identity bond; the ecological relations that carry the function (sequence, production, comparison) are ordered. Scale: first-order direct contact through third-order cascade; one condition through changed condition. Fig 9 names it a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It must be unbundled before search.
- **Domain 1, trait and function frame.** Actors: species as trait bundles, the measured process. Currency: effect on a named function. Anchor: the per-slot measurement under substitution. Question type: partition within a level (identity or not).
- **Domain 2, habitat and contact frame.** Actors: species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground reading of the function under that condition. Question type: union of condition and cascade, so it needs its own loop.
- **Domain 3, verification frame.** Actors: ecologists, reviewers, datasets, experiments. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on the same evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Domain 4, nomenclature only.** Actors: names on a functional-group list. Currency: labels. Anchor: none. **Not a domain yet.** Excluded, and recorded as the source of the misleading reading.
- **Order by dependency.** Measure per slot before aggregating; have terms for what each species affords before stating what counts as no loss; settle the bond before composing over it; describe the residual before choosing a literature frame (Fig 9 :27-28). The middle link is usually uninstrumented.

Strain test applied: the aggregate saturation curve and the per-slot rule strain only if the curve is treated as a verdict. Re-census: they govern different quantities, a budget filter and a witness (Fig 9 :60). A second strain, the literal reading of identity between two organisms, is dissolved by *"indiscernibility is not identity"* from the canonical README: at most the function is indiscernible under substitution, and the lineage is kept.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Interchangeability is the identity bond for a named function, settled by substitution at each species-slot, in a stated habitat, over a horizon that includes indirect effects, and checked by an independent frame. Trait similarity and group membership are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Apparent redundancy misleads when the reading is aggregated across slots, confuses ingredients with arrangement, stops at direct contact, is borrowed from one environmental condition, or comes from a shared evidence pool. This is a `general` structural shape with `specified` ecology-facing claims, ungrounded. No species pair is named. The bespoke strain is that figure-habitat is not ecological niche; the source essay declines that mapping and the retraction is visible.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The decision procedure states its conditions and its refuter before the conclusion, so an error can be found rather than hidden. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a group label as if it were a verdict; Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The flat reading is removable without touching the derivation. An aggregate score can be replaced by per-slot measurements, a single-condition verdict by a two-condition assay, and a group consensus by an independent frame. Nothing has to be withdrawn from the record; the retracted habitat/niche mapping stays visible as a correction.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A reciprocal substitution assay showing zero residual at every slot including third-order effects under two contrasting conditions would settle a pair and reverse the claim that its redundancy is apparent; an aggregate curve that predicts substitution outcomes as well as per-slot readings would reverse the per-slot rule; a trait-only method that licenses the bond would reverse the settlement requirement; a bond case the Operators table cannot express would send the census back. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
