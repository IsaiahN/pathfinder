## Two things in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Fetzer et al. 2015 is the closest ground reading available in the sources.** It reports that the *shape* of the biodiversity-function relationship changes with environment: a saturation curve in one environment reads as redundancy, a near-linear curve in another reads as every added species mattering. The paper states that initial conclusions about redundancy *"can be completely misleading when based on a BEF relationship for a given environment alone."* That is an empirical instance of the figure rule that a global reading with one live slot is a legal state. It does not settle any particular species pair; it shows why the question must be asked per slot and per condition.
2. **Keystone species literature is a caution about the aggregate reading.** Paine's Pisaster was a small part of the biomass and removing it collapsed the community from 15 species to essentially one. The Wikipedia article also records that the same starfish was *"just another sea star"* where its mussel prey was absent. Those two facts together match the figure rule that valence is relative to the residual and not carried by the actor. The mapping is structural, not a claim that figure-habitat equals ecological niche: the source essay declines that mapping explicitly (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).
