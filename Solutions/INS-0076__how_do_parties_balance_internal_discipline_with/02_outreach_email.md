# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two live constraints in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 are stated honestly in any reply. The output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about party discipline and diverse views

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about parties. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how do parties balance internal discipline with representation of diverse views?" assumes one dial. The framework says that is the wrong object. Agreement changes kind with what it is about: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41), while *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Those are two different quantities with two different anchors. Discipline is efficient for the first. It cannot certify the second, because members of one party usually read the same briefings.
>
> What follows is a separator, not a number:
> 1. Discipline is a convention. It fails when *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47).
> 2. Diverse views are the independence a verdict needs, but only where frames draw on different evidence. *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* (Fig 2 :42).
> 3. Residual stays indexed per member and constituency. *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22).
> 4. A party needs a refusal seat. Without negation a vocabulary *"can only accumulate"* (Operators table :58). A free vote or a recorded minority report is the analogue, not a derived institution.
> 5. Which views belong in the room is a function of the current question. *"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure."* (Fig 10 :72). No standing list of good factions survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this returns an optimal whip strength, a cohesion percentage, or a universal mix. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The ratio is a declared gap, and *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134). I am also not claiming that a free vote is literally the framework's negation operator; that mapping is an analogue with provenance.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. A verbatim figure line that returns a universal discipline-to-diversity ratio.
> 2. A party where internal agreement about a world-facing fact, among frames sharing one evidence pool, moved a decision toward a non-updating ground rather than only producing a convention.
> 3. A party that corrected a false held term with no refusal seat and no minority record, while every member could reconstruct the line.
> 4. A fixed room that kept paying at the ground across several changed questions with no substitution of actors.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Party discipline and factions: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_discipline and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faction
> - Consensus, dissent and groupthink: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupthink
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a party you know well and can check against a ground (an election, a membership decision, a recorded vote). Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and any output can be contrived to fit. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>

| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
