# The Tether's answer: party discipline and diverse views

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural separator, `gap` on a universal ratio. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A party must act as one and must keep a live signal from heterogeneous members and constituencies. The question treats these as opposite settings of one dial. The figures say the dial does not exist: the two jobs have different anchors, different currencies and different operators. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

## 2. The shape, per domain

Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**D1, convention and coordination.** Discipline is efficient for a convention. Fig 2 :41: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* A shared brand and a coordinated vote are the product here, not a corruption. It fails when promoted to a verdict: Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. A binding line that substitutes for a member's reading of the constituency is that promotion. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 :41 and Fig 10 :47), with the party's unavoidable stake declared under Fig 10 :59.

**D2, verdict and world-facing judgment.** Diverse views are useful for the same reason two instruments are better than one reader: independence. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* This holds only at low overlap. A faction that reads the same briefing as the leadership is one frame wearing two names. The anchor must not move when the party argues: Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 8), with the low-overlap condition named.

**D3, habitat and room composition.** Which views belong is a function of the question. Fig 10 :72: *"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A faction is not good or bad by type; Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Expelling a member changes the party's closure, not the habitat (Fig 11 :61). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 :71-72 and Fig 11 :49-51).

**D4, budget and measurement.** No anchor for a universal number. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The figures return the rule for buying, not the quantity bought. Verdict: `general` on the rule, `gap` on the number. Unreachable is a verdict, not silence (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. The four moves that follow

1. **Index the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Report where the live disagreement is, not only how united the party looks.
2. **Install a refusal seat.** Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A vocabulary that cannot say no can only add planks. The institutional analogue is a free vote, a recorded minority report or an outside frame. This is an analogue with provenance, not a derivation of ¬.
3. **Keep the bond visible.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* A disciplined and a pluralist party over the same members are different substances, not different amounts. The whip and the big tent are different bond classes (ordered versus symmetric), not ends of one scale.
4. **Hold the currencies apart.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Description cost in bits and settling cost in actions do not sum into a cohesion score.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a cohesion percentage, an optimal whip strength, a number of factions, or a universal mix. That is the declared `gap`.
- Not "diversity is always good." Shared-evidence diversity adds no surplus and can corrupt a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- Not "discipline is always bad." It is efficient for a convention (Fig 2 :41) and a party without a bond cannot act at all (Fig 12 :5).
- Not that a free vote, a primary or a caucus is literally a figure operator. Each is a binding onto a held institutional atom, recorded as such.
- Not that expelling a dissenter removes the view. It changes the party's closure only (Fig 11 :61).
- Not `grounded`. No election result or party record was measured here.

## 5. What would settle it, and the instruments owed

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened) says an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case, not a debate. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Source test for cohesion.** Does a given 99.6 percent party-line rate come from shared conviction, a shared evidence pool, career incentives or a sanction? An instrument that separates these would name the source instead of reading it off the projection.
- **Anchor test.** Does the constituency verdict move when the caucus changes its line? If it does, the proposed anchor is another frame and the arrangement has drifted coherently (Fig 2 :34).
- **Refusal test.** Can a member refuse the line and have the refusal enter the record without expulsion? If not, the party can accumulate but cannot carry a correction (Operators :58).
- **Room test.** When the issue changes, does the set of voices in the room change? If not, the room is fixed while the question moves (Fig 10 :72).
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a figure line returning a universal ratio; shared-pool agreement improving a world-facing verdict at a non-updating ground; correction without any refusal seat; a fixed room tracking several changed questions; the convention and verdict collapsing into one quantity; the two currencies trading in one unit.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, and partly underivable in principle.** The figures return a rule, a separator and a shape. They do not return a number, and no amount of additional reading inside the closed set will add one (Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*). Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The record stops where the instruments stop distinguishing, and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say how much discipline is the right amount, because the question is the wrong shape. Internal agreement does two different jobs: it is efficient for a shared meaning, and it is worthless as evidence about the world when the people agreeing read the same sources. So a party needs coordination and independence for different reasons, with different anchors, and the two cannot be traded on one dial. What is returned is a separator: keep the disagreement indexed per member and constituency rather than averaging it into a unity score, install a real refusal seat so the party can correct itself, let the set of voices change when the question changes, and buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. A disciplined party and a pluralist party over the same members are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, not points on a scale. The universal ratio is a declared gap. The falsifiers were written before this run, and an election or a recorded vote, not an agreement among readers, is what would close it.
