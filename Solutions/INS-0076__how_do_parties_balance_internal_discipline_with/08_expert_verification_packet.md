# Expert verification packet: the Tether on party discipline and diverse views

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political-science researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural separator, `gap` on a universal ratio. Not `grounded`.** No party was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How do parties balance internal discipline with diverse views?" asks for one dial. The framework says that is the wrong object. Agreement changes kind with what it is about: it is *"efficient for a convention"* (Fig 2 :41) and *"disqualifying for a verdict"* when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). Discipline is a convention and fails when it decides an outcome; diverse views are the independence a verdict needs, but only at low overlap and in proportion to the cost of being wrong. The residual stays indexed per member and constituency, a refusal seat is required so the party can carry a correction, and the room composition is a function of the current question. A disciplined party and a pluralist party over the same members are isomers, same atoms under different bonds, not points on a scale. No figure line returns a universal mix, so that is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** the two valences of agreement are stated in one figure and govern different quantities. Discipline is efficient for a convention; it stops being a convention when it decides an outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 - *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a binding party procedure that fixes an outcome yet still lets the ground refuse it, with no seat able to say no; or collapse the two valences into one quantity with correct out-of-sample prediction.

### C2. Political-science facing, checkable against the parties literature

- **Statement:** a party whose members draw on one evidence pool gains no verdict-validity from internal agreement. What is required is independence at the evidence level, bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, plus a functioning refusal seat. Fig 2 :42 - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **What to check:** does the discipline literature distinguish a cohesion *reading* from the *source* of that cohesion? The opened sources report a 99.6 percent party-line rate in Canada, describe pressure on members to compromise beliefs, and record that factions *"require a weaker party discipline"* while shifting the party along the ideological spectrum. The groupthink literature adds the key qualifier that dissent, not the absence of cohesion, is what prevents a bad decision. Does that pattern hold in cases you know?
- **Refuter:** a party that corrected a false held term with no refusal seat and no record of minority concerns, where every member could reconstruct the line; or a case where shared-pool agreement moved a decision toward a non-updating ground and was not merely a convention effect.

### C3. Framework-internal derivation, checkable by reading Figure 12 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** over a fixed membership set, a disciplined and a pluralist party are isomers. Their difference is the bond, not the amount. A whip is an ordered bond (sequence plus threshold); a big tent is a symmetric bond. The room must change when the question changes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 10 :72 - *"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **Refuter:** show the two parties with identical bonds and only a scalar whip strength differing, still the same substance at the ground; or show a fixed room tracking several changed questions and continuing to pay at the ground with no substitution of actors.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a cohesion percentage, an optimal whip strength, a faction count or a universal mix. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not "diversity is always good" and not "discipline is always bad." Both have a domain where they are efficient and a domain where they fail.
- Not a derivation of any particular institution. Free votes, primaries, minority reports and caucuses are bindings onto held institutional atoms, provenanced as analogues.
- Not that expelling a member removes the view from the habitat. It changes the party's closure only (Fig 11 :61).
- Not that a party is one of the six substrate terms. The six-term set is not being used as a political taxonomy here; the scale analogue is marked and re-censused in `11_audit.md`.
- Not `grounded`. No election or party record was measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a consensus. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer settled the case.

1. **Source test:** can an instrument separate a party-line rate produced by shared conviction from one produced by a shared briefing or a sanction?
2. **Anchor test:** does the constituency verdict move when the caucus changes line?
3. **Refusal test:** can a member refuse the line and have that refusal enter the record without expulsion?
4. **Room test:** does the set of voices change when the issue changes?
5. **Standing falsifiers:** a figure line returning a universal ratio; shared-pool agreement improving a world-facing verdict at a non-updating ground; correction without a refusal seat; a fixed room tracking several changed questions; the two currencies trading in one unit.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :39-42, Fig 10 :47 and :72, Fig 11 :51 and Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own cases: when a party looks united, can you tell from public records whether that is a shared meaning or a shared evidence pool?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate the sources of a cohesion reading, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
