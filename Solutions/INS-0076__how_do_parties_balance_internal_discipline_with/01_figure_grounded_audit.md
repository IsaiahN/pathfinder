## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question is phrased as a balance: party discipline on one side, representation of diverse views on the other, with some optimal mix in between. Stated as an effect instead:

- A party is a bounded frame that must **act as one** (deliver a common line, a coordinated vote, a recognizable brand) while **keeping a live signal from heterogeneous members and constituencies** (factions, backbenchers, minority views, dissenting readings of the same facts).
- Discipline is what lets a party *act*. Diversity is what lets a party *range against a ground that does not update on the party's own line*.
- The two appear to trade off because the question aggregates them onto one dial. The figures say to check that before accepting it.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"):** a separator that says which quantity each mechanism governs, what anchor settles it, and what would reverse it, plus an explicit statement that a universal numerical mix is not returned. I write this down and let it accrue a residual like any other term; the pre-registered refuter in section 4 is how it can be refused.

**Effect of each option:**
- Treating this as one dial (Option A) invites a single number, treats agreement as evidence, and selects checkers likely to agree. It spends their independence before they have looked at the ground.
- Treating it as a union with per-domain verdicts (Option B) keeps the per-slot signal, preserves a refusal seat, and leaves the numerical mix marked as unreached. It is checkable from outside.

Fig 9 :4-5 splits exactly this case: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The question is a **union**, not a single continuous problem with a right setting.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least two conditions with overlapping intersections (coordination/convention, verdict validity, habitat composition, budget). "Balance" is a binary framing over a union.
- **Symmetry:** discipline and representation are not symmetric coordinates of one scale. They have different currencies and different anchors (Fig 2 :39-41). A pooled a pooled single amount of each assumes a symmetry the figures do not grant.
- **Scale:** per member or per constituency slot (intra-party), and party-versus-ground at the electoral scale. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Convention and coordination | whips, caucus, leadership, party brand, shared meaning | agreement about a shared meaning; coordination | the party's capacity to act as one, checked later by an election that does not update on the line | yes (electoral outcome, not internal sentiment) |
| D2 Verdict and world-facing judgment | factions, backbenchers, internal critics, outside peer frames | independent evidence, residuals per slot | an election, a constituency result, a policy outcome that does not move when the party argues about it | yes (Fig 2 :29) |

"No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet" applies to D4: it is retained as a declared limit, not smuggled in as a fourth domain with a hidden anchor.

### Partition or union

**Union.** The conditions overlap. A disciplined party can still triangulate if its members hold genuinely independent evidence; a diverse party can still fail as a verdict if all members read one source. A single lever (more whipping, or more internal democracy) improves the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Unbundle before searching again (Fig 9 :54).

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Order for this question:

1. **Refuse before mint** (formula step 2): refuse the false "one dial" framing first. The sought shape is held as a preference, not a term, until bound in section 0.
2. **Anchor test** (D2): what settles the question, and does it move when the party pushes on it? Fig 2 :29.
3. **Convention role** (D1): what agreement is *for*, given it cannot be a verdict. Fig 2 :41.
4. **Room composition** (D3): which views are in the room, and does the room track the question. Fig 10 :72.
5. **Budget and gap** (D4): what is owed when no figure line returns a number. Fig 9 :32.

The middle link of Fig 3 is the one that usually breaks: having terms for what a party *can* do with dissent, and being able to say what counts as progress here. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1. Agreement changes kind with what it is about (decisive)

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :41: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*.

These two clauses are in the same figure and govern two different quantities. Internal discipline produces the second kind of agreement: a common meaning, a recognizable brand, a coordinated action. That is efficient for a convention. It cannot produce the first kind, because members of one party typically draw on the same briefings, the same whips' notes and the same media diet. When they agree about the world, that agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :19-21).

Therefore "balance discipline with representation" is not a trade between two amounts of one thing. It is a request for two different jobs. The honest form is: discipline is efficient for a convention, representation supplies the independence a verdict needs, and the two are not commensurable.

- **Refuter:** show a case where internal agreement about a world-facing fact, among frames known to share one evidence pool, moved a decision toward the ground and was not merely a convention effect. That would break the valence distinction.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 :39-41 and Fig 2 :31-32 (two different passages, low internal overlap).

### F2. The anchor must not be the party's own line

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

A whip count, a caucus consensus, a focus group, a internal approval rating: each moves when the party argues about it. None is an anchor. The election and the constituency outcome are closer: the voter does not change their answer because the caucus voted one way. If a party treats its own cohesion metric as the thing to be maximized, panel 3 of Fig 2 turns into panel 1: the reference becomes another frame and everything drifts together coherently, which is why nobody notices (Fig 2 :34).

World check. The Wikipedia article on party discipline reports that an independent evaluation found Canadian MPs voted the party line 99.6 percent of the time between 2015 and 2019, and describes *"immense pressure for parliamentarians to compromise their beliefs"* when they conflict with the leadership decision. A cohesion figure that high is a reading of the frame, not a reading of the ground.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a party whose internal cohesion score changed its electorate's verdict at the next election, with the electorate held independent. That would make the internal metric an anchor.
- **Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side if taken alone, but triangulated with Fig 7 :35 (elections select between rooms).

### F3. Aggregation destroys the per-slot signal

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

"Representation of diverse views" is the per-slot residual made institutionally visible. Each member, faction and constituency is a slot carrying its own live mass. Collapsing them into a single party position is exactly the aggregation Fig 1 forbids when the question is where the live signal is. The framework's own quantity, `R`, is a slice, not a global number (Symbols table).

Consequence: a party that reports only its unity score has thrown away the only signal that says where to look. The answer is not "less discipline" but "do not aggregate the residual when you need to locate it."

- **Refuter:** show a decision where a single aggregated party-line residual located the failing slot as well as per-slot residuals did, across repeated cases. That would rehabilitate aggregation.
- **Status:** `specified`, single-source (Fig 1), corroborated by world evidence on factions (see section 9), so not upgraded to triangulated on the figure side.

### F4. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

So the amount of internal diversity a party should carry is not a constant and not a cultural preference. It is a function of the cost of being wrong on the decision in front of the party. Low-cost, reversible, convention-like decisions (a common press line, a ceremonial vote) need little independence and are better served by discipline. High-cost, hard-to-reverse, world-facing decisions (war, constitutional change, a budget that cannot be undone) need independence bought in proportion to that cost.

This is why "what is the right balance?" has no figure answer. The figures return the *rule for buying*, not the quantity bought. The quantity is residual-relative, which is the shape-not-a-name limit.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a universal independence setting that pays at the ground across high-cost and low-cost decisions alike. That would break the proportionality rule.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 :42 and Fig 8 :47-48.

### F5. A party needs a refusal operator, not only a position list

Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Without negation a system can add positions and cannot refuse one. A party whose institutions only accumulate planks, and in which every member can reconstruct the line on demand, has no internal check. The framework names this failure twice: the vocabulary cannot carry a correction, and the verifier that can reproduce the claim is inside the frame it was to check.

The institutional instances are visible in the world literature: the conscience vote or free vote, in which *"party discipline is waived"* and members vote to individual preference (Wikipedia, Party discipline); and the consensus literature's rule that *"Most robust models of consensus exclude uniformly unanimous decisions and require at least documentation of minority concerns"* (Wikipedia, Consensus decision-making). Both are ways of installing the unary operator ¬ inside a group that would otherwise only accumulate.

Caveat, kept visible: ¬ is a unary operator, not one of the seven bonds (Operators table amendment). A conscience vote is an analogue, not a derivation. Whether a real expulsion or a real free vote is the exact institutional shape of ¬ needs an instrument, not an argument.

- **Refuter:** show a party that corrected a false held term with no refusal seat of any kind, where every member could reconstruct the line and no minority record was kept. That would break the necessity claim.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across the Operators table and Fig 10 :42, with the institutional mapping marked as an analogue.

### F6. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

Discipline is legitimate while it remains a convention about shared meaning. It fails when it is promoted into a verdict: a binding whip that substitutes for the member's own reading of the constituency, a party line declared true because the party declared it, an internal process whose output fixes the answer before the ground is consulted. Declaring a shared meaning does not ground the claim it makes about the world.

The party's stake is structural and unavoidable. Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* An undeclared interest is modelled anyway, and an unnamed model runs unchallenged.

World check: the consensus literature records the mirror-image failure. Giving every member a block *"gives an enormous advantage to anyone who supports the existing state of affairs"* (Wikipedia, Consensus decision-making). A rule that was meant to protect dissent becomes a verdict for the status quo. Both excesses are the same genus: a convention deciding an outcome.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a binding party procedure that fixes an outcome yet still allows the ground to refuse it without any seat able to say no. That would break the promotion rule.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 :47/:49 and Fig 10 :59-60.

### F7. The room must move when the question moves

Fig 10 :71: *"The room composition is a function of the question."* Fig 10 :72: *"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

This is the figure-grounded answer to "which diverse views should be represented?" No standing list. The membership and the factions in the room should be a function of the residual the party currently faces. A coalition assembled for an industrial question is the wrong room for a question about digital privacy, even though the people are the same and well meaning.

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A faction is not a good faction or a bad faction. The same faction is benefactor to one residual and malfactor to another. So the framework cannot rank factions by type; it can only ask whether the current room matches the current question.

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A party can add a member or admit a faction; it cannot remove the fact that the view exists in the electoral habitat. Expelling a member changes the party's closure, not the habitat. This distinction matters because a party that "solves" dissent by expulsion has changed its own vocabulary, not the ground.

- **Refuter:** show a fixed room that tracked a changed question and still paid at the ground across several issue shifts, with no substitution of actors. That would break the function-of-the-question rule.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 :71-72 and Fig 11 :49-51/:61.

### F8. Diversity pays only where closures differ

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :48 (above) makes independence the parameter. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Internal "diversity" that shares one evidence pool is one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49). It improves neither a verdict nor a surplus; it only raises the vocabulary. Genuine representation of diverse views therefore requires low overlap at the evidence level, not just different biographies or different rhetorical styles. A faction that reads the same briefing as the leadership is not a second frame.

Where a genuinely differing closure exists, the import needs a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap). A new faction admitted because it explains a gap the party cannot see is an import that may move the wall. A new faction admitted because it makes the ticket look broad is a wrong atom, and Fig 6 :24 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives.

World check: Scott Page's diversity prediction theorem is quoted in the wisdom-of-the-crowd literature as *"the squared error of the collective prediction equals the average squared error minus the predictive diversity"*, and the same article notes that *"Organizations that leverage the wisdom of the crowd may undermine the learning of their own members"* because *"contrarian opinions are marginalized and not acted upon."* That is the low-overlap condition and the discipline failure, stated in another field.

- **Refuter:** show a surplus produced by two internal frames with measurably shared evidence and no described residual. That would break the independence requirement.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 :45/:47/:48 and Fig 6 :23-24.

### F9. Disciplined and pluralist parties are isomers, not degrees

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Two parties with the same membership atoms can be different substances under a different bond. A disciplined party and a pluralist party over the same members are not "the same party with less or more of something"; they are isomers. Which bond is at issue matters: a whip is a sequence/comparison structure; a factional primary is a different arrangement. `06_isomers_and_order.md` works the bond detail and its falsifier.

Why this matters for the question: if the two are isomers, then searching for the single optimal *degree* of discipline is searching on the wrong axis. Type and bond are the variable. A list of member names will not tell you which substance you have, because the bond is the half nothing was recording.

- **Refuter:** show that a disciplined and a pluralist party with identical membership and identical bonds (only a scalar whip strength differing) remain the same substance at the ground. That would collapse the isomer claim to a degree claim.
- **Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition (Fig 12), with the membership-identity condition named as a live constraint, not hidden.

### F10. The two currencies do not add

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

The cost of describing a common line (bits: the length of the platform, the complexity of the compromise) and the cost of settling whether it holds (actions: votes, deliberations, by-elections, losses) are in different units. A "balance" that adds them, or a score that trades one against the other, is the error this figure names. A very short line can take a whole budget to settle; a long platform can be cheap to refute. Neither figure predicts the other (Fig 12 :128-129).

This is a second reason the single dial fails. Even if you had the two quantities, summing them would not produce a verdict.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a common unit in which description cost and settling cost trade linearly and correctly predict outcomes across cases. That would break the non-additivity claim.
- **Status:** `general` (structural), single-source (Fig 12 :125-127), corroborated by the budget rule at Fig 2 :42.

### F11. No figure line returns a universal mix (gap)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine census, no figure line returns a discipline percentage, an optimal number of factions, a span of internal democracy, or a universal whip strength. What is returned is a rule (buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong), a separator (the two valences of agreement), a composition type (isomer over fixed atoms), and a refusal operator. The universal number is a `gap`, of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind for any specific party and partly underivable in principle as a universal, because valence is residual-relative.

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Any number produced by scoring parties would be a budget filter, not a witness.

- **Refuter:** point to a verbatim figure line that returns the ratio. I re-censused the figure text for `ratio`, `percentage`, `mix`, `balance`, `degree`, `discipline` and `party`; none governs such a quantity. If one is found, the gap is closed.
- **Status:** `gap`, explicitly a verdict.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is: **there is no single discipline-versus-diversity dial; there is a union of conditions with per-domain verdicts, and the universal mix is a gap.**

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A verbatim figure line is found that returns a universal discipline-to-diversity ratio or optimal party cohesion level.
2. A party is shown where internal agreement about a world-facing fact, among frames demonstrably sharing one evidence pool, improved a verdict at a non-updating ground rather than only producing a convention.
3. A party is shown that corrected a false held term with no refusal seat of any kind and with no record of minority concerns, while every member could reconstruct the line.
4. A fixed room is shown to track a changed question and keep paying at the ground across several issue shifts, with no substitution of actors.
5. The convention/verdict split collapses: a shared meaning is shown to settle a world-facing question without any independent anchor.
6. Description cost and settling cost are shown to trade in one common unit with correct out-of-sample prediction.

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Drop the single dial. State the two jobs separately and attach a mode to each sentence (F1, F4).
2. Keep the residual indexed per member and per constituency slot. Do not report only a unity score (F3).
3. Name the anchor for each domain and check that it does not move when the party argues (F2).
4. Install or preserve a refusal seat. A free vote, a recorded minority report, an outside frame: something that can say no (F5).
5. Treat a whip that fixes an outcome as a defect, not as strong leadership (F6).
6. Make room composition a function of the current question, and declare every unavoidable stake (F7).
7. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and record the provenance of any imported faction (F4, F8).
8. Stop at the gap. Send the separator and the refuters, not a number (F11). Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

## 8. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One dial?** No. Fig 9 :55 union; Fig 2 :39-41 two valences.
2. **What settles it?** A non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29), not the cohesion score. Per domain, two anchors.
3. **What is discipline for?** A convention (Fig 2 :41). It stops being one when it fixes an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
4. **What is diversity for?** Independence for a verdict (Fig 2 :40, Fig 8 :47), only at low overlap.
5. **Which views?** A function of the question (Fig 10 :72), with residual-relative valence (Fig 11 :49). No standing list.
6. **Import or re-arrange?** A differing faction is an import with source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :53); a whip change is a bond change over fixed atoms (Fig 12 :117).
7. **Ratio?** None. Gap. Unreachable is a verdict (Fig 13 :134), not silence.

## 9. Updated verdict

The separator survives: two quantities with different anchors, a union of conditions, per-slot residuals, a required refusal operator, a room that tracks the question, and an independence budget proportional to the cost of being wrong. What does not survive is the question's own framing: there is no single balance to strike, and no figure line sets a number. Strains after re-census: two live constraints (fixed atoms for the isomer claim; ¬ versus its institutional analogue), both named. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
