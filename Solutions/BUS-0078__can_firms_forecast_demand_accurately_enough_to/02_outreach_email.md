# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no per-slot measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about demand forecasts, waste and shortages before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about demand forecasting that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a sufficiently accurate forecast is not what removes waste without creating shortages. Two conditions have to hold at once:
>
> 1. the firm's channel to realized demand stays reachable at SKU and period level, meaning its readings are not stale, pooled into an average, or blind to the supply line;
> 2. the firm's forecasting reach can represent the demand that actually arrives, importing signals from ways of seeing that differ from its own sales history when the history has no atom for a shift.
>
> These are two different failure modes with two different instruments. The stronger claim is that a perfect point forecast is not sufficient: what a manager does with the supply line can amplify orders even when the forecast is right. And the literal target of zero shortage is not a target the canonical model pursues; its optimum deliberately tolerates some shortage because overage and underage costs trade off. Accuracy is a filter for deciding where to look, not a verdict about whether waste fell.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which model, algorithm or policy wins, that any particular firm is a case in point, that this is settled by evidence, that the forecasting literature independently corroborates it, or that it is the only account of demand planning. I am offering a shape that any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a SKU loses forecast contact while all the named channel indicators measure healthy and non-degrading;
> 2. a model predicts a demand discontinuity from historical sales alone, with no new signal imported from anywhere;
> 3. accuracy by itself, with no cost outcome, prospectively predicts which named SKU will fail and in which direction;
> 4. model complexity and the cost of acting on the forecast turn out to be commensurable and their single sum predicts the operational outcome per SKU;
> 5. a perfect point forecast plus unchanged channel conditions reduce both waste and shortage across several demand regimes;
> 6. the canonical optimum has zero overage and zero underage for all positive cost pairs.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and canonical usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a forecasting problem you know well and can check against a real ground, that is, realized demand and the cost counters. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Two failure modes kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended accuracy score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Accuracy not called the anchor | *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27) |
| Zero shortage refused as written | *"A goal is a comparison"* (Fig 12 :112), with the two-cost optimum from the operations literature |
| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No name for the winning model | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Once the recipient has the framework, their later agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-22) |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, an opt-in competition, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A direct reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
