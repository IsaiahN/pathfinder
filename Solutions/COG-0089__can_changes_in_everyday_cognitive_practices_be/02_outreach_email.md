# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about reconstructing past cognitive practices from records

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (in effect terms):** we have present-day categories for everyday cognition (reading, remembering, counting, keeping time, telling a story) and a set of historical records (texts, ledgers, diaries, marginalia, objects, digitised corpora). We want to know what a change in the practice would have to look like for these records to be what they are. The question is not whether the records exist. It is whether the route from practice to record can be read back off the artefact.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** changes in everyday cognitive practices can be reconstructed only as a **shape**, meaning a set of constraints on what the practice must have been like, never as a name of a particular past mental state. Three structural reasons, each checkable:
>
> 1. The route that produced a record is not written on the record. The order of a composed thing is recoverable from the lineage, not from the artefact.
> 2. The archive is a habitat, not an anchor. It changes when corpora, catalogues and OCR change, so it cannot settle a question about a past mind by itself.
> 3. Records that agree from a shared source are one witness, not several. Independence, not document count, decides whether agreement means anything.
>
> **What I recorded as the limits before running this:** archival silence is a search report, not an absence; present-day categories are held as a preference until written down as a checkable term; a written record carries context and some subtext but almost never the "you had to be there" part; a global average such as "literacy rose" can hide a live local change.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A record whose features uniquely determine the cognitive practice that produced it, with a ground that discriminates at least two candidate practices.
> 2. An archive whose composition is invariant under the interventions that move real corpora, so it can act as an anchor that does not update.
> 3. Two historically independent records with no shared evidence pool whose agreement survives a check against a third ground that influenced neither.
> 4. An instrument that distinguishes the cognitive sources that yield the same artefact, the way a spectrometer distinguishes the sources of green.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local extract; branch `general-agent` figures on GitHub)
> - The eight-step loop and its mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Worked architecture and citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory withheld | `Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."` The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | `Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."` |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: general, specified, grounded; state which. |
| Shape, not a name | `Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."` The framework returns a shape; naming a past mental state from an artefact is a binding, provenanced as such. |
| Per-slot verdicts | `Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."` |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | `Fig 4 :22-23 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."` A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| Ask the recipient to state their own ground and refuter | The formula's Direction paragraph: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## Recording the verdict before stage 2

Before sending any link, write down in your own notes:

- the recipient's stated failure condition, in their words;
- their verdict on stage 1, given without the framework;
- the date and the exact claim they were shown.

Their later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration. `Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."` A reply that refutes the claim is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
