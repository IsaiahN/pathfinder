# Expert verification packet: the Tether on reconstructing everyday cognitive practices

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian, archivist or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the limits, `general` on the structural rule, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a specific past cognitive practice is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A change in everyday cognitive practice can be reconstructed from historical records only as a shape: a set of constraints on what the practice must have been like, never as a name of a particular past mental state. The route that produced a record is not written on the artefact; the order is recoverable from the lineage. The archive is a habitat that updates with corpus composition, cataloguing and OCR, so it is not an anchor. Records that agree from a shared evidence pool are one witness, not several. An absent record is a search report, not proof that a practice never existed. Verdicts are per practice and per stratum, never averaged. The limiting biases are structural and can be checked from outside; what would close the question is a discriminating trace or instrument, not agreement with a theory.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13 and 12

- **Statement:** the artefact does not carry the route that produced it. A reconstruction of past practice from a record alone can constrain a shape, not name the practice. Two candidate practices with the same surviving features stand as isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 13 :128-129 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."` `Fig 13 :129 "The order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."` `Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."`
- **What to check:** do these lines say what the claim says, or is the claim importing a stronger conclusion? Note the case dependent qualification: a trace can recover a route in some cases.
- **Refuter:** a record whose features uniquely determine the producing practice, with a ground that discriminates at least two candidates. Isotope ratios distinguishing mined from lab grown diamonds are the positive form of this refuter.

### C2 - Archive-facing; checkable by a historian, archivist or bibliometrician

- **Statement:** the archive is not an anchor. Its composition moves with digitisation, cataloguing and OCR, so a frequency curve or a document count is a reading of the habitat as much as of past cognition.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."` `Fig 2 :29-30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."` `Fig 2 :31-32 "two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."`
- **What to check:** Pechenick, Danforth and Dodds (PLOS ONE, 2015) report that the 2009 and 2012 Google Books corpora differ, that scientific texts dominate the unfiltered sets, and that the fiction-labelled set was saturated with medical literature. Is the independence condition, not the document count, the right test in your field?
- **Refuter:** an archive whose composition is invariant under the interventions that move real corpora, or two records with independent provenance whose agreement survives a check against a third ground that influenced neither.

### C3 - Method-facing; checkable by a historian of the human sciences or a cognitive scientist

- **Statement:** the sought shape is held as a preference until written down as a term, and the middle link between a practice and its record has no instrument of its own.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 5 :62-63 "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."` `Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."` `Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."`
- **What to check:** what would you expect to see in a record if a given everyday practice changed? Can that expectation be stated so that someone with a different vocabulary could check it against the world? The tacit knowledge essay's test is the model: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see."*
- **Refuter:** an instrument that returns a reading on the middle link, improved from a worse instrument already returning something, or a reconstruction that states its sought shape as a checkable term and survives a ground check.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that historians can never establish facts about the past. Artefacts constrain shapes; provenance and traces can name slots.
- Not that any particular past cognitive practice has been identified here.
- Not that present categories are forbidden. They are bindings, provenanced as such, and add no atom to the past.
- Not that all records are equally weak. Independence and provenance decide.
- Not that absence in the record means absence in the past.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement of past cognition.
- Not that this simulated derivation validates itself. It cannot.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A discriminating trace identifying which of two candidate practices produced an artefact, confirmed at a ground.
2. An archive invariant under the changes that move real corpora, so it can serve as an anchor that does not update.
3. Two independent records, no shared evidence pool, whose agreement survives a third ground that influenced neither.
4. An instrument on the link between practice and record, returning a reading when the practice changes.
5. A case where an aggregate statistic predicts every per-slot change with no residual.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :128-130 and Fig 12 :117, three short passages, against C1.
2. Check C2 against your own experience of a corpus or archive that changed its answer when its composition changed.
3. State one thing you would expect to see in a record if an everyday practice changed, without using the framework's vocabulary.
4. Name one refuter from section 5 that you think could fire in your field, or say why none could.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
