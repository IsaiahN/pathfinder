## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 13 :128-130 (artefact versus lineage), Fig 11 :73 (habitat not ground), Fig 2 :40 (independence), Fig 9 :27-31 (describe before looking, borrowed words).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: a record constrains a shape, not a name; the archive updates; agreement needs independence; absence is a search report; verdicts are per slot.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a historical case, for example "a change in reading practice in this period is visible in the marginal marks in these ledgers".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- which candidate cognitive route produced the marks, because the artefact does not decide it (Fig 13 :129);
- whether the corpus or archive used for the reading changed its own composition in the period (Fig 11 :73 and Pechenick et al.);
- whether the documents agree independently or from one shared source (Fig 2 :40);
- the order of the producing practice, because a flat chain carries only → sequence (Fig 13 :129 and Operators table).

Conversely, several things survive cleanly: the artefact is what it is; a habitat must be enumerated from contact; a global rate is frame-internal; absence is not proof. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists for a specific past practice. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
