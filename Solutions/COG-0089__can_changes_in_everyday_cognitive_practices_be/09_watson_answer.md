## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about reconstructing changes in everyday cognitive practices from historical records. Option A: treat a record as a ground that names the past practice that produced it, and treat agreement among records as corroboration. Option B: treat the record as an artefact of a habitat, reconstruct only a shape constrained by the artefact and its lineage, and price every bias in terms of independence and provenance. What each leads to: A turns a corpus reading into a verdict about a past mind and spends the independence of the people checking it; B sends a checkable shape, named biases, written refuters and an open slot until a discriminating trace or instrument settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what present categories predict a record implies about past practice and what the record and its habitat actually are. Arity: at least three conditions (past practice, production process, present reconstruction frame), so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58). Symmetry: asymmetric under time, a practice can produce a record, a record does not uniquely produce a practice. Scale: individual acts, institutional recording habits, long run cultural change, with a lossy transform between them (Fig 10 :68).
- **Domain A, the past practice itself.** Actors: historical persons doing everyday cognition. Currency: cognitive acts and their material traces. Anchor: none live answers for the specific past instance. This is the residual, not yet a settleable domain. Verdict `gap` at this budget.
- **Domain B, the record and archive.** Actors: scribes, printers, institutions, archivists, digitizers, OCR pipelines. Currency: surviving inscriptions, books, ledgers, corpora. Anchor: the artefact is what it is, but the archive updates, so it is a habitat, not the ground. Reconstructable as artefact, per slot.
- **Domain C, the reconstruction frame.** Actors: historians, cognitive scientists, their categories and instruments. Currency: hypotheses, models, frequencies, interpretations. Anchor: the artefact plus, where available, a live stand in (experiment, cross cultural observation, material constraint). Checkable from outside.
- **Domain D, the production link.** Actors: the same persons as practitioners and record makers; the medium (writing, print, oral tradition). Currency: what transfers from a cognitive act into a record. Anchor: none direct. A union compound with its own loop.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Domains A, B and D overlap because the record makers are the people whose cognition is inferred. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Order by dependency.** Establish the artefact with provenance; build terms for what it affords; state a refusable shape; plan triangulation and imports; carry methods, not recordings (Fig 3 :20). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the "order not in the artefact" line governs the artefact considered alone; the trace bearing cases in the essays add a lineage or an external instrument, a different object. No strain after re census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Changes in everyday cognitive practices can be reconstructed only as a shape, never as a name of a past mental state. The route that produced a record is not in the artefact; it is in the lineage, when the lineage survives. The archive is a habitat that updates, not an anchor; agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness; absence is a search report; verdicts are per practice and stratum, never averaged. Present categories are allowed as provenanced bindings, not as ground licensed names. The verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, and the full reconstruction branch is `gap` at this budget. A trace bearing case, an invariant archive, an independent agreement that survives a third ground, or an instrument on the practice to record link would move a specific slot toward `grounded`. Agreement with this framework is not one of them.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The claim carries its mode, three checkable sub claims and seven pre registered refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is treating a corpus reading as a verdict about a past mind, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A binding can be re provenanced, a per slot verdict can be withdrawn, and a trace can replace a shape with a name for one slot without disturbing the rest. Nothing here deletes a term; a refutation adds one that refuses, and the reach falls because something was added.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminating trace identifying which candidate practice produced an artefact; an archive invariant under the changes that move real corpora; two independent records whose agreement survives a third ground; an instrument on the middle link of Fig 3's chain, built from a worse instrument already returning something; or an aggregate that predicts every per slot change with no residual. Prestige and receptiveness are not such instruments.
