# Are a record and the practice behind it isomers? Order, structure and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

We hold one artefact and two or more candidate cognitive practices that could have produced it. What is the structural relation between them, and can the order of the producing practice be recovered from the artefact? The question needs a structure claim (are they different substances or one substance under two names?) and an order claim (is the sequence in the record the sequence in the practice?).

## STRUCTURE: ISOMER, FUNCTIONAL GROUP, OR IDENTITY

`Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."`
`Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."`

Two candidate cognitive practices that yield the same record can share the artefact's atoms while differing in the bond, that is, in the route by which those atoms were produced. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same surviving features, different arrangement, different substance, and the difference is invisible in the artefact itself. The consequence for historical reconstruction is sharp: a perfect match between a reconstruction and a record does not identify the practice, because a different practice under a different bond can produce the same artefact.

If the candidates do not share the full atom set but only a common substructure (say, both involve a mark made by hand), then they are not isomers but members of a **functional group**: Fig 12 :124, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The weaker claim is easier to defend and is the honest default when the record is thin.

The identity reading (Fig 12 :68, *"two names, one referent"*) is available only when a provenance or a discriminating trace shows that the two descriptions pick out the same route. Without that, calling the record the practice is an identity claim with no witness.

## ORDER: SEQUENCE IN THE RECORD VERSUS SEQUENCE IN THE PRACTICE

`Fig 13 :129 "The order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."`
`Fig 13 :130 "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."`

The Operators table says of →: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."* A chronicle, a ledger and a diary are flat chains. They impose a sequence on whatever they record, because that is the only ordering their notation can carry. Fig 12 :48 defines a chain as *"the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."* Reading the chain's order as the practice's order is the flat formula error: the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the test case: *"The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted."* The artefact does not say which. The lineage does. The same essay adds the asymmetry that keeps this honest: provenance is recoverable where a trace exists (isotope ratios), and not recoverable where the transformation left no trace (a green reading). So the order claim is case dependent, not a universal impossibility.

## ANALOGUES THAT BEAR, AND THEIR LIMIT

1. **Chemistry.** Silver fulminate and silver cyanate, same composition, different properties; Berzelius named isomerism in 1830 (Fig 12 :97-98). This licenses the structure claim but not any particular cognitive content.
2. **Video frame versus photograph.** Same apparent image, different warrant, no mark of origin (essay 1). This licenses the source-underdetermination claim.
3. **Mined versus lab grown diamond.** Same appearance, different isotope ratios, distinguishable once an instrument exists (essay 1). This licenses the refuter: the route may be recoverable when a trace survives.
4. **Corpus versions.** The 2009 and 2012 Google Books corpora differ in composition, so the same word frequency can carry a different warrant in each (Pechenick et al. 2015). This licenses the habitat-not-anchor claim, but it is an analogue, not a figure line.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

The isomer and order claims are refuted for a given case if:

1. a discriminating trace in or around the artefact identifies which candidate practice produced it, with a ground check confirming the trace; or
2. two candidate practices with the same artefact are shown to be identical in route, so the isomer relation collapses into identity; or
3. the artefact's sequence is shown to be the practice's sequence by an independent record of the producing act, not by the artefact itself.

If any fires for a case, that case moves out of the shape-only verdict and the reconstruction earns a name for that slot. Until then the claim is `specified` and ungrounded.

## CONCLUSION

A record and a candidate practice stand to each other as isomers when they share the surviving atoms but differ in the route, and the difference is invisible in the artefact. When they share only a substructure, the weaker functional-group claim applies. The sequence in a flat record is the record's sequence, not automatically the practice's sequence, because the only bond a flat chain can express is →. Both claims are checkable from outside: ask for the trace or the lineage, and ask what would distinguish the candidates. Neither claim names a past mental state.
