# Expert verification packet: animal cognition and machine intelligence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a comparative cognition researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No animal was tested and no machine was measured.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A comparison between animal cognition and machine intelligence cannot return a faculty list, a species rank or a universal percentage. It returns a shape. Capability lives in the agent together with its habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing and a harness solve proves wiring rather than capability. The comparison is a union of three conditions with different anchors: capability and contact, anchor legitimacy, and frame independence with surplus. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The animal frame's usable contribution is an axis imported with a source gate and a shadow test, not a name for a faculty. The faculty list is a declared gap, reported as unreached rather than silence, with refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11 and 2
- **Statement:** the substrate question dissolves into a contact question; capability is agent-and-habitat, and an agent-only improvement with no contact change cannot move the ground. Animal or human resemblance is an instrument for a machine question, not the anchor, because the question is not constitutively about resemblance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
- **Refuter:** show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (the figure's own standing falsifier, Fig 11 :71-72), or state a question constitutively about animal-likeness, in which case the animal is a legitimate anchor and C1's anchor half does not apply.

### C2: comparative-methods-facing, checkable against your own literature
- **Statement:** comparative methods are diagnostic of link location rather than a global rank; the animal frame supplies low-overlap axes, not faculty names. Early cross-species ranking attempts floundered because task demands and species choice were not sophisticated enough, which is a vocabulary-and-objective failure (Fig 3 link 2 and 3), not a missing general faculty.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :29-30 "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist." Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- **What to check:** is the description of cross-species ranking failures faithful to the comparative literature you know (Tinbergen's four questions, Morgan's Canon, the anthropomorphism problem)? Does a low-overlap sensorium change the categories a system can represent, rather than only the data it receives?
- **Refuter:** a global benchmark score that predicts link location better than a per-link reading on a non-updating ground; or evidence that the animal frame only relabels held atoms and imports no new axis.

### C3: machine-learning-facing, checkable against benchmark practice
- **Statement:** a test harness is a substituted habitat; a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability. Two failures are silent until the goal moves or the inherited actors act: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- **What to check:** is this an accurate reading of why in-distribution success fails to transfer? Does the two-currency rule match the difference between describing a behaviour cheaply and paying to settle whether it holds in a new habitat?
- **Refuter:** a harness solve that transfers to a substituted habitat with no unreproduced requirement, no unchosen inherited actors and a ground that pays there; or a single unit in which description cost and settlement cost can be added without loss.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that animals are unintelligent or that machines are. Fig 11 :36 concerns a mode with no maintainer; Fig 11 :37-38 gives work to a seat.
- Not that an animal or a human is the anchor for a machine question. It is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure.
- Not a faculty list, species rank, universal percentage or named missing primitive. That is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreached (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134).
- Not that the comparison is identity. It is an ordered threshold `⋛` (Fig 12 :87; Operators :51). Isomer status needs a fixed atom set the frames as constituted do not share; the functional-group claim stands (Fig 12 :123).
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that this derivation settles anything. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency.
