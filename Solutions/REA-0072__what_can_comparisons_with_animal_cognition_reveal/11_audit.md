## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :36-40 and :66-70, Fig 2 :29-38, Fig 8 :45-53, Fig 9 :32 and :55-60, Fig 12 :87 and :117-125, Fig 13 :115 and :134.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: capability is agent-and-habitat; harness equals substituted habitat; comparison is an ordered threshold, not identity; the faculty list is a gap; animal resemblance is an instrument, not an anchor.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a given machine and a given species, for example that a model's failure on an animal-derived task is a habitat-reproduction failure, or that an animal sensorium supplies an importable axis.

**R_T is nonzero.** Five things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the ground** in each concrete domain. The shorthand says "a non-updating ground"; a concrete claim must name an instrument, counter, board or outcome that does not move when the frames think (Fig 2 :29-30).
- the **enumerated habitat**. "Agent-and-habitat" compresses a list that must be read off the world, not composed (Fig 11 :43-44, :48). A concrete claim that skips enumeration is dead reckoning.
- the **source gate and shadow test** for any imported axis. The shorthand says "animal sensoria are candidate axes"; a concrete import still owes provenance and a gap it explains (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :50-52).
- the **two-currency price**. "Description cost and action cost do not add" is a structural rule; applying it requires pricing a particular description in bits and a particular settlement in actions (Fig 12 :125-127).
- the **fixed-atom condition** on the isomer claim. The shorthand says "isomers over a shared set"; the concrete frames do not actually share one, so the claim falls back to functional group.

Conversely, three things survive cleanly: capability is agent-and-habitat; a harness is a substituted habitat; the faculty list is a gap with a stated refuter. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
