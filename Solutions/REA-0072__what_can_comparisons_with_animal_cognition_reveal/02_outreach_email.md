# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the claim

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the reader can see the residual and the refuter in stage 1 without any of the framework's interior. If the stage-1 claim cannot be checked on its own, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** one checkable claim about what animal comparison can tell us about machines

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** comparing animal cognition with machine intelligence cannot return a list of faculties a machine lacks or a rank of who is more intelligent. The comparison returns a shape. Capability lives in the agent together with its habitat, not in the agent alone. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves. The useful comparison is therefore not "does the machine do what the animal does" but "which parts of the habitat were reproduced, which link in the dependency chain does this system stop at, and which axes can its primitives even represent". A harness solve proves wiring, not capability.
>
> What I am explicitly not claiming: that an animal is the anchor for a machine question; that resemblance to an animal is evidence of machine capability; that any particular faculty can be named as the missing one. Resemblance is an instrument here, not the ground that settles the question.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. an agent-only improvement, with no change in contact, that moves the ground;
> 2. a harness solve that transfers to a substituted habitat with no unreproduced requirement, no unchosen inherited actors, and a ground that pays there;
> 3. two frames drawing on one evidence pool whose agreement still predicts a non-updating ground;
> 4. a figure line that returns a standing list, a rank or a universal percentage.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the claim. If any one holds, I retract. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figure text and the formula are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
