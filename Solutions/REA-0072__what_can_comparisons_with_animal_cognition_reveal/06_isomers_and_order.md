## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond is "comparison"?

Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Operators :51: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

So `⋛` is **ordered** and **threshold-seeking**. It asks where a boundary lies, not whether two operands are the same thing.

- Treating the animal/machine relation as `≡` identity would assert two names, one referent. Fig 12 :67: "two names, one referent". A machine process and an animal process are not one referent merely because a reading cannot discriminate them; the source essay's metamerism point applies (section 4 of `05`).
- Treating it as `+` conjunction would assert "both hold at once" (Fig 12 :57) and would hide the threshold question entirely.
- Correct reading: the comparison asks where the boundary sits between two arrangements of contact, relative to a residual.

**Falsifier Q1:** a figure line defining a cross-frame comparison as identity or as symmetric conjunction, with no threshold term, would break this. None was found. Operators :68 states that a relation failing all seven tests "is a claim that has not been made yet", not a missing operator.

### Q2: Are animal cognition and machine intelligence isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- As the two frames are actually constituted, they do **not** share a fixed atom set: different sensoria, different currencies, different habitats. The animal frame's currency is survival contact, the machine frame's a scoring rule (F3 of `01`). So the figure's fixed-atom condition is not met and the isomer claim does not go through as stated.
- If both cores are rewritten over the full shared settled set, with differences only in which structure mediates which contact, then they are isomers under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the formula and decisive in behaviour. This is the **stronger** and more interesting claim.
- What survives without the fixed-atom condition is the weaker **functional-group** reading: both share a contact-resolving substructure, and the qualifier says which member. "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom" permits each settled frame to become an atom at the comparison level, but it does not erase the difference in atom sets below.

**Falsifier Q2, written before the run:** if either frame can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. The fixed-atom condition is recorded as a **live strain** in `01` section 6 and `11_audit.md`; it is not hidden.

### Q3: What is the order of the derivation?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

The order is therefore:

1. **Refuse before mint.** Formula step 2 routes a term held that is false to REFUTED and owes a refusal, not an explanation. The "what is intelligence" framing is refused before any faculty is minted (Fig 11 :35).
2. **Bind the sought shape as an ordinary term.** Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."
3. **Describe the residual before choosing the frame.** Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
4. **Enumerate the habitat.** Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
5. **Locate the broken link.** Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."
6. **Settle before comparing.** Fig 12 :104 lets a settled molecule become an atom. Each frame's first-order capability must be settled against its own ground before the second-order comparison is attempted.
7. **Import last, with gates.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Reversing steps 3 and 4 is the apophenia error named in the exemplar: pick the frame first and any faculty will seem to fit.

### Q4: Catalysts and functional groups in the comparison

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 12 :123, quoted in Q2.

A structural feature that survives its own use (a reusable method, a sensorimotor loop that keeps returning a residual) is a catalyst. A feature that fixes the kind of outcome regardless of which member of the group carries it is a functional group. A faculty named from one occasion is neither; it is a recording (Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."). Where the arrangement is not recorded as a method, "the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again" (Fig 12 :107-110, as carried in the tacit-knowledge essay).

**Falsifier Q4:** show that a one-occasion recording behaves exactly like a regenerating catalyst across uses, with the same cost and the same transfer to a substituted habitat. If it does, the method/recording distinction collapses and F2 of `01` must be withdrawn.

### Q5: Analogues that are not arguments

- **The lift move.** Fig 11 :42: "The lift move is Cayley's, 1799: separating the forces from the flapping is what made powered flight a problem anyone could work on." The animal/machine comparison should separate contact from surface, not rank flapping styles.
- **Ecosystem engineering.** Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated." A machine introduced into a habitat inherits unchosen actors; the comparison must enumerate the cascade before calling the result a limit. The niche-construction source lists the same Jones et al. 1994 paper (bibliographic cross-check only; the paper was not opened).
- **The spectrometer.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." A discriminator, not another analogy, settles a source question.
- **The substituted habitat.** Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

These analogues corroborate the shape. None of them decides it; the figures do.

## CONCLUSION

The comparison bond is an ordered threshold `⋛`, not identity. The derivation runs refuse, bind, describe, enumerate, locate, settle, import. Animal and machine cores are isomers only under a fixed-atom condition that the frames as constituted do not satisfy; the functional-group claim stands without it. One-occasion recordings are not catalysts. The animal frame can contribute an axis by import with gates, not a name for a held faculty.
