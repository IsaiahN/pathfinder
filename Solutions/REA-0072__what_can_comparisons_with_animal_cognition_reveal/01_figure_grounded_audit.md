## 0. The claim under audit

The question as written is: What can comparisons with animal cognition reveal about the limits and possibilities of machine intelligence?

Two readings are put in competition before any figure is chosen.

- **Reading A, the template reading.** Animal cognition is a catalogue of faculties. A machine is limited where it lacks one and possible where it can reproduce one. This reading asks "what is intelligence" and answers by resemblance.
- **Reading B, the contact reading.** Animal cognition is a frame with a different closure, a different currency and a different anchor. The comparison reveals the shape of capability, where a system's dependency chain stops, and which axes its primitives cannot represent. It returns no faculty list and no rank.

What each leads to: A produces a `general`-mode verdict with a named list and a borrowed anchor, and it spends the independence of the people asked to check it. B produces a `specified` shape with per-domain verdicts, a stated gap, and a refuter written in advance. The distinction is made before the run so that the sought-for shape is an ordinary term and therefore refusable (formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else").

## 0b. Residual, in effect terms

- **Arity.** The residual is a union of at least three conditions, not one scalar question: capability and contact; anchor legitimacy; frame independence and surplus. Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." So verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Symmetry.** The comparison bond is `⋛`, a threshold relation, not `≡` identity and not `+` symmetric conjunction. Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Operators :51 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Asking "is the machine like an animal" treats an ordered threshold as identity, which is a type error, not a hard question.
- **Scale.** First order inside each frame: an animal in its habitat, a model in its harness. Second order at the comparison itself. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom".
- **Text of R.** The gap between what a cross-frame comparison can license, namely a shape about contact, and what is usually claimed from it, namely a name, a rank or a standing faculty list. R is indexed per slot, not global: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 2. Findings

### F1 (decisive): the question as posed is the substrate question the figure replaces

Fig 11 :34-35: "What is intelligence? The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :41-42: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not." The lift move is attributed in the figure to Cayley, 1799: "separating the forces from the flapping is what made powered flight a problem anyone could work on."

So a comparison with animal cognition does not reveal which faculties a machine lacks. It reveals that the interesting object is not the agent but the agent-and-habitat, and that an improvement which leaves contact unchanged changes nothing. **Mode:** `general` on the dissolution of the substrate question, `specified` on the contact consequence. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 :34-42 and the maintained/unmaintained distinction; the comparative psychology source independently reports that "intelligence" in that literature is defined as whatever is closest to human performance, which is the same anchor error from the other side. **Refuter:** show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground. Fig 11 :71-72 supplies the figure's own standing falsifier: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."

**Correction C1, kept visible.** A first pass of this finding read Fig 11 :36 as a denial that animals are intelligent. That is withdrawn. The figure says the word "does no work" where nothing maintains anything, and Fig 11 :37-38 immediately adds, "Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground. That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice." The distinction is between two modes of a process, not between smart and dumb creatures. No rank of species is implied by the figure and none is asserted here.

### F2: a harness is a substituted habitat, so a benchmark solve is not a capability result

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

This is the part of the question about **limits**. A machine that fails an animal-derived task may be failing because the habitat was substituted and something required was not reproduced; a machine that passes may be showing wiring, not capability. Both failures are silent until the goal moves or the inherited actors act. The machine learning source independently describes the same asymmetry: overfitting is when "a model begins to 'memorize' training data rather than 'learning' to generalize from a trend", and an overfitted function "is likely to be less portable than a simple one". That is the world-side version of recording crossing where a method should. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 :66-70 and the overfitting source, with the anchor legitimacy test of F5 as the dependency. **Refuter:** a harness solve that transfers to a genuinely substituted habitat with no loss, no unchosen inherited actors, and a ground that pays in the new habitat. Until such a transfer is measured, "limit" is a reading of a setup as much as of a system.

### F3: the comparison is a union of two modes, so verdicts stay separate

Fig 11 :55: "Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."

Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."

Fig 11 :57: "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."

Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."

The animal frame is described by selection without a maintainer; the machine frame has a seat that chooses instruments and introduces actors. Their currencies differ (survival contact versus a scoring rule) and their grounds differ until a shared non-updating anchor is named. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." One lever applied to both frames leaves the intersections standing. **Mode:** `specified` on the union, `general` on the refusal to average. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 modes plus Fig 9 union rule). **Refuter:** exhibit a single currency and a single non-updating anchor under which both frames' results are the same quantity; then a pooled verdict would be legitimate.

### F4: independence decides whether the comparison teaches anything

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice."

Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Animal cognition is useful exactly where its closure differs from the machine frame's, that is, where its sensoria and primitives compress reality along axes the other frame cannot represent. The framework essay "They're Scanning Books When They Should" states the same point in world language: "It would be studying animals, sensoria that compress reality along axes ours never had, because the scarce resource is not more of the human window, but any window that is not ours." If the comparison is assembled first and the residual described afterwards, any faculty will seem to fit, which is the echo-without-shadow error named in the exemplar. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 9 and Fig 2; the essay is high-overlap corroboration, counted as one witness with the figures. **Refuter:** show the two frames draw on one evidence pool and still predict a ground (then the independence parameter is zero and the surplus verdict fails).

### F5: the animal is an instrument, not the anchor, for a machine question

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

Fig 2 :37-38: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

Fig 2 :33-34: "The anchor must not update. If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

The question here is constitutively about machine capability, not about animal-likeness. Resemblance to an animal is therefore an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (echolocation is the comparative psychology source's own example of a behaviour that human-centred definitions neglect). Using the animal as the anchor converts triangulation into a mirror chain: Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 7 plus comparative psychology). **Refuter:** state a question constitutively about animal-likeness, for example "is this behaviour what a corvid would do", in which case the animal is a legitimate anchor and F5 does not apply to that question.

### F6: the standing list of faculties is a gap, reported as unreached, not as silence

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again. Figure 9 puts it plainly: you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides." And on the limit of naming: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

No figure line returns the faculty list, the percentage of human-like capability, or the rank of species and machines. That is the declared `gap` at this budget, of the shape-not-a-name kind rather than a claim of underivability in principle. Fig 13 :134 supplies the required verdict form: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." **Mode:** `general` on the gap. **Status:** single-source for the figure rule itself but triangulated with the canonical figures README and the Library of Babel essay's "a general shape of the answer". **Refuter:** produce a figure line that returns the list or a rank without an import; that would break the figure's own set-closed rule (Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists.").

**Correction C3, kept visible.** "Shape, not a name" governs a *missing* primitive or axis, not a binding onto an atom already in the library. Naming a held atom as the occupant of a slot is provenance, not a forbidden invention and not an import that moves the wall. This narrows the first-pass reading of F6 and is recorded in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6 and `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1.

### F7: the comparison is diagnostic of link location, not a global score

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."

Comparative methods are good at locating which link a subject stops at: perception, vocabulary of affordances, objective, planning, learn and carry. The comparative psychology source reports that early cross-species ranking attempts "floundered" because they "were not sufficiently sophisticated, either in their analysis of the demands of different tasks, or in their choice of species to compare", which is a vocabulary-and-objective failure, not a lack of general intelligence. Tinbergen's four questions, reported in the same source, separate mechanism, development, adaptive significance and phylogeny, which is an enumeration of links rather than a score. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 3 plus the comparative psychology source). **Refuter:** a global benchmark score that predicts link location better than a per-link reading, measured on a ground that does not update.

### F8: description cost and action cost do not add

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

Fig 12 :126-127: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."

A comparison that prices a faculty only by how compactly it can be described, or only by how cheaply a benchmark can score it, will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. The benchmark is one currency; the cost of settling whether the arrangement actually changes contact is the other. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 9). **Refuter:** exhibit one unit in which both the description and the settlement can be priced without loss, and show it predicts which comparisons pay off.

### F9: isomer is conditional on a fixed atom set; the weaker functional-group claim stands

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Fig 12 :104, quoted in F1, lets a settled molecule become an atom at the next level. If an animal core and a machine core are written over the same settled atom set and differ only in arrangement, they are isomers under Fig 12's definition. As actually constituted, the two frames have different sensors, currencies and habitats, so the atom sets differ and the fixed-atom condition is not satisfied. The honest claim is the weaker one: they share a functional group (a contact structure, a resolver of some residual) with different qualifiers. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source on the figure definition, with the fixed-atom condition live as a strain (see section 6). **Refuter:** show one frame requires an atom the other does not; that kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing. Developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### F10: what comparison can add is an axis, not a name

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 6 :12-13 names the gates: "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap".

Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

The "They're Scanning Books" essay states the world-side version: "Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all, and only the second reaches structure the current primitives cannot represent." And: "Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what." An animal sensorium is therefore a candidate source for a new axis, subject to a source gate and a shadow test, not a source of names for faculties the machine frame already has. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 13 and the essay; the essay shares the framework author's evidence pool and is one witness, so the figure verdict is primary. **Refuter:** show that the comparison only relabels held atoms and imports no new axis; then there is no surplus and F10 fails.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the exemplar's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim fails if any of the following holds.

1. An agent-only improvement, with no change in contact, moves the ground. This is the figure's own standing refuter (Fig 11 :71-72) and would break the capability claim.
2. A synthetic harness solve transfers to a substituted habitat with full capability, no unreproduced requirement and no unchosen inherited actors (breaks F2).
3. The animal and machine frames draw on one evidence pool, yet their agreement predicts a non-updating ground (breaks Fig 2 :40 and F4).
4. A figure line is found that returns a standing faculty list, a rank of species or a universal percentage without an import (breaks F6).
5. A direct identity `≡` between an animal faculty and a machine component is admitted, paid for by a ground and not merely a binding (breaks the ordered-comparison verdict and F9).
6. No new axis is imported from the animal frame and the comparison only renames held atoms (breaks F10).

A seventh item is not a refuter but the ground that would settle the question: a non-updating anchor that is constitutively about machine capability and against which both frames can separately range. Until such an anchor is named, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the contact question, not the faculty list. State that capability is agent-and-habitat and that the comparison returns a shape (F1).
2. Report the union and keep the three domain verdicts separate; never average an animal result with a benchmark result (F3).
3. Ask what ground settles each claim, and reject animal or human resemblance as the anchor for a machine question (F5).
4. Use comparative methods to locate the broken link, and price description and settlement in separate currencies (F7, F8).
5. Treat any faculty borrowed from the animal frame as an import needing provenance and a shadow test; the valuable import is an axis, not a name (F10).
6. Record the gap for the faculty list as unreached rather than inventing a list (F6).
7. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the derivation and the refuter, not a conclusion.

## 6. Round trip and honest stop

The condensation from figures to this answer is lossy. What survives the trip up and back down cleanly is: capability is agent-and-habitat; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing; the comparison is a union with per-domain verdicts; a harness is a substituted habitat; the faculty list is a gap. What does not survive is the exact anchor test for each domain, the typed bond in each concrete comparison, the source gate for any borrowed axis, and the two-currency price of settling it. Those are enumerated as the round trip gap R_T in `11_audit.md`.

The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments. No animal was tested and no machine was measured here, so no claim in this folder is `grounded`. A source-discriminating instrument, a transfer experiment into a substituted habitat, or a non-updating anchor would move the verdict. Indiscernibility is not identity, and a comparison that finds no difference is not proof of sameness (Fig 8 :39).
