# The Tether's answer on animal cognition and machine intelligence

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks what a comparison between animal cognition and machine intelligence reveals about machine limits and possibilities. The residual is not "which is more intelligent". It is the gap between what a cross-frame comparison can license, a shape about contact, and what is usually claimed from it, a name, a rank or a faculty list. It is a union of three conditions: capability and contact; anchor legitimacy; frame independence and surplus (Fig 9 :55-57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the animal frame (unmaintained).** Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." The animal is understood by enumeration of its habitat, not by a faculty list: Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus the comparative psychology source).

**D2, the machine frame (maintained).** Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A benchmark pass or fail is a reading of a setup as much as of a system. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus the overfitting source).

**D3, the comparison.** Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." The surplus exists only where closures differ and the residual was described first (Fig 8 :47, :53; Fig 9 :27-28). Animal resemblance is an instrument, not the anchor, because the question is constitutively about machine capability (Fig 2 :36-38). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 8, Fig 9).

**D4, the sought shape.** A frame cannot check the direction of its own question. The shape is written down as an ordinary term and made refusable (Fig 5 :65-66). What it yields: a non-updating anchor per domain, a reproduced habitat, an enumerated link structure, and an imported axis with provenance and a shadow test. Verdict: `general` structural, `specified` where a figure line governs, triangulated.

## 3. What the answer says the comparison reveals

1. **Limits are mostly habitat failures before they are agent failures.** Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." An animal-derived task that a machine fails may be measuring an unreproduced requirement.
2. **Passes are not capability.** A synthetic solve proves wiring. The machine learning source calls the analogous failure memorising training data rather than learning to generalise, and notes an overfitted function "is likely to be less portable than a simple one".
3. **The useful diagnostic is link location, not a score.** Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed." Comparative methods are good at this; early cross-species ranking attempts floundered because task demands and species choice were not sophisticated enough.
4. **The animal frame's real gift is an axis.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The essay "They're Scanning Books When They Should": "It would be studying animals, sensoria that compress reality along axes ours never had, because the scarce resource is not more of the human window, but any window that is not ours."
5. **The faculty list is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The list is owed an instrument, not an analogy.
6. **The comparison is a threshold, not an identity.** Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Operators :51 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Identity is refused. Isomer status is conditional on a fixed atom set that the frames as constituted do not share; the functional-group reading stands (Fig 12 :123).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that animals are unintelligent, and not that machines are. Fig 11 :36 is about a mode where nothing maintains anything; Fig 11 :37-38 gives work to a seat. No worth ranking follows.
- Not that an animal is the anchor for a machine question. It is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not that any named faculty, species rank, percentage or universal list follows. That is the declared gap.
- Not that a benchmark score is a verdict. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not `grounded`. No animal was tested and no machine was measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground, not more agreement. The discriminators owed are:

1. A source-discriminating instrument that tells whether a behaviour came from a reproduced contact structure, a memorised recording, or an unenumerated imported actor. The Hard Problem essay: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."
2. A substituted-habitat transfer test with the required contact reproduced and the inherited actors enumerated, so Fig 11 :66-70 can fire.
3. A non-updating anchor that is constitutively about machine capability and against which both frames can separately range (Fig 2 :29-30, :36).
4. Standing refuters from `01` section 3: an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; one evidence pool whose agreement still predicts a ground; a figure line that returns the faculty list.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The animal comparison returns a shape and an axis to import, subject to gates. It does not return the name of the missing thing, because naming it would mean already holding it. The answer stops at the limit of the instruments available and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity, and a comparison that finds no difference is not proof of sameness (Fig 8 :39).

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Comparing animal cognition with machine intelligence does not produce a faculty checklist or a ranking of who is more intelligent. It produces a shape. Capability lives in the agent together with its habitat, so an improvement that leaves contact unchanged changes nothing, and a harness solve proves wiring rather than capability. What the comparison can reveal is which parts of the habitat were reproduced, where a system's dependency chain stops, and which axes its primitives cannot represent: the animal sensorium is a candidate import of a new axis, with provenance and a shadow test, not a name for a faculty. Animal resemblance is an instrument, not the anchor, for a question about machines. The faculty list stays a declared gap until a source-discriminating instrument or a non-updating ground settles it. The refuters were written before the claim; a failure is more useful than agreement.
