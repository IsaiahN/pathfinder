## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 adds that it "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."

So a **shape** here is the narrowed form of what capability requires, which can be stated before any faculty is named. It constrains; it does not fill. "Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" is a shape. "The machine lacks Theory of Mind, which corvids have" is a name, a rank and a borrowed anchor in one sentence.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "Your search space will narrow with every vocabulary word that you believe is in the answer or associated with the answer."
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." The same essay: "The understanding has to be rebuilt by the learner, and the way it gets rebuilt is contact: trying, being corrected, and trying again, in both directions."
- Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." The animal comparison is useful as an enumeration prompt, not as a template.

For this question the shape is reached by asking what any capable system needs in contact with a ground, then letting the animal frame enumerate what the machine frame's human-shaped corpus does not hold. That is the difference between an axis and a label (section 5).

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

Fig 11 :48 governs the enumeration: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." Fig 11 :51 adds "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

## 4. The source error this question is prone to

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; the same reading could come from several physical arrangements. "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." It "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Applied here: a benchmark reads a behaviour and cannot comment on the source. A behaviour that looks like animal tool use, or like an animal's apparent Theory of Mind, may come from a reproduced contact structure, from a memorised recording, or from an arrangement nobody has named. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error, and the remedy is a discriminator, not a new adjective. *How Intelligence Actually Works* says "Perception is the reception of a projection by something that is not its source" and "The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect."

This is why F2 and F5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` refuse the template reading. The comparison is an instrument with limited axes, and instruments "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37-38).

## 5. Q2: what can the animal frame actually add?

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* answers this more sharply than the figures alone: "It would be studying animals, sensoria that compress reality along axes ours never had, because the scarce resource is not more of the human window, but any window that is not ours." And: "Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what." Also: "every reasoner, biological, artificial, or composed, can only be surprised along axes its primitives can already represent."

So the animal comparison can add an **axis**, which Fig 6 calls an atom imported from a frame whose closure differs and which the essay distinguishes from a term: "Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all, and only the second reaches structure the current primitives cannot represent." It cannot add a faculty name, because a name would already be in the machine frame's vocabulary.

The same essay warns: "General intelligence is not reliable success on the novel, nobody has that." A comparison that declares a machine limited because it lacks an animal's novel-task success is measuring a threshold on a familiar axis, not capability.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* supplies the direct bridge. In that essay's own words: "What turns new agents into capable agents is not a smarter agent. It is being born into a group that has already learned things: generations of agents, sharing a place where knowledge is stored, sorted and passed on." The same essay then quotes the figure verbatim. Fig 11 :39 states it: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Capability is inherited from a habitat, which is exactly what a harness fails to reproduce (Fig 11 :66-70).

## 6. Correction to my own section 3 and 4

I initially applied "shape, not a name" as if it forbade naming any faculty at all. The canonical phrasing is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". It governs a primitive not held in the library. Naming an already-held atom as the occupant of a slot is a **binding** from a world frame and a provenance question, not the invention of a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure").

My section 4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: a benchmark reading still does not determine its source, and an instrument that cannot discriminate sources is inadequate. What is narrowed is the reason. The failure is the missing discriminator, not a blanket ban on naming held atoms. Both readings are kept so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." And: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying" (Fig 10 :59).

An appeal to a famous comparative psychologist, or to a large benchmark leaderboard, is prestige. It can get the claim a hearing. It cannot settle whether the comparison reveals a limit. The only settlement is a ground that does not update.

## 8. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

The same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *Arrival of the Fittest* calls unverified outputs "simulacra" and warns that answers may be "contrived to fit the formula".

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of any animal or machine and cannot settle anything it reports on. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." No ground was ranged against here.
