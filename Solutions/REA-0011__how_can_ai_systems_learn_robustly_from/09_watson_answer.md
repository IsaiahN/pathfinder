## QUESTION

How can AI systems learn robustly from far fewer examples than current data-intensive methods often
require? Restated as an effect: how does the per-slot residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` go down after a
few examples and stay down under a changed habitat? `R` is indexed per slot, never aggregated
(Fig 1 :19). Option A: locate the answer in a named technique or in more data. Option B: locate it
in a head start over held atoms, maintained ground contact and independent checking, and leave the
specific binding open. A gives a name where the framework returns a shape and confuses a budget with
a witness. B gives a checkable shape with refuters written in advance.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

`R` by arity, symmetry and scale: arity is one residual per object or task slot, never a global
number; symmetry is a threshold on a quantity (few versus many examples), and the learner-ground
relation is ordered; scale runs from a single learner loop, through a population of tasks and frames
across the membrane, to a deployment habitat over time.

**Union, not partition.** The four domains overlap; this is a compound of support, head start,
contact and independence (Fig 9 :54, :57). Dependency order: perception -> vocabulary -> objective
-> planning -> learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The break is at vocabulary and objective, the least
instrumented link (Fig 3 :27).

Quote: "The goal of meta-learning is to train a model on a variety of learning tasks, such that it can solve new learning tasks using only a small number of training samples." Prediction: a head start makes the new search cheap. **Corroborates** D2 and Fig 4 :22. Overlap: high, AI pool.
- **Cognitive science and AI.** Brenden M. Lake, Ruslan Salakhutdinov and Joshua B. Prediction: one-shot learning rides on composition plus learning to learn. **Corroborates** Fig 12 and Fig 4. Overlap: same AI pool as MAML, so one witness.
- **Human cognition, developmental.** Susan Carey, "Beyond Fast Mapping", Language Learning and Development 6:184-205, 2010. Quote: "these children had formed a representation of this novel word and located it in an appropriate portion of semantic space" after one exposure. Prediction: one-trial binding with prior semantic structure. **Corroborates** D2. Overlap: lower, human cognition.
- **Human cognition, child psychology.** Gedeon O. Deak and Alexis J. Prediction: the head start may be general associative history, not a module. **Conflicts** with a specialized-module reading; returned to census, which narrowed D2 to the shape-not-a-name limit. - **Animal behaviour, comparative cognition.** Jian-You Lin, Joe Arthurs and Steve Reilly, "Conditioned taste aversions: From poisons to pain to drugs of abuse", Psychonomic Bulletin and Review 24(2):335-351, 2017. Quote: "The literature is replete with examples of one-trial learning" and "many minutes or even hours separate the CS from the US." Prediction: evolved preparation supplies prior structure for one-trial learning. **Corroborates** Fig 4 :22 and Fig 6 :21. Overlap: genuinely low.
- **Mathematics, philosophy of induction.** "The no-free-lunch theorems of supervised learning", Synthese, 2021. Quote: "every mechanical learning procedure, understood as a mapping from possible data to conclusions, must possess an inductive bias." Prediction: no data-only universal learner. **Corroborates** Fig 6 :21. Overlap: genuinely low.
- **Mathematics, learning theory.** David H. Wolpert, "What is important about the No Free Lunch theorems?", arXiv:2007.10928, 2020. Prediction: alignment to the ground is what matters, not volume. **Corroborates** Fig 2 :29 and Fig 6. Overlap: low, same mathematical pool as the Synthese paper, so one witness.
- **Statistical learning theory.** Aditya Bhaskara and Zhao Chang, Theory of Machine Learning Lecture 3, University of Utah, 2017. Prediction: fewer examples require a restricted class, which is a prior. **Corroborates** Fig 6 :21 and the D2 head start. Overlap: low, same theory pool as the two NFL sources.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a named method does not. Data efficiency is a per-slot support question (D1),
few examples select among atoms held in a head start (D2), robustness is maintained contact across a
substituted habitat (D3), and independence decides whether checking means anything (D4). Verdicts
are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Absence and budget are different failures with
different remedies. The specific winning architecture is a declared `gap`, reported as unreached
rather than silence (Fig 13 :134). Mode `specified` on domain claims, `general` on structure, not
`grounded`. The Deak and Toney conflict was returned to the census and narrowed D2; it did not flip
the verdict.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected effort. A team facing an absent atom would be told to gather more
  examples; a team that has lost habitat contact would be sold a bigger model; a single lever would
  be applied to a union of four conditions and would leave the intersections standing. In outreach,
  an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was not the right shape. The cost
  is real but bounded and mostly reversible, and it is avoidable by leading with the per-slot
  residual and the refuter.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a refuter fires; a refusal is added and the reach falls
  because something was added rather than deleted (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its six refuters are
  recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot audit showing a mint with zero live
  residual; a habitat substitution with unchanged contact and preserved accuracy; a demonstrated
  absent atom fixed by more examples alone; shared-evidence agreement that predicts an independent
  ground; a measured vocabulary link working while failure persists; or a figure line that names a
  specific algorithm. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
