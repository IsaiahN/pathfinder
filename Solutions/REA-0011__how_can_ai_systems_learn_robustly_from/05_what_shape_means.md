## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would
have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer
itself."* Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot
say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small
and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your
question." And: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and
deepen your vocabulary."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains;
it does not fill. For this question, *"a head start that supplies held atoms, few examples that
select a composition, a ground that settles it, independent frames that check it"* is a shape.
*"Use algorithm X with prior Y"* is a name.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then track
  "new terms that overlap, correlate, or are even used interchangeably with your known terms." The
  question is the tether: "This anchor is provided by your own question."
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it
  in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than
  the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain
  description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also
  be the starting place." The paradox is that by then "you have already composed the answer through
  the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what
   pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the
   available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a
   source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the reason a reading cannot be a name. Metamerism: an
instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or
RGB, or CMYK could all produce that reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then
behavior is a projection with lossy information." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that
resolved it: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Applied here, a few-shot
accuracy reading does not name the source of the generalization. Several mechanisms can produce the
same score, so the score is a projection. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* adds: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent,
or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." A reported few-shot result from
a frame that also designed the benchmark is a self-validation, not a settlement.

## 4. Few-shot as a shape, not a technique list

The question asks "how can AI systems learn robustly from far fewer examples". The figure-derived
shape, stated before any theory, has five constraints:

1. **Per-slot residual, not an average** (Fig 1 :19, :22). The answer must say where the learner
   failed, not only how much.
2. **A positive support condition** (Formula step 3). With few examples, the licensed term must be
   bought by a live residual on some slot.
3. **A head start over held atoms** (Fig 4 :22, :24; Fig 6 :21). The few examples select a
   composition; they do not, by themselves, create a new atom.
4. **Maintained contact across a substituted habitat** (Fig 11 :39-40, :70). Robustness is a
   learner-and-habitat property.
5. **Independent checking against a non-updating ground** (Fig 2 :29, :31, :35). Agreement inside
   one evidence pool cannot settle it.

No figure line selects a specific algorithm, prior or architecture. That is the `gap` recorded in
`01` F7 and `09`, and it stays open.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming an inductive bias? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST PASS

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."*
  The subject is a primitive **not held** in `Γ`.
- A prior, an inductive bias or a head start is not a missing primitive. It is either a binding onto
  atoms already held, or a method that descended across the membrane (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods
  cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
- ⇒ Naming a head start is **not** forbidden. What is forbidden is inventing a seventh primitive,
  or claiming that binding a head start moves the wall (Fig 6 : *"IMPORT adds an atom"*: a head
  start adds no new atom to the world).
- **I overstated this on my first pass.** "The framework returns a shape, not a name" governs
  missing primitives, not the selection of methods already available. Retracted as applied to head
  starts and priors; kept as applied to the missing architecture. Triangulated (README + Fig 4 +
  Fig 9).

## 6. Why this derivation is a simulation, not a test

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with
the formula found in the architecture post." It will often be better than a generic answer, but
"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the
formula yourself." So `01`, `06`, `07` and `09` are simulated derivations, checked for internal
consistency against the figures. They are not a ground reading and cannot settle the question.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From the same essay: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action
a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground
Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous few-shot researchers who agree with the
shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the shape a budget to
be tried and let a ground settle it.

**Decoding Tacit Knowledge on contact.** "Sounding right isn't the same as being checked against the
world, which is why I call the Turing test a test of fluency, not of accuracy." A few-shot demo that
looks impressive in a chat is not thereby checked against a ground. This is why stage 1 of the
offer asks the recipient what they would expect to see if the claim were true.
