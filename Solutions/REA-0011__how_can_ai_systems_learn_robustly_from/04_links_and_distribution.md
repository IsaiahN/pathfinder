## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Learning to learn is composition plus a head start, not a bigger corpus.** Lake et al. (2015)
   describe their one-shot model as bringing together "compositionality, causality, and learning to
   learn"; MAML (2017) says it "trains the model to be easy to fine-tune." These are the ML and
   cognitive-science perspectives on Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come
   down."* Both are in the same broad AI evidence pool, so they count as one witness, not two.
2. **There is no data-only universal learner.** The Synthese NFL paper and Wolpert (2020) both say
   that a learning procedure must carry an inductive bias, and that generalization depends on
   alignment with the target distribution. That is the outside mathematical corroboration of
   Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* The Utah lecture notes
   add the budget form: sample need scales with the complexity of the hypothesis class. This is a
   genuinely low-overlap field and the strongest independent corroborator here.
3. **A conflict, kept visible.** Deak and Toney (2013) report that word learning "follows
   non-specialized memory and associative learning processes", and the encyclopedia entry by Brooks
   and Kempe notes fast mapping is "far from inevitable". This **conflicts** with any reading that
   turns "prior structure" into a named, specialized innate module. It was returned to the domain
   census, not used to flip the verdict. The re-census narrows D2: the head start may be general
   associative history rather than a dedicated module, which is exactly the shape-not-a-name limit
   (Fig 9 :32). The verdict survives; the strong reading is withdrawn.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 4 :22-25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the shape and its refuter first; offer links only after.
- Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before the verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Prefer a checker whose closure differs from the ML evidence pool, for example a cognitive scientist or an animal-behaviour researcher.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) a question about
what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the
summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 sends the residual described in effect terms and the pre-registered refuter. Stage 2 is
opt in: if the recipient is curious, offer the three public links. Stage 3 offers the formula as a
method to reuse, with each user writing their own refuter and stating their own mode. The first
verdict must be recorded before stage 2, because agreement after seeing the theory is not evidence
(Fig 2 :40). No bulk send, no receptiveness ranking, no personal emails. A failure is more useful
than an endorsement.
