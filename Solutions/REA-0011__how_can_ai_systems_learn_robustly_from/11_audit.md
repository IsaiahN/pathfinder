## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down,
`gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it
to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually
uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, Fig 4 :22, Fig 6 :21 and :23,
   Fig 11 :39 and :70, Fig 2 :35 and :40, Fig 9 :32.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: index per slot, few examples
   select held atoms via a head start, robustness is contact, independence decides checking, and
   the method name stays open.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete lab claim, for example "MAML with this prior achieves 60
   percent on this benchmark," or "a larger pretrained model is therefore more robust."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **typed bond between a head start and a replay** (Fig 4 :22-25). The shorthand says "prior
  structure helps"; the concrete claim cannot tell a method from a recording of one occasion;
- the **per-slot index** (Fig 1 :19). The concrete claim returns a mean accuracy and has thrown
  away the live slot;
- the **habitat substitution test** (Fig 11 :70). The concrete claim is a harness result and proves
  wiring, not capability;
- the **independence of the checker** (Fig 2 :40). A concrete benchmark suite usually shares an
  evidence pool with the method that designed it.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a few examples do not by themselves add an atom, so the
head start and the provenance of any import are the load-bearing terms. That is the residue worth
sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded
qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed
version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error
Fig 4 :30 names.
