# Expert verification packet  - the Tether on local knowledge and credentials

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in local knowledge, ethnobiology, history of
science or science studies can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the
conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`  - not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed. This is a shape and
a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Trust in a knowledge source is routed per decision slot, not per community. On a given decision
the source that wins is the one whose channel to the outcome in that place is live and whose
independence from the claim is intact. A credential is a cheap filter deciding who gets a
hearing, which is a budget decision; it is not a reading of the outcome and cannot by itself
settle the verdict. When a specialist channel stops tracking local conditions, the ground has not
moved, the channel has, and reliance shifts to the frame whose channel still reaches the outcome.
Local and specialist knowledge are isomers over the same atoms of observation, classification,
inference, transmission and correction, bonded differently to place and lineage versus standard
and certifying gate. The framework returns this shape, not the name of a community or a period.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed by decision slot, and the valence of a source is relative
  to that residual, so no stable cross-slot ranking of local versus credentialed sources follows.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** is there any figure rule that assigns fixed valence to a source *kind*? The
  claim is that there is not; valence is a relation to R.
- **Refuter:** one source type winning across slots with different grounds and different outcomes,
  with no change in contact. That breaks C1 and C3.

### C2  - framework-internal and field-facing; checkable by reading Figure 2, Figure 9 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** the credential is a budget filter, not a witness. A certifying institution
  treated as the party that settles the dispute becomes another frame, and coherent drift follows.
  When the channel decays, the ground does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never
  let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :47 "A ground listed as one of the parties has already
  become a frame." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does."
- **What to check:** does the credential in your field function as a permission filter (who may
  speak as an expert) or as an outcome reading? World source, sociology/education: "Credentialism
  is a reliance on formal qualifications or certifications to determine whether someone is
  permitted to undertake a task, speak as an 'expert' or work in a certain field" (read
  2026-11-23). If it is a permission filter, C2 describes it.
- **Refuter:** a credential that predicts local outcomes better than local testimony across
  several slots and does not move when the certifying frame changes its mind. That makes it an
  anchor in Fig 2's sense and breaks C2.

### C3  - structural derivation; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Formula

- **Statement:** local and specialist knowledge are isomers over a shared atom set; the decision
  between them is a comparison `⋛` against the ground; a failed credential claim is a REFUTED case
  owing a refusal before any new term is minted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The
  difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a
  term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
- **What to check:** is the difference between the two knowledge systems a difference of atoms or
  of bonding? World source, ecology/anthropology: TEK is "a family of culturally situated rules
  and practices rooted in specific places and ecological worldviews", acquired through
  "long-term observation, cultural methods, and experiential learning, rather than the
  standardized experimental and quantitative methods typical of Western ecological science".
  Same observation, different validation bond.
- **Refuter:** a standardised instrument reading shown to be a genuinely new primitive rather
  than a re-arrangement of observation breaks the isomer claim and turns the gap into an import.
  Also, evidence that the real break in the chain is at planning or carrying, not at vocabulary
  and objective, breaks the supporting diagnosis in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F5.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that local practitioners are intrinsically more reliable, or that elders are wise by
  nature. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that credentials are worthless. They are efficient for a convention (Fig 2 :41) and useful
  as a filter for who gets a hearing.
- Not that every community's reliance on local knowledge is rational, or that all institutions
  have lost contact. Whether contact is live is a question for the ground, per slot.
- Not that a community's consensus corroborates the claim. Agreement inside one evidence pool is
  the pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a name, region, date or magnitude. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in producing this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot outcome comparison in a named place: record local and specialist predictions
separately in checkable effect terms, measure each one's contact with the outcome, measure overlap
of evidence pools, then compare both against the outcome. Apply the five refuters in
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. One case corroborates; independent cases across different
grounds would settle.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :49, Fig 2 :47, Fig 9 :60 and Fig 10 :24. Five short passages.
2. In your own field, name one decision where the credential is a permission filter rather than
   an outcome reading. If you cannot, C2 does not apply to your field; say so.
3. Name one instrument or outcome that could show whether a specialist channel still tracks local
   conditions, or say why none exists.
4. Decide whether the local and specialist knowledge systems in your case share atoms and differ
   in bonding, or require different atoms.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
