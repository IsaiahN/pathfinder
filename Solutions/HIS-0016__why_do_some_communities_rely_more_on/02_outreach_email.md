**Subject:** One checkable claim about trust in local knowledge, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Trust in a knowledge source is routed per
> decision, not per community. On a given decision the source that wins is the one whose channel
> to the outcome for that place is live and whose independence from the claim is intact. A
> credential is a cheap filter for who gets a hearing, which is a budget decision. It is not a
> reading of the outcome, so it cannot by itself settle which source is right. When a
> specialist channel stops tracking local conditions, the ground has not moved, the channel has,
> and the community turns to the frame whose channel still reaches it.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a name. It does not say that elders are intrinsically wiser, that
> credentials are worthless, or which community or period is being explained. It says what any
> answer must look like if it is to be checked.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that local knowledge is always better; that all distrust of
> specialists is rational; that credentials are merely prestige. A credential is efficient for
> deciding who gets a hearing, and that is a legitimate convention. The claim is only about what
> may count as a verdict about the outcome on this decision.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. A decision where the credentialed source repeatedly matches the local outcome better than
>    the local source, and the community still routes to the local source with no change in
>    contact.
> 2. A case where the two sources draw on the same evidence pool almost completely, so there is
>    no independence to triangulate, yet reliance still shifts.
> 3. A credential that predicts the outcome better than local testimony across several decisions
>    and does not move when the certifying frame changes its mind.
> 4. Evidence that the real break is in planning or carrying lessons, not in having terms for
>    what the local situation affords and what counts as progress there.
> 5. One source type winning across decisions with different grounds and different outcomes,
>    with no change in contact.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me
> than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and the derivation are below. The figures are
> the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a decision
> you know well and can check against an outcome. Write down what would refute your result before
> you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
>  - [your name]
