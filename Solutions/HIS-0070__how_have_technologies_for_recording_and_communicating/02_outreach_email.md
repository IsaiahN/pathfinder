# Outreach draft: check the boundary claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim and refuter below survive the reader's own check. If the boundary claim fails, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what a recording actually preserves

> Hello,
>
> I am writing about a single output, not asking you to read a theory or to agree with one. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to consider.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, not `grounded`):** a new recording or communication technology does not simply increase the stock of preserved knowledge. It changes a boundary. Three things decide what crosses that boundary:
>
> 1. Whether the instrument was already returning something that failed to resolve. A new medium can extend a slot that existed as a live but unreadable signal. Where nothing is returning anything at any resolution, there is no edge to extend from.
> 2. Whether the relation among the items crosses with them. A record that carries only its contents has recorded half of itself. The arrangement, the source, the date, the method and the correction history are part of the term, not documentation about it.
> 3. Whether a later frame can still read the item against a ground that does not update. The ground does not decay; the reader channel does.
>
> **What would refute it:** (a) a bare recording of one occasion, with no method and no bond, from which a later frame composes new ground-settled terms; (b) a case where library growth and reachable knowledge grow together with no instrument change and no import; (c) a skill shown to be preserved by a recording with no practitioner and no change of contact; (d) a frame shown to be a sealed room whose completeness can be proved from inside.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular medium is the winner, that a quantity of preserved knowledge can be computed, that all tacit skill can be captured, or that the absence of a record proves nothing was known. The framework here returns a shape, not a name. A named technology is a binding from the historical record, provenanced as such, not something the framework selected.
>
> **If the output survives, and only then,** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - VERIFIED LINK 1: local framework figure text with line numbers, `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cite as `Fig N :line`.
> - VERIFIED LINK 2: Vannevar Bush, "As We May Think", The Atlantic, July 1945, https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/
> - VERIFIED LINK 3: W3C PROV-O Recommendation, 30 April 2013, https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/
> The complete list, with access dates and quotes, is in `10_source_ledger.csv` of the packet.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step formula is in the framework. Run it on a historical case you can check against a source. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The reader checks the boundary claim without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Refuter before the links | The standing seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No named winning medium | Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; the canonical README's "shape, not a name" limit governs a missing primitive, so a named medium is a provenanced binding, not a derivation. |
| No "more information means more knowledge" | *"A library that grows is not a reach that grows."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`, formula attribution). |
| No relevance ranking of recipients | Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; selecting for receptiveness manufactures that pool. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| Ask each reader for their own ground and refuter | Fig 9 :27, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*; a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## Distribution constraint

One recipient at a time. No mail-merge, no shared thread, no request to forward. Public institutional and opt-in channels only, as listed in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. No personal emails were collected or used. A reply is a conversation and never corroboration; if it contains a refutation, it is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
