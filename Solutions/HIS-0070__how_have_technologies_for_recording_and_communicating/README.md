# Solution HIS-0070

**Question:** How have technologies for recording and communicating information changed what can be preserved as knowledge?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 85

**Section:** 7. Methods, technologies, and ways of knowing

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`. Every substantive file states that it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Restatement as an effect

A community holds a library of records. A new recording or communication technology arrives. The question asks what changes about the boundary between a live occasion (an observation, a skill, a measurement, a decision, a correction) and an item that persists and can be composed over by a later frame. Option A: the technology adds to the stock of preserved items. Option B: the technology changes which kinds of content can cross the boundary at all, and which crossings remain composable. What an answer would look like is a boundary condition stated in effect terms, per slot, plus a named failure condition. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` section 0 so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## Verdict in one line

Recording and communication technologies do not simply preserve more knowledge. They change the membrane between an occasion and a record: what crosses, whether the bond and lineage cross with it, and whether the later reader can still range the item against a ground that does not update. More records grow the library, not necessarily the reach.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. Fig 10 :42 says *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interiors.
