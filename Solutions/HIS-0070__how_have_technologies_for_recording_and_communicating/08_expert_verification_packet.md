# Expert verification packet: the Tether on recording, communication and preserved knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of technology, an archivist or a documentation scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the boundary condition, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and the whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

Recording and communication technologies do not simply increase the stock of preserved knowledge; they change a boundary, per slot. A medium opens a slot only where an instrument was already returning something that failed to resolve, or where it imports from a frame whose closure differs. What persists as knowledge carries the bond and the lineage, not only the contents: over a fixed atom set, a record with its derivation and correction history and a record without them are different substances, not variants of one item. A fixed reading can anchor later frames; a feed that updates on its readers becomes another frame. The ground does not decay while the reader channel does, and the transform from occasion to record is lossy, so the round-trip gap must be measured. Skill remains a property of practitioner and habitat, so a recording alone does not preserve a craft. No medium is named as the winner and no preservation quantity is returned.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the bond and the lineage are part of the preserved term, not documentation about it. A bare recording of one occasion is not a term a later frame can compose over.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 13 :130 - *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."* Fig 4 :25 - *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
- **What to check:** read those three lines and ask whether the project's own distinction between a content item and its derivation is being used consistently. The engineering corroboration is W3C PROV-O (2013), which makes `wasDerivedFrom`, `hadPrimarySource` and `wasRevisionOf` first-class relations.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a bare recording of one occasion, with no method, no source and no bond, from which a later frame composes new ground-settled terms. Or show a case where the relation among stored items is fully recoverable from the items alone.

### C2 - history-facing; checkable against a known case

- **Statement:** the effect of a medium is per slot and can flip sign. Writing preserves a legal rule and erodes a mnemonic practice; digitisation preserves a bitstream and can close the reader channel; a searchable database preserves a measurement and can remove the habit of re-deriving it. No standing list of good media survives a change of residual.
- **What to check:** pick one case you know well (the Viking tapes, the BBC Domesday disc, the Loss of the Alexandria library, the shift from workshop apprenticeship to manual, or a documentation project you have worked on). For each of three slots, say what the medium opened and what it closed. If the same medium is preservative for every slot, the claim fails.
- **Figure line:** Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* And Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Refuter:** a documented case where library growth and reach growth coincide without an instrument change and without an import from a differing closure; or a skill shown to be preserved by a recording with no practitioner and no change of contact.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a named strain

- **Statement:** a fixed reading can be an anchor; a living feed cannot. The transform from an occasion into a record is lossy, and the gap must be reported rather than set to zero.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **What to check:** is there a case where a continuously updated record is genuinely the anchor for a historical question? The claim says no: such a record is a frame because it updates on its readers. If you know a counter-case, that is a refutation, not a nuance.
- **Refuter:** a frame shown to be a sealed room whose completeness is provable from inside; or a lossless transform between a live occasion and its record with a measured round-trip gap of zero.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, and not a ranking of media. The standing rule is no last room.
- Not that the digital dark age means knowledge or ground was lost. It is a channel failure (Fig 10 :24).
- Not that an absent record proves an absence (Fig 9 :44 - *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*).
- Not that tacit skill is codifiable, and not that a transcription preserves a craft.
- Not that the framework named a winning medium. A named medium is a provenanced binding from the historical record, not a derivation from the figures.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this session.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A bond discriminator: does the presence of a typed derivation relation change the predictions a later frame can make from a record with the same contents?
2. A reader test: can a frame that did not author a record still unpack it using only standards it did not have to invent?
3. A source discriminator: can an instrument tell which of several sources produced a reading?
4. A contact test: does a practitioner who learned only from a recording change contact with the ground?
5. The four pre-registered refuters in section 2.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, Fig 13 :130, Fig 4 :25, Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :24, five short passages.
2. Take one historical case you know and name three slots: what opened, what closed, and whether the bond crossed.
3. Decide whether the typed relation in your case is present, absent or merely implicit. If absent, say which subtraction occurred.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate among the possible sources of a preserved reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
