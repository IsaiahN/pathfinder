# Expert verification packet — the Tether on soil degradation rates

**What this is:** a claim in a form a soil scientist or agricultural researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the bindings, not `grounded`.** No rate is claimed as settled by this derivation. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how quickly can soil degradation reduce crop productivity, water retention, and ecosystem resilience?" is a union of three slots, not one rate. Productivity, water retention and resilience must each be measured with its own instrument, kept in its own loop and never averaged into a single "speed of soil degradation." The figures supply that shape, not the number. The number is a ground result imported from soil science, arrives as several mutually non-equivalent readings, and stays unaveraged. Resilience is a cascade effect reached second or third contact from the soil change, and management is permanent work because introduction is available while exclusion is not. What is offered is the shape plus refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Keep the three slots separate (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9)

- **Statement:** crop productivity, water retention and ecosystem resilience are three slots; averaging them destroys the signal that says which one is failing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **What to check:** is there a soil case where yield holds while water retention or resilience falls (for example fertilizer compensation masking loss of soil biota)? If so, a single number hides it.
- **Refuter:** show one scalar predicts all three outputs with equal fidelity, so slot-specific measurement adds no residual anywhere. Then a single rate is legitimate and this sub-claim fails.

### C2 — The rate is a ground question; the figures return a shape, not a number (framework-internal + measurement-facing)

- **Statement:** the figures contain no soil-science rate. They give the form of the answer and stop; the numeric rate must be imported from a measurement with provenance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134 — *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
- **What to check:** the four literature numbers in `07` §3 (about 0.4 percent per year; 1.75 percent yield-gap increase per 10 percent degradation; median 3 percent in affected cells; up to 80 years to equilibrium). Do they measure the same quantity? If not, the metamerism reading stands.
- **Refuter:** derive a rate for any one slot from the figure composition alone, with no empirical input, matching a measured rate within its uncertainty band. Or show the four numbers are estimates of one quantity on one scale. Then the gap verdict and the metamerism reading fail.

### C3 — Resilience is a cascade slot and management is permanent (checkable against soil ecology)

- **Statement:** the decisive resilience effect sits second or third contact from the soil change, not at first contact; and recovery is not the reverse of decline because you can add to a habitat but cannot subtract it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 — *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :61 — *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :63 — *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
- **What to check:** is there a measured degradation effect on soil biota or vegetation cover that appears in resilience with no first-contact proxy? Does a restored soil return exactly to its prior state by exclusion, or does it need continued introduction (Lal 2023: up to 80 years to a new equilibrium)?
- **Refuter:** show that resilience impacts are strictly first-contact with no trophic or structural intermediate, and that a managed soil returns exactly to its prior state by exclusion with the same anchor reading before and after. Then this sub-claim fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a single rate for all three outputs, and not a "speed of soil degradation."
- Not that the figures contain a rate constant or settle which literature number is right.
- Not that the four reported rates agree or that the largest is the true one.
- Not that yield loss predicts resilience loss, or that recovery is the inverse of decline.
- Not `grounded` by this derivation. No measurement was taken here.
- Not a claim that the figure set was written about soil. The mapping of soil quantities onto figure quantities is `specified`, an analogy checked for structural fit.

## 5. What would settle it

A slot-specific instrument, not a further argument.

1. **Slot test:** one scalar predicts all three outputs with equal fidelity (kills the three-slot claim).
2. **Yield anchor:** a field trial reporting yield decline per unit erosion with uncertainty, rather than a model median.
3. **Water anchor:** an infiltration or water-retention time series under the same soil and management.
4. **Resilience anchor:** a recovery experiment with a diversity or food-web measure, testing first-contact versus three-steps-out (Fig 11 :52).
5. **Asymmetry anchor:** a before-and-after measurement of whether a managed soil returns exactly to its prior state by exclusion (Fig 11 :61).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 9 :54 and Fig 11 :52, :61, :63. Six short passages.
2. Name one soil case where yield and resilience diverge, or say why none exists.
3. Say which slot each of the four literature rates actually reads, and whether any two read the same slot.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish first-contact from cascade resilience effects, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
