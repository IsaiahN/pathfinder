## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the number does not come from the figures. There are three output slots, they form a union that must be unbundled, and their rates must be kept separate and unaveraged. The numeric rate is a gap from the figure frame and a ground result from soil science, imported with provenance. Resilience is a cascade slot reported three steps out through soil biota and vegetation, and management is permanent work because introduction is available while exclusion is not. Recovery is not the inverse of decline. The divergence among the reported rates is metamerism, several instruments reading different slots, not error around one speed. This is a `general` / `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest figure-grounded point is the refusal of a single aggregate, which Fig 1 :22 states directly.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states refuters before the conclusion and records provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a single average number instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the three-slot shape and its refuter, and by reporting each rate with its own anchor.
- **undoable:** yes. The unaveraged format is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state it. A reported rate can be superseded by a better measured one per slot, and no soil primitive has to be withdrawn. If the slot test fires, the three-slot claim is narrowed to one number, which is a small, reversible edit to the shape.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A field trial showing one scalar predicts all three outputs with equal fidelity would break the three-slot claim; a rate derived from the figures alone matching a measured rate would break the gap verdict; a recovery experiment showing resilience effects are strictly first-contact would break the cascade claim; a before-and-after measurement showing a managed soil returns exactly to its prior state by exclusion would break the asymmetry claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
