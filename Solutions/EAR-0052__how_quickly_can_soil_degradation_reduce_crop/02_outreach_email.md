**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three-slot claim and its refuter below survive your own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

**Subject:** A question about "how quickly" soil degrades, and one thing I would like you to refute

> Hi [name],
>
> I am [your name]. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about soil. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the bindings, not `grounded`):** the question "how quickly can soil degradation reduce crop productivity, water retention, and ecosystem resilience?" is not one rate. It is three outputs measured by three different instruments. Productivity is a yield measurement, water retention is an infiltration or soil-moisture measurement, and ecosystem resilience is a recovery or diversity measurement reached through the food web, not through the crop. The figure-grounded derivation says these three must be kept in separate loops and never averaged into a single "speed of soil degradation." A single number hides which slot is actually failing. The numeric rate itself is not given by the framework. It is settled by a measurement, and the framework returns only the shape the answer must have.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures contain a rate constant, that the literature numbers agree, or that a number from one slot transfers to another. Reported rates differ because they measure different things at different scales. I am asking which slot your instrument actually reads, not asking you to reconcile the numbers.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. One scalar predicts all three outputs with equal fidelity, so that slot-specific measurement adds nothing.
> 2. A rate for a slot is derived from the framework alone, with no empirical input, and matches a measured rate within uncertainty.
> 3. Degradation impacts on resilience are strictly first-contact, with no second- or third-order pathway through the food web.
> 4. A managed soil returns exactly to its prior state by exclusion, with the same anchor reading before and after.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - IIASA open-access paper that reports a median 3 percent annual yield reduction in affected cells: https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/17449/
> - Nature Food open-access paper that reports a 1.75 percent yield-gap increase per 10 percent increase in degradation: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-026-01382-5
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
