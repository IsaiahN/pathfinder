# Degradation mechanisms as isomers, and the order of the three slots

**Mode: `specified` on the bindings, `general` on the Fig 12 structural rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes three slots and a ground-set rate. Two questions remain: (1) are the degradation mechanisms distinct substances or variants of one thing, and (2) in what order must the three slots be read so a reading below a break is not a reading of nothing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the soil frame: mineral particles, organic matter, pore space, water, nutrients, soil biota, vegetation cover. Bonds available: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. No new soil primitive is assumed; a mechanism that needs one must import it with provenance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are erosion, salinization, compaction and carbon loss isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."*

Reading the mechanisms over the shared atom set:

| mechanism | dominant bond (Fig 12 / Operators) | atoms acted on | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| water or wind erosion | subtraction (`−`, Fig 12 :38) | topsoil, organic matter, pore space | physical removal; a difference is spent |
| salinization | conjunction (`+`, Fig 12 :55) or addition of an operand | salts, water, root zone | chemical state with same mineral base |
| compaction | conjunction of loads raising bulk density | pore space, roots, water | physical structure change, no removal |
| SOC loss / nutrient depletion | subtraction (`−`) | organic matter, nutrients | chemical and biological capital drawn down |

Over the full shared atom set these are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the stronger, checkable claim. Over the narrower sets used in practice they are **functional groups**: physical, chemical, biological, ecological, which Lal (2023, opened 2026-11-22) lists as "physical processes such as crusting, compaction, drought-flood syndrome and accelerated erosion by water and wind; chemical processes such as salinization, acidification, nutrient depletion, pollution and contamination; biological processes such as a decline in soil organic matter content or a reduction in activity and species diversity of soil biota; and ecological processes such as alterations in soil temperature and moisture regimes."

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one bond accounts for all four mechanisms with no remainder (for example all are pure subtraction of a single capital stock, so that one scalar predicts every outcome), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading collapses into a single mechanism. That is the same test as F1's slot refuter in `01`, approached from structure rather than from measurement.

### Q2 — What order do the three slots run in?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

Dependency order for this question (reading the habitat from contact outward, Fig 11 :43-44):

1. **Perception:** is there a measurable degradation state? Instrument: soil survey, remote sensing, field trial. Without it, every later rate is undiagnosable.
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for the soil functions at stake (water-holding capacity, aggregate stability, organic matter, food-web cover)? This is the middle link most likely to break, because it has neither obvious failure nor obvious test (Fig 3 :25-27).
3. **Objective:** what counts as decline in each slot, in that slot's own currency (yield percent, infiltration rate, recovery time)?
4. **Planning:** which intervention is available? Only introduction is available (Fig 11 :61), so the plan is an addition, never a subtraction of the habitat.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next field (Fig 3 :15).

Among the three output slots the dependency is partial: water retention and nutrient supply feed productivity, while resilience depends on biological cover and is reached further out (Fig 11 :52). They are not reduced to one chain; they remain three loops sharing an upstream soil state.

### Q3 — What is the analogue, and where does it come from?

Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

The DDT/eagles case is the figure's own analogue for the resilience slot: a soil or water contaminant acts first on a lower trophic level, then biomagnifies, and the measured effect (eggshell thinning) appears three steps from the original contact. Applied here, a degradation process acting on soil biota can have its decisive productivity or resilience effect several contacts away. The analogue is cited with provenance because Fig 8 :50-52 warns that convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them.

**Falsifier for the analogue:** show that for a given degradation process the effect on resilience is fully measured at first contact, with no trophic or structural intermediate carrying residual. Then the cascade analogue fails for that process.

### Q4 — Is recovery an isomer of decline, or an inverse under a different bond?

Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."*
Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Recovery and decline share atoms but not bonds. Decline spends a difference (Fig 13 :22, *"a difference that can be spent"*); recovery requires continued introduction into a habitat that cannot be subtracted back. They are therefore not inverses under one bond. Lal (2023, opened 2026-11-22) supplies the ground horizon: "reaching a new equilibrium of SOC stock may take up to 80 years after land-use change." A rate measured in the decline direction cannot be negated to obtain a recovery rate.

**Falsifier:** a managed soil returns exactly to its prior state by exclusion, with the same anchor reading before and after. Then decline and recovery are inverses and this finding fails.

## CONCLUSION

The four degradation mechanisms are isomers over the shared soil atom set and functional groups over the narrower practical sets. The three output slots must be read in dependency order, with the vocabulary link (what counts as decline in each slot) named as the most likely break. The DDT/eagles case is the figure's own cascade analogue for resilience, cited with provenance. Recovery is not an inverse of decline because the bonds differ. Each of these claims carries its own falsifier; none is a ground reading.
