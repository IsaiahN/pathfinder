# The Tether's answer on how quickly soil degradation reduces the three outputs

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the bindings, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a rate. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. The numeric ranges below are ground results from soil-science sources, reported as imports, not derived here and never averaged into one number.

## 1. The residual it answers

"How quickly" asks for a rate. In effect terms the question bundles three outputs measured by three different instruments: crop productivity (yield), water retention (infiltration or water-holding capacity), and ecosystem resilience (recovery time or diversity, reached through the food web). Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* So this is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground per slot, not one hard question.

## 2. The shape (verdicts kept separate, never averaged)

1. **There are three output slots, not one.** Productivity, water retention and resilience each need their own currency, anchor and mode. A single "speed of soil degradation" is not a well-formed readout. Mode `general`, triangulated: Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 9 :54, :55.
2. **The rate is not in the figure closure.** The figures give the form of the answer, not the number. Fig 2 :29 names the anchor as a measurement or experiment; Fig 13 says "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." From the figures the rate is a `gap`; from soil science it is a `ground` import. Mode `general`, triangulated: Fig 2, Fig 1 :23, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134.
3. **The three slots form a union of overlapping conditions.** They share drivers (organic-matter loss degrades all three) but not anchors, so unbundle before measuring. Mode `general`, single-source: Fig 9 :54, corroborated by the *Library of Babel* essay (shared author, so not independent).
4. **Resilience is the cascade slot, reported three steps out.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Effects on soil biota and vegetation cover reach resilience through second- and third-order contact, not through the crop. Mode `general`, single-source: Fig 11.
5. **Management is permanent work; recovery is not the reverse of decline.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Mode `general`, single-source: Fig 11.
6. **Degradation spends a directional difference; the figure gives direction, not slope.** Fig 13 :22 calls a gradient "a difference that can be spent." Mapping this onto soil fertility/structure is `specified`; reading it as a rate was strained and re-censused (audit §5.1).

## 3. The ground numbers, reported unaveraged (world imports, not figure outputs)

These are the closest public measurements found and opened on 2026-11-22. Each is a reading of a different slot, instrument and scale. They are **not** to be combined.

| slot | reported rate | source and mode |
|---|---|---|
| Productivity, global yield trend | about 0.4 percent per year global crop-yield reduction due to erosion; 0.3 percent per year cropland productivity loss over the last century | FAO soil-erosion assessment (2019) and World Bank SOC MRV Sourcebook (2021); `ground` for those datasets |
| Productivity, yield gap | 1.75 percent increase in yield gap per 10 percent increase in overall degradation; up to 6 percent in hotspots | Hadi et al., Nature Food (2026); `ground` for that model, cross-sectional |
| Productivity, affected cells | median 3 percent annual yield reduction in grid cells affected by water erosion | Carr et al., Agriculture, Ecosystems & Environment (2021); `ground` for that model, affected cells only |
| Water retention | compaction "restricts root penetration, water infiltration and retention"; conservation agriculture increased water-holding capacity over two decades | Lal (2023) and Alvarez-Sagrero et al., SOIL (2026); `ground` for those trials |
| Resilience / recovery | up to 80 years to a new soil-carbon equilibrium after land-use change; loss of habitat and diversity increases drought vulnerability | Lal (2023) and FAO newsroom (2025); `ground` for those observations |

The disagreement among the top three rows is not error around one speed. It is metamerism: several instruments reading different projections. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, OPENED): "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not an average. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single rate for all three outputs. Not a "speed of soil degradation."
- Not a figure-derived number. The figures hold no soil physics; the number is an import with provenance.
- Not that the literature numbers agree, and not that the highest one is the real one.
- Not that yield loss predicts resilience loss. They are different slots with different anchors.
- Not that recovery is the inverse of decline.
- Not `grounded` by the figures. Nothing in this answer is asserted as settled by a measurement taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

A slot-specific instrument, not a further argument.

- **Slot test:** show one scalar predicts all three outputs with equal fidelity, so slot-specific measurement adds nothing. That would collapse the three-slot claim.
- **Yield anchor:** a field trial that reports yield decline per unit erosion with its uncertainty, rather than a gridded model median. Fig 2 :29: the anchor is a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it.
- **Water anchor:** an infiltration or water-retention time series with the same soil and management, so the water slot gets its own rate rather than being inferred from yield.
- **Resilience anchor:** a recovery experiment with a diversity or food-web measure, testing whether the effect is first-contact or three steps out (Fig 11 :52).
- **Asymmetry anchor:** a before-and-after measurement of whether a managed soil returns exactly to its prior state by exclusion (Fig 11 :61).
- **Standing refuters from the audit:** derive a rate from the figures alone matching a measured rate; show no cascade pathway to resilience; show decline and recovery are inverses.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The three-slot shape survives the figures; the rate does not live in them. The figure set stops at the limit of what its instruments can distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Underivable from the figure frame is not unknowable: the soil-science frame already holds the numbers, and they are reported above with provenance. What the figures cannot do is certify which number is the right one for a given field. That is the ground's office, not the framework's.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a single "speed of soil degradation," and it does not claim the figures contain a rate. It says the question is three questions wearing one coat: crop productivity, water retention and ecosystem resilience are measured by three different instruments, so their rates must be kept in separate loops and never averaged. The figures return that shape, not the numbers. The numbers come from soil science and they disagree (roughly 0.4 percent per year global yield loss from erosion, a 1.75 percent yield-gap increase per 10 percent degradation, a median 3 percent annual reduction in affected cells, and up to 80 years to a new soil-carbon equilibrium), because they read different slots at different scales. Resilience is the effect reported three steps out through the food web, and management is permanent work because you can add to a soil habitat but cannot subtract it back. What the package offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close each slot. The rate stays open until the ground settles it, slot by slot.
