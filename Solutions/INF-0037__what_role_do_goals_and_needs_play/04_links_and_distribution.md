## What in the wider literature bears on this question

The world sources split cleanly by field, which is the point of the search. None of them decides the verdict, but each predicts the same shape from a different closure:

- **Cognitive pragmatics (relevance theory).** Relevance is comparative and is a function of cognitive effects relative to the individual's goals and the effort spent. This predicts that usefulness is a relation, not a carried property. It corroborates F1. Overlap with the framework is zero: different author, different venue.
- **Decision theory (value of information).** The value of an item is what a decision maker would pay for it before a decision, and it is never negative because the item can be ignored. This predicts that the same item has different value in different decision situations. It corroborates F1 and the asymmetry in Fig 11. Overlap: zero.
- **Information science (relevance in IR).** Relevance is defined against the *information need of the user*; the field explicitly separates topical relevance from user relevance, and maximal marginal relevance scores an item by the new information it brings given previous results. This predicts both the relational claim and the subtraction structure in F2. Overlap: zero.
- **Behavioural ecology (optimal foraging).** What counts as a profitable item is a hypothesis about the forager's currency and constraints, and those can change. This predicts valence relative to the residual. Overlap: zero.
- **Cognitive psychology (attention).** Goal-directed, endogenous orienting is a distinct mechanism from stimulus-driven orienting. This predicts that goals decide what gets selected for processing. Overlap: zero.
- **Philosophy of information (SEP).** Shannon information is deliberately weak on meaning and truth, so it cannot by itself answer a question about usefulness. This corroborates Fig 1's disqualification of frame-internal scores (F5). Overlap: zero.
- **Framework essays (high overlap, weak corroboration).** All seven essays are by the framework's author, so their agreement with the figures is expected. The Library of Babel essay is the most directly on point: *"The problem you have determines what makes a book relevant to you or not."* That is a corroborating witness from a different genre, not an independent one.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape claim and its refuter first. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement, and do not select recipients by likely agreement.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the claim were true, before showing them the article.
- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away gives away no answer.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A later agreement with this folder's claim is never pooled as corroboration.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the bound shape claim `USEFUL(i | R, goal)`, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask: *what would you count as a failure here?* Record the answer before any theory is shown.

**Stage 2, opt in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the figures and formula links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

**Stage 3, method only.** Offer the formula as a reusable method. Require each user to write their own refuter and state their own mode before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (Formula, Direction; Fig 5 :66).

**Honest limit.** This is a simulated derivation. It cannot settle the question it reports on. A recipient's failure is more useful than their endorsement, because a failure is ground-visible and an endorsement is not.
