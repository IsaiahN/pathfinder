# The Tether's answer: the role of goals and needs in determining which information is useful

**Mode: `general` on the structural role, `specified` on the per-slot routing prediction, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a relation, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system faces a stream of candidate items and treats some as useful and most as noise. The question asks what assigns that valence. Option A says usefulness is carried by the item: its entropy, length, truth or rarity. Option B says usefulness is a relation between the item and a live residual, where goals and needs set what the residual is a residual of. The residual this answer addresses is: *why does the same item count as useful for one system and noise for another, and can a system score its own usefulness without leaving the frame?*

## 2. The shape

1. **Usefulness is valence relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50-51: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds. No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* An item of information is a habitat actor. Calling it useful assigns it a valence, and the goal or need supplies the residual that fixes the valence. **Triangulated** with Fig 1.

2. **Goals and needs are bond compositions, not primitives.** Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*; :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*. A need is `state ⋛ target` for the goal and `current − target` for the distance. A vocabulary without these ordered bonds can name a state but cannot state a goal or a route to one (Fig 12 :111). **Triangulated** between the bond grammar and Fig 3's objective link.

3. **The goal decides which slot is live, and the slot decides what can matter.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :15: *"where to look: broken vs novel"*. Symbols table :38-40: R is *"the aim, not only the error signal"*. The chain is: comparison fixes a target, miss puts positive mass on one slot, the live slot sets where to look, and an item that reduces that slot's residual is useful. **Triangulated** between Fig 1 and Fig 9.

4. **Frame-internal scores cannot do the job.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Coverage, compression achieved, predicates minted and credibility accrued are all frame-internal. Shannon entropy, by itself, cannot decide usefulness. This is **single-source** within the figures but externally corroborated by the Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Information.

5. **The residual selects the frame and the habitat.** Fig 9 :26: *"R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ"*. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 7 :40: *"The room is summoned by the residual rather than composed."* Describe the need in effect terms before choosing a field, or any field will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28). **Triangulated** across Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 9.

6. **Goals select only when bound.** Fig 5 :62: *"holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term"*. A goal held as an unstated preference for a particular answer predicts nothing, accrues no residual, and steers the search without being checkable. The remedy is to write the shape down as an ordinary term and let it be refused (Fig 5 :66). This is the caution, not a rejection of goals. **Triangulated** with Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that usefulness is a number, or that this derivation computes one. `USEFUL(i | R, goal)` is a structural relation.
- Not that goals are the only determinant. Contact matters too: Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- Not that a need is a seventh primitive. That reading is retracted in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.
- Not which specific item is useful in any particular domain. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No decision outcome was measured here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An item's being attended to is a reading, not a source. Several sources can produce the same reading. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and what is owed is an instrument that discriminates, not a name. Concretely:

- **Per-slot test.** In a setting with two tracked objects, perturb only the slot that is live under the stated goal and then only the other slot. Does attention follow the live slot rather than the total residual? This would settle the routing claim.
- **Aggregation test.** Compare a per-slot residual against a global average for the same sequence of observations. Does the average lose the routing signal, as Fig 1 :22 predicts?
- **Frame-internal test.** Score the same items by coverage or compression and by reduction of the live residual. Which score predicts the decision outcome?
- **Direction test.** Withhold the written shape in one run and bind it in another. Does the unbound run produce a preference that survives no ground check?

Standing falsifiers are in §3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. Any one firing withdraws the corresponding finding.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures license the relational shape and the bond structure. They do not price a specific item's usefulness, and this simulated derivation cannot manufacture a ground reading. *They're Scanning Books*: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* A system without a comparison operator may not have goals in the sense derived here. The stop is recorded so a later reader can locate it. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Usefulness is not carried by an item of information. It is a relation between the item and a live residual. Goals and needs do two things in the Tether: they are bond compositions, a comparison that states the target and a subtraction that measures the distance still to go, and through those they decide which object slot carries positive residual and therefore which information can matter. The same item can be useful to one system and noise to another, and no standing list of useful items survives a change of residual. A global average destroys the routing signal, and a frame cannot score its own usefulness with a quantity it also produces, such as coverage or compression. Goals help when written down as testable terms; held as unstated preferences, they steer the search without being checkable. What the framework offers is the shape any useful item must have and the refuters written in advance, not a named item. The specific judgment stops until a decision outcome settles it.
