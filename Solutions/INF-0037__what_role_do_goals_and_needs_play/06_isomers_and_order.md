### Q3. What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Mapped to this question:

1. **Vocabulary (link 2).** Have terms for what a situation affords, including the comparison and subtraction bonds. Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*.
2. **Objective (link 3).** Say what would count as progress in the system's own terms. Fig 3 :10 *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*, Fig 3 :11 *"what the search is for"*.
3. **Perception (link 1).** Measure R per slot against the ground. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
4. **Planning (link 4).** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Valence and habitat.** Assign usefulness relative to the live residual, then enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering. Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

The middle link, vocabulary to objective, is where this question sits. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The honest reading is that this derivation has an instrument for the structure (the bond tests) but no ground measurement for a specific usefulness judgment. That is a `specified` result, not a `grounded` one.

**Falsifier:** show that link 3 can operate when link 2 is absent, that is, a system that can say what counts as progress with no comparison or subtraction operator. That breaks the dependency order.

### Q4. Are the outside analogues isomers or just homonyms?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

- **Relevance theory** (Sperber and Wilson, read via Wikipedia 2026-11-23): relevance is comparative, a function of positive cognitive effects and processing effort, and cognitive effects are defined relative to the individual's goals. This is not the same atoms as the figures (no six-term substrate, no closure), so it is **not an isomer**. It is a convergent framing at maximum independence: it reaches a relational usefulness with a different currency and a different ground.
- **Value of information** (decision theory): value is what a decision maker would pay before a decision, and cannot be negative because the item can be ignored. Different atoms, different currency (money, expected utility). **Convergent, not isomeric.**
- **Relevance in information retrieval**: relevance is how well a document meets the *information need of the user*, and the field distinguishes topical from user relevance. Same relational shape, different primitives. **Convergent, not isomeric.**
- **Optimal foraging**: the currency is a hypothesis about what the forager is maximizing, and goals and currencies can change. **Convergent, not isomeric.**
- **Goal-directed attention**: endogenous orienting is allocation based on goals or instructions, distinct from stimulus-driven orienting. **Convergent, not isomeric.**

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* The record here is explicit: the figure verdict comes from the figures, and the five outside fields are witnesses with their own grounds. Their agreement is corroboration only, and because they are low-overlap with the framework and with each other, it is weak but real corroboration.

**Falsifier:** exhibit an outside field that reaches the same per-slot routing prediction from a closure that shares no relational notion of goal, need or information need. If one is found, the convergent-reading claim must be re-censused as a shared assumption rather than independent arrival.

### Q5. Where do the analogues strain?

Optimal foraging is the strained one. Its currency is usually net energy per unit time, which is a quantity in a closure the figures do not hold. Reading "currency" as "the live residual" is an analogy, not a derivation. The figures themselves warn at Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect that transfers is: *what is selected depends on what the selector is trying to get*, not the particular currency. That is as far as the analogue is pushed. The strain is named and not smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

Goals and needs are **bond compositions**, not primitives and not atoms: comparison for the target, subtraction for the distance, sequence for a route, negation for a refusal. Their dependency position is link 3 of Fig 3, between vocabulary and planning, and the figures say that link is exactly where instruments are usually missing. Five outside fields reach a relational usefulness independently, but they are convergent witnesses, not isomers, because they do not share the six-term substrate. No ground measurement in this derivation prices a specific item's usefulness, so the result stays `specified`.
