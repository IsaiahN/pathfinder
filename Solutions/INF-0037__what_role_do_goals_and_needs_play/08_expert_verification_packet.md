# Expert verification packet: goals, needs and which information is useful

**What this is:** a claim in a form a relevance researcher, information scientist or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural role, `specified` on the per-slot routing prediction, not `grounded`.** No decision outcome was measured. This is a relation and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Usefulness is valence relative to a live residual, not a property carried by the item. Goals and needs set what the residual is a residual of: a goal is a comparison against a target, progress is a subtraction of the distance still to go, and through those the system decides which object slot carries positive residual. Information that reduces that slot's residual is useful to that system; the same item can be noise to a system whose live slot differs. A global average loses this routing, and a frame cannot score its own usefulness with a quantity it also produces. Goals select when written down as testable terms; as unstated preferences they steer the search without a receipt.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 1

- **Statement:** item valence is relative to the residual. There is no standing list of useful items that survives a change of goal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50-51 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds. No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 1 :15 *"where to look: broken vs novel"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit one item whose assigned usefulness is invariant across two residuals with different live slots, where the framework must assign the same valence in both, or exhibit a standing useful-item list that survives a change of residual.

### C2. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** a goal is a comparison `⋛`, progress is a subtraction `−`, a plan is a sequence `→`, and a refusal is a negation `¬` which is not a bond. Goals and needs are bond compositions over held atoms, not primitives and not a seventh member of the six-term set.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Operators table :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*; :46 *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*.
- **Refuter:** state a goal, its progress and a route using only the symmetric bonds `+ ∥ ≡`, or derive a goal as one of the six primitives with no ordered operator. Either result breaks C2.

### C3. Mixed, framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by an information scientist

- **Statement:** the routing must be per slot, not global; frame-internal scores such as coverage and compression cannot decide usefulness; and the residual must be described before a frame is chosen.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 9 :28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check against your own field:** information retrieval defines relevance against the *information need of the user* and separates topical from user relevance; maximal marginal relevance scores an item by the new information it brings given previous results. Relevance theory defines relevance as cognitive effects relative to the listener's goals against processing effort. Do those definitions have the same relational shape as `USEFUL(i | R, goal)`?
- **Refuter:** show a global average residual routing to the live slot more reliably than the per-slot signal across repeated trials, or show a frame scoring its own usefulness with a quantity it also produces and having that score settle a decision at the ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical utility function and not a computed usefulness score.
- Not that goals are the only determinant; contact matters too (Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*).
- Not that a need is a primitive or a seventh member of the substrate. That reading is retracted.
- Not that the framework names a specific useful item. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No decision outcome was measured.
- Not corroborated by the framework essays. Those are by the framework's author and carry high overlap with the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

1. **Per-slot perturbation.** With two tracked objects, perturb only the slot that is live under the stated goal and then only the other. Does attention follow the live slot?
2. **Aggregation comparison.** Run the per-slot residual and a global average over the same observations. Does the average lose the routing signal?
3. **Score discrimination.** Compare coverage, compression and residual reduction as predictors of a decision outcome.
4. **Direction check.** Withhold the written shape in one run and bind it in another. Does the unbound run produce an unrefusable preference?

From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*. Attending to an item is a reading, not a source. The instrument that discriminates among goal-driven selection, stimulus-driven salience and shared habit would settle more than an argument.
