# Outreach draft: check one prediction about usefulness before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter are both in the recipient's hands before any theory. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the order is reversed.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about what makes information useful

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output from a derivation I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the routing prediction, not `grounded`):**
>
> Usefulness is not carried by an item of information. It is a relation between the item and a live residual. Goals and needs set what the residual is a residual of: a goal is a comparison against a target state, and progress is a subtraction of the distance still to go. The comparison puts positive residual on one object slot, and that live slot decides which information can matter. Information that reduces the live slot's residual is useful to that system; the same item can be noise to a system whose live slot is different.
>
> Two consequences I would want checked:
> 1. A global average of the residual should lose the routing signal. A per-slot residual should retain it.
> 2. A frame should not be able to score its own usefulness with a quantity it also produces, such as coverage or compression achieved.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which specific item is useful in your domain. The framework returns a shape, not an item list. I am also not claiming that goals always help. A goal written down as a testable term can select; a goal held as an unstated preference for a particular answer steers the search without being checkable, and I treat that as a failure mode.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. One item whose usefulness is invariant across two residuals with different live slots, where both valences must be assigned the same.
> 2. A goal or a progress term expressible with no ordered comparison or subtraction operator.
> 3. A global average residual that routes to the live slot better than the per-slot signal.
> 4. A frame that certifies the direction of its own question without writing the sought-for shape down, and survives the ground.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The thirteen figures and THE FORMULA (SVG and text, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The architecture article: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
