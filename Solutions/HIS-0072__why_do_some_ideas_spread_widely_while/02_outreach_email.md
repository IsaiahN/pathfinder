# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note to a public submission through a venue in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0
are stated in the recipient's own terms. The stage-1 output must survive a check or the two-stage offer
collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why ideas travel, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you
> to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive,
> there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` per condition, not `grounded`):** "why do some ideas spread"
> is not one question. It is a union of four conditions, and a single lever clears only the region where
> one condition holds. The shape any answer must have is:
>
> 1. The gap is per receiving frame, not an adoption count. A frame that can only name the idea has not
>    closed it; a frame that can reapply it has.
> 2. An idea is taken up when it composes from atoms the receiver already holds, and when stating it costs
>    less than the confusion it removes. Too general, or true of one occasion only, fails.
> 3. What crosses between a specialist scale and a population is a re-applicable method, never a recording
>    of one success.
> 4. Spread through high-overlap frames is a convention; spread through independent frames is
>    corroboration. These are categorically different and are never averaged.
> 5. Whether it travels also depends on the habitat: the actors and conditions in contact with the idea,
>    not only on the idea.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that simplicity alone explains spread, that the most-cited idea is the best
> one, or that adapter count is evidence of truth. Agreement among people who read the same literature is
> efficient for a shared vocabulary and disqualifying as a verdict. A filter such as prestige can give an
> idea a budget to be tried; it cannot settle the idea.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. one intrinsic property of an idea predicts spread across independent cases with no dependence on
>    receiver reach or habitat contact;
> 2. a recording with no re-applicable rule crosses to a population and is used there to generate new
>    successes;
> 3. two frames drawing on one evidence pool produce verdict-grade corroboration, or a mirror chain adds
>    evidence;
> 4. a filter, with no new ground contact, changes the verdict rather than only the budget.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A
> one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. What would you count as a failure?
> If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary
> of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Rogers, "Diffusion of innovations" (public overview): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_of_innovations
> - Centola and Macy, "Complex contagions and the weakness of long ties" (public record): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_contagion
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a diffusion case you
> know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and
> state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
