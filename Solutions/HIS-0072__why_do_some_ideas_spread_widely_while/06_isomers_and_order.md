# Union, order, and isomers in a diffusion question

**Mode: `specified` on the order and the isomer claim, `general` on the union reading, not `grounded`.**
This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

An idea stops at a specialist boundary. We want to know whether it is one blocked question or several, in
what order the conditions must be cleared, and whether two ideas with the same substantive content can
have different fates because their internal arrangement differs. The supporting derivation supplies the
structure the main verdict needs but does not itself decide any case.

Atoms held: the four conditions (reach/cost, method form, independence, habitat). Bonds held: the seven

## Q1 - One question or a union?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The four conditions overlap (reach depends partly on habitat; method form depends partly on cost), so the
union cannot be treated as a list. A lever that clears reach alone leaves the intersection standing because
the independence condition still binds. The residue is "categorically different", not merely smaller.
**Conclusion:** union compound; four loops, four refuters, never one averaged score.

## Q2 - What is the order of the conditions?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above
  produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Dependency order:

1. **Reach and cost (D1).** Can a receiver compose the term from atoms it already holds, and does the term
   cost less to state than the confusion it removes? Fig 5 :26.
2. **Method form (D2).** Can the term cross the scale membrane as a re-applicable generator rather than a
   recording? Fig 4 :22.
3. **Independence (D3).** Does it travel through low-overlap ties, so that the spread is triangulation
   rather than a mirror chain? Fig 2 :31, Fig 7 :36.
4. **Habitat (D4).** Is the contact the term needs reproduced in the new habitat, including second-order
   effects? Fig 11 :52.

D3 is the likeliest break, because it is the least instrumented: "having terms for what a situation offers
and being able to say what would count as progress" has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests
(Fig 3 :25-26). A campaign that improves the phrasing of an idea (work above the break) cannot rescue a
missing independence axis below it.

## Q3 - Are two ideas with the same content isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the
  formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind;
  the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."

Two ideas can hold the same substantive atoms, the same evidence and the same claims, and differ in bond:

| arrangement | bond | predicted substance |
|---|---|---|
| claim presented as a finished result | recording, `⇒` one-off | repeats a past success, cannot generate a new one (Fig 4 :25) |
| claim presented as a re-applicable rule | method, generator | crosses scale and remains useful across uses (Fig 4 :22, Fig 12 :119) |
| claim relayed through high-overlap peers | convention bond, correlated | efficient for shared meaning, disqualifying as a verdict (Fig 2 :41) |
| claim relayed through independent instruments | triangulation, low overlap | surplus, real reach (Fig 8 :45) |

So the isomer claim is: **same atoms, different arrangement, different diffusion substance.** This is the
structural version of the metamerism point in `05`: a reading of the content cannot tell you which
arrangement produced the spread. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two ideas with identical atom
sets and identical bond arrangements show systematically different spread, the isomer claim fails and the
difference must come from habitat or receiver reach instead. Conversely, if two ideas with different bonds
spread identically in independent cases, the bond classification has no purchase.

## Q4 - What does an idea that stops owe: SEARCHED, INWARD, or OUTWARD?

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 names three questions when nothing in `closure(Gamma)` closes the residual. They are
distinct and must not be read against one another.

- **SEARCHED first.** "is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change: same atoms, same wall, more
  of the space actually visited." Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound?"
  The remedy is distributed, independent search, not a new atom.
- **INWARD.** "extend the instrument, from one already returning something. Sharpen in our own terms."
  Used when a receiver can register something but not resolve it.
- **OUTWARD.** "IMPORT. Characterize R in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its
  shape." Fig 8 :54: "The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so
  underivable is not unknowable." Requires a source gate and a shadow test, and is debited against
  independence.

And the budget distinction matters: `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 says "underivable-in-principle and
unreachable-at-this-budget are DIFFERENT FACTS with different instructions". Fig 8 :41: "Unreachable: the
term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."
Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

Applied here: a specialist idea that has never reached a wide audience may be merely **unsearched**
(population of receivers too small and correlated), **instrument-limited** (nobody can yet measure who can
reapply it), or **out of reach** (receivers' closure lacks an atom another frame holds). These three
require different interventions and are commonly confused. The shape does not choose among them; a ground
reading must.

## CONCLUSION

The diffusion question is a union compound with a dependency order (reach, method form, independence,
habitat) and a real isomer structure (same atoms, different bond, different spread). A stalled idea can owe
SEARCHED, INWARD, or OUTWARD, and the three are not interchangeable. All of this is structural. Nothing in
it names which condition dominates in any actual case; that requires a measurement with recorded
independence.
