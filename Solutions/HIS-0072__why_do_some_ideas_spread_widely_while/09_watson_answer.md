**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the four domain predictions, not `grounded`.**

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what governs the difference between an idea that reaches many frames and one that
stays inside a specialist community. Option A: treat spread as one intrinsic property of the idea and rank
ideas by that property. Option B: treat spread as a union of four conditions, each with its own ground and
its own refuter, and keep the convention/corroboration distinction explicit. What each leads to: A gives a
single lever that clears only the region where one condition holds, mistakes an adoption count for the
ground, and lets a filter hand the verdict; B gives a checkable shape with four refuters, refuses to average
the conditions, and leaves the name of the winning mechanism to a measurement.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or
union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** For a candidate term `phi`, the gap between how many frames can compose and reapply
  it and how many people can only name it. Arity: a term plus a set of receiving frames, minimum two
  frames but the effect only appears at three or more. Symmetry: directed and threshold gated, not
  symmetric; the relevant bonds are `→`, `⇒` and `⋛`, not `+`. Scale: one specialist frame versus a
  population, plus a same-scale cross-frame axis. Fig 9 names this a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, the idea as candidate term.** Actors: `phi`, atoms, bonds, receivers' `Gamma`. Currency:
  description cost in bits, actions to settle, residual removed. Anchor: the ground, which settles truth but
  does not settle spread. Question type: partition (which compositions cross the two kinds).
- **Domain 2, cross-scale transmission.** Actors: one agent or specialist frame, a population. Currency:
  method versus recording. Anchor: re-applicability, tested by a receiver generating a new success. Question
  type: a membrane rule, not a wall.
- **Domain 3, cross-frame spread.** Actors: frames and ties. Currency: agreement versus evidence. Anchor:
  the ground, never agreement. Question type: a categorical split (convention versus corroboration), not a
  continuum.
- **Domain 4, the habitat.** Actors: actors, conditions, relations in contact with the residual. Currency:
  contact cascades. Anchor: the ground, which keeps what survives. Question type: enumeration, not
  composition.
- **Order by dependency:** D1 reach and cost, then D2 method form, then D3 independence, then D4 habitat
  (Fig 3 :20). The residual selects before any frame is named (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: Fig 6 reachability and Fig 11 habitat both concern what a receiver already has, but
Fig 6 governs atoms in `Gamma` while Fig 11 governs actors and conditions in contact with the residual.
Different objects, no strain after re-census.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; they do not decide. Every source below was opened 2026-11-23.

- **Business and innovation research.** Everett Rogers, "Diffusion of innovations", Wikipedia (secondary
  summary, original book 1962): "This gap between niche appeal and mass (self-sustained) adoption was
  originally labeled 'the marketing chasm'." Predicts a threshold between local and wide spread; predicts
  failed diffusion that is "widely adopted within certain clusters but fail[s] to make an impact on more
  distantly related people". **Corroborates** D1 and D3. Overlap: moderate with other diffusion sources.
- **Network science.** Damon Centola and Michael Macy, "Complex contagion", Wikipedia summary of the 2007
  paper: "multiple sources of exposure to an innovation are required before an individual adopts the change
  of behavior." Predicts a threshold `q` and a need for reinforcement; four mechanisms (coordination,
  credibility, legitimacy, emotional contagion) are habitat conditions. **Corroborates** D3 and D4.
- **Mathematical sociology.** Mark Granovetter, threshold model, Wikipedia summary: "the number or
  proportion of others who must make one decision before a given actor does so". Predicts different
  thresholds per receiver, so an aggregate curve hides the structure. **Corroborates** D1 and D3.
- **Network ties.** Mark Granovetter, "Interpersonal ties", Wikipedia (1973 paper summarized): "more novel
  information flows to individuals through weak rather than strong ties", because close friends' information
  "overlaps considerably with what we already know". Predicts that low-overlap bridges carry novel reach.
  **Corroborates** D3.
- **Marketing science.** Frank Bass, diffusion model, Wikipedia summary of the 1969 paper: "adopters can be
  classified as innovators or as imitators". Reports a negative-imitation case where the market saturates
  below full adoption. **Corroborates** D1 and the union reading; **conflicts** with any single-lever reading.
- **History and philosophy of science.** Thomas Kuhn, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962),
  Wikipedia summary: periods of "normal science" with shared exemplars are interrupted by revolutions, and
  "scientists with different paradigms engage in different concrete laboratory manipulations". Predicts the
  high-overlap specialist community and the need for a different frame. **Corroborates** D3 and D4.
- **Sociology of science.** Robert Merton and Harriet Zuckerman, Matthew effect (1968), Wikipedia summary:
  "credit will usually be given to researchers who are already famous"; preferential attachment in network
  science. Predicts that filters steer attention without settling truth. **Corroborates** the filter verdict.
- **Evolutionary anthropology.** Boyd and Richerson, dual inheritance theory, Wikipedia summary: "conformist
  biases make it difficult for novel cultural traits to spread through a population", and model-based biases
  include prestige, skill, success and similarity. Predicts specialist confinement and filter steering from
  a field with low overlap with the figures. **Corroborates** D3 and the filter verdict.
- **Memetics.** Richard Dawkins, *Meme* (1976), Wikipedia summary: "Memes that replicate most effectively
  enjoy more success, and some may replicate effectively even when they prove to be detrimental to the
  welfare of their hosts." Predicts that spread and settlement are different axes. **Corroborates** D1.
- **Knowledge management.** Lave and Wenger, communities of practice (1991), Wikipedia summary: a group
  that "share[s] a concern or a passion for something they do and learn how to do it better as they interact
  regularly", with tacit knowledge that "cannot easily be captured, codified and stored". Predicts that
  local know-how resists transmission as a recording and needs contact. **Corroborates** D2 and D4.
- **Framework essays (high overlap, counted as one evidence pool).** Isaiah N, *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*
  (2026): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to
  see, because anyone can check that against the world." **Corroborates** the describe-the-residual-first
  outreach order. Overlap: high, same author as the figures.
- **Framework essays (high overlap).** Isaiah N, *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026): "Prestige can
  fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground
  can settle it." **Corroborates** the filter verdict. Overlap: high.

**Conflict check.** Centola and Macy find that weak ties can slow *complex* diffusion, while Granovetter
finds weak ties carry novel information. These govern different quantities (novel information transfer
versus reinforcement-based adoption), so the re-census keeps both as conditions of D3 rather than flipping
either verdict. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. Agreement from the essays is one pool,
not corroboration.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any single-mechanism name does not. Four conditions govern wide spread, they overlap as
a union and are never averaged: receiver reach and cost, method form, independence, and habitat. Adoption
count is frame-internal and cannot stand as the ground. High-overlap spread is a convention and is
disqualifying as a verdict; low-overlap spread is the only kind that carries evidence. What crosses scales
is a generator, not a recording. Whether an idea moves at all depends on the habitat, not on the idea alone.
This is a `general` shape with `specified` domain predictions, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the shape states its refuters before any case is
  examined. The real cost is treating a convention as a verdict and letting a filter hand the decision,
  which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is
  avoidable by separating the evidence axis from the contact axis and by recording evidence overlap.
- **undoable:** yes. The four conditions are separate loops; a failure in any one narrows the shape without
  withdrawing the others. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be
  withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measurement that for each receiver records whether a
  new success can be generated, records evidence overlap among adopters, and records the habitat contact
  reproduced; or any one of the four pre-registered refuters firing; or a re-census that shows two of the
  four conditions are one quantity after all. Until one of these lands, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
