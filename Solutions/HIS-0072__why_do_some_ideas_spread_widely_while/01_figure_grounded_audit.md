## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

For a candidate term `phi` (an idea) held in some frame's library, the effect to be explained is a gap
between two readings of the same term:

- how many receivers can **compose and reapply** it (a reach question), and
- how many people can merely **name** it (a frame-internal count).

`R` is per receiver slot, not an average. Fig 1 :19 says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not
aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
So an adoption curve is not the ground of this question. It is a quantity the spreading frame also
produces, and using it as the verdict is the named frame-internal failure.

**Bound shape (Direction, written down before the run so it can be refuted).** The answer we are looking
for is *a four-condition shape with an explicit independence axis*: (i) a reachability and cost condition
on the term relative to receivers, (ii) a cross-scale condition on what form the term must take to cross,
(iii) an independence condition separating convention from corroboration, and (iv) a habitat condition on
what contact is reproduced when the term moves. Any answer that collapses these into one intrinsic
property of the idea fails this shape. This paragraph binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term per
`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction", so the preference is refusable rather than invisible.

**Strain test applied before any figure line was chosen.** The question "why do ideas spread" initially
looks like a single lever question. Fig 9 :55 says "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping
conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 says "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of
either." A single "stickiness" factor would force four different figure lines to strain at once, so the
census below treats the residual as a **union compound**, not one hard problem.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** A term plus a set of receiving frames. It is not a two-body problem: the same idea meets many
  frames with different libraries. Minimum arity is two (origin frame, receiving frame), but the observed
  effect only appears at three or more (origin, bridge, population).
- **Symmetry.** Transmission is directed and threshold gated, not symmetric. A term can move from A to B
  without B moving it back; adoption is contingent on how many other frames already hold it. The relevant
  bond is ordered (`→` sequence, `⇒` production) or comparative (`⋛`), not the symmetric `+`.
- **Scale.** Two scales (a specialist frame or single agent, and a population of frames), plus a
  same-scale cross-frame axis. The membrane between scales is the object of domain 2; the independence
  axis across frames is the object of domain 3.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

1. **D1, the idea as candidate term (composition and cost).** Actors: `phi`, the atoms and bonds it
   composes from, the receiving frames' libraries `Gamma`. Currency: description cost in bits, actions to
   settle, residual removed. Anchor: the ground, which settles truth but does not settle spread. This is
   a domain because it has a nameable anchor (a measurement or demonstration that pays).
2. **D2, cross-scale transmission (the membrane).** Actors: one agent or specialist frame and a population
   of frames. Currency: what crosses, a re-applicable method or a recording of one occasion. Anchor:
   re-applicability, tested by whether a receiver can generate a new success. Nameable, so a domain.
3. **D3, cross-frame spread (independence).** Actors: frames and the ties between them. Currency: agreement
   versus evidence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Nameable, so a domain.
4. **D4, the habitat of the idea (contact).** Actors: actors, conditions, relations in contact with the
   residual. Currency: contact cascades. Anchor: the ground, which keeps what survives. Nameable, so a
   domain.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** The four conditions overlap: reachability (D1) is partly a property of the
habitat (D4), and method form (D2) is partly a cost property (D1). Because the overlap is real, the
residual is a compound with its own loop, and each condition must be cleared before the next is read.
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above
produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Dependency order:
D1 (can the receiver compose it at all?) precedes D2 (can it cross as a method?), which precedes D3 (is
the crossing independent or a mirror chain?), which precedes D4 (did the habitat reproduce the contact?).
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." D3 is
the usual break: independence is the least instrumented of the four.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8 (verbatim quotes, refuters, verdict status)

### F1 - The gap is per receiver, not an adoption curve (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

An adoption curve counts names, not composition events. A frame that can only repeat the name has not
closed the residual; a frame that can reapply the method has. Counting both as "adopters" is the
aggregation error. **Refuter:** show that the per-receiver residual does not predict any difference in
subsequent reapplication, while the aggregate count does. **Status: triangulated** across three distinct
Fig 1 lines (indexing, aggregation, frame-internal scoring). Mode `general`.

### F2 - Reachability and cost govern whether a receiver can take the idea at all (triangulated)

Fig 5 :18: "seeks the gap that is large and compressible"
Fig 5 :26: "does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"
Fig 5 :43: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains
one occasion perfectly."
Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom
is what expands the closure."

A receiver whose `Gamma` already holds the atoms can mint the idea locally; the idea is in its reach. An
idea that needs an atom the receiver lacks requires an import with a source gate and a shadow test.
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure
differs." And the bargain is a description-length test: too general says nothing, too specific explains
one occasion. This is why technical vocabularies stay specialist: the import cost is high and the
compression gain is local. **Refuter:** a case where a term requiring a genuinely new atom in the receiver
spreads as fast as one composed from held atoms, with no source gate and no shadow test. **Status:
triangulated** across Fig 5 (MDL bargain) and Fig 6 (closure), two figures with low overlap in subject
matter. Mode `specified`.

### F3 - Only a method crosses the scale membrane, never a recording (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in
decide what it becomes."
Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."

What spreads widely is a generator the receiver can reapply, verified and compressed to essentials. A
result, a slogan, or a single demonstration is a recording. It can be replayed and can produce the same
past success, but it does not let a new frame generate a new one. A catalyst survives its own use: an
idea that remains useful after being applied many times behaves differently across uses from a spent
one. Fig 4 :19: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for
naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand." **Refuter:** a recording, defined as
a term with no re-applicable rule, that crosses to a population and is then used to generate new
successes in receiver frames. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 4 (membrane) and Fig 12 (catalyst,
level-relative irreducibility). Mode `specified`.

### F4 - Wide spread comes in two categorical kinds, and only one carries evidence (triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption
of it"
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the
residual it surfaced."
Fig 8 :29: "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"
Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

A specialist community is a high-overlap set of frames: same journals, same instruments, same evidence
pool. Its internal agreement is efficient for a convention (a shared vocabulary, a standard method), but
it is disqualifying as a verdict about the world. Fig 7 :13 says agreement compounds while evidence does
not; a chain of mirrors is motion without progress. The same idea can then spread widely for two
different reasons: as a convention through correlated frames, or as corroborated structure through
low-overlap frames. These are not degrees of one quantity. Fig 8 :50 gives the discriminator: "Convergent
derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from
separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." **Refuter:** show that agreement
between two frames drawing on the same evidence carries the same verdict weight as agreement between
two independent instruments, or that a mirror chain adds evidence. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 2,
Fig 7 and Fig 8, three figures with different mechanisms. Mode `general` for the category split,
`specified` for the prediction about any given case.

### F5 - Spread is a property of agent and habitat, not of the idea alone (single-source)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles:
first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

A term that works in a specialist habitat need not work when moved, because the habitat was never
composed: it was enumerated from the world. Contact cascades, so the effect that matters can be two or
three steps out from the direct audience. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is
substitution of one habitat for another." A specialist community is a substituted habitat, and moving the
idea into a wider one inherits actors nobody chose. Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible
until the goal fails". **Refuter:** an agent-only change (a better phrasing, a simpler proof) that moves
the ground, that is, changes who can reapply the idea without changing any contact. **Status:
single-source** for the habitat mechanism (Fig 11 is the only figure governing it); Fig 4 corroborates
the weaker claim that size changes the rule, but governs a different quantity. Mode `specified`.

### F6 - This is a union compound, so no single lever settles it (triangulated)

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

D1 through D4 overlap. A single lever can clear the region where only one condition holds and leaves the
intersections standing, because the other conditions still bind. The residue after such a lever is
categorically different, not merely smaller. **Refuter:** a variable that clears all four conditions at
once across independent cases, with no residual compound left. **Status: triangulated** across three Fig 9
lines, with Fig 3 supplying the dependency order. Mode `general`.

### F7 - Filters are not witnesses (single-source for the verdict rule)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a
verdict."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Prestige, platform size, funding and media attention are filters: they give an idea a budget to be tried.
From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to
be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be
blind to prestige." And: "The prestige loop confirms itself. The high-prestige person is believed because
they are high-prestige. The action is assumed to work because the person is believed. The ground is never
consulted." That loop looks like agreement and feels like truth, which is exactly the diffusion
phenomenon being asked about. **Refuter:** a case where a filter, with no ground contact added, changes
the verdict rather than only the budget. **Status: single-source** for the figure rule (Fig 9), with the
essay as a corroborating world source that shares the framework's evidence pool. Mode `general`.

### F8 - The round trip from figure to slogan and back loses the conditions (triangulated)

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning:
confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

The condensed public version of the shape ("ideas spread when they are simple and catchy") drops D3 and
D4 entirely. That is the round-trip gap `R_T`, measured in `11_audit.md` as the set of dropped constraints,
not assumed zero. **Refuter:** a condensation from which all four conditions survive back-translation.
**Status: triangulated** across Fig 4 and Fig 10. Mode `general`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach. Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument,
not only an honesty device." The four-condition shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single intrinsic property of the idea predicts spread across independent cases with no dependence on
   receiver reach or habitat contact (breaks F2 and F5).
2. A recording, with no re-applicable rule, crosses to a population and is used there to generate new
   successes (breaks F3).
3. Two frames drawing on one evidence pool produce verdict-grade corroboration, or a mirror chain adds
   evidence (breaks F4).
4. A filter, with no new ground contact, changes the verdict rather than only the budget (breaks F7).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed to the surviving conditions. Until one is run, nothing
here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Replace "spread" with a per-receiver residual: who can reapply the idea, not who can name it (F1).
2. Score an idea by receiver reachability and description cost before scoring its content (F2).
3. Distinguish the method from the recording in any communication about the idea (F3).
4. Record the independence axis explicitly: high-overlap spread is a convention, low-overlap spread is
   corroboration, and they are not averaged (F4).
5. Enumerate the habitat before moving the idea; ask what contact would have to be reproduced (F5).
6. Treat reach, method form, independence and habitat as four loops with four refuters (F6).
7. Send the generator (shape claim plus refuter), not the conclusion (Fig 4 :22, Fig 10 :42).
