## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For BUS-0057, the shape is: index the residual per ownership form, keep one kernel with re-bound actors, currency and anchor, treat the forms as isomers, unbundle incentives from obligations, and keep the ground reachable. "One uniform code for all" and "a wholly bespoke code for each form" are both names where the framework returns a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* - the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the residual was described in effect terms (a governance prediction versus an outcome, indexed by ownership slot) before any ownership taxonomy or theory of agency was chosen. The three forms entered as slots named by the question, not as frames picked to make a preferred conclusion fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

So a specific rule, for example "public firms must have a majority-independent board", is licensed only by a ground reading that pays for it, or by a provenanced import. Neither is available here. Governance codes are visible choices (Fig 10 :45-47), not discoveries of nature's own list.

## 4. Why a source named from a reading is the forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* - one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a governance index that reads "good governance" from a board-composition score cannot comment on the source of that score. The same score in a public firm and a founder firm may come from different arrangements (Fig 12 :117, isomers). Naming one source, such as "founder vision" or "sponsor monitoring", from the reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. What the shape licenses and what it refuses for this question

**Licensed by the figures:** the per-slot indexing rule (Fig 1 :19), the invariant kernel with re-bound cast (Fig 4 :19-20), the isomer reading of ownership forms (Fig 12 :117), the refusal of a standing ranking (Fig 11 :51), the unbundling of a union (Fig 9 :54-57), and the convention/verdict guard (Fig 10 :47).

**Refused by the figures:** a named winning ownership form, a named concrete rule per form, a `grounded` verdict, and a claim that this derivation settles the normative question. Those are names where the framework returns a shape.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially wrote "shape, not a name" as if it forbade naming public, private-equity-owned or founder-controlled firms at all. That is an overstatement, and it is recorded so the correction is visible rather than silent.

The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Ownership forms are not proposed as new primitives; they are **bindings from the governance frame onto arrangements of atoms already held** (capital, management, boards, workers). Naming them is therefore not inventing a seventh term. What remains forbidden is claiming the framework can name *which specific rule* belongs to which form, because that would require a source-discriminating instrument that is not held. The analog in the exemplar is the narrowing of "shape, not a name" to a binding onto a held atom, not a prohibition on naming held atoms.

My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: a governance reading still cannot name its source without a discriminator. The reason is the missing instrument, not a ban on naming ownership forms. Both readings are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers famous governance scholars to endorse the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried by people whose public work touches the residual, and let the ground, outcomes measured per form, settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the governance proposal and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
