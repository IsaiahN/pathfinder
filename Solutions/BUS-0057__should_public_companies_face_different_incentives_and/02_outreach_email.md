**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the governance proposal is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation in corporate law and governance. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is whether public companies should face different incentives and obligations from private-equity-owned or founder-controlled firms. The derivation does not answer that with a blanket yes or no. It produces a design shape with four parts. First, the residual, meaning the gap between what a governance regime predicts and what its ground returns, must be measured per ownership form and per firm, never aggregated into a sector average. Second, one governance kernel runs across all three forms; only the actors, the currency and the anchor change. Third, the three forms are structured like isomers, sharing the same atoms of capital, management and boards but joined by a different ownership bond, so the same rule can behave categorically differently. Fourth, the question is a union: incentives and obligations are two conditions, and the three forms differ in several conditions at once, so it must be unbundled before any lever is chosen.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that any ownership form is inherently better governed. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and it refuses a standing ranking of forms because an actor's valence is relative to the residual it is being asked to solve. I am also not naming the specific rules, for example which board-composition or disclosure duty belongs to which form. That is a declared gap, not a hidden answer. And I am not offering a theory of everything about corporate law. This is a candidate generator with an open slot and written refuters.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a governance rule producing the same residual distribution across all three forms after controlling for habitat, so ownership form carries no live signal;
> 2. a demonstration that the governance loop itself changes kind between forms, so one kernel cannot describe all three;
> 3. an ownership form whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the problem it is solving;
> 4. a single lever that restores both the incentive and the obligation conditions in all three forms with no categorically different residue left over.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a governance problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
