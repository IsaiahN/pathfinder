## Wider literature that bears on the claim (all opened 2026-11-22)

These are the world sources read for this solution. They are secondary corroboration only, never the verdict. Full detail, including overlap, is in `10_source_ledger.csv` and `09_watson_answer.md` section WORLD.

1. **Jensen and Meckling 1976**, *Theory of the Firm: Managerial Behavior, Agency Costs and Ownership Structure*, Journal of Financial Economics 3(4):305-360. Defines agency costs as *"the monitoring expenditures by the principal, the bonding expenditures by the agent, and the residual loss"*. The founding statement of the separation-of-ownership-and-control problem that the question presupposes. Governance evidence pool.
2. **Fama and Jensen 1983**, *Separation of Ownership and Control*. Studies *"the survival of organizations in which decision agents do not bear a major share of the wealth effects of their decisions"*. Governance pool.
3. **Witney 2017**, *The corporate governance of private equity-backed companies*, LSE PhD thesis. Identifies *"the shareholder(s)' anticipated investment horizon, and the level and impact of legal and reputational risk"* as the main differences between PE-backed, family and listed firms. Governance pool.
4. **Wright, Amess, Weir and Girma 2009**, *Private Equity and Corporate Governance: Retrospect and Prospect*. *"The diffuse ownership structure of the public corporation, while allowing risk to be efficiently allocated, is not conducive to the effective monitoring of managers, because free-riding can occur."* Governance pool.
5. **Federo 2020**, *Bringing owners back on board: A review of the role of ownership type in board governance*. Reviews *"six distinct ownership types"*, namely *"family, lone founder, corporation, institutional investor, state, and venture capitalist"*, which *"shape board governance"*. Governance pool.
6. **Filatotchev, Jackson and Nakajima 2013**, *Corporate governance and national institutions*. *"a 'one size fits all' approach is undesirable"*; a contingency perspective *"rejects the notion of universal best practices"*. Governance pool.
7. **Steel 2018**, *Proxy Access and Optimal Standardization in Corporate Governance*, Fordham Corporate Law Journal. Reports the opposing axiom: *"According to the conventional wisdom, 'one size does not fit all' in corporate governance"*, then argues *"shareholders prefer standardization because they must incur considerable transaction costs to exercise control rights that contain idiosyncratic terms"*. Governance pool. Genuine internal conflict, resolved at the layer boundary (see `01` re-census R2).
8. **Järlefelt and Mikkelsen 2026**, *Beyond the Deal: How Founder-Led Firms Interpret and Adapt to Private Equity Governance*, Lund University thesis. *"Private equity (PE) investment changes not only who owns firms but also how they are governed."* Governance pool.
9. **Goodhart's law via CNA 2022**, Stumborg, Blasius, Full and Hughes, *Goodhart's Law: Recognizing and Mitigating the Manipulation of Measures in Analysis*. *"when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"* and *"Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes."* Measurement policy / anthropology, low overlap. Corroborates Fig 10 :47.
10. **Wikipedia, Mutualism (biology)**, read 2026-11-22. *"Under certain conditions species of fungi previously in a state of mutualism can turn parasitic on weak or dying plants"*; citing Sachs and Simms 2006, *"One mutualist shifts to parasitism, and no longer benefits its partner"*. Ecology / biology, low overlap. Corroborates Fig 11 :49-51.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the design shape and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the design shape and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict on stage 1; (4) only then the three framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with the optional third stage

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** A recipient's later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: methods cross up. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode, and names their own ground.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are the stage-1 output. If one fails, repair or retract before sending anything.
