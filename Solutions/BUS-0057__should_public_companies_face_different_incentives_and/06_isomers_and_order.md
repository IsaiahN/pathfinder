# Are ownership forms isomers, and what is the order of the union?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

BUS-0057 asks whether public, private-equity-owned and founder-controlled firms should face different incentives and obligations. Two questions are owed before any lever is chosen: (1) are the three forms **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, so that the same rule is a different substance in each, and (2) what is the **order** of the union, so that unbundling is not confused with ranking?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in D3, the governance convention library: capital, management, labour, board, assets, disclosure, incentive devices. Bonds held: the seven, including → sequence and ⋛ comparison, plus negation. Ownership forms are not new atoms; each is an **arrangement** of these atoms under a control bond. No new primitive is available without import. The anchor for the derivation is realized outcomes per form (Fig 2 :29), never agreement among governance raters.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three forms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- All three forms hold the same atom set: capital, managers, workers, a board, assets. What differs is the **bond**: dispersed and transferable claims (public), concentrated and time-limited sponsor control plus high-powered management equity (PE), or concentrated and durable founder control (founder).
- ⇒ Under Fig 12 :117 the three are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a flat list of "governance provisions" and decisive in behaviour. This is why the same formal rule can leave a different residual in each form without being applied more or less diligently.
- They also share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): "a firm with owners and a board". The group says what kind; the qualifier, the ownership bond, says which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two ownership forms reduce to the same bond over the same atoms and produce the same residual distribution on the same rule, the isomer claim fails and the weaker claim that they are merely different points on one scale stands instead. Triangulated (Fig 12 :117 and :124 are one figure, but the habitat reading in Fig 11 :49-51 is a distinct low-overlap route).

### Q2 - Is this a union or a partition, and what must be unbundled?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The bundle has three overlapping splits: incentives versus obligations; three ownership forms; and two conditions C1 (contact maintained) and C2 (reach renewed).
- ⇒ The question is a **union**, not a partition. A public firm failing only C1 and a founder firm failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure. They are different compounds and need different instruments.
- **Falsifier:** a single lever that restores both incentive and obligation conditions in all three forms and leaves no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### Q3 - What order does the dependency chain impose?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Order: 1 perception (can the regime read the residual per form?) → 2 vocabulary (does the governance library have terms for what each form affords?) → 3 objective (can it say what progress would count as, per form?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry.
- The middle link is where the chain usually breaks (Fig 3 :24): having terms for what each ownership form affords and being able to say what counts as progress has neither the obvious failure nor the obvious test that perception and planning have. So it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :25-27).
- ⇒ The first question for a regulator is not "which form is best?" but "at which link does this form's chain stop, and was that measured?" Budget spent on more disclosure rules above the break cannot rescue a vocabulary that cannot say what the change affords. Single-source (Fig 3), corroborated structurally by Fig 12 :113-115 within the same framework.

### Q4 - What is the level order: first-order rules, second-order comparison?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- A governance rule that has settled against the ground in one form becomes an atom that can compose at the next level. Comparing regimes across forms is a **second-order** composition: (settled rule in form A) ⋛ (settled rule in form B), where ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87).
- This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall. It is legal only because the first-order rules settled.
- **Type caveat, still open:** at second order the operands are molecules-now-atoms. Whether an atom counts as a *quantity* at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom; the Operators table restricts ⋛ to two quantities. I record this as a strain, not a correction.

## CONCLUSION

The three forms are **isomers** over a shared governance atom set: same atoms, different ownership bond, different substance, decisive in the residual the same rule leaves. They also share a functional group, so "a firm with owners and a board" is the kind and the ownership bond is the member. The question is a **union**, not a partition, and must be unbundled along incentives versus obligations, three forms, and two conditions before any lever is chosen. The dependency chain is ordered perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry, with the break usually at the middle link. Regime comparison is a second-order composition over settled first-order rules, using ⋛ as a threshold between quantities, with the operand type still a named strain.

This framing preserves what is defensible in the question without pretending to answer it: it explains **why** one formal rule can behave differently across forms and **what** the regulator should measure first. It does not say which rule belongs in which form. That is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.
