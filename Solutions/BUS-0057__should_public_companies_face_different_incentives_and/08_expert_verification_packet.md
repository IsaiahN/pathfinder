# Expert verification packet - the Tether on public versus private-equity-owned versus founder-controlled firms

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate-law or governance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the design shape, `general` on the structural laws, `gap` on the specific rule content. Not `grounded`.** No firm-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The governance residual, meaning the gap between what a governance regime predicts and what its ground returns, must be measured per ownership form and per firm, never aggregated into a sector average. One governance kernel runs across public, private-equity-owned and founder-controlled firms; only the actors, the currency and the anchor change. The three forms are isomers over a shared set of governance atoms (capital, management, labour, board, assets), joined by a different ownership bond, so the same formal rule is a different substance in each and can leave a categorically different residual. Incentives and obligations, and the three forms, and the contact and reach conditions, form a union that must be unbundled before any lever is chosen. Governance rules are conventions authored by the regulator seat, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. The framework does not name which specific rule belongs to which form; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 12

- **Statement:** the governance kernel is invariant across ownership forms; the forms differ only in cast, currency and anchor; as isomers they share atoms but differ in the ownership bond.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 - *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** do public, PE-backed and founder-controlled firms really share one governance loop (perceive, bet, act, predict, name what worked), with differences only in who acts, what is counted and what settles? Or does the loop itself change kind between forms? Is the atom set genuinely shared, with ownership as the bond?
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the governance loop changes kind between forms, so one kernel cannot describe all three; or that two forms reduce to the same bond over the same atoms and produce the same residual distribution on the same rule.

### C2 - governance-facing; checkable against the ownership and control literature

- **Statement:** ownership form is a live slot variable, so residual must be measured per form; no form has standing valence because valence is relative to the problem being solved.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is concentrated ownership always a monitor and dispersed ownership always a free-rider problem, or does the sign flip with the problem (shirking versus minority expropriation, risk allocation versus oversight)? Do the canonical sources already encode the per-form difference (Jensen and Meckling 1976 on agency costs; Wright et al. 2009 on diffuse ownership and free-riding; Witney 2017 on investment horizon as the main difference between PE-backed, family and listed firms; Federo 2020 on six ownership types shaping boards)?
- **Refuter:** a form or governance capability whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual; or a form-blind average that predicts prospectively and per named firm which slot will lose contact next with no loss of signal.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a world check; checkable by reading Figures 9, 10 and 2

- **Statement:** the question is a union to unbundle, and governance rules are conventions that stop being checkable once they become targets.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 2 :43 - *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **What to check:** do incentives and obligations, the three forms, and the contact and reach conditions behave as overlapping conditions needing different instruments rather than one lever? Does an obligation tied to a proxy lose its relationship to the ground once it becomes a reward target (Goodhart's law, CNA 2022: *"when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"*)?
- **Refuter:** a single lever that restores both conditions in all three forms and leaves no categorically different residue; or a governance metric that retains an unchanged, independently measured relationship to the ground after being made a target.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a `grounded` answer. No measurement here settles which form should be treated differently, or how.
- Not a ranking of public, PE or founder firms, and not a claim that any form is inherently better governed.
- Not "one uniform code for all" and not "a wholly bespoke code for each". Both are names where the figures return a shape.
- Not a specific policy: no board-composition rule, disclosure duty or incentive scheme is named. That is a `gap`.
- Not a theory of everything about corporate law, and not a derivation of one ownership form from another.
- Not a claim that the world sources independently corroborate the verdict. They are one governance evidence pool plus two low-overlap witnesses for the mechanism shape; agreement from a shared pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a request for agreement, trust or endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the analogous case.

1. Per-form residual measurement on the same governance choice, with outcomes that cannot be gamed by the choice. Identical residuals after controlling for habitat would break C1 and C2.
2. A documented change of residual in which a governance capability changes sign. None would weaken C2 to `general`.
3. A metric that keeps its ground relationship after being made a target would break C3.
4. A single lever restoring both conditions in all three forms with no different residue would break C3.

2. Check C2 against their own knowledge: does the sign of concentrated versus dispersed ownership really flip with the problem? Name a case where it does not.
