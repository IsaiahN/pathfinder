**Subject:** Check one output about attention and practice before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`):** a line of work I am using treats "training attention" as a change in the contact between a person and the environment or practice they are in, not as a change in an isolated person. The residual is indexed per observer, regime and feature, not pooled into one score. Two training modes are kept separate: unmaintained exposure, where nothing selects and what survives is what the ground kept, and maintained practice, where a teacher or institution can introduce instruments and head starts. What transfers between regimes is a method, not a recording of one occasion. Over a fixed set of mechanisms, two regimes differ by their arrangement, as two molecules can share ingredients and differ in substance. Practice rearranges what is already reachable; it does not manufacture a new kind of information.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names which features a given environment trains, or how many trials it takes. It returns a shape, not a name. Saying "attend to context" or "attend to gaze" is a binding from your field onto a held slot, and until it is provenanced it carries no evidentiary weight. I am also not claiming a single universal training effect size. The effect is per slot, and a pooled average hides the signal.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an agent-only change that moves the ground with the habitat held fixed;
> 2. a pooled verdict that reproduces every per-slot verdict, so the slot discipline changes nothing;
> 3. a clean partition between environment and practice, or between visual and social information, with no shared machinery;
> 4. an alleged training anchor that changes when people push on it;
> 5. a practice variation that introduces a new cognitive primitive rather than a new member or binding of a shared kind.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule (figures README): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
