## What is being decided, in effect terms

Stated as an effect: given a person, an environment or daily-practice regime, and a kind of information (visual or social), predict which features that person comes to attend to, ignore, or treat as important, and how the weighting changes with exposure and practice. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching (Formula step "Direction"): an answer is a rule mapping (environment, practice, information kind) to attentional weighting, plus an account of why the mapping differs across regimes. Now it can accrue a residual like anything else.

An answer would have to do two things: (1) state what changes in contact when a practice regime changes, and (2) name, per feature and per society, which information gains or loses weight. The figures can deliver (1) as a shape. They cannot deliver (2), which is why the feature list is a `gap`.

## The verdict in one paragraph

"Training attention" is not a change in an isolated agent. It is a change in the contact between agent and habitat (Fig 11 :39, :40). The residual is three-indexed and per-slot (Fig 1 :19, :22), and the environment and practice conditions form a union of overlapping conditions rather than a partition (Fig 9 :54, :55). Two modes are distinguished by the figures: unmaintained immersion, where nothing selects and what survives is what the ground kept, and maintained practice, where a seat chooses instruments and introduces what was not there (Fig 11 :56, :58). What crosses a practice boundary is a method or head start, never a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22, :25). Over a fixed atom set two regimes are isomers (Fig 12 :117), and the recurring machinery is a functional group whose members and bonds vary (Fig 12 :123). Practice composes inside its closure and cannot add a new kind of information (Fig 6 :21; Fig 8 :32); the wall moves only on contact with a differing closure (Fig 6 :23). The specific feature list and the numerical time course are unreached from the figures and are reported as `gap`, a verdict and not silence (Fig 13 :134).
