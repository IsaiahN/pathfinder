# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the account is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the pre-registered refuter in stage 1. If the output cannot be refused, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about training data and what a model can learn in principle

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-derived account of how the structure of training data bounds what a model can learn in principle. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **Mode: `general` on the structural laws, `specified` on the application to corpora, not `grounded`.** This is a shape of dependence, not an experiment.
>
> **The output, in one paragraph.** What a system can learn in principle is its reach: the set of terms it could represent and compose. The corpus does not set the reach by its size. It sets the reach by four things it supplies: (1) the atom inventory, the primitive distinctions the data lets the system hold; (2) the bond inventory, the relations (order, production, comparison, absence, refusal) the data carries; (3) the fidelity of the channel, including whether the signal is indexed per item or averaged away; and (4) the independence of the closures the data contacts. Search inside a fixed inventory is idempotent: no amount of it adds an atom. The wall moves only on contact with a differing closure. Training volume, parameter count and benchmark score are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the reach.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A learner at fixed atom inventory attains a ground-verified new atom by more in-closure search alone, with no contact with a differing closure.
> 2. A learner acquires a ground-checked plan, goal, progress measure or refusal from a pure ingredient list with zero relational signal, zero relational bias and zero external relation supervision.
> 3. A ground-checked per-slot live residual and objective are learned from purely global aggregate signal, with no per-slot structure and no comparison signal.
> 4. An agent-only change with the habitat and the contact held fixed moves a ground-measured capability.
> 5. A ground-verified new atom enters the reach from sources whose evidence overlap is one.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the account is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that this settles any empirical scaling debate; that I can name the numeric reach of any particular corpus; that a corpus "is" the learner's library; that more data can never help (it can buy budget and search coverage); or that my derivation is a ground reading. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. The numeric boundary is a declared gap, and the missing thing is an instrument described in effect terms, not an answer smuggled in under another name.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight essays, starting with "They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature": https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/theyre-scanning-books-when-they-should
> - Canonical figures README, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
