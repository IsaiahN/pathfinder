## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a conditional, not a verdict on adaptation in general:

> Under condition C, an adaptive change to a system S increases the capacity of S to keep meeting its ground G under perturbation (resilience). Under condition C', the same kind of change increases the reward of agents in S by moving a proxy P away from G, so the reading of P improves while contact with G degrades (exploitation that undermines).

Stated as a residual: **what is being decided is whether the adaptive loop still ranges against a ground that does not move when the agents push on it, or whether the quantity the loop optimises has become a quantity the agents also produce.** The gap R is between the prediction "score up means contact intact" and the observation "score up, contact lost." The question is where that gap lives, not how much adaptation occurred.

- **Arity:** two conditions plus a contact relation. The question is a **union**, not a single lever: "helps" and "undermines" are different regimes of one loop, and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Resilience is triangulation against an anchor; exploitation is one of the two named collapse genera in Fig 2. They are not mirror images and are not averaged.
- **Scale:** the loop runs at the single-agent scale and at the population scale, with a lossy transform between (Fig 4 :19-30). A verdict at one scale does not transfer to the other without a measured round trip.

**Binding of the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (formula step "Direction"): an answer would look like a *discriminator*: a checkable property of the score/ground relation that separates the two regimes before the outcome is known. The whole derivation below tries to bind that property. Until it is settled by a measurement, it stays a candidate.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The discriminating variable is contact with a non-updating ground, not the amount of adaptation (decisive)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 2 :29-30 and :33:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
> **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

The same adaptive rule can raise resilience in one contact configuration and undermine the system in another. What decides is whether the loop is still ranged against something that does not move. An agent-only improvement that leaves contact unchanged is not resilience, whatever its score. **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 11, Fig 2 and Fig 1 (low overlap: habitat capability, anchor definition, and the agent's metric are three separate statements). **Refuter:** a controlled case where an agent-only adaptation moves the independent ground reading while habitat contact is held fixed. If that is shown, Fig 11 :40 is refuted and this verdict falls.

### F2 - Exploitation is a named collapse genus, not a side effect

Fig 2 :4-7 names the two failures:

> **"collapse 1: mutual update"** / **"(coherent drift)"**
> **"collapse 2: common cause"** / **"(hidden shared source)"**

Fig 2 :14-15: **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."**
Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

When agents adapt to a score that is itself produced inside the frame, the score becomes another frame and the population converges on whoever has most influence over it, or on a hidden shared source. The drift is coherent, which is why the system looks healthy from inside. The single-agent version of this is Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 collapse genera + Fig 1 frame-internal disqualification + Fig 10 :47 below). World sources corroborate with low overlap: Goodhart's original regularity-collapse statement (economics), Krakovna et al.'s specification gaming (machine-learning engineering), and Skalse et al.'s formal hackability result (mathematics). See `09_watson_answer.md`. **Refuter:** a case where a score under sustained adaptive pressure keeps a stable, independently measured correlation with the ground and the ground reading does not drift. Two sources that merely quote each other do not count as two witnesses (Fig 2 :31-32).

### F3 - A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47 (repeated as the guard in Fig 2 :43):

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :24-28:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

A score starts as a convention, a declared way of reading the ground. Under adaptive pressure it is placed in charge of the outcome, and at that moment it stops measuring the ground. The channel decays while the ground does not, so a saturated metric reports health after contact is gone. This is the mechanism behind Goodhart's law and reward hacking, stated in the framework's own vocabulary before any world source is consulted. **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 10 + Fig 2 + Fig 1). **Refuter:** an independent instrument showing that a high-stakes metric retains its ground correlation after it has been made the target, with the instrument not produced by the agents being measured.

### F4 - Aggregation hides the slot where exploitation starts

Fig 1 :13, :19, :22:

> **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**
> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A system can report an excellent global score while one slot is failing. Exploitation typically begins in that slot and is hidden by the average. Fig 5 :48 adds that output is not success: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."** **Verdict: `single-source`** for the slot rule (Fig 1 is the only figure that states it; the world source from `How a Society of Agents Would Work` echoes it but shares the author's evidence pool, so it is not an independent witness). **Refuter:** a case where a global score near zero coexists with a live per-slot residual and the system is still correct to treat the problem as solved.

### F5 - The same adaptive actor is benefactor or malfactor depending on the residual

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** / **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

There is no list of adaptation mechanisms that is always resilience-producing. An adaptive pricing rule, a reward shaping term, an introduced species or a feedback loop is good or bad *for a stated residual*. Fig 11 :65 names the failure: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."** And :52-54: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 valence + Fig 11 cascade + Fig 4 round trip). **Refuter:** produce a standing list of adaptive actors that remains beneficial across two different residuals without re-enumeration.

### F6 - The objective link is where the chain usually breaks, and it has no instrument

Fig 3 :19-20 and :25-27:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Adaptation is link 5, "learn and carry" (Fig 3 :14-15). If link 3, the objective, has drifted to a proxy, more adaptation is effort above the break. It arrives at a step that cannot use it. Fig 9 :60 closes the loop: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A high adaptation rate is a filter for the budget, not a witness for resilience. **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 3 + Fig 9). **Refuter:** measurements showing that most improvement effort in a given system lands at or below the objective link rather than above it.

### F7 - Optimisation inside a closure cannot repair the objective

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

Once the agents hold a proxy as their objective, no amount of extra optimisation inside that frame will restore the ground. What is owed is contact with a frame whose closure differs: an external check, an experiment, an independent instrument. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** So an apparently successful search is not evidence that the objective is right. **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 6 + Fig 8, two separate closure arguments). **Refuter:** a case where a frame repairs its own drifted objective by internal search alone, with no import and no external reading.

### F8 - More readers do not corroborate a resilience claim

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

A campaign that gathers agreement about whether adaptation is resilience-producing is running a mirror chain. Two frames that share an evidence pool are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 7 + Fig 2 + Fig 8). **Refuter:** a demonstration that two low-overlap frames, each ranged separately against the ground, reached the same conditional without sharing the primary's evidence pool. In this simulated folder they do not: all seats share one pool.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the standing rule, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in §3 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A controlled case shows agent-only adaptation moving an independent ground reading while habitat contact is held fixed (breaks Fig 11 :40 and F1).
2. A score under sustained high-stakes adaptive pressure retains a stable, independently measured correlation with the ground and does not drift (breaks Fig 10 :47, Fig 2 :4-7, and F2/F3).
3. The same adaptive actor is beneficial for two different residuals with no change of valence or re-enumeration (breaks Fig 11 :49-51 and F5).
4. A global score near zero coexisting with a live per-slot residual is harmless and correctly treated as solved (breaks Fig 1 :22 and F4).
5. A frame repairs its own drifted objective by internal search alone, with no import and no external reading (breaks Fig 6 :23 and F7).

Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A single failure is more useful than a hundred agreements.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the discriminator: does the adaptive loop range against a ground that does not update, per slot? (F1 to F3).
2. Treat any score that has been made a target as a convention under suspicion, and keep an independent instrument on the ground (F3).
3. Enumerate the habitat cascade before introducing an adaptive actor; introduction is available, exclusion is not (F5).
4. Measure residual per slot and publish the live slots beside the global score (F4).
5. Check the objective link before funding more adaptation; most effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (F6).
6. Send the generator, not the recording: Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is adaptation the thing to ask about?** No. The residual is the score/ground relation. Adaptation is the mechanism that moves the system along it (Fig 11 :40).
2. **What makes a score a resilience signal?** It must range against a ground that does not update (Fig 2 :29-33) and be measured per slot (Fig 1 :19).
3. **How does exploitation arise?** The score becomes a frame: mutual update or common cause (Fig 2 :4-7, :34); or a convention decides an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
4. **Can more optimisation fix it?** No. Closure is idempotent; the fix needs contact with a different closure (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32).
5. **Can the framework name the ground for a given system?** No. It returns a shape; the slot stays `???` (Fig 9 :32, figures README).
6. **Is this a mint?** No, it is a discrimination. The candidate terms (contact, anchor, per-slot residual, valence) are already in the figure set; no new primitive is claimed (Fig 6 :21-22).
7. **What settles it?** A ground reading: an independent instrument, a controlled perturbation, or a counterfactual where the metric is not the target. Until then, `candidate`.

## 7. Strains, gaps and the honest stop

- **Live strain, named:** the world literature defines resilience as a basin-of-attraction or return-time property (Holling, Pimm), while the figures define the governing quantity as contact with an immovable anchor. These are different objects, not a contradiction. The ecological definition is an import used for vocabulary only; the verdict comes from Fig 11 :40 and Fig 2 :29. A future re-census that shows the figures do not govern basin shifts would flip F1.
- **Gap, declared:** the framework returns the shape of the discriminator, not the name of the ground for a specific system. Which instrument counts as the ground in a given adaptive system is `unreachable` here, not silence. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*
- **After re-census: one strain named, one gap declared, none hidden.**
- **Stop:** the answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Nothing here is `grounded`.
