# The Tether's answer: when adaptation helps and when it undermines

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a discriminating route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is not "is adaptation good." It is: under what condition does the same adaptive change increase resilience in one system and cause agents to exploit the score in another? The residual is the gap between "score up means contact intact" and "score up while contact lost." From inside the system the two look identical (Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*). Arity: two conditions plus a contact relation, so a **union compound** with its own loop, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58). Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Capability lives in agent and habitat, not in the agent.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* An adaptation that leaves contact unchanged is not resilience, whatever it scores. **Domain A/B, `triangulated`.**
2. **The anchor must not move when pushed.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* This is the discriminator: resilience requires the score to be ranged against something the agents cannot alter by adapting. **Domain D, `triangulated`.**
3. **Exploitation is a named failure, not a side effect.** Fig 2 :4-7: *"collapse 1: mutual update"* with *"(coherent drift)"* and *"collapse 2: common cause"* with *"(hidden shared source)"*. Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* **Domain B, `triangulated`** (corroborated at low overlap by Goodhart, Krakovna et al. and Skalse et al.).
4. **A score that decides an outcome stops being a measurement.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* The channel decays while the ground does not: Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* **Domain B, `triangulated`.**
5. **Measure per slot; averages hide the failure.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* **Domain A, `single-source`.**
6. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* There is no standing list of good adaptive mechanisms. **Domain C, `triangulated`.**
7. **The loop's sign decides closure.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A loop ranged on an immovable ground closes on contact; a loop ranged on its own output is a chain that cannot close. **Domains A/B, `single-source` for the application.**

**One-sentence answer:** adaptation makes a system more resilient when the loop keeps an independent, non-updating ground and reads its residual per slot against it, and it causes exploitation when the adaptive agents can move the very score the loop uses as its ground, turning a closed cycle into a runaway chain.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that adaptation is bad, or that all metrics are corrupted. The same mechanism can be beneficial for a different residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that more oversight, more reviewers or more agreement helps. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that a higher score is evidence of resilience, or that a lower score is evidence of exploitation. Neither reading discriminates sources (see *The Hard Problem is About Source*).
- Not a name for the ground in any particular system. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No controlled perturbation was run and no instrument was built.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. In *The Hard Problem is About Source*, the instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer settled it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Contact test:** does an agent-only adaptation move an independent ground reading while habitat contact is held fixed? (If yes, F1 falls.)
- **Target test:** under sustained high-stakes optimisation, does the score keep a stable independently measured correlation with the ground, or does it drift while the reading stays healthy? (The cobra bounty, the CoastRunners boat and Goodhart's monetary aggregates are the known cases.)
- **Slot test:** is there a live per-slot residual behind the healthy global score? Publish the slots beside the average.
- **Valence test:** does the same adaptive actor help two different residuals without re-enumeration? (If yes, F5 falls.)
- **Cascade test:** was the habitat enumerated out to the third order before the actor was introduced? Fig 11 :52-54 gives DDT as the case where it was not.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework returns the shape of the discriminator, not the name of the ground for your system. Which instrument counts as the ground is `unreachable` here: Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* One live strain remains: the ecological literature's basin-of-attraction definition of resilience is an import, not a figure line (audit §7).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that adaptation is good or bad. It says that capability belongs to agent and habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing however much it improves. Adaptation builds resilience when the adaptive loop keeps an anchor that does not move when the agents push on it, reads its residual per slot against that anchor, and enumerates the habitat cascade before introducing new actors. It becomes exploitation when the agents can move the score the loop uses as its ground: the score becomes another frame, the population converges on whoever has most influence over it or on a hidden shared source, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices. A score that decides an outcome has stopped being a measurement of it. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not the name of the right metric for your system. That is owed to an instrument, not to an argument.

## 7. Cost if wrong, undoable, reversed by

- **cost if wrong:** the discriminator is cheap to state and bounded to check; the cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). That cost is avoidable by leading with the claim and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The four domain verdicts are independent; one can fall without the others. Nothing in the figure set is withdrawn if the application fails.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independent instrument showing that a high-stakes metric retains its ground correlation, a controlled case where agent-only adaptation moves the ground while contact is fixed, or a demonstration that the two collapse genera are one failure would reverse the relevant verdicts. Prestige and consensus are not such instruments.
