# Outreach draft - check the discriminator first, then decide about the claim

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the verbatim figure text by someone who has not read the rest of this folder. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable question about adaptation and resilience, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The same kind of adaptive change is reported to make one system more resilient and to make another system exploit its own score. What decides which regime you are in? In effect terms, the question is whether the adaptive loop still ranges against a ground that does not move when the agents push on it, or whether the quantity being optimised has become a quantity the agents also produce. A score can rise while contact with the ground falls, and from inside the system those two look the same.
>
> **The shape claim I would like checked.** Capability is a property of agent and habitat, not of the agent alone. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves. An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks; if it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor. So the discriminator I propose is: does the adaptive loop keep an immovable, independently read ground and measure its error per slot, or has the score been made the target and the ground stopped being read? A score that decides an outcome has, in this reading, stopped being a measurement of it.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming all adaptation is harmful, or that all metrics are corrupted, or that I can name the right metric for your system. I am not claiming a number, a simulation result, or a ground reading. This is a structural discriminator, a shape, not a name. Which instrument counts as the ground in a given system is exactly what I am asking you to help identify.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a controlled case where an agent-only adaptation moves an independent ground reading while habitat contact is held fixed;
> 2. a score under sustained high-stakes adaptive pressure that keeps a stable, independently measured correlation with the ground and does not drift;
> 3. the same adaptive actor remaining beneficial across two different residuals with no change of valence;
> 4. a global score near zero coexisting with a live per-slot residual that is nevertheless correctly treated as solved.
>
> If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the package. The relevant lines are Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 2 :29-34, Fig 1 :19-27, Fig 10 :24-47.
> - Canonical figures README and the eight-step formula, same package.
> - Public literature that bears on the residual: Goodhart's own account of the regularity collapse (Le Libellio, 2013); Krakovna et al., "Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuity" (DeepMind, 2020); Skalse et al., "Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking" (NeurIPS, 2022); Walker et al., "Resilience, adaptability and transformability in social-ecological systems" (Ecology and Society, 2004).
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the package. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author's essays warn that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## One-to-one discipline

Each recipient gets the claim and the four refuters. Their verdict on stage 1 is recorded **before** any theory is shown. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* No bulk send, no automation, no receptiveness ranking.
