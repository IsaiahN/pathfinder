## RESIDUAL

The main audit decides that exploitation is a distinct failure genus, not a side effect of too much adaptation. Two questions remain: (1) are the two collapse genera in Fig 2 structurally different things or two spellings of one thing, and (2) in what order must the checks be applied for the discriminator to be usable?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two collapse genera isomers?

- Fig 2 :4-7 names them separately: **"collapse 1: mutual update"** with **"(coherent drift)"**, and **"collapse 2: common cause"** with **"(hidden shared source)"**.
- Fig 2 :14-15 describes the first: **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."** Fig 2 :18-21 describes the second: **"one evidence pool"**, **"they never talk, and still agree"**, **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Both collapses contain the same atoms: two or more frames, a score, an agreement. What differs is the bond. In collapse 1 the frames are bonded by **sequence** (each updates on the last: →). In collapse 2 they are bonded by **identity of source** (≡), or equivalently they share an unacknowledged operand. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- ⇒ They are **isomers** in Fig 12's precise sense: *"The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"* (Fig 12 :117). The outward symptom is the same (agreement without ground), which is why a flat reading cannot tell them apart. This is why the remediation differs: break the update chain in one case; find the shared source and add an independent frame in the other.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single intervention removes both collapses at once in a controlled system (for example, one instrument that both stops mutual updating and removes the shared source), the isomer claim fails and they are one failure with two descriptions. Triangulated for the structural reading (Fig 2 + Fig 12), unmeasured in practice.

### Q2 - The sign of the feedback loop

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- An adaptive loop is a cycle: the score produced this round is the objective for the next. The sign decides the regime. When the loop ranges against a ground that does not update, the cycle closes on contact (resilience). When the loop ranges against its own output, it is a chain with no outside term, and a chain cannot close: the score grows while contact runs away.
- This gives the cleanest one-line version of the answer: **resilience is a closed cycle with the ground inside it; exploitation is a chain that mistakes its own output for the ground.**
- **Falsifier:** a case where a loop that ranges only on its own output nonetheless stays in contact with the ground over a long run without any external correction. That would refute the sign claim in Fig 12 :121 as applied here. Single-source for the application (the figure states the general law; the application is ours).

### Q3 - Functional group, not a standing list

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 11 :49-51: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** / **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
- ⇒ "adaptive mechanism" is a **functional group**: what fixes the outcome is not the mechanism's name but its bond to a residual. There is no standing list of resilience-producing mechanisms. This is the structural reason behind F5 in the audit.
- **Falsifier:** produce a list of adaptive mechanisms that is beneficial across two residuals with no re-enumeration of contact. If such a list survives, the functional-group reading fails.

### Q4 - Dependency order of the checks

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link (terms for what a situation affords, and what would count as progress) has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Order the checks as follows:

| step | check | figure | why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the anchor reachable and immovable? | Fig 10 :29; Fig 2 :33 | every later reading depends on it |
| 2 | Is the residual read per slot? | Fig 1 :19, :22 | a global average can hide the live slot |
| 3 | Is the objective still the ground, or has it become a score? | Fig 10 :47; Fig 3 :9-10 | the usual break; no instrument |
| 4 | Which collapse genus is active, if any? | Fig 2 :4-7 | remediations differ (Q1) |
| 5 | Was the habitat cascade enumerated before the actor was introduced? | Fig 11 :43-44, :65 | second- and third-order effects arrive late |
| 6 | Was the result carried across scale with a measured gap? | Fig 4 :30 | otherwise dead reckoning |

**Falsifier for the order:** a case where step 5 can be run correctly while step 3 is broken, with the same outcome. If remediation at step 5 works despite a drifted objective, the dependency order is wrong and must be re-censused.

### Q5 - Two currencies, held apart

- Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- In this question the two currencies are: the cost of stating the discriminator (bits: a short rule about contact and per-slot residual) and the cost of finding out whether it holds (actions: an independent instrument, a perturbation, a controlled run). A cheap rule can be expensive to settle; an expensive rule can be cheap to refute. Summing them into one "adaptation score" is the error Fig 12 names, not the fix.
- **Falsifier:** a single accounting that prices both correctly without loss. If one exists, the two-currency claim in Fig 12 :125-130 fails as applied here.

## CONCLUSION

The two collapse genera are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different remediation. The adaptive loop is a **cycle** whose sign decides the regime (Fig 12 :121), and "adaptive mechanism" is a **functional group** whose valence is relative to the residual (Fig 12 :123, Fig 11 :49-51), not a standing list. The checks are strictly ordered (Fig 3 :19-27), and the two currencies of description and testing must be held apart (Fig 12 :125-126). No new primitive, import or naming of a seventh term is required.
