**Subject:** Check one output about RNA's building blocks before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about prebiotic chemistry. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "could RNA's building blocks form naturally?" hides a union of two different conditions. The first is reach: can an activated ribonucleotide be composed from plausible feedstocks at all? The second is contact: does a realistic early-Earth habitat actually supply the concentrations, cycling and order that the route needs? These are not one hard problem. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, so a single lever, however elegant, leaves the intersection standing.
>
> A second output: the phrase "building blocks" is the flat-formula reading. A nucleotide is not ribose plus a base plus phosphate. It is a bonded arrangement, and the bond carries the identity. The successful laboratory routes do not start from free ribose and the free nucleobases; they compose the molecule through intermediates and let the habitat supply the order. This is a prediction about the shape of any satisfactory answer, not a claim that I know which Hadean route ran.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a laboratory synthesis proves the reaction happened on the early Earth, that no route exists, that the RNA world is established, or that any one published route is the answer. A synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability. Whether any of this actually happened is left as `candidate`, because the historical ground is mostly unreachable and a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a single-condition, no-purification one-pot experiment that yields all four activated ribonucleotides under conditions independently shown to be present on the early Earth;
> 2. a demonstration that the sugar-base conjunction is symmetric, so that ingredient order and bonding carry no information;
> 3. geochemical evidence that discriminates the competing routes at the ground, in either direction;
> 4. evidence that self-assembly is invariant across habitat substitution, so that the second condition is not separate.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figures and line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this package, cite as `Fig N :line`.
> - Powner, Gerland and Sutherland, Nature 2009: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08013
> - Sutherland, Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology 2010: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2845210
> - Yadav, Kumar and Krishnamurthy, Chemical Reviews 2020: https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.chemrev.9b00546
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
