## 0. The claim under audit and the residual in effect terms

The question as asked: could the building blocks of RNA form naturally under realistic early-Earth conditions?

**Restated as an effect.** Let a frame Γ be a proposed prebiotic reaction network, with belief b the assumed feedstock inventory and geochemical setting, action a the run of the network, and observation o′ what the ground actually delivers. R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is the gap between *a molecule can be made in a laboratory* and *the molecule forms, persists and accumulates under a setting that the early Earth could have supplied*. It is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The slots are enumerated, not assumed: ribose, purine nucleobases, pyrimidine nucleobases, phosphate activation, the glycosidic bond with its correct anomer and regioisomer, and the coupled geochemical regime that supplies concentrations and cycling.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (formula step "Direction"). An answer would look like this: a `grounded` yes requires a closed reaction sequence from plausible feedstocks, run under a single self-consistent or plausibly cycling geochemical regime with no laboratory-only purification or reagent ordering, yielding activated ribonucleotides in sufficient amount and purity to feed polymerization, with the prediction discriminated by a measurement or by a diagnostic signature in the rock record. A `grounded` no requires a measurement that rules the sequence out at the ground. Anything short of either is `candidate`. This binding is written here before the derivation so it can accrue a residual like any other term.

**Why the question is not itself a chemistry oracle.** The figures are a domain-agnostic metatheory. They decide the shape of a composition answer, what counts as settling it, and what cannot be concluded from closure alone. They do not decide which Hadean reaction ran. Any claim that they deliver a yes or no on the historical fact is a `general`-mode verdict where only a `grounded` one would do.

### Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

Production and sequence are not interchangeable with the symmetric conjunction that a flat ingredient list assumes. Scale: molecular reaction, laboratory reaction network, geochemical habitat, and the historical Hadean record.

**Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. Let C1 be *the molecule is reachable from plausible feedstocks* (D1 plus D3) and C2 *the habitat actually supplies the conditions, concentrations and cycling* (D2). D4 decides whether either obtained. A single lever, one elegant laboratory synthesis, clears the region where only C1 holds and leaves the intersection standing, because C2 still binds (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Hud and Fialho state the same structural fact from inside the field: *"no single geochemical scenario has generated both"* (2019). C1 and C2 each need their own loop and their own ground.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the failing slot → settle the composition (which bonds, flat or priced) → test reach in the lab closure → test contact in the habitat → let the ground decide. Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Effort spent improving a laboratory catalyst arrives at a step that cannot use it if the habitat link is broken.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The subject phrase "building blocks" is the flat-formula collapse (decisive for framing)

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :97: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"*
Fig 12 :101: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."*

A ribonucleotide is not ribose plus a nucleobase plus phosphate. It is a bonded arrangement: the base joined at N9 (purines) or N1 (pyrimidines) to C1′ of the furanose as the β anomer, phosphate at 3′ or 5′ or as a 2′,3′-cyclic activation. The same atoms under a different bond are a different substance. The clause "formed from their constituent parts (ribose and nucleobases)" is precisely the reading Powner, Gerland and Sutherland 2009 report as failing: *"it is far from obvious how such ribonucleotides could have formed from their constituent parts (ribose and nucleobases)"*. Their successful route *"bypasses free ribose and the nucleobases"* and composes the molecule through bonded intermediates instead. Sutherland 2010 makes the notation point explicitly: the three-building-block picture is what a nucleotide looks like *"in terms of their appearance to the human eye"*, and that is exactly why it misleads.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 12 notation rule plus Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*, with the chemistry as a low-overlap corroborator, not as the deciding figure). Mode `general` for the rule, `specified` for the nucleotide application. **Refuter:** show that under the figure bond set the sugar-base conjunction is symmetric, so that base + sugar and sugar + base are the same term; or exhibit a full ribonucleotide whose identity is fixed by its ingredient multiset alone.

### F2 — The residual is per building block, not one global yes or no

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The six slots have different statuses in the world sources. Shapiro 1988: *"The evidence that is currently available does not support the availability of ribose on the prebiotic earth, except perhaps for brief periods of time, in low concentration as part of a complex mixture, and under conditions unsuitable for nucleoside synthesis."* Larralde, Robertson and Miller 1995: *"The ribose half-lives are very short"*, so a free-ribose route faces a persistence problem. Powner et al. 2009 solve the pyrimidine activation slot while bypassing free ribose. Becker et al. 2019 report a one-pot wet-dry route to pyrimidine nucleosides that is *"compatible with purine synthesis, allowing the concurrent formation of all Watson-Crick bases."* Yadav, Kumar and Krishnamurthy 2020 stress that *"there are-and will continue to be-many more questions than answers from the synthetic, mechanistic, and analytical perspectives."*

A single verdict on "RNA building blocks" would average a solved pyrimidine slot, a partly solved purine slot, a recalcitrant ribose slot and an open geochemical slot. By Fig 1 :22 that average throws away the only signal that says where to look.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 1 with Fig 9 :22's arity, symmetry and scale description, corroborated by the field's own slot-by-slot reviews). Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** demonstrate that all six slots share one mechanism and one ground, so that indexing them separately adds no information.

### F3 — It is a union of reach and contact, not one hard problem

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

C1 (reachable in a lab closure) and C2 (supplied by the habitat) are distinct conditions. The Hud and Fialho perspective confirms the intersection is the hard part: disparate syntheses for the two nucleoside classes existed, but *"no single geochemical scenario has generated both"* before Becker et al. Vázquez-Salazar 2026 describes the native setting as *"heterogeneous mixtures shaped by cycling, gradients, interfaces, and compartments"*, with both *"obstacles"* and *"opportunities"*. A union cannot be cleared by one lever.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 plus Fig 11, distinct overlap between the unbundling rule and the habitat rule). Mode `general` for the union diagnosis, `specified` for its application here. **Refuter:** show one mechanism for which reach and contact are the same variable, so the intersection vanishes.

### F4 — Dependency order: a lab result cannot rescue a broken habitat link

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Sutherland 2010 supplies the field's own version of the middle-link failure: *"the route as operated thus far in the laboratory is associated with several steps, and the conditions for these steps are different. Furthermore, purification in between certain steps was carried out to make analysis of the chemistry easier. Clearly, these issues need to be addressed before the synthesis can be seen as geochemically plausible."* That is a diagnosis of the link between D1 and D2, stated by the chemist who built the route. The instrument for that link is a geochemical sequence experiment, not more yield optimisation in D1.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 3 dependency ordering with Fig 11 :70, plus a world source that independently names the same gap). Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** a single-regime, no-purification experiment that runs the whole sequence at once while leaving yield and selectivity unchanged.

### F5 — The habitat is enumerated from the world, and it supplies the order

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*

The order of reagent addition in a laboratory route is authored by a chemist. On the early Earth there is no seat. The order must be supplied by conditions that are read off the world: wet and dry, day and night UV, hot and cold, acidic and neutral. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one"*; Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* The Chemical Science 2023 study of the autocatalytic formose network reports that *"regular fluctuations in pH between alkaline and neutral conditions may afford periods of autocatalytic sugar and 2-NH2Ox production followed by 2-NH2Ox-dependent synthesis of tetrose and pentose aminooxazolines"*, in a hydrothermal field where springs of varied pH mix. That is a habitat-supplied order, not an authored one. Because exclusion is unavailable (Fig 11 :61), the tars and side products cannot simply be removed; they must be outcompeted, diluted or destroyed, which is what the UV sanitising step in the Sutherland route does.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 habitat rule plus Fig 12 order rule, with two world sources that name cycling and fluctuation). Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** a route whose required order is invariant across all plausible habitats, so that no environmental sequencing is needed.

### F6 — Closure: a laboratory route is a substituted habitat, not the ground

Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

A successful laboratory synthesis proves the wiring of a reaction network. It does not prove that the network had contact with the early Earth (Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*). Conversely, failure to find a route so far is not a proof that no route exists; the closure is not sealed. Fig 13 :134 makes the honest report a verdict, not silence. Yadav et al. 2020 close their review with a caution against *"coming to conclusions as to whether the problem of chemistry of prebiotic nucleotide synthesis has been solved."* Sutherland 2010 uses almost the same words: *"it is premature to conclude that the newly discovered self-assembly route to activated pyrimidine nucleotides (Powner et al. 2009) is geochemically plausible, the signs are looking good!"*

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 plus Fig 8, with two field reviews of independent authorship as corroboration). Mode `general` for the closure rule, `specified` for the application. **Refuter:** a direct geochemical measurement that shows the laboratory sequence did or did not run, which would move D4 from `candidate` toward `grounded` in whichever direction it points.

### F7 — The anchor is a measurement, not agreement among chemists

Fig 2 :29: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The cost of being wrong here is high: a wrong yes would steer research effort toward a route that never ran, and a wrong no would close a live line of inquiry. So independence is worth buying, but only from frames whose evidence pools differ. A panel of prebiotic chemists selected for agreement about the RNA world is one pool talking. The ground is an isotope ratio, a mineral association, a reproduced one-pot geochemical sequence, or the absence of a predicted intermediate where it should be detectable.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 and Fig 7, with Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*). Mode `general`. **Refuter:** show that for this question the ground returned by an instrument is constitutively a vote among frames, which would collapse Fig 2 :36's anchor test.

### F8 — Convergent routes: corroboration at maximum independence only if the pools differ

Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*
Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*

Powner et al. 2009 reach activated pyrimidine ribonucleotides through amino-oxazole and anhydronucleoside intermediates from cyanamide, cyanoacetylene, glycolaldehyde, glyceraldehyde and phosphate. Becker et al. 2019 reach pyrimidine and purine nucleosides from small molecules and ribose under wet-dry cycles with phosphate minerals, a different mechanism and a different feedstock set. Vázquez-Salazar 2026 frames the wider picture as *"networked chemistry can also enhance robustness when different routes converge on shared intermediates"*. The low-overlap philosophy literature names the same shape: Polger 2024 defines multiple realizability as *"one cognitive kind or process being actually or possibly realized by more than one relevantly different physical realizer"*, and Sober 1999 notes that *"many scientists would find it more illuminating to be shown how the same effect is reached by different causal pathways"*.

**Strain test.** The philosophy sources and the chemistry sources are genuinely low overlap, but their agreement is about the *shape* (same product, distinct routes), not about the chemistry fact. The binding of the philosophical kind to a nucleotide is unmeasured, so it corroborates Fig 13 :121 only as an analogue. Fig 8 :51 also warns that convergence and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record of where each route came from separates them. The two laboratory groups share funding ecosystems, review pools and a common body of prior chemistry, so their agreement is stronger than a single group's word but weaker than two disjoint evidence pools.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** for the shape (Fig 8 plus Fig 13, with the low-overlap philosophy frame as analogue), and `single-source` for the claim that these specific routes converge independently in the required sense. Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** show that the second route imports its key intermediate from the first rather than reaching it separately, which would downgrade corroboration to adoption.

### F10 — SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD are three questions, not one (formula step 7)

When no figure line closes the residual, ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, keeping them distinct.

- **SEARCHED.** Is the route already in the laboratory closure and merely unfound? Fig 6 :49 *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change."* The field has searched for decades; more search inside the same feedstock set is cheap but cannot add an atom.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from something already returning something. Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Here there are edges: lab reaction networks return partial readings, isotopic analyses of ancient rocks return partial readings, and meteoritic organics return partial readings. So the historical question is **unreached**, not unreachable in principle.
- **OUTWARD.** Find a frame whose closure predicts the shape of R. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The residual was described in effect terms in §0 and §1 before any frame (Sutherland route, formamide route, wet-dry route, environmental filtering) was named.

Importing against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :40-43). No new framework primitive is imported by this solution.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single-set-of-conditions, no-purification, one-pot geochemical experiment produces all four canonical activated ribonucleotides (A, G, C, U) at concentrations and purities sufficient for polymerization, under conditions independently shown to have been present on the early Earth. This collapses C2 into C1 and moves D4 toward `grounded`.
2. A figure re-census shows that the sugar-base conjunction is symmetric under the figure bond set, so that base + sugar and sugar + base are the same term. This breaks F1.
3. Direct geochemical evidence, for example a mineral or isotopic signature diagnostic of one specific proposed intermediate, discriminates between the competing routes at the ground. A positive result plus (1) moves the verdict toward `grounded`; a negative result narrows the habitat without proving absence elsewhere.
4. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool demonstrates that self-assembly of the relevant nucleotides is invariant across habitat substitution, so that C2 is not a separate condition. This dissolves the union reading.
5. An agent-only improvement, a better catalyst or a cleverer protecting group, that measurably moves the ground. That would break the capability claim in Fig 11 :40.

Until one of these fires, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F3: the "building blocks" phrase hides the bond, and the reach/contact union needs two loops. Do not answer the question with a single average verdict.
2. Keep the six slots separate in any report. Ribose, purines, pyrimidines, activation and habitat have different statuses (F2).
3. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The deliverable is the shape, the per-slot census and the refuter, not a claim that the question is settled.
4. Put the field sources after the shape claim and refuter, with provenance and overlap declared (F8). Ask an outside reader what they would count as a failure before showing them the framework.
5. Treat the historical yes/no as `candidate` and say so. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

## 6. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the question one question?** No. Six slots by arity, and a union of C1 reach and C2 contact (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :55).
2. **Are "building blocks" the right atoms?** No. The ingredient list is the flat formula; the bond is the missing information (Fig 12 :5, :101).
3. **Is the molecule reachable in a lab closure?** For pyrimidines, yes via Powner et al.; for the coupled purine and pyrimidine set, a one-pot wet-dry route is reported by Becker et al.; "solved" is premature per Yadav et al. (Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32).
4. **Does reach imply contact?** No. Lab purification and step-separated conditions are a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70).
5. **Who supplies the order?** The habitat, through cycling and fluctuation, not an author (Fig 11 :46, :48; Fig 12 :112).
6. **Can a search settle the historical fact?** Not by closure alone. SEARCHED and INWARD are distinct; partial readings exist, so unreached rather than unreachable in principle (Fig 6 :34-35, :49).
7. **What is the verdict?** A shape plus a stop. Six per-slot verdicts kept separate, the union unbundled, the ground named, and the specific Hadean route left as a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134).

**Final status after re-census: one strain named (F8 independence), none hidden. The shape survives; no chemistry verdict is claimed.**
