# The Tether's answer: could RNA's building blocks form naturally?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural composition rule, NOT `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading and not a chemistry measurement.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is not one question. It is six slots (ribose, purines, pyrimidines, phosphate activation, the glycosidic bond with its anomer and regioisomer, and the coupled geochemical regime), and it is a union of two conditions:

- **C1, reach:** can an activated ribonucleotide be composed from plausible feedstocks at all?
- **C2, contact:** does a realistic early-Earth habitat supply the concentrations, cycling and order the route needs?

D4, the historical ground, decides whether either obtained. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Fig 1 :28 warns what happens instead: *"A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

## 2. The shape

1. **A nucleotide is a bonded arrangement, not an ingredient multiset.** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* The base-to-sugar bond, the β anomer, the furanose form and the cyclic phosphate carry the identity. This is why the successful laboratory routes bypass free ribose and the free nucleobases.
2. **Reach and contact are separate loops with separate grounds.** Fig 9 :57. Laboratory success proves wiring, not capability: Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
3. **The habitat is enumerated and supplies the order.** Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*. Wet-dry cycling, UV exposure and pH fluctuation supply the sequence that a chemist authors in the lab (Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence."*).
4. **Cycle and catalyst are the molecular structures that matter.** Fig 12 :121 *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The formose network can run toward ribose or toward inert tar. Phosphate *"survives its own use"* (Fig 12 :119) and later becomes part of the molecule.
5. **No route found is not no route possible.** Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
6. **Convergent routes are corroboration only with provenance.** Fig 8 :50 *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* The source ledger records overlap so this is not assumed.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that RNA building blocks did form on the early Earth. That is a historical fact requiring the ground, and the ground is mostly unavailable.
- Not that they did not. Fig 8 :39 forbids converting a failed search into an absence proof.
- Not that any named route, Sutherland, Becker, formamide or otherwise, is the route that ran. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the RNA world is established, that RNA was the first polymer, or that nucleotides preceded peptides and lipids. Those are different questions in the same section of the source file and are not answered here.
- Not that the laboratory chemistry is wrong. It is excellent chemistry. The claim is only that it proves wiring, not contact.
- Not `grounded`. No Hadean measurement settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument or a geochemical sequence, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a spectrometer, not a debate, discriminated among sources of a green reading. Here the discriminating tests are:

1. **One-pot geochemical test (collapse C2 into C1):** a single set of conditions, no purification, that yields all four canonical activated ribonucleotides at polymerisation-ready concentration and purity, under conditions independently shown to have been present on the early Earth.
2. **Habitat-substitution test (break the union):** run the same network across a deliberately varied set of pH, wet-dry and UV regimes and measure whether product identity tracks the regime. If it does not, C2 is not a separate condition.
3. **Source-discriminating ground test (move D4):** a mineral or isotopic signature diagnostic of a specific proposed intermediate, or its demonstrable absence where it should be detectable. Either direction narrows the habitat; a positive result plus test 1 moves the verdict toward `grounded`.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`:** symmetric sugar-base conjunction; a figure re-census that collapses the union; invariance of self-assembly across habitat substitution; an agent-only improvement that moves the ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* There are edges here: laboratory reaction networks return partial readings, isotopic analyses return partial readings, meteoritic organics return partial readings. So the historical question is unreached, not banned. Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

This solution cannot settle the question it reports on. It can say what any answer must look like, which slots are live, what would settle each, and what has been imported and from where.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not say that RNA's building blocks did or did not form on the early Earth. It says that the question is a bundle: six building-block slots, and a union of two conditions, whether an activated nucleotide is reachable from plausible feedstocks and whether a realistic habitat supplies the concentrations and cycling the route needs. It also says that "building blocks" is the wrong picture, because a nucleotide is a bonded arrangement and the bond, the anomer and the cyclic phosphate carry the identity; the successful laboratory routes work by bypassing free ribose and the free nucleobases and building the molecule through intermediates. In the lab a chemist writes the order; on the early Earth the order has to come from the habitat, through wet-dry cycles, UV exposure and pH fluctuation. A test-tube synthesis proves the wiring of a network, not its contact with the ground, and a search that has not found a route is not proof that none exists. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the question. The historical verdict stays open until the ground settles it.
