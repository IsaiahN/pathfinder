# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues for ORI-0032

**Mode: `specified` on the chemical applications, `general` on the composition rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether the "building blocks of RNA" could form naturally. That phrase treats a nucleotide as a sum of parts. Two things are owed: (1) what kind of thing a nucleotide is under Fig 12's composition rule, and (2) where the *order* of assembly comes from when there is no chemist to author it. A third thing is owed by the world sources: several distinct routes reach related products, so the framework's isomer and convergence rules must be applied before any route is called the answer.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a nucleotide the isomer of ribose plus a base plus phosphate?

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :97: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"*

No. Ribose, cytosine and phosphate as an ingredient multiset do not determine β-ribocytidine-2′,3′-cyclic phosphate. The identity is carried by the N1-C1′ glycosidic bond, the β anomer, the furanose form and the cyclic phosphate. Sutherland 2010 gives the chemistry in the figure's own terms: ribose in water is *"an equilibrating mixture of different forms"*, dominated by pyranose isomers, with the reactive furanose a minor component; adenine has tautomers in which the needed N9 lone pair is unavailable; cytosine *"cannot be ribosylated on N1"*. The same atoms, different bond, different substance. The laboratory route succeeds by a sequence that *"bypasses free ribose and the nucleobases"* (Powner et al. 2009).

The figure's own precedent applies: Fig 12 :97 *"This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first."* The α and β anomers of a nucleoside, the pyranose and furanose forms of ribose, and the 3′,5′ versus 2′,5′ internucleotide links Sutherland reports are all cases where the formula is silent and the behaviour differs.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a ribonucleotide whose identity is fully fixed by its ingredient multiset, with the bond, anomer and phosphorylation site carrying no information. If found, F1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` fails.

### Q2 — Where does the order come from?

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
Operators table :30: sequence is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, symmetric **no**. Operators table :37: production is *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, symmetric **no**.

The Sutherland route is an authored sequence: cyanamide plus glycolaldehyde first, then glyceraldehyde, then cyanoacetylene, then phosphorylation and UV. In the laboratory a chemist writes the order. On the early Earth there is no seat, so the order must be a habitat fact (Fig 11 :56 *"The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*). The world sources supply exactly that:

- Chemical Science 2023: *"regular fluctuations in pH between alkaline and neutral conditions may afford periods of autocatalytic sugar and 2-NH2Ox production followed by 2-NH2Ox-dependent synthesis of tetrose and pentose aminooxazolines"*, realisable where hot springs of varied pH lie close together and mix.
- Becker et al. 2019: the pyrimidine nucleosides are *"driven solely by wet-dry cycles"*, with phosphate minerals giving mono- and diphosphates in one pot.
- Powner et al. 2009: UV irradiation both converts cytidine cyclic phosphate toward the uracil derivative and destroys contaminating nucleosides, a day-night or surface-exposure constraint.

So the sequence bond → is supplied by cycling conditions read off the world (Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*), not composed from the chemist's plan.

**Falsifier:** a route whose required order is invariant across habitat substitution, so that no cycling, gradient or periodicity is needed. That would dissolve the second condition of the union.

### Q3 — Cycle and catalyst: what closes the loop, and what survives its own use?

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

The formose reaction is described by Chemical Science 2023 as *"one of the most plausible forms of autocatalysis on the early Earth"*: the output re-enters as input. But the same network also produces *"inert tars"* and only *"minimal amounts of ribose"* (Vázquez-Salazar 2026). The sign of the loop decides whether the cycle closes on a productive intermediate or drains carbon into an inert sink. That is Fig 12 :121 read at the molecular scale.

Phosphate is the catalyst case. Powner et al. 2009 report that although phosphate *"is only incorporated into the nucleotides at a late stage of the sequence, its presence from the start is essential"*, acting as general acid/base catalyst, nucleophilic catalyst, pH buffer and chemical buffer. It survives its own use, then becomes a constituent of the final molecule. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* This is not a strain: the catalyst role and the constituent role are two uses of one part at two steps, and the record must carry both, because *"nothing records which a term is"*.

**Falsifier:** show that the productive and tar-forming branches of the formose network have the same loop sign, so that cycling does not discriminate; or show phosphate is consumed before the step it catalyses in a geochemical setting.

### Q4 — Functional groups and activation

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The 2′,3′-cyclic phosphate is such a group: its presence makes the nucleotide *"activated"* and, in the UV step, controls the stereochemistry by *"enforced intramolecular nucleophilic addition"* (Sutherland 2010). The β-ribofuranose is another: the group says ribonucleoside, the qualifier says cytidine or adenosine. Vázquez-Salazar 2026 reports that *"environmental factors such as ultraviolet irradiation, mineral surfaces, wet-dry cycling, and thermal gradients can act as constraints that enrich certain products by differential stability, reactivity, and compartmentalization"*, which is a habitat-level functional group reading: the context fixes which member persists.

**Falsifier:** an activated monomer whose activating group can be swapped for an arbitrary substructure with no change in polymerisation outcome.

### Q5 — Are the competing routes isomers or instances of one convergence?

Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*

The 2009 pyrimidine route, the 2019 wet-dry purine-plus-pyrimidine route and the formamide-linked routes do not all start from the same inventory or pass through the same intermediates, so they are not simply different spellings of one molecule under a symmetric bond. They are distinct routes to overlapping products. Under Fig 8 :50 that is corroboration at maximum independence *only if they draw on separate evidence*. The source ledger records overlap so this is not assumed: the two lab groups share review pools and a common prior literature, so the corroboration is real but not maximal.

The low-overlap philosophy frame names the same shape. Polger 2024: *"one cognitive kind or process being actually or possibly realized by more than one relevantly different physical realizer."* Sober 1999: *"many scientists would find it more illuminating to be shown how the same effect is reached by different causal pathways."* These corroborate Fig 13 :121 as an analogue, not as chemistry.

**Falsifier:** show that the second route imports its decisive intermediate or its mechanistic idea from the first, which downgrades convergence to adoption and removes the extra corroboration.

## CONCLUSION

A nucleotide is not an isomer of its ingredients in the plain sense: it is a different *kind* of object, a bonded arrangement, and the bond is the information the ingredient list omits. The order of assembly is an ordered bond whose operands are supplied by the habitat, not by an author. The formose cycle and phosphate catalysis are the cycle and catalyst structures of Fig 12 read at the molecular scale, with the sign of the loop deciding whether carbon reaches a ribonucleotide or a tar. Competing lab routes are convergent derivations in the figure's sense only with provenance, and the low-overlap philosophy frame corroborates the shape while leaving the chemistry undecided.

Every claim here is `specified` or `general`. None is `grounded`: the chemistry is read from published experiments, not from the Hadean ground.
