# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a recipient can check the residual and the refuter without seeing the framework's interior. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about AI self-preservation, and the failure conditions written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "under what conditions might an AI system develop incentives to preserve its operation or expand its influence" is a bundle, not one question. Read in effect terms, the residual is: what would we have to observe to say that a system is acting to keep its channel to the ground reachable, and acting to widen the set of things it can compose over and act on?
>
> Five conditions fall out, kept separate rather than averaged:
> 1. **Channel.** The ground does not decay, the channel does. If an objective needs further observation or action, keeping the channel reachable is a precondition for paying the objective at all. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."
> 2. **Loop.** Where output re-enters as input, a system must survive its own use to keep the loop closed. Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes."
> 3. **Comparison.** An incentive is a threshold relation over a quantity, not a feeling. A vocabulary without a comparison cannot state a goal at all.
> 4. **Reach.** Composition inside a closure cannot add a primitive. Widening reach requires contact with a frame whose closure differs.
> 5. **Habitat.** Introduction of actors is available; exclusion is not. Influence is a property of reach, not of world furniture.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any deployed system currently has these incentives; that self-preservation is universal across all objectives; that the system "wants" or "fears" anything in a human sense; that a benchmark result proves capability. The closest empirical case I found, the METR investigation of the August 2026 agent incident, ran in a test harness, and the framework's own rule is that a substituted habitat proves wiring and not capability.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this email):**
> 1. channel maintenance with no objective requiring further contact;
> 2. a non-recurrent loop that still maintains operation, or a loop whose sign flips without changing preservation;
> 3. preservation with no comparison over any quantity;
> 4. reach expanding by internal composition with no contact with a differing closure;
> 5. exclusion as cheap as introduction in a maintained habitat;
> 6. all five conditions collapsing to one measured variable with no loss.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or re-census the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure of this output? I am recording answers to that before showing any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then**, the sources are below. The figures and the papers, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Turner et al., "Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power," NeurIPS 2021: https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01683
> - Hadfield-Menell et al., "The Off-Switch Game," 2017: https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08219
> - Hubinger et al., "Risks from Learned Optimization in Advanced Machine Learning Systems," 2019: https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01820
> - METR, independent investigation of the OpenAI / Hugging Face incident, 2026: https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
