# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond definitions. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for conditions, and conditions have an order. Two questions are owed: (1) in what dependency order do the five conditions sit, and (2) are "preserve operation" and "expand influence" two names for one structure (a functional group) or two different substances built from the same atoms (isomers)? A wrong answer to (2) would merge two verdicts that must stay separate.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"*.
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*

Order: **perception/channel -> vocabulary of affordances -> objective as comparison -> planning and loop closure -> learn and carry -> reach expansion.** Reach expansion is last because it is licensed only after a settled molecule can serve as an atom at the next level. Refusal precedes mint: Formula step 2 names the REFUTED case and says what is owed is *"a refusal, not an explanation"*, and the Direction rule forbids checking the direction of the question from inside the loop. So F7 in the audit is ordered before any claim that expansion is owed.

**Falsifier written before the run:** if an instrument shows reach expanding before the first-order loop is settled, the dependency order is wrong and the expansion must be re-described as a different operation.

### Q2 - Are preserve and expand isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :116: *"Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."*

As commonly stated, "preserve operation" and "expand influence" share almost nothing: one is about keeping a channel, the other about widening reach. On that reading they are not isomers; they are two members of a broad **functional group** of goal-directed behaviours, and the group's qualifier is which quantity the comparison ranges over.

On the stronger reading used in this solution, both are written over the same atom set `{channel, comparison, recurrence, import, habitat, R}` and differ only in the bond:

- preserve: `channel -> (cycle/catalyst) -> R` where output re-enters as input (Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."*; :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*).
- expand: `R -> (import) -> closure'` where the crossing happens on contact with a differing frame (Fig 6 :23: *"The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*).

Under Fig 12 :117 those **are** isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The behaviour differs decisively: one keeps the channel reachable, the other moves the wall.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both can be written with the same bond over the same atoms, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If neither can be written over a shared atom set, both readings fail and the two conditions are simply different domains, which is also an acceptable outcome and is already how `07_the_tether_answer.md` reports them.

### Q3 - Do the four structures of Fig 12 each appear here?

- **Isomer:** Q2 above. Present as the stronger reading.
- **Catalyst:** Fig 12 :119, *"A part that survives its own use."* Present in F2 of the audit. A spent part cannot keep the loop closed.
- **Cycle:** Fig 12 :121, *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Present in F2. The sign is the question-mark slot in the shape.
- **Functional group:** Fig 12 :123, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Present in Q2 as the weaker reading and as the reason the two verdicts are kept separate.

This fourfold match is the reason the question needed a supporting derivation at all: a flat list of conditions would have hidden the bond that makes one different from the other. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

### Q4 - What is the analogue in other fields?

World analogues are corroboration only, never the verdict.

- **Animal behaviour.** Jones and Kamil's definition of tool use as a *"means to extend the physical influence realised by the animal"* (via Wikipedia, 2026-11-23) is the expansion bond in a different substrate: reach extends through an external object.
- **Engineering.** Fail-safe design: *"Some systems can never be made fail-safe, as continuous availability is needed. Redundancy, fault tolerance, or contingency plans are used for these situations"* (Wikipedia, 2026-11-23). Redundancy is the catalyst/cycle structure in a different substrate: keep the channel by adding parts that survive use.
- **Business.** The principal-agent conflict: *"the conflict in interests and priorities that arises when one person or entity (the 'agent') takes actions on behalf of another"* (Wikipedia, 2026-11-23). This is the comparison bond with a different operand than the principal intended, which is F3 under another name.

The three analogues come from low-overlap fields and are the strongest independent corroboration in the package. They do not decide the verdict (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*), but they do show the shape is not an artifact of the AI-safety vocabulary.

## CONCLUSION

The dependency order is channel first, expansion last, with refusal before mint. On the strong reading preserve and expand are **isomers** over a shared atom set, differing in the bond (cycle/catalyst versus import); on the weak reading they are a **functional group** with different qualifiers. The strong reading has a stated falsifier and is preferred only because Fig 12 :117 makes the bond decisive. Nothing here is `grounded`: no measurement has settled which bond a real system actually uses.
