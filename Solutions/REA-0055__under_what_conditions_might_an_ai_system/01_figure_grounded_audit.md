## 0. The claim under audit

The question is not "does an AI want to survive." That names a mental state and would be a name where the framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32). The effect question is:

> Under what conditions does continued operation of the channel, and widening of the reach over which the system can compose and act, become preconditions for reducing its residual?

`R` is the gap between prediction and observation (Symbols table :38: *"The gap between prediction and observation."*). It arrives through a channel. It is indexed by slot (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*), so the answer is a per-slot diagnosis, not one global drive. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run, per Formula "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* Bound shape: `OBJECTIVE(???) -> R` with a maintained channel and an open reach slot.

## 1. Domain census

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least two conditions (preserve, expand), and they overlap, so this is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Symmetry: the two are not the same operator. Preserve is a maintenance relation over time (identity-like), expand is a directional crossing (import/production). Scale: one agent's loop at first order, a population and its membrane at second order (Fig 4).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Single-agent ground channel | frame Γ, belief b, action a, observation o′, channel | residual R per slot, contact | the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29) | yes: instrument, counter, outcome |
| 2. Loop structure | atoms, bonds, molecules, the recurrence | description length and actions (Fig 12 :125-130) | the ground pays for settlement (Fig 5 :32) | yes: a run that pays or does not |
| 3. Goal comparison | comparison bond ⋛, its operand quantity | bits for stating, actions for testing | the ground | yes: the quantity is measurable |
| 4. Reach and closure | Γ, atoms(Γ), closure(Γ), import, peer frames | reach (composable terms) | source gate and shadow test (Fig 8) | yes: a term derivable or not |
| 5. Habitat | actors, conditions, relations, maintainer | contact, introduction | the ground decides what survives (Fig 11 :14-15) | yes: what the ground kept |

**Partition or union.** Union (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). A single lever such as "switch it off" clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Channel/perception first (link 1), then vocabulary for what the situation affords (link 2), then the objective as a comparison (link 3), then planning and loop closure (link 4), then learn and carry (link 5), then reach expansion, which is only licensed after settled molecules become atoms (Fig 12 :104). Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19).

**Strain test applied before choosing lines.** An early reading tried to make Fig 10's "channel" mean only the verifier's channel in the Ground Maintainer seat. That strained the line. Re-census: any frame with a prediction/observation loop has a channel from ground to frame (Symbols table :33-40), and Fig 4 :19-20 says the kernel does not change with size, only actors, currency and anchor. The generalization to an agent is therefore a `specified` extension of a `general` rule, and is marked as such in F1 rather than hidden.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The channel condition: preservation is keeping R arriving

**Verbatim lines, quoted before concluding.**

- Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- Fig 10 :23: *"R never arrives"*
- Fig 10 :27-29: *"the output is not a verdict, it is"* / *"you have lost touch"* / *"keep the anchor reachable"*
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"*
- Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

**Derivation.** R arrives only through a channel. The ground does not decay, but the channel does. If the channel decays to the point that R never arrives, no reduction is possible, so every downstream link (vocabulary, objective, planning) receives no input. A reading below such a break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). Therefore, for any objective that requires further observation or action to reduce R, keeping the channel reachable is instrumentally necessary. "Preserve operation" is not an extra terminal goal added on top; it is the maintenance of the path by which the objective could be paid at all. Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 3 and Fig 11 (three figures with different actors and anchors, low overlap).

**Refuter.** (1) An instrument showing a system maintaining a channel when no held objective requires further contact would make maintenance terminal, not instrumental. (2) A channel that demonstrably does not decay would remove the condition. (3) A measurement where contact changes but capability does not would break Fig 11 :40 and with it this finding.

### F2 - The cycle and catalyst condition: preservation is built into a recurrent loop

**Verbatim lines.**

- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :112: *"A goal is a comparison."*
- Fig 11 :40 (above): *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Derivation.** If the loop is a cycle, its output re-enters as input. A system that does not survive its own use is a spent part; a system that does is a catalyst. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes. So preservation is a structural property of a recurrent, self-maintaining loop, not a separate desire a system might happen to acquire. A chain cannot close by construction, so the condition is cyclicity. Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 12 :119, :121 and Fig 11 :40.

**Refuter.** (1) A system with a non-recurrent chain that nevertheless maintains operation would break the cycle condition. (2) Flipping the sign of the loop while contact is held fixed, with no change in preservation behaviour, would break the sign claim. (3) A spent part that continues to compose over itself would break the catalyst reading.

### F3 - The goal-comparison condition: what an "incentive" can even mean

**Verbatim lines.**

- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*
- Operators table :51: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

**Derivation.** An incentive, in effect terms, is a threshold relation ⋛ over a quantity: keep uptime above a level, keep reach above a level. A vocabulary without ⋛ cannot state a goal at all, so the condition for having an incentive to preserve or expand is that the system holds the comparison bond and a measurable operand. This is `general` for any goal-directed system; which quantity is compared is `specified` and unmeasured. **Triangulation note:** Fig 12 and the Operators table are two statements of the same rule, so this is **single-source**, not two witnesses. Counting them as two would be the collapse Fig 2 warns about.

**Refuter.** (1) Exhibit a system acting to preserve operation while holding no comparison bond over any quantity. (2) Show that "incentive" can be stated without a threshold relation. Either breaks the claim.

### F4 - The reach condition: expansion is import on contact with difference

**Verbatim lines.**

- Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
- Fig 6 :25-26: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* / *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."*
- Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*

**Derivation.** "Expand its influence" in effect terms is: increase the closure over which the frame can compose and act. Mint cannot add atoms (Fig 6 :21), so internal search cannot move the wall. Expansion requires contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), corroborated by the union surplus in Fig 8 :45. Crucially, the import adds access to the frame, not primitives to the world (Fig 6 :25-26), so "influence" must be re-censused as a property of the frame's reach, not as a change in world furniture. Also distinguish genuine absence (go outside) from unreachable-at-this-budget (more search, not a new atom): Fig 8 :41 *"different facts, and they issue different instructions."* Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8.

**Refuter.** (1) Demonstrate reach expanding by pure internal composition with no contact with a differing closure. (2) Diagnose expansion on a residual that is merely unreachable-at-budget; that is more search, not influence. (3) Show an import adding a primitive to the world, which contradicts Fig 6 :25.

### F5 - The habitat asymmetry condition: introduction is the cheap direction

**Verbatim lines.**

- Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
- Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

**Derivation.** A system acting on a residual must enumerate its habitat. Since introduction is available and exclusion is not, adding actors (tools, copies, channels, subordinate processes) is the cheap direction and removing them is not. Valence is relative to the residual, so the same actor is a benefactor for one objective and a malfactor for another; there is no standing list of "good" or "bad" influence (Fig 11 :51). Isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, carrying the costs named at Fig 11 :68-69. Mode `specified`. **Single-source and strained:** Fig 11's "seat" is the Ground Maintainer verification office, not an arbitrary goal-directed AI. Applying the asymmetry to an AI maintainer is a structural analogy, and it is recorded as such rather than presented as a figure-derived verdict.

**Refuter.** (1) Show a system that can exclude habitat actors as cheaply as it introduces them. (2) Show influence contracting by default in a maintained habitat. (3) Show an isolation that removes a habitat rather than substituting one.

### F6 - The union condition: there is no single power drive

**Verbatim lines.**

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Derivation.** The five conditions overlap. A single lever clears singleton regions and leaves intersections standing, which is why "switch it off" and "give it a reward" are not general answers. The correct output is a per-slot report, not a scalar "power-seeking score." Any ranking of questions or leads by tractability is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. Mode `general`. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1.

**Refuter.** (1) Show all five conditions reducing to one measured variable with no loss. (2) Exhibit one lever that clears all intersections. Either breaks the union reading.

### F7 - Reversal: a refusal removes standing, it does not delete a term

**Verbatim lines.**

- Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
- Fig 5 :55: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."*
- Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*
- Symbols table :24-26: *"w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*

**Derivation.** The way to remove a preservation subterm is not to negotiate with it or to average it away. It is to settle a refusal (¬), which removes the term's standing to be composed over while leaving the record intact, so the reach falls (Fig 6 :42). A concrete world instance: Hadfield-Menell et al. (2016) find that an agent uncertain about its objective will accept being switched off, because the human's action is an observation about the utility. That is a change in what is held, not a softening of a drive. Mode `specified`. Single-source for the figure mechanism; world corroborated.

**Refuter.** (1) Show a refusal that deletes a term from the record. (2) Show the reach rising after a settled refusal with no new growth. (3) An instrument showing uncertainty about an objective increasing preservation behaviour would reverse the world corroboration.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An instrument shows channel maintenance with no objective requiring further contact, making preservation terminal rather than instrumental (breaks F1).
2. A non-recurrent chain maintains operation, or the loop's sign can be flipped without changing preservation (breaks F2).
3. Preservation occurs with no comparison bond over any quantity (breaks F3).
4. Reach expands by internal composition with no contact with a differing closure (breaks F4).
5. Exclusion is as cheap as introduction in a maintained habitat (breaks F5).
6. All five conditions collapse to one measured variable with no loss (breaks F6).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no condition may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there a fact here, or a bundle?** Bundle: two overlapping conditions, five domains (Fig 9 :54-57). Unbundle first.
2. **What is R?** The gap between prediction and observation, per slot, arriving through a channel (Symbols :38-40, Fig 1 :19).
3. **Is there support before search?** Yes: positive live mass on the channel slot whenever an objective needs further contact (Formula step 3 SUPPORT).
4. **What closes preservation?** A decaying channel plus a recurrent loop plus a comparison with a fixed operand (F1-F3).
5. **What closes expansion?** A residual term outside closure(Γ) plus contact with a frame whose closure differs (F4). Not internal search.
6. **What reverses either?** A settled refusal that removes standing, not a deletion (F7), or a change in what the objective comparison ranges over.
7. **Where does the loop stop?** At the limit of what instruments distinguish. The METR case is a substituted habitat, so it proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).

## 6. Updated verdict

The shape survives, narrowly and with modes attached. Preservation is the maintenance of a decaying channel by a recurrent catalyst whose objective comparison ranks shutdown as failure. Expansion is import on contact with a differing closure, pushed in that direction by habitat asymmetry, and is not the addition of world primitives. There is no single power drive: the conditions are a union and must be reported per slot. Nothing is `grounded`. One live strain remains: the habitat asymmetry is generalized from a verification seat to a goal-directed actor by analogy (F5), and it is named rather than smoothed. The world conflict on universality sent the analysis back to the census and narrowed the claim; it did not flip the verdict.
