# The Tether's answer on trade deficits

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures and the sources actually opened (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7). It is not a ground reading and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

A persistent gap between imports and exports registers as a loss in a contest frame and as borrowing in an accounting frame. R is indexed per country-slot and per sector-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The question's either/or bundles at least four conditions (accounting, competitiveness, distribution, financing), so it is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55). Each condition keeps its own verdict and they are never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **The balance is an instrument, not the anchor (D1, D2).** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :37-38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." The question is constitutively about outcomes; the balance moves when exchange rates, saving and investment move. Using it as a scoreboard is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :24-27).

2. **"Deficit equals national loss of competitiveness" is refused (D2).** Fig 5 :51 "The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing. None of them sorts a gap by what is wrong." Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." What is owed is a negation, not a new theory of trade. Nothing is deleted; the reach falls because something is added (Fig 6 :42-43).

3. **A deficit is definitionally saving below investment, mirrored by capital inflow (D1).** Import this with provenance from the national accounts frame; do not claim the figures derived it (Fig 6 :23). It is a `general` identity and a filter for the budget, never a witness for the welfare verdict (Fig 9 :60). It does not by itself settle causation, which can be read trade-driven or capital-driven.

4. **National and local verdicts stay separate (D3).** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Local import competition raises unemployment and lowers wages and participation in exposed places; the national "not harmful" reading must not be played back down without measuring the round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30). Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same capital inflow can be a benefactor to the investment slot and a malfactor to a factory town.

5. **Harm is conditional on use and financing composition (D4).** Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." The sign of the net balance discards the composition (currency, maturity, gross exposures) that carries reversal risk. The deficit that finances productive investment with stable long-horizon liabilities is not the deficit that finances consumption with short-term foreign-currency debt. This is a difference of arrangement, and the balance formula cannot tell them apart (Fig 12 :5: "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording.").

6. **The contest frame is a documented cognitive and historical pattern (D5).** Zero-sum beliefs are defined in the psychological literature as the view that one party's gains come at others' expense across international relations (Davidai and Tepper, 2023). Mercantilism read prosperity as a zero-sum game and used trade balances as the score (Oxford, mercantilism). These sources corroborate the *frame persistence* claim only, never the economics verdict (Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not").

7. **Which particular deficit is harmful is a declared gap (empirical).** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The framework returns the conditions a verdict must satisfy. It does not name the cause of a named country's deficit, and no dataset for such a country was run here.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "trade deficits are harmless". That is the opposite horn of a false either/or and would erase D3 and D4.
- Not "trade deficits are a loss of competitiveness". That term is refused, not merely qualified.
- Not that the accounting identity gives the direction of causation. It is an identity, not a model.
- Not that any named country (the US, for example) is benefited or harmed by its deficit. That needs a ground reading.
- Not that a surplus is evidence of strength. The same per-slot and composition rules apply with the sign reversed.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of real outcomes for a named period was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the discriminators are:

- Saving and investment data for a named country and period, to see whether the deficit is investment-led, consumption-led or fiscal-led.
- The currency, maturity and holder composition of external liabilities, and the gross exposure data, to test reversal risk (BIS 2015; World Bank 2018).
- Local labour-market outcomes by exposure, over a decade, to measure the cross-scale gap (Autor, Dorn and Hanson 2013; NBER Digest 2025).
- A counterfactual in which exchange rates or saving are pushed and the balance and real outcomes are watched separately, to test whether the balance is an anchor or an instrument.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: a deficit with no matching capital inflow; a balance that behaves as a true anchor; uniformly distributed local harms; identical reversal risk regardless of use; a partition governed by one variable.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The missing thing is a ground reading for a named country, characterized in effect terms (outcomes, per-slot adjustment, financing composition) but not held here. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured; this package records the shape of the gap and stops. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The verdict stays `candidate` until data, not agreement, moves it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer the question's either/or, because that either/or bundles four different conditions. A trade deficit is definitionally saving below investment, mirrored by a net capital inflow; that is an accounting identity, not a verdict on welfare. The balance is an instrument, not the anchor, because the question is about real outcomes and the balance moves with exchange rates, saving and investment. The claim that a deficit is a national loss of competitiveness is refused as a frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces. Whether a deficit actually harms depends on conditions the balance number does not hold: which local slots carry the adjustment, what the borrowing finances, and the currency and maturity of the external liabilities. National and local verdicts therefore stay separate, and a source-discriminating reading of the data, not more argument, is what would name the cause of any particular deficit. What the framework returns is the shape any answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The empirical verdict stays open until the ground settles it.
