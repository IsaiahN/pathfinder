# Solution ECN-0079

**Question:** Are trade deficits economically harmful, or do they often reflect saving, investment, and capital flows rather than a simple loss of competitiveness?

**Source line:** `Questions/economics questions.txt` line 104

**Section:** 8. Trade, globalization, and industrial policy

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive shape, `general` on the structural claim. Nothing here is `grounded`, because no measurement of a particular country's welfare is taken in this derivation.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this work: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By the framework's own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, Isaiah N, 2026-11-19): *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It returns a shape and a set of falsifiers, not a verdict on any real deficit.

## Restatement as an effect

The effect to be explained is a persistent gap between what a country imports and what it exports (R, a comparison between two quantities, per country-slot and per sector-slot). Option A reads that gap as a loss of competitiveness and a national harm. Option B reads it as the mirror of saving and investment decisions and net capital inflows. What each leads to: A treats a frame-internal score as an anchor and averages away concentrated sectoral losses; B unbundles the question into accounting, distribution, sustainability and cognitive-frame domains and sends each to its own ground.

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

A checkable answer is a statement of the *conditions* under which a deficit is harmful: the per-slot distribution of the adjustment, what the borrowing finances, and the composition and stability of the financing. Any claim of the form "all deficits are harmful" or "no deficit is harmful" is a shape that fails the per-slot and union tests and is therefore refused. This binding is written down before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else (figure 5, amendment on direction).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

The figure reasons are recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md`: a room of agreeing readers is not an anchor (Fig 2 :35), selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation, and a verifier who has already absorbed the source cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42). Legally, unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, a practical failure on top of the structural one.
