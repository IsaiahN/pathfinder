**Subject:** One checkable claim about trade deficits, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "are trade deficits harmful, or are they saving and capital flows?" bundles at least four different conditions. An accounting fact (a current account deficit is, by definition, saving below investment, matched by net capital inflow), a competitiveness claim, a distributional claim about particular workers and places, and a financing and sustainability claim. Those conditions can hold separately. So the either/or in the question is not a partition, and no single sentence on either horn can be a verdict.
>
> Three things follow, all stated before you see any source:
>
> 1. The trade balance is an instrument, not the anchor. The question is constitutively about outcomes (income, employment, the ability to service external liabilities), and the balance moves when exchange rates, saving and investment move. An instrument can be wrong on the axes it does not measure.
> 2. "A deficit is a national loss of competitiveness" is not a gap in need of a new theory. It is a term I am refusing: it scores the balance with a quantity the contest frame itself produces. A refusal is owed, not an explanation.
> 3. A national "not harmful" verdict must not be averaged down to local labour markets, where adjustment costs concentrate and persist. And the sign of the net balance does not hold the information in the *composition* of the financing, which is what matters for reversal risk.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every deficit is harmless, that a deficit never costs specific workers, that the accounting identity tells us the direction of causation, or that any particular country's deficit is good or bad. Those need a ground reading (data for a named country and period), which this output does not contain. I return a shape and a set of falsifiers, not a verdict on a real deficit.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a persistent deficit with no matching net capital inflow and no change in foreign claims;
> 2. a balance that does not move when exchange rates or saving move, with real outcomes tracking its sign one-for-one across every slot;
> 3. import-competition harms uniformly distributed across places, with no local concentration and no slow adjustment;
> 4. deficits financing investment and deficits financing consumption having the same reversal risk and growth outcome, with no dependence on financing composition;
> 5. a demonstration that the four conditions form a partition governed by one variable, not a union of overlapping conditions.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (project file)
> - Dallas Fed, "Are trade deficits good or bad, and can tariffs reduce them?", 2025: https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2025/0904
> - BIS Working Paper 525, "Capital flows and the current account: Taking financing (more) seriously", 2015: https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-525-capital-flows-and-current-account-taking-financing-more-seriously.pdf
> - Autor, Dorn and Hanson, "The China Syndrome", American Economic Review, 2013: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.103.6.2121
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
