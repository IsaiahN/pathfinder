## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, between two readings of a persistent gap between a country's imports and exports. Reading A: the gap is a loss of competitiveness and an economic harm. Reading B: it reflects saving, investment and capital flows rather than a simple loss of competitiveness. What each leads to: A treats a frame-internal score as an anchor and averages away concentrated sectoral losses; B unbundles the question into four conditions and sends each to its own ground, ending in a declared empirical gap rather than a verdict on a named country.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, followed by partition or union and dependency order.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: a comparison between two quantities, imports against exports and saving against investment (the ⋛ comparison bond). Symmetry: deficit and surplus are inverses under the identity, but the contest frame assigns valence asymmetrically to the sign; that asymmetry is under audit, not assumed. Scale: national accounts, sector and local labour markets, and the global capital market that finances the slot. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **D1 National accounting.** Actors: households, firms, government, foreign sector. Currency: saving, investment, net capital inflow. Anchor: the national accounts identity and observed flows. Admitted.
- **D2 Competitiveness / contest frame.** Actors: industries, workers, trading partners. Currency: market share, the balance as score. Anchor: none nameable beyond the balance itself, which moves when exchange rates and saving move. Admitted as a frame, not a ground.
- **D3 Distribution and adjustment.** Actors: specific industries, local labour markets, workers. Currency: jobs, wages, participation, transfers. Anchor: observed local employment and wage data. Admitted.
- **D4 Financing and sustainability.** Actors: foreign creditors, domestic borrowers, reserve authorities. Currency: gross and net external positions, currency and maturity of debt. Anchor: rollover outcomes, spreads, reversal events. Admitted.
- **D5 Cognitive and historical frame.** Actors: mass publics, elites, mercantilist writers. Currency: zero-sum perception, fairness judgement. Anchor: survey experiments and the historical record. Admitted for the frame question only.
- **X1 "The deficit itself is the national loss".** Actors: the balance number. Currency: a score. Anchor: none nameable when pushed. Not a ground; refunded to D2.

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The four conditions can hold separately and in intersections, so no single lever answers the either/or.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** perception of flows, then vocabulary (S - I, financial account, per-slot, gross versus net), then objective (what counts as harm), then planning (which lever), then learn and carry. The contest frame breaks at the objective link: it has terms for winning and losing but no instrument for progress.

**Strain test applied.** My first pass read surplus and deficit as Fig 12 isomers; that strained, because the bond between imports and exports is a threshold comparison, not an arrangement over a shared atom set. Re-census resolved it: ⋛ comparison retained, isomer name withdrawn and kept in the record. No live strain remains.

- **International macroeconomics.** IMF staff, "Understanding Global Imbalances", IMF policy paper, 2026. Read via indexed page excerpt 2026-11-23. - **Development economics.** CGD, "The Financial Realities of the US Trade Deficit that Tariffs Can't Change", 2025. Indexed excerpt, direct fetch 403. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** the question's either/or is a false partition. The balance is an instrument, not the anchor; "deficit equals national loss of competitiveness" is a refused term; the identity is definitional and causal-direction-neutral, a filter for the budget and not a witness for welfare; harm is conditional on per-slot adjustment, use of borrowing and financing composition, and these verdicts remain separate by domain. Which particular deficit is harmful is a declared `gap`, unreached at this budget. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim. World sources corroborate by pool: Pool A for the identity, Pools B and C for the per-slot and composition conditions, Pools D and E for the persistence of the contest frame. Pools B to E are low-overlap for the *mechanism*, never for the accounting fact.

- **Cost if wrong:** a misdirected policy argument. Treating the contest frame as the anchor invites tariffs aimed at the balance while leaving saving and investment untouched, and averages away concentrated local losses that need their own instrument. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is real but bounded, and it is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuters.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a refutation fires; the refusal is added to the record and the reach falls because something was added, not because something was struck out. Fig 6 :42-43 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A persistent deficit with no matching capital inflow; a balance that behaves as a true anchor; uniformly distributed local harms with no slow adjustment; identical reversal risk regardless of use and composition; or a single variable governing all four conditions as a partition. Any of these moves the verdict, and until one fires the answer stays `candidate`.
