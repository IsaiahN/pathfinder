## RESIDUAL

## DERIVATION

### Q1 What bond is the balance? CORRECTION TO MY OWN first pass in `01`

- Operators table :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Fig 12 :87: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- My first pass tried to read surplus and deficit as Fig 12 isomers (same atoms, different bond). **Retracted.** The balance is one comparison ⋛ of X against M (and S against I), not two arrangements over a shared six-term atom set. The isomer reading requires both signs to be composed from the same atoms with a different bond and different behaviour; this comparison has one sign variable against a threshold.
- ⇒ The correct bond is ⋛ (comparison). A deficit is "below threshold"; a surplus is "above threshold". The valence attached to each side is the contest frame's contribution, not the bond's.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a source shows that surplus and deficit are two different molecules over the same atom set whose difference is carried by a bond other than a threshold comparison (for example a production bond where one sign creates an atom the other does not), the comparison reading fails and the isomer reading is restored.
- Status: triangulated (Operators table + Fig 12), with the re-census recorded rather than deleted.

### Q2 Union or partition? The decisive order question

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- The four conditions are: A accounting (deficit equals S below I, mirrored by inflow), B competitiveness (the deficit as a score), C distribution (local adjustment), D financing composition and sustainability. These overlap: a deficit can be A and C, or A and D, or all four. They are not mutually exclusive, so they form a union.
- ⇒ "Not harmful" clears A alone. It leaves A∩C and A∩D standing. This is why the question's either/or is not answerable as posed.
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Falsifier:** a variable that predicts A, C and D jointly with no residual intersection would collapse the union to a partition and refute this.

### Q3 Dependency order: which link can be diagnosed if the answer is wrong?

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- The chain for this question: perception of the measured flow (balance of payments data) → vocabulary (S - I, financial account, per-slot, gross versus net) → objective (what would count as harm: a ground outcome) → planning (which lever: fiscal, investment, exchange rate, trade policy) → learn and carry (name what worked and verify it).
- The competitiveness frame breaks at link 3: it has terms for winning and losing (link 2) but no instrument for what would count as progress, so it attracts "the least attention and the fewest instruments" (Fig 3 :26). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- ⇒ Debating tariffs (a link-4 lever) before link 3 is settled is effort spent above the break. This is exactly what the Dallas Fed and PIIE sources warn about: tariffs do not reliably change the balance, because the balance is set by saving and investment.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that measures "competitiveness" as an outcome with the same independence as employment or income would move the break to link 4 and refute this reading.

### Q4 Functional group analogue

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The four conditions share the substructure "external financing": a deficit is always borrowing from the rest of the world. That substructure fixes the *kind* (a capital-importing episode). The qualifiers (what the borrowing finances, currency and maturity of the liabilities, which local slots carry the adjustment) say *which member*. Two deficits with identical balance numbers but different qualifiers are different substances in the figure's sense: the balance formula is the same, the behaviour is not.
- This rescues the useful part of my first-pass isomer intuition without the error: not "surplus and deficit are isomers" but "deficits with different financing and local adjustment are different members of one functional group".
- **Falsifier:** if the qualifier (use and composition) has no predictive power for outcomes once the net balance and its size are held, the group/qualifier distinction is idle and the coarse balance description is sufficient.

### Q5 Cross-scale round trip: national to local

- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 4 :31-32: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."
- Send up (x): the national balance and the identity. Abstract (T_A): "the deficit is financed by inflow, so welfare is not reduced." Concretise (T_E): the local labour market where the import-competing plant closes.
- R_T is nonzero. What the coarse national description drops: which slots lose, over what period, with what persistence, and what the borrowed funds finance. The Autor, Dorn and Hanson result is the measured gap, not a refutation of the identity. Treating "national net welfare" as identical to "every local slot is fine" would set R_T to zero and be the dead-reckoning error the figure names.
- **Falsifier:** a round trip in which the national description plays back down without loss, meaning local outcomes are fully predicted by the national balance alone, would set R_T to zero and refute this.

## CONCLUSION

The balance is a threshold comparison ⋛, not an isomer pair (my first pass was retracted and the record kept). The question is a union of four conditions, not a partition, so the either/or fails. The dependency chain breaks at the objective link in the competitiveness frame, which is why trade-policy levers are aimed too early. Deficits are best read as members of one functional group whose qualifiers decide the outcome, and the national to local transform carries a nonzero R_T that must be measured, not assumed.

All four sub-claims carry written falsifiers. None is `grounded`. The empirical verdict on any named country remains a `gap`.
