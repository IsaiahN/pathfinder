# Expert verification packet: the Tether on trade deficits

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim, not `grounded`.** No data for a named country was run. This is a shape and a route, not a verdict on a real deficit. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are trade deficits harmful, or are they saving, investment and capital flows?" is a union of four conditions, not a partition, so neither horn is a verdict. A deficit is definitionally saving below investment, mirrored by net capital inflow, and the balance is an instrument rather than the anchor for a question about real outcomes. "Deficit equals national loss of competitiveness" is refused, not replaced. Whether a deficit harms depends on conditions the net balance does not hold: which local slots carry the adjustment, what the borrowing finances, and the composition, currency and maturity of external liabilities. The framework returns these conditions, their falsifiers and the instruments that would close them. It does not name a verdict on any named country.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the balance is an instrument, not the anchor; the contest term is a frame-internal self-score, and the either/or is a union.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :37-38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** a ground reading in which real outcomes track the sign of the balance one-for-one across every slot, and the balance does not move when exchange rates or saving are pushed, would promote it to an anchor; or a single variable shown to govern all four conditions as a partition.

### C2 Economics-facing, checkable against the national accounts and the data

- **Statement:** a current account deficit is S below I, mirrored by net capital inflow; this is an identity, not a causal claim, and it does not hold the information that decides harm. The sign of the balance is a coarse description; use and financing composition are the discriminators.
- **What to check:** Dallas Fed 2025 states the identity with S minus I on the left and net exports plus net foreign income on the right, and "Trade deficits are not inherently bad. They reflect foreign capital inflows and can support domestic investment or fiscal expansion." BIS WP 525 (2015) argues that "Investors don't stop financing current accounts, they stop financing debt", so gross exposures, not the net balance, drive sudden stops. World Bank brief 17 (2018) says a deficit is "neither good nor bad" in itself but harmful when it "fuels consumption rather than investment" alongside other gaps. Are these characterizations faithful? Does any of them derive causation from the identity alone?
- **Refuter:** cross-country evidence that deficits financing investment and deficits financing consumption have identical reversal probabilities and growth outcomes, conditional on size and openness, would make composition idle and refute this.

### C3 Labour-facing, checkable against the China Shock literature

- **Statement:** a national reading cannot be played back down to a local slot without measuring the round-trip gap; local adjustment costs are concentrated and slow.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** Autor, Dorn and Hanson (AER 2013) find that "Rising imports cause higher unemployment, lower labor force participation, and reduced wages in local labor markets that house import-competing manufacturing industries." The 2025 NBER Digest summary reports that places recover after 2010 while incumbent workers do not. Are those findings consistent with a nonzero national-to-local gap that persists for a decade?
- **Refuter:** evidence that local harms are uniformly distributed across slots, with no concentration and no slow adjustment, would set the round-trip gap to zero and refute this.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that trade deficits are harmless. That is the opposite horn of a false either/or.
- Not that deficits are necessarily a loss of competitiveness. That term is refused.
- Not that the accounting identity yields the direction of causation.
- Not a verdict on the US deficit or any other named country, and no dataset was run here.
- Not that surpluses are evidence of strength; the same rules apply with the sign reversed.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- The eight essays by Isaiah N are used for the canonical reading of "shape" only, not as independent world witnesses. The macro-accounting sources are one evidence pool, not six.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating reading of the data for a named country and period, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. Saving and investment data to classify the deficit as investment-led, consumption-led or fiscal-led.
2. Currency, maturity and holder composition of external liabilities, plus gross exposures, to test reversal risk.
3. Local labour-market outcomes by exposure over a decade, to measure the national-to-local gap.
4. A push on exchange rates or saving, watching the balance and real outcomes separately, to test anchor versus instrument.

Any of the six pre-registered falsifiers in `01` §4 firing would also move the verdict.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :37-38, Fig 9 :55 and Fig 1 :19 (four short passages).
2. Check C2 against the national accounts: is the deficit an identity with no causal content, and does composition carry what the sign discards?
3. Check C3 against the China Shock findings: are the local effects concentrated and slow, as the round-trip gap predicts?
4. Name one discriminator that would tell an investment-led deficit from a consumption-led one in the data, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
