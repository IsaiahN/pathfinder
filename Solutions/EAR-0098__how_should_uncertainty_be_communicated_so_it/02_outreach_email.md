# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two-stage order below is respected. The stage-1 output must survive without the theory being shown, or the offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one communication shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about communicating uncertainty. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A statement of uncertainty is a message travelling down a channel between an assessment and a decision. Two failures are named in the question: overstating uncertainty, and having uncertainty read as absence of risk. The figures treat both as the same defect at two ends of that channel, namely a loss of contact with the ground. The ground here is the realised outcome the decision bears on, not the comfort of the wording. Three consequences follow, and they are checkable without my theory:
>
> 1. Report risk per slot (per place, impact, population, time). A global average can be near zero while one slot is live. Aggregation is how the live signal disappears.
> 2. Treat uncertainty words as declared meanings until someone can check them against the ground. A label that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
> 3. Keep the provenance of the estimate attached. Two numbers with the same content can have entirely different sources, and only the record separates them.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming the framework dictates the exact words to use, or that it settles which risk label is correct. It returns a shape, not a phrasing. The specific calibration of "likely" or "95 percent" belongs to the assessment practice, and I am not asserting it here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single global uncertainty statistic shown to preserve a live slot-level risk signal with no loss;
> 2. a message whose fidelity to the ground does not degrade under pooled readings or saturated metrics;
> 3. an uncertainty label shown to be checkable against the ground with no instrument beyond the label itself, while also deciding the outcome.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The local verbatim figure text with line numbers (FIG 1, 2, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13) is in my package, file `10_source_ledger.csv` lists it as a `local:` path.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Risk framing and calibrated uncertainty language: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-1
> - Systematic review of uncertainty communications: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/abb265
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a communication problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name / institutional affiliation]
