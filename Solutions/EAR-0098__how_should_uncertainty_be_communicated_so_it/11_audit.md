## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19-22 (per slot, no aggregation), Fig 10 :24-29 (channel, anchor), Fig 9 :41, :60 (reversible cuts, filters), Fig 8 :50 (provenance).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `02_outreach_email.md`: report per slot, keep the anchor reachable, make labels checkable, carry provenance.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** an actual public uncertainty statement, for example a headline with one global number and a confidence label.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **tails and the slot**: a public headline keeps the central number and drops the per-slot range;
- the **checkability of the label**: "likely, medium confidence" is published as a verdict rather than as a statement of what would be observed;
- the **provenance**: the source and evidence pool are separated from the number;
- the **reversibility of the framing**: one wording is published as final, so the ranked alternatives disappear.

What survives cleanly is the demand that the ground remain reachable and that the risk be indexed somewhere. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is a condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
