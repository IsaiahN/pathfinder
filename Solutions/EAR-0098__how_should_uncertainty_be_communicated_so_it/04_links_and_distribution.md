## How the world literature bears on the shape claim

The world sources are secondary. They do not decide the verdict, and several share an evidence pool with each other (the IPCC note and the AR6 chapter are one institution; Kause et al. and Taylor et al. share an author). Overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. What they add is a check on whether the figure-derived shape is useful in practice:

- The IPCC practice of traceability and of giving full distributions including tails is the operational form of "keep the anchor reachable" and "report per slot." It corroborates F1 and F2, from a different field.
- Painter's documented mechanism, that stressing uncertainty leads audiences to discount or dodge the problem, is the world-side statement of the exact failure the question names. It corroborates F2 and F3 without sharing the framework evidence pool.
- Hoekstra et al., Slovic and Sunstein predict that a number or a label alone will not carry calibrated meaning to a recipient. They corroborate F3 and F7 from statistics and cognitive psychology, two low-overlap fields.
- RAND and the toolkit lesson predict that with deep uncertainty the remedy is robust strategies over many futures, not a single optimised forecast. They corroborate the ranking and reversibility requirement in F5 from decision science.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Agreement across an IPCC-internal group is one witness, not two.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, read the theory. The stage-1 verdict must already be recorded, because agreement after seeing the interiors is not evidence (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
