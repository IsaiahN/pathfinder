## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (fetched this session): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For EAR-0098 a **shape** is therefore the narrowed form of a good uncertainty communication, statable before the exact words are chosen. It constrains: the ground must stay reachable, the slot must stay visible, the label must be checkable, the provenance must travel. It does not fill: it does not supply the phrase, the decimal place or the confidence term. "A range per region with the tail shown and the source attached" is a shape. "Use the wording: likely, medium confidence" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (fetched): use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (in-repo record, not re-fetched): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is F3's "install what can be violated" in plain language.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place," and the paradox that by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it." For communication, this means the check the recipient performs is the same check the communicator should have performed before sending.

## 3. What licenses a name (here, a phrasing)

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and a shadow test. The IPCC calibrated language is such an import from assessment practice. It is real and useful, but it is not a figure-derived phrasing.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (in-repo record, not re-fetched): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Adopting IPCC's confidence/likelihood vocabulary adds a dimension to a communicator's frame; it does not derive from the six-term set.

## 4. The source blind spot, and why it matters for communication

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched this session): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The green reading does not determine the source. "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

Apply this to a risk message. A recipient's calibrated-looking response does not tell you which source produced it: a traceable assessment, a repeated slogan, or an affect-driven guess can all produce the same surface reading. This is F6 in cognitive form. The corresponding demand is not a better phrase but a better discriminator: ask for the provenance and the checkable prediction, not the tone.

It also explains the two failures in the question. Both "overstating uncertainty" and "reading uncertainty as no risk" are different sources projecting to a similar surface behaviour (a recipient who does not act). A message that only manages surface behaviour cannot tell them apart. That is why the shape claim is about the channel and the anchor, not about phrasing.

## 5. Q: does the framework dictate how to word an uncertainty statement?

**No.** This is F10. Nothing in the eight essays or the figures supplies a phrase, a threshold or a confidence label. What the essays supply is the discipline: write the sought-for takeaway down as an ordinary term (*Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* and Fig 5 :65), keep the anchor reachable, and record the lineage at the stop. The exact wording remains a gap and is recorded as such in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6.

The honest reason is structural: "shape, not a name" governs a *missing* primitive. Here the missing thing is not a word but a checkable route from an estimate to a decision under a ground. The route has a shape; the words are a local choice. Promoting a local choice to a figure verdict would be the apophenia Fig 9 :27 warns about: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

## 6. Prestige is not action, and this derivation is a simulation

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (fetched): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A communication strategy endorsed by famous communicators has a budget to be tried, not a verdict.

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the communication shape and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Correction to my own reading

I initially treated "shape, not a name" as if it forbade naming any component of a communication, including the anchor and the slot. That overstates it. The rule governs a missing primitive, not the selection of things already held. The figures already hold the ground (Fig 2 :29), the slot (Symbols :43) and the channel (Fig 10 :24). Naming those is naming held structure, not inventing a new one. What is forbidden is promoting a locally chosen phrase or confidence term into a figure-derived name. Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost.
