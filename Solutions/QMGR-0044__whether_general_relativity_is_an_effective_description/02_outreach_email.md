**Subject:** Check one structural output about general relativity before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` as applied to GR, not `grounded`):** irreducibility is a fact about a level, not about the world. A term that has settled at one level can serve as an atom for whatever composes over it, and the framework's rule is that there is no top and no bottom. Applied to general relativity, this means GR cannot be a terminal primitive in this framing: it is a settled term that can be composed over. The relation to any deeper description is a lossy transform, not an identity, so the round trip from the deeper level back to GR throws information away and cannot reconstruct it. What is missing is a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that string theory, asymptotic safety, or loop gravity is the completion. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It can say that a deeper level is owed and what it would have to look like, but it cannot select among candidate theories. I am also not claiming GR is wrong in its tested regime; its residual is already small there. If several microscopic sources give the same effective reading, the reading cannot comment on the source.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a terminal level or a bottom admitted by the framework, or one of its six base terms derived from the other five;
> 2. a demonstration that the round trip between the effective and deeper description has zero gap, so the effective theory is invertible;
> 3. a source-discriminating measurement at the relevant scale that names a unique microscopic source for the GR reading;
> 4. a measurement showing GR cannot serve as an atom for any deeper composition.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
