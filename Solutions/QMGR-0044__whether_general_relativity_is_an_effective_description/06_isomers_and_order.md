# Levels, order and isomers in the GR question

**Mode: `general` on the level rule, `specified` on the application to GR.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

GR predicts well in its tested regime, yet its standing as a term is unsettled: terminal primitive or lossy composition over a deeper level? Two questions are owed: (1) what is the **order** of the composition, and (2) is the relation between GR and a deeper theory an **isomer** relation (same level, different bond) or a **level** relation (different strata)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) + Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven, including `→` sequence, `≡` identity, `⋛` comparison, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Two strata in play: the GR stratum and a deeper stratum not held in Γ. Anchor for the order claim: the figures. Anchor for the deeper stratum: none yet, which is why X1 is "not a domain yet" in `01`.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What order is the composition?

- Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*
- Fig 13 :110: *"And each composition becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
- Fig 13 :111-112: *"A settled composition enters the library as a term, and is then an operand like any other."*

So the order is fixed by settlement, not by importance or by chronology: first a term settles against the ground at its own level, then it becomes an atom available to a deeper composition. GR is settled (decades of experiment), so it is already an atom at its level. The deeper theory is **not** settled, so it cannot yet serve as a level above GR in the framework's terms; it is a shape, not a held operand. This is the dependency order of Fig 3 applied to levels: a link below a break cannot be read, and the missing link here is the instrument that would settle the deeper level (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).

**Order verdict:** `general` for the rule, `specified` for GR. Refuter: a composition in which an unsettled term serves as an atom, or a demonstrated case where settlement is not required before promotion.

### Q2 - Is the relation to a deeper theory an isomer relation?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

An isomer relation is **within one level**: same atoms, different bond. A level relation is **across levels**: the output of one level becomes an operand of the next. GR and a deeper microscopic theory are not isomers, because they are not at the same level and do not share the same atom set in the relevant sense (the deeper atoms are precisely what is not held). Calling them isomers would be a category error.

The genuine isomer question lives **within** the GR stratum. If two formulations of GR (for example the metric formulation and a connection-based formulation) use the same six-term atoms with different bonds, they would be Fig 12 isomers: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* That is a checkable structural claim, and it does not require any new primitive.

**Isomer verdict:** refused across levels, admitted as a test within a level. Refuter: a demonstration that GR and its microscopic completion share one atom set at one level, in which case the isomer reading would replace the level reading.

### Q3 - What is the transform, and what does the round trip cost?

- Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*
- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- Fig 4 :31-32: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."*
- Symbols table: R_T is *"the gap between what was sent up and what came back down"*, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.

The round trip here is: send GR up as a coarse description of the deeper level (T_A), bring a candidate deeper theory back down to GR-scale predictions (T_E), and compare. R_T is nonzero by construction of an abstraction. The physically familiar version is integrating out high-energy degrees of freedom: the low-energy theory is form-preserving under the coarse graining but its parameters flow. The canonical article tags Wilson 1975 as *a parallel* for Fig 4 with the words *"lossy, non-invertible, form-preserving with flowing parameters"* (read in the article, not in Wilson's paper). Physics corroboration: Donoghue 1994, *"a natural separation of the (known) low energy quantum effects from the (unknown) high energy contributions."*

**R_T verdict:** nonzero, `specified`, not measured numerically. Refuter: a round trip whose gap vanishes at the relevant scale, i.e. an invertible effective-to-microscopic map.

### Q4 - Why is the missing thing an instrument, not an argument?

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; several sources can give one reading; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The case closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument"* and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

The figure's own instrument rule is Fig 6 :34-35: *"It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Here there **is** something already returning a failing reading: precision tests of GR and the computed low-energy quantum corrections. So INWARD is a legitimate route: sharpen the instrument from one already returning something. OUTWARD, importing a candidate completion, is also legitimate but opposed and must not be read against INWARD (Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*).

**Instrument verdict:** `specified`, ungrounded. Refuter: a source-discriminating instrument that names a unique microscopic source, which closes the `gap` and would also test F3 of the audit.

### Q5 - The functional-group reading

If the question is weakened to "is GR a member of the class of effective descriptions?", the answer is yes in `general` mode: every settled term can be composed over, so every held theory can be an effective description of a deeper composition. That is a **functional group** claim (Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*): the group is `settled term that can be an atom`, and the qualifier, the specific microscopic theory, is not supplied. This is the honest weaker form of the public claim.

## CONCLUSION

The order is fixed by settlement: a settled GR becomes an atom for a deeper composition, and the deeper level is not yet settled so it is a shape. The relation across levels is **not** an isomer relation; it is a lossy transform with nonzero R_T. The isomer claim is real only within one level, among formulations sharing an atom set. The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument, and the honest weaker claim is the functional-group one: GR is a member of the class of effective descriptions, with the specific completion left open.

This framing keeps what is defensible without a new primitive, without claiming the framework names the deeper theory, and without turning a structural rule into a measurement.
