**Subject:** One checkable claim about adaptation and displaced risk

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an adaptation has not avoided risk if it has only moved it. The claim is a constraint on what would count as non-shifting, not an approved list of measures:
>
> 1. Risk is indexed per slot, never as a regional total. A program average can fall while one downstream community carries a live residual. The framework calls that a legal state, not a success.
> 2. The adapting frame cannot score its own shift. The anchor is measured outcomes in the affected slots, a gauge, an erosion rate, a loss record, that do not change when the builder revalues them.
> 3. The habitat is enumerated outward, not scoped to the project footprint. The neighbor is second order; the future generation is reached through lock-in and deferred cost, often third order.
> 4. Neighboring communities and future generations are two loops, not one harder question. A lever that clears one can leave the intersection standing.
>
> This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement. It does not name seawalls, mangroves, zoning, or managed retreat as the answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular measure is maladaptation; that managed retreat is always better than protection; that a present-day proxy can settle what a future generation will experience. Those are bindings and conventions, and they must be provenanced as such. A convention that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this was sent):**
> 1. An adaptation that closes residual on every enumerated slot, including second and third order, against a ground neither frame can move, with those slots shown not to be in contact with it.
> 2. A framework result that admits an aggregate index with one live positive slot as a settled success, or a present convention that fixes the future reading.
> 3. A one-off adaptation that closes all residual permanently with no further handoff, or a chain of reviews whose agreement increases its evidence.
> 4. A single frame that sees every displaced slot, including the future chain, from inside its own closure by searching harder.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Stage order in one line

Stage 1: send the shape and its refuter, ask what they would count as a failure, record the verdict. Stage 2, opt in only: send the three links. Stage 3: offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
