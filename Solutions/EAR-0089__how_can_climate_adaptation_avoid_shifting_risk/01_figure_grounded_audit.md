## 0. The residual in effect terms

State R, not the theory. An adaptation A is built in slot s (a community at time t). A's own accounting reports R(s) falling. The same A changes exposure, sediment, water, price, or obligation in other slots s' (a neighboring community downstream or downwind, a community whose mangrove belt was removed, a successor generation that inherits the wall's maintenance bill and the options the wall foreclosed). Those slots carry positive mass R⁺(s') that A's frame did not count. The observed effect is a relocation of residual, not its removal.

Three consequences follow before any figure is chosen:

- R is a vector over slots, not a scalar. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A global fall with one live slot is a legal state, Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".
- The actor's frame has no standing to score the shift. Fig 1 :24-27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- The two shifts named in the question are spatial and temporal. Both must remain in the census. Collapsing them into "risk" is the aggregation error again.

The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step, Direction): **non-shifting = every slot in the enumerated habitat, including the future chain, carries zero live residual against a ground that does not update on the actor's valuation, and the bound moves only when the affected frames are imported.** This shape is refusable by the pre-registered refuter in section 4.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** several slots (indexed by community and generation), so a vector. Not one hard question with one residual.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The same structure is a benefactor to the protected slot and a malfactor to the neighbor: Fig 11 :49-51 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Risk transfer has direction (→ sequence, − subtraction), not a symmetric join.
- **Scale:** first order at the project and the community; second order at the basin, the region, and the intergenerational chain. Contact cascades beyond direct contact: Fig 11 :52-53 "the residual that mattered was three steps out".

### One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none is nameable, it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the local adaptation frame | planner, design firm, the measure (wall, levee, wetland, zoning, retreat) | claimed avoided damage in the frame's own metric | none if it ranges only against its own model; a self-scored index is not an anchor | the actor's closure; must not be collapsed with D2 |
| D2, the habitat (neighboring communities) | upstream/downstream and downwind communities, sediment and water, markets, migration, ecosystems | physical exposure and harm, R⁺ per slot | measured outcomes (gauges, erosion rates, flood levels, loss records) that do not move when the planner revalues | partition of roles: actor versus ground |
| D3, the temporal chain (future generations) | successors, locked-in infrastructure, debt, depleted buffers, foreclosed options | options retained or foreclosed, deferred cost | future measured states; present actors cannot see them, so a convention is owed, not a verdict | a second loop, not a harder instance of D2 |
| D4, verification and evaluation | monitoring and evaluation systems, auditors, affected communities, peer frames | residuals and refuters | the ground (outcomes), never agreement | must have independence from D1 to mean anything |

### Partition or union

D1 and D2 are a partition of roles (actor versus ground) that collapses into the mutual-update failure if merged (Fig 2 collapse 1). D2 and D3 form a **union of two conditions**, not one problem: Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Two loops are run; their verdicts are never averaged.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** enumerate the habitat and its slots, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11).
2. **Vocabulary:** terms for what an action affords, what it can move, block, or shift. This is the middle link that has neither obvious failures nor obvious instruments: Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
3. **Objective:** define risk in effect terms per slot and per generation, not as an aggregate.
4. **Planning:** route the adaptation through the populated habitat.
5. **Learn and carry:** monitoring as a method that can be reapplied, not a recording of one success (Fig 4 :22).

Effort spent at step 4 cannot rescue a missing step 2: Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 : Risk must be measured per slot; an average hides the displaced slot

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

A program that reports "total regional risk down 30 percent" while one downstream community's live residual rises is in a legal figure state, not a success. Fig 1 :19 is the governing line; the other two triangulate it from the aggregation direction.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural rule), applied `specified` to adaptation programs.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 per-slot rule with Fig 2 anchor rule, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** show that the framework admits an aggregate index with a live positive slot as a settled success, or show a slot-wise measure whose global sum is zero while a slot stays positive. If either holds, F1 fails.

### F2 : The adapting frame cannot be its own anchor

Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 1 :24-27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

An avoided-damage figure produced by the builder of the wall is a frame-internal quantity. The anchor is the measured outcome in the affected slots: a gauge, an erosion rate, a loss record, a court, a market. Fig 2 :31-32 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Upstream and downstream instruments pointed at the same river are two frames; two consultants reading the same model are one.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2, different figures, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** an anchor that changes its answer when the actor revalues it, or two frames that share the same model yet count as independent witnesses.

### F3 : Enumerate the habitat to second and third order, not direct contact only

Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :43-44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :52-53 "the residual that mattered was three steps out"
Fig 11 :49-51 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

The neighbor in the question is a second-order slot; the future generation is a slot reached through lock-in and deferred cost, often third order. Scoping to the project footprint is the DDT error: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect." The seawall is a benefactor to the protected slot and a malfactor to the eroding neighbor, so "protective" is a relation to a residual, not a property of the wall.

- **Mode:** `specified` (the enumeration must be done for each adaptation).
- **Status:** single-source for the isolation and cascade law in Fig 11; triangulated for the describe-first procedure with Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". The exemplar and `60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md` note the isolation law fits known cases and has not been tested forward.
- **Refuter:** a measured adaptation whose R⁺ is zero at second and third order on every slot, with the enumeration published. Until such a measurement exists, this stays `specified`.

### F4 : Spatial and temporal transfer are a union; unbundle into two loops

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55-57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Neighboring communities (space, via network) and future generations (time, via lock-in and deferred cost) are two conditions. A single lever can clear the region where only one holds and leave the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds. The Library of Babel essay gives the same instruction from a low-overlap field: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

- **Mode:** `general` for the union rule, `specified` for its application here.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, low overlap between figure metalaw and search essay).
- **Refuter:** a single instrument that resolves both transfers as one condition, or a demonstration that the intersection behaves exactly like either condition alone.

### F5 : Introduction is available; exclusion is not; adaptation is permanent work

Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :63-64 "a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"

The seat can add a structure to a habitat it did not author. It cannot subtract the river, the sediment budget, or the market. A wall is an introduction; the current it deflects remains contact, and contact does not consult the planner. Therefore an adaptation is not a solved state but a maintained one, and maintenance is a duty owed to the future slot.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source (Fig 11). Corroborated, not derived, by IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17: "path dependencies are avoided in order to not limit future options for climate resilient development, and maladaptations across sectors are avoided".
- **Refuter:** an implemented measure that removes the hazard from the habitat for all slots without adding a new dependency or requiring continuing maintenance.

### F6 : Relocation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove the residual

Fig 11 :66-67 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :68-69 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :68-69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

Managed retreat is the cleanest apparent answer to the future-generation problem, and the figure says exactly what it costs: you must reproduce everything the goal required, and you inherit actors you did not choose. Failure of reproduction is invisible until the goal fails; the introduced actor is invisible until it acts. This is a warning about retreat, not a refusal of it.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source (Fig 11).
- **Refuter:** a relocation that reproduces all goal-relevant conditions, introduces no unchosen actor, and whose residual stays at zero in both habitats over the relevant horizon.

### F7 : Verification must range against the ground separately; agreement is not a verdict

Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Current stakeholders evaluating their own adaptation have an unavoidable interest in the outcome and a disqualifying interest in the reading: Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying". Future generations cannot occupy the room, so their slot must be represented by a convention (a standing proxy, an ombudsman, a legal duty). The convention is authored by the seat; it makes the slot statable. It must not decide the outcome, or it has stopped being a convention. Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

- **Mode:** `general` for the rule, `specified` for the proxy requirement.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** a verdict that survives only because frames sharing one evidence pool agreed, or a future-generation convention whose declared meaning fixes the measured result.

### F8 : There is no last room: adaptation is iterative, not terminal

Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room"
Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 7 :30 "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"
Fig 13 :90 "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."

An adaptation program presented as a final closure is the terminal room the figures forbid. Success is a handoff to a later room selected against the residual the last one surfaced, with monitoring as the bracket. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 13, different figures).
- **Refuter:** a figure-licensed one-off adaptation that closes all residual permanently with no further handoff, or a program whose agreement with itself increases the evidence.

### F9 : The framework returns a shape, not an approved technology

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
The canonical figures README (local): "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)".

The shape is: per-slot closure across the full habitat and the future chain, against a ground that does not move, with affected frames imported and the local frame's wall thereby moved. The framework does not name seawall, mangrove, zoning, or retreat. Naming one is the practitioner's provenanced binding, not a figure output. Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 and the canonical README).
- **Refuter:** a verbatim figure line that selects a specific adaptation technology, or a demonstration that the local frame alone sees all displaced slots without an import.

### F10 : Risk has no magnitude without a structure; re-census of the composition rule

Fig 13 :29 "A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"
Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels"
Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"

An initial reading worried that risk moving between communities joins two states directly and so breaks Fig 13 :75. Re-census resolves it. A community is a state; the network is the structure, "that entities relate, and influence travels"; the receiving community is the next state. `community -> network -> community` is a legal cross-kind composition. The temporal chain is `present -> gradient -> future`, where the gradient is "a difference that can be spent" (deferred cost, lock-in, a spent or regenerating buffer). The quantity "risk" is not a seventh member; it is a derived spectrum-over-a-state or gradient-over-space, and Fig 13 :29 says it has no magnitude until the ordering and the across-what are named. A risk number with no ordering and no domain is frame-internal noise.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source for the composition rule (Fig 13), with the strain dissolved by re-census, recorded in section 7.
- **Refuter:** a settled risk figure reported with no ordering and no across-what, or a demonstration that `state -> structure -> state` is not legal for communities and generations.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument" (Seat Map, failure genera). The shape claim in section 0 and in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is refused if any of the following holds:

1. **Per-slot closure without the habitat.** An adaptation closes R⁺ to zero on every directly affected slot, measured by a ground neither frame can move, while slots at second and third order are demonstrably not in contact with it. This breaks F3.
2. **Aggregate success is admitted.** The framework admits an aggregate risk index with one live positive slot as a settled success, or a present-generation convention that fixes the future reading. This breaks F1 and F7.
3. **No last room is wrong.** A one-off adaptation permanently closes the residual with no further handoff, or a chain of adaptation rooms whose agreement increases its evidence. This breaks F8.
4. **No import is needed.** A frame sees every displaced slot, including the future chain, from inside its own closure by searching harder. This breaks F6 and F9.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Note the fourth refuter is the cheapest to test and is asked first (formula step 7: SEARCHED before INWARD and OUTWARD).

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Score per slot, never aggregate.** Publish R⁺ for each affected community and each generation band alongside any program total (F1).
2. **Enumerate the habitat to second and third order before approval.** Name the actors, conditions, and relations in contact with the residual, outward until the cascade stops mattering (F3).
3. **Range each affected frame against a ground it cannot move.** Upstream gauges, downstream gauges, and a future-generation proxy are corrected separately; their agreement is never pooled (F2, F7).
4. **Run two loops, not one.** Spatial transfer and temporal transfer are unbundled, each with its own refuter (F4).
5. **Refuse before mint.** When "risk reduced" is actually "risk moved," the formula step 2 owes a refusal (¬ is not a bond). Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." What is owed here is the fourth: the refusal that the claim was settled.
6. **Send the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The packet leads with the shape and its refuter; links come only after a verdict is recorded.

## 7. Strains, re-census, and honest stop

- **Fig 11 isolation law, not tested forward.** Recorded as single-source and `specified`. An adaptation whose third-order residual is measured at zero would not refute the law by itself, but repeated such measurements would move it toward `grounded`.
- **⋛ at second order.** Not used in the verdict. The Operators table defines comparison as a threshold relation between two quantities; if a later derivation compares two settled adaptation programs, whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.
- **No ground reading.** No instrument settled any particular adaptation in this run. Status stays `specified`, candidate. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

**Stop.** The shape is checkable from outside: the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` carry verbatim lines and refuters, and `11_audit.md` re-censuses independently. What would move this to `grounded` is a per-slot measurement of an actual adaptation against an unmoving ground, not more agreement.
