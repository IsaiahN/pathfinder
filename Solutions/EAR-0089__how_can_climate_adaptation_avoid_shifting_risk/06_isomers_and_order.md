Two further questions are owed: (1) what bonds and what order compose an adaptation's risk path, and (2) are two adaptations with the same ingredients **isomers** under Fig 12, so that the same physical parts arranged differently give different risk outcomes?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What bonds carry a displaced risk?

- Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- `→` sequence: the wall is built, then the current is deflected, then the neighbor erodes. Reversing the order gives a different thing, so the bond is ordered (Operators table: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes.").
- `−` subtraction: avoided damage here is the hazard absent here, not the hazard gone from the habitat. Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- `⋛` comparison: a threshold relation between two quantities (Operators table). A safety standard compares exposure to a limit. It is a goal, not a field.
- `¬` negation: needed to refuse "risk reduced" when the residual only moved. Fig 5 amendment: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at". Negation is not a bond and is not subtraction.
- `+` conjunction: a wall and a wetland both hold at once; the bond is symmetric, so their spelling order carries nothing (Fig 12 :92-93 on k! spellings and one term under a symmetric bond).
- **Result:** an adaptation path is a molecule, not an ingredient list. A vocabulary with only ingredients "can name a state and cannot name a route to one" (Fig 12 :111).

### Q2 : Are two adaptations isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Write two responses over the same shared atom set {a barrier, a vegetated buffer, a building-code rule, an inspection duty, a downstream community, a future generation}:
  - R1: `barrier → (deflect) → downstream community` with the duty attached to the present owner only.
  - R2: `vegetated buffer + building-code rule → downstream community`, with the inspection duty carried across generations.
- Same atoms, different bonds and different duty attachment, so they are **isomers** by Fig 12 :117: the ingredient list looks similar in a budget table, and the behavior at the ground differs. The shared substructure (a maintained buffer with a duty) is a **functional group**: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two responses over the same atom set with different bonds produce identical residual vectors on every slot across the full horizon, the isomer claim fails and only the weaker functional-group claim stands. Conversely, if one response requires an atom the other does not (for example a downstream community with no vegetative buffer available), they are not isomers at all.

### Q3 : What order composes the verification loop?

- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The order is dependency order: enumerate slots, then name affordances, then define the per-slot objective, then plan, then carry methods forward.
- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- A settled per-slot measurement becomes an atom at the next level: a basin-wide risk assessment composes over settled community measurements, not over raw claims. A claim that has not been settled cannot be composed over. "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part" (Fig 12 :106).
- **Falsifier:** show a settled per-slot measurement that cannot serve as an operand for a basin assessment, or an unsettled claim that can.

### Q4 : Catalyst, cycle, and the intergenerational slot

- Fig 12 :119 "A part that survives its own use." A maintained mangrove belt or a standing inspection duty is a **catalyst**: spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing in a budget line records which one a term is.
- Fig 12 :121 "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Monitoring makes adaptation a cycle; a one-off construction is a chain and cannot close by construction.
- Fig 13 :90 "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route." The future-generation slot is the next turn of the cycle, not the end of the chain.
- **Falsifier:** show a one-off adaptation whose output does not re-enter as input and whose residual nonetheless stays at zero for all future slots. That would refute the cycle requirement in F8 of the audit.

## CONCLUSION

The risk path of an adaptation is a priced molecule: sequence for the cascade, subtraction for avoided-here, comparison for the standard, and negation for the refusal when "reduced" actually means "moved". Two responses over the same atom set with different bonds are isomers by Fig 12, with a stated falsifier. Verification is ordered by dependency, and a settled per-slot measurement becomes an atom for the basin level. The intergenerational slot is carried by a catalyst (a part that survives its use) and closed by a cycle, not by a one-off chain.

This rescues the checkable part of the question without naming a technology: the order and the isomer test need no new primitive and no import that moves the wall. Which measure to bind remains the practitioner's provenanced choice.
