## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about an adaptation that lowers measured risk in the acting community while positive residual rises in a neighboring community or a future generation. Option A: treat the falling local or aggregate risk figure as success and offer more of the same. Option B: treat the displacement as the residual to be closed, bound the sought-for shape as per-slot closure across the full habitat and the future chain against an unmoving ground, and refuse the "risk reduced" claim until a ground pays for it. What each leads to: A averages a live slot away and spends the independence of the people asked to approve it; B yields a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an open slot where a specific technology would be named.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Positive mass in slots the adapting frame did not count: a downstream community, a downwind community, a successor generation inheriting lock-in and deferred cost. Arity: several slots, indexed by community and generation, so a vector. Symmetry: not symmetric, because the same structure is benefactor to one slot and malfactor to another (Fig 11 :49-51). Scale: first order at the project, second and third order at the basin and the intergenerational chain. Fig 9 :55-57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- **Domain 1, the local adaptation frame.** Actors: planner, design firm, the measure. Currency: claimed avoided damage in the frame's own metric. Anchor: none if it ranges only against its own model; a self-scored quantity is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30). This is the actor's closure.
- **Domain 2, the habitat (neighboring communities).** Actors: upstream, downstream, and downwind communities, water, sediment, markets, migration, ecosystems. Currency: measured exposure and harm per slot. Anchor: gauges, erosion rates, loss records, outcomes that do not move when the planner revalues them. Partition of roles with Domain 1: actor versus ground.
- **Domain 3, the temporal chain (future generations).** Actors: successors, locked-in infrastructure, debt, depleted buffers, foreclosed options. Currency: options retained or foreclosed, deferred cost. Anchor: future measured states; present actors cannot see them, so a convention is owed but must not decide the outcome (Fig 10 :47). A second loop, not a harder instance of Domain 2.
- **Domain 4, verification and evaluation.** Actors: monitoring systems, auditors, affected communities, peer frames. Currency: residuals and refuters. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is one pool talking.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate slots (perception), then name what an action affords (the middle link with no instrument, Fig 3 :27), then define the per-slot objective, then plan, then carry methods forward (Fig 4 :22). Split the union before searching (Fig 9 :54); refuse before mint when the residual only moved (formula step 2).

Strain test applied: an initial reading of Fig 13 as forbidding risk movement between communities was sent back to census. `community -> network -> community` and `present -> gradient -> future` are cross-kind compositions and legal. Different objects, so no strain remains (see `01` F10).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any claim that a specific measure is the answer does not, and no measure is named. Risk must be measured per community and per generation, the adapting frame cannot be its own anchor, the habitat must be enumerated to second and third order, spatial and temporal transfer are two loops in a union, introduction is available while exclusion is not, relocation substitutes a habitat, and there is no last room, so success is a monitored handoff. The framework returns this shape, not a technology. Which measure to bind remains an open, provenanced choice, and whether any real adaptation achieves per-slot closure is unmeasured. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, four refuters, and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside reviewers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A defect in a binding is removable without touching the shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already withhold all technology names. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A published per-slot residual vector for an actual adaptation, measured against outcomes no frame can move, would settle whether risk was removed or shifted; a convention that demonstrably fixes the future reading would break the proxy rule; a terminal adaptation with zero residual for all future slots and no feedback loop would break no-last-room; a single frame that sees all displaced slots from inside its closure would break the import requirement. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
