# What kind of term is Hilbert space? Convergence, imports, and order

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether a structure is an atom or a derivative. Before that can be answered, the term "Hilbert space structure" must be typed. Three questions are owed: (1) is it an atom, a molecule, or a left-over of composition; (2) do the many reconstructions make it an isomer or a convergent derivation; (3) what is the order of the derivation if a substrate is imported?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is Hilbert space an atom, a molecule, or a left-over?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* A formalism is not a list of axioms; the bonds (how states compose, how probability is assigned) carry the content.
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands"*. So "structure" is a bond pattern, not an ingredient.
- Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* This licenses a third kind of answer: Hilbert space may be a regularity that some composition leaves behind. It would then be neither atom nor imported atom, and the binary would be a false dichotomy.
- Fig 13 :43-44 gives the precedent: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."* A once-fundamental-looking structure was reclassified as derivative without adding a primitive.
- **Result:** three types are live: (a) atom of the current physics closure (the "fundamental" claim, level-relative only, Fig 12 :103-104); (b) molecule composed from imported substrate atoms (the "emergent" claim, gated); (c) left-over of a composition, which is what the figures most directly support at this budget. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* applies to choosing among them.

### Q2 - Are the reconstructions isomers of one another?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*
- Hardy 2001, Solèr/Gleason routes, and the Heunen-Kornell categorical axioms do **not** share the same atoms, so by the figure's own definition they are **not isomers**. They start from different primitives (information-theoretic axioms, orthomodular lattices, categorical structure). What they share is the **target substance**: a formalism equivalent to complex Hilbert space quantum theory.
- Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* That is the correct figure for them. The exemplar's chemistry precedent applies: different chains can compose functionally identical terms.
- World note: the Hilbert space Wikipedia page records that C*-algebra formulation made "no reference to an underlying Hilbert space" while preserving the spectral theorem. That is a genuine case of the same predictive substance reached over a different atom set, Fig 13 :121 again, not an isomer of the Hilbert formulation (the atoms differ).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two reconstructions are shown to use the same atoms with different bonds, the isomer claim would hold for them; if a reconstruction is shown to make a novel prediction that differs from Hilbert-space quantum theory, it is a different substance, not a convergent route. Either finding would change the classification. Neither has been supplied.

### Q3 - What order is the derivation if a substrate is imported?

- Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*.
- So Hilbert space, settled against decades of experiment, is itself a settled molecule and may serve as an atom for a higher-level composition. That is what Van Raamsdonk's entanglement-to-geometry result uses: Hilbert-space entanglement as the settled atom, spacetime connectivity as the derived molecule (opened 2026-11-23, reverse direction relative to this question).
- If a substrate is proposed for Hilbert space itself, the order is: substrate atoms (lower level) produce the Hilbert molecule, which is then settled and can serve as an atom at the next level. Fig 12 :104 licenses demotion to atom only after settlement. Fig 5 :69: *"AMENDMENT · settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."*
- Formula step 2 applies: refuse before mint. The literal claim "Hilbert space is fundamental" is refused as a level-confusion before any substrate is minted.
- **Strain kept visible:** whether a substrate atom can be "more basic" while itself being unobserved is exactly the no-anchor problem of X1 in `01`. Fig 6 :34-35 requires an instrument already returning something. `01` F7 covers this; it is not hidden here.

### Q4 - What is the bond between the current formalism and a candidate substrate?

- Fig 12 :5 and :46 say the bond carries the information the atom list does not. A substrate that lists "bits" or "relations" but does not specify the bond that produces linearity, the Born rule, tensor composition and unitarity has recorded half of itself.
- Operators table :68: *"A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."* "More basic than Hilbert space" as a bare phrase fails all seven tests. It is a claim not yet made, not a missing operator.
- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* The route from substrate to formalism is what is owed, not the name of the substrate.

## CONCLUSION

Hilbert space is a settled molecule in the physics closure and may be an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104). Whether it is *the* atom (fundamental) or a molecule over imported atoms (emergent) is not decidable from the figures: both readings require a discriminator that does not yet exist. The reconstructions are convergent routes to the same substance (Fig 13 :121), not isomers (different atoms) and not independent witnesses (Fig 2 :40). The third possibility, that Hilbert space is a left-over of a composition rather than a member (Fig 13 :124-125), is live and is the one the figures license most directly.

What this rescues: the question is not a false binary in the sense that both options are empty. It is a false binary in the sense that it suppresses a third structural type and five distinct slots. The isomer claim fails as stated; the convergence claim survives; the import claim survives only with gates; the fundamental claim survives only as level-relative irreducibility.
