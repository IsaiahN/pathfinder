## What the wider literature bears on

1. **Reachability is not ontological priority.** Hardy, Solèr and the categorical axiomatisations show the Hilbert formalism can be arrived at from several different, much simpler starting points. That bears on slot s2 (Born rule) and s3 (dimension) as a reachability result. It does not pick a substrate. The figures' rule is Fig 8 :51: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.
2. **A structure can be reformulated without its original primitive.** The Hilbert space Wikipedia page records that C*-algebras were defined so as to make "no reference to an underlying Hilbert space" while preserving the spectral theorem. That is a real instance of a formalism surviving the removal of what looked like its foundation. It is an analogue for "emergence", not a derivation of it.
3. **A candidate discriminator exists in principle.** The objective-collapse review makes concrete, testable predictions that differ from standard quantum mechanics. That is the instrument owed for slots s1 and s5. No confirmed deviation is reported in the page read; the slot stays open.
4. **Source discrimination governs the answer.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green cannot comment on the source; several sources yield one reading; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Applied here, quantum predictions read the formalism but do not by themselves disclose whether it is an atom or a derivative.
