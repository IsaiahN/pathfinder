# Expert verification packet: the Tether on Hilbert space, fundamental or emergent?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a quantum foundations researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain findings, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which substrate is real, NOT `grounded`.** No measurement at the relevant regime is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "fundamental or emergent Hilbert space?" bundles at least five structural features: linearity and superposition, inner product and Born rule, dimension, tensor product and entanglement, and unitarity. The residual must be indexed per feature (Fig 1 :19), not aggregated into one binary. "Fundamental" survives only as level-relative irreducibility, never as a certified world floor (Fig 12 :103-104). "Emergent" survives only as an import claim requiring a source gate, a shadow test, and a discriminating instrument (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :12-13); no such gated import is supplied here. The several axiomatisations of quantum theory are convergent derivations of one target formalism, hence one evidence pool, not independent witnesses (Fig 7 :13, Fig 2 :40). A third structural type is live: a left-over of composition rather than an atom or an import (Fig 13 :124-125). Which substrate (if any) is real is reported as a `gap`, with the instruments that would close it named.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the residual is per feature, and "fundamental" is level-relative, not a world verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 12 :103 *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"* (the source line continues *"and there is no bottom"*). Fig 13 :41 *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*
- **Refuter:** show that one figure line governs the whole bundle "Hilbert space structure" as one term; or derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five; or produce a seventh member that the six and their bonds cannot compose.

### C2 - mathematics-facing; checkable by a mathematical physicist against the reconstruction literature

- **Statement:** the axiomatisations (Hardy 2001, Solèr/Gleason routes, Heunen-Kornell categorical axioms) are convergent routes to the same target formalism, not independent witnesses for a substrate and not isomers of one another, because they do not share the same atoms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- **What to check:** do the cited reconstructions indeed start from different primitives but target an equivalent formalism? Do they share a substantial evidence pool? Are any of them making a novel prediction that differs from Hilbert-space quantum theory?
- **Refuter:** two reconstructions with provably disjoint evidence pools and distinct novel predictions would be two witnesses; two reconstructions with the same atoms and different bonds would be isomers. Either result changes C2.

### C3 - experiment-facing; checkable by an experimentalist against the collapse-test literature

- **Statement:** no confirmed instrument result currently discriminates a non-linear substrate from exact Hilbert linearity in the relevant regime, so the linearity and unitarity slots stay `candidate` and the substrate question stays `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Formula step 5: *"SETTLE - the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
- **What to check:** the objective-collapse review states that collapse models "make predictions that differ from standard quantum mechanical predictions" and that deviations "can potentially be detected in dedicated experiments." Is there a confirmed, independently replicated deviation? Is there a proof that none is possible in principle? (The latter would be a real underivability result and would move the `gap` to a different kind, not confirm a substrate.)
- **Refuter:** a confirmed deviation from linearity or unitarity in the regime where candidate substrates differ; or a demonstrated impossibility of any such measurement. Either fires the pre-registered refuter and narrows the verdict.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that Hilbert space is definitely emergent, and not that it is definitely fundamental in an absolute sense.
- Not that a particular substrate (qubits, entanglement, category, relational events, geometry) is the answer. Those are bindings without provenance and a shadow test.
- Not that quantum theory is wrong, or that unitarity has been observed to fail.
- Not that the entanglement-to-geometry result derives Hilbert space; it runs in the reverse direction and is an analogue only.
- Not a derivation of one physical theory from another, and no new primitive or seventh member.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a theory of everything. There is no last room.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:
