**Subject:** Check one output on quantum structure before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question:** is the Hilbert space structure of quantum theory fundamental, or does it emerge from something more basic?
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which substrate is real; not `grounded`):** the binary is not one question. "Hilbert space structure" bundles at least five things that can have different answers: linearity and superposition, the inner product and Born rule, dimension, tensor product and entanglement, and unitarity. The figures require the residual to be indexed per feature rather than aggregated. So the honest answer keeps five verdicts, not one.
>
> What survives:
> 1. "Fundamental" can only mean irreducible relative to a level, never a certified floor. The framework states that irreducible is a fact about a level, not about the world, and that there is no bottom.
> 2. "Emergent" is an import claim. Any proposed substrate (qubits, entanglement, information, category, relational events, geometry) must arrive with a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (it explains a named gap), and an instrument must return its distinct prediction. Without those it is a name where a shape is all that is held.
> 3. The many axiomatisations of quantum theory show that the formalism is reachable from several starting points. They do not show which starting point nature uses. They are convergent derivations, not independent witnesses.
> 4. A structure can also be what a composition leaves behind rather than a member or an import. The binary has no slot for that.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that Hilbert space is definitely emergent from qubits or entanglement, that a particular axiom set is the real one, or that any measurement has already fired. I am not claiming the framework settles the question. It returns a shape, not a name, and here it returns the shape any substrate answer must have plus a list of instruments that would close it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written down before this run):
> 1. one figure line shown to govern the whole bundle as one term, or one of the six substrate terms derived from the other five;
> 2. a substrate import produced with provenance, a shadow test, and an instrument returning its distinct prediction;
> 3. an instrument showing linearity and unitarity hold exactly in the regime where candidate substrates differ, together with a unique derivation of the Born rule, collapsing the five slots into one verdict.
>
> If any holds, I retract. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local, path in `10_source_ledger.csv`): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

> - Reconstruction of quantum theory from five axioms, Hardy 2001: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101012
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
