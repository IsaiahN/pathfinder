**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter survive the check in section 1. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why galaxy shapes differ, and where mergers do and do not matter

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output, stated as a shape (`general`):** galaxy morphological types are best read as different arrangements of a shared set of components (stars, gas, a dark halo, angular momentum), in the same sense that isomers share atoms but differ in bond and substance. A bar or a bulge behaves like a functional group: its presence fixes the family, and the qualifier picks the member. The channels that do the arranging have to be enumerated from the galaxy's habitat: mergers, bars and secular evolution, gas accretion, halo spin and local density. No single one of them has a fixed valence. A merger transforms a gas-rich disk one way and a gas-poor massive system another, and does very little in a medium-mass disk.
>
> **The second part, kept separate (`specified`, imported from the astronomy ground, not derived from the figures):** there is no universal "how often." The merger contribution to structure is conditional on mass, gas fraction, environment and epoch. Observationally the frequency rises with redshift; in simulations mergers dominate the morphology of the most massive galaxies, are negligible for Milky Way mass systems, and halo spin matters mostly for dwarfs. I am not offering a single percentage. If you know of one that holds across mass and epoch, that would refute the shape.
>
> **What the framework does not claim:** that it derives the Hubble sequence, that it names which component sits in which slot, or that it produces a merger rate. It returns a shape, not a name, and a frequency has to be imported with its source shown.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:** (1) a figure line from which a population frequency follows; (2) two morphological classes sharing no common component set; (3) one mechanism setting morphology uniformly at all masses and redshifts; (4) a mass- and epoch-independent merger fraction that survives a per-object check. I wrote these down before running the derivation. If one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If the shape survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Kormendy and Kennicutt 2004, secular evolution and pseudobulges: https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March10/Kormendy/Kormendy1.html
> - Conselice 2014, evolution of galaxy structure: https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March14/Conselice/Conselice6.html
> - Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2017, mergers and halo spin in shaping morphology: https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09498
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
