## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Morphology is descriptive before it is causal.** Conselice (2014) says "In many ways morphology is still a descriptive science" and notes the Hubble sequence "does not 'work' at high redshifts where most galaxies cannot be classified into a single Hubble type." This is the world counterpart of Fig 9 :27-28, "describe it before you go looking." Cite it when explaining why the shape claim comes before any mechanism claim.

2. **The merger answer is conditional, not universal.** Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004) put mergers and secular evolution side by side and call for their "relative importance ... as a function of galaxy type and luminosity." Dressler (1980) supplies the environmental axis. Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2017) supplies the mass axis and shows the trend breaks below about 10^11 solar masses. Together they justify refusing a single percentage and reporting a function instead (Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average").

3. **The two Illustris papers are one pool.** Both use Illustris merger trees and share authors. Their agreement is not independent corroboration (Fig 2 :40). The independent comparison is between the observational frame (Conselice, Dressler, Kormendy and Kennicutt) and the simulation frame (Illustris), each ranged against the ground.

4. **Merger indicators are lossy.** Conselice (2014) reports that structural selection finds roughly half of real mergers because "galaxies involved in the merger process are only quantitatively asymmetric for about a third of the lifetime of the merger." This is the round trip gap of Fig 4 :30, measured by the literature itself.

5. **Cognition corroborates the classification shape.** Rosch and Mervis (1975) show categories are organised by family resemblance rather than necessary and sufficient features. That is a low-overlap analogue of Fig 12's functional group: the group says what kind, the qualifier says which member. Use it to explain why "spiral" is a family, not a definition.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source," and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method**, which is permitted: Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it and labels the mode of what they get.
