# Expert verification packet: galaxy morphology and the merger question

**What this is:** a claim in a form a galaxy morphologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the world-sourced mechanism and frequency claims, `gap` at the figures for the numeric answer. No measurement in this packet is claimed to settle the question.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Galaxy morphological types are different arrangements of a shared component set (stars, gas, a dark halo, angular momentum), related as isomers or functional groups rather than as unrelated kinds; a bar or bulge fixes the family and the qualifier picks the member. The channels that arrange them must be enumerated from the habitat: mergers, secular evolution, gas accretion, halo spin and environment. No channel has a fixed valence. Mergers dominate the morphology of the most massive galaxies, are negligible around Milky Way mass, and give way to halo spin in dwarfs. Consequently "how often mergers set structure" has no universal answer outside a stated mass, gas fraction, environment and epoch. The figures supply the shape and then stop; the frequency is an import from the astronomy ground with its source shown. Falsifiers were written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** morphological types stand as isomers or functional groups over a shared atom set; the difference is in the arrangement, not the ingredients.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Also Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." And Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- **Refuter:** show two types that require different atoms, or show the taxonomy is a clean partition with no graded family resemblance. The cognitive analogue (Rosch and Mervis 1975) would fail with it.

### C2 - habitat and valence; checkable against the morphology literature

- **Statement:** the arranging channels are enumerated from the world, and each actor's effect is relative to the residual rather than fixed. Concretely, mergers dominate above roughly 10^11 solar masses, are not significant near Milky Way mass, and halo spin matters mostly in dwarfs; environment enters across the full density range.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** is the mass dependence as stated? Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004) put mergers beside secular evolution and call for their relative importance by type and luminosity. Dressler (1980) gives the morphology-density relation. Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2017) report that below about 10^11 solar masses "mergers do not seem to play any significant role in determining the morphology," while above it "mergers play a dominant role in shaping the morphology of massive galaxies." Are those three consistent with the valence claim, or does one of them break it?
- **Refuter:** a single mechanism that sets morphology uniformly at all masses and redshifts, or a closed channel list derived by composition that excludes environment or secular evolution.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation of the gap

- **Statement:** no frequency is derivable from the figures; "how often" is an import. The aggregates reported in the literature are conditional, and a single number loses the per-object signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** Conselice (2014) defines the merger fraction within mass and redshift bins and reports that it rises with redshift; Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2016) find accreted fractions from about 10 per cent at Milky Way mass to over 80 per cent at the top of the range. Is any single percentage defensible across those bins? If yes, C3 fails.
- **Refuter:** a figure line from which a population frequency follows, or a mass- and epoch-independent fraction that survives a per-object check.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a derivation of the Hubble sequence, and not a name for which component occupies which slot.
- Not a universal merger percentage and not a claim that mergers are unimportant.
- Not a last classification. The Hubble sequence is a room, not the last room (Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room").
- Not `grounded`. No experiment or observation in this packet settles the shape claim; the cited papers are world sources, ranged against their own ground.
- Not an independent corroboration between the two Illustris papers. They share one simulation and one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A source-discriminating instrument: kinematic and merger-tree evidence that separates merger-built spheroids from secularly built pseudobulges per object. Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004) list the discriminators (ordered versus random motion, exponential profiles, nuclear bars, star formation).
2. A frequency measured in declared mass and redshift bins with declared indicators, compared against an independent simulation rather than pooled with one.
3. Any of the pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :94, :117, :124 and Fig 11 :43, :49, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: is the mass dependence (dominant above roughly 10^11, negligible near Milky Way mass, halo spin in dwarfs) right, or is there a clean counterexample?
3. State whether a universal merger fraction is defensible in your field. If it is, C3 fails.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate merger-built from secularly built structure per object, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
