## 2. The shape

1. **Morphological types are arrangements, not unrelated kinds.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Types share the same gross atoms (stars, gas, a halo, angular momentum) and differ in arrangement. A bar or bulge is a functional group: "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member" (Fig 12 :124). This is `general`, and it is triangulated across Fig 12 and the cognitive-science family-resemblance result.

2. **The framework does not name which component sits in which slot.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The Hubble roster is an import from astronomy, not a figure derivation.

3. **The arranging channels are enumerated from the habitat.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." List mergers, secular evolution (bars), gas accretion, halo spin and environment, then read their valence relative to the residual. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A merger is transformative for a gas-rich massive system, negligible for a Milky Way mass disk, and subordinate to halo spin in dwarfs. That is `specified` from the world frame, corroborated across observational and simulation sources but with the two Illustris papers counted as one pool.

4. **"How often" is a ground reading, a `gap` at the figures.** Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." The figures cannot generate a frequency. The number enters by OUTWARD import with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23). Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence. The imported answer is conditional: frequency rises with redshift (Conselice 2014), mergers dominate massive galaxies and are negligible around Milky Way mass (Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2017), and accreted stellar fractions run "from about 10 per cent for Milky Way-sized galaxies to over 80 per cent" at the top of the mass range (Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2016). There is no universal percentage.

5. **The aggregate hides the per-object signal.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Report the merger contribution as a function of mass, gas fraction, environment and epoch, or state the bin the number belongs to.

6. **The condensation is lossy.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The gap from a full morphology to a binary "merger set it" flag contains gas fraction, mass ratio, epoch, the secular channel and environment. It is measured qualitatively, never set to zero.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a derivation of the Hubble sequence, and not a name for which component occupies which slot.
- Not a universal merger fraction, and not a claim that a single mechanism sets morphology at all masses.
- Not "mergers do not matter." They dominate the most massive systems; the claim is that their valence is conditional.
- Not a last classification. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room"; the Hubble sequence is a room, not the last room.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles the claim; the figures explicitly return a shape and a gap.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," and the colour case was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

- **Source discrimination:** kinematic classifications and merger trees that separate merger-built spheroids from secularly built pseudobulges, applied per object rather than averaged. Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004) already set the discriminating criteria (rotation versus random motion, exponential profiles, nuclear bars and star formation).
- **Frequency:** a merger fraction measured in declared mass and redshift bins with declared structural indicators, compared against an independent simulation rather than pooled with one.
- **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** a figure line yielding a frequency; two types with no shared component set; one mechanism uniform across mass and epoch; a mass-independent fraction surviving the per-object check.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not derive the Hubble sequence and do not produce a merger rate. They say that galaxy morphological types are different arrangements of a shared component set, in the sense of isomers or functional groups: a bar or a bulge fixes the family, and the qualifier picks the member. They also say that the channels doing the arranging have to be enumerated from a galaxy's habitat, and that no channel has a fixed valence. A merger dominates the morphology of the most massive galaxies, does little around Milky Way mass, and gives way to halo spin in dwarfs. So "how often mergers set structure" has no single answer outside a stated mass, gas content, environment and epoch. The figures return a shape and then stop; the frequency is an import from astronomy, with its source shown. What is offered is that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The question stays open until the ground settles it.
