# Supporting derivation: isomers, order and analogues in galaxy morphology

**Mode: `general` for the structural reading, `specified` for the world-sourced analogue check. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).**

## RESIDUAL

The record needs a second derivation beyond the main audit: are morphological types genuinely isomers over a shared atom set, and what is the dependency order of the three domains so that the "how often" question is not answered before the "why shapes" question? Two questions are owed:

1. Under Fig 12's definition, do elliptical, lenticular and spiral galaxies count as isomers, or only as members of a functional group?
2. What is the order of classification, mechanism and frequency, and where does the habitat enumeration stop?

Atoms held in the astronomy frame: stars, gas, a dark matter halo, angular momentum, and the structural features built from them (bulge, disk, bar, spiral arms, rings). Bonds available: the figure's seven typed relations, of which arrangement and sequence matter most here. No new primitive is available without import.

Domains as in the main audit: D1 classification (currency: description length, anchor: the image), D2 habitat (currency: causal contribution, anchor: simulation and observation), D3 frequency (currency: counts, anchor: the measured rate). Order by dependency: D1 before D2 before D3.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the types isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the types are normally written (E, S0, Sa, Sb, Sc, Irr), they share the same gross atoms: stars, gas in varying proportion, a halo, angular momentum in varying amount. What differs is the arrangement: spheroid-dominated, disk plus spheroid, disk with a bar, flocculent spiral. Under Fig 12 that is an **isomer** relation over the shared atom set. The difference is "invisible in the formula" (the mass budget does not uniquely name the type) and "decisive in the behaviour" (star formation history, kinematics and merger response differ).
- The weaker reading is also available and safer: a bar or a bulge is a **functional group**. Its presence fixes the family, and the qualifier (SB(r)b, for example) says which member. Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004) effectively use this reading when they discuss how "every letter in the above classification has a clear-cut meaning in terms of formation physics."
- World corroboration from a low-overlap field: Rosch and Mervis (1975) found that members of a category share overlapping attributes rather than one defining feature, and concluded that "family resemblance offers an alternative to criterial features in defining categories." A morphological type is therefore a family, not a definition. This is triangulated across Fig 12 and cognition.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two types require different atoms (for example if an elliptical requires a component a pure disk cannot contain), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. Also falsified if the taxonomy is a clean partition with no graded membership, which would break the family-resemblance analogue.

### Q2 - What is the order of the three domains?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Classification (D1) must precede mechanism attribution (D2), otherwise every morphological type will seem to fit whichever mechanism we picked first.
- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Per-object classification and mechanism must precede the population frequency (D3).
- Order therefore: D1 (describe) -> D2 (enumerate the habitat and read valence) -> D3 (count). An answer to "how often" given before D1 and D2 is a reading below the break, which Fig 3 :22-23 calls "a reading of nothing."
- **Falsifier:** show that a merger frequency can be defined and measured without first specifying the morphological classes being counted. Conselice (2014) refutes this in practice: his merger fraction is defined within a mass and redshift bin using structural indicators that are themselves morphological measurements. The frequency depends on the classification.

### Q3 - Where does the habitat enumeration stop?

- Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
- Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
- For a galaxy's morphology, first-order actors: the merger history, bars and secular evolution, gas accretion and star formation, halo spin. Second-order: the local density environment (Dressler 1980), the hot intracluster medium, the cosmic web supply of gas. The cascade stops when a further actor no longer changes the morphological residual. Dressler's result that the morphology-density relation holds "over four orders of magnitude" gives an empirical stopping shape: environment is in contact all the way from the field to cluster cores, so it does not drop out.
- Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Mergers are not "good" or "bad" for morphology in general. Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2017) show them dominant above about 10^11 solar masses and "not ... significant" below it, with halo spin taking over at low mass. That is valence relative to a mass-dependent residual.
- Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." We may add an actor to the habitat list (for example a newly measured gas-accretion channel); we may not subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. This is why the channel list grows rather than being pruned to a favourite mechanism.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a closed, finite channel list derived by composition from the figures that excludes environment or secular evolution. None exists; the figure rule requires enumeration from the world.

### Q4 - What about the continuum of types?

- The six-term substrate's alternation (Fig 13 :28: "They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from") was tested against the apparent continuity of the Hubble sequence and found strained. Re-census: the alternation governs state/structure composition in physics, not the ordering of a taxonomy. Retracted as applied to morphology, recorded in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5b.
- The continuity of types is instead handled by the family-resemblance analogue in Q1 and by the round trip gap in F7 of the main audit: collapsing a continuum into discrete bins is a lossy abstraction, and Fig 4 :30 requires the gap to be measured, not assumed zero.

## CONCLUSION

The types read as **isomers over a shared atom set**, with bars and bulges acting as **functional groups** that fix the family. The analogue is corroborated from cognitive science rather than asserted from astronomy alone. The dependency order is classification, then habitat enumeration, then frequency; answering the third first is a reading below the break. The habitat is enumerated, never composed, and an actor's valence is relative to a mass- and environment-dependent residual, which is exactly what the opened astronomy sources report. Every sub-claim carries a falsifier.
