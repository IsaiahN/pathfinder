## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question is not one question. It is a union of two conditions (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Restated in effect terms:

- **R1, the structural residual (why different shapes).** There is a gap between the observed diversity of galaxy morphologies (elliptical, lenticular, spiral, barred, irregular and the quantitative structural indices) and any account that says why the diversity has the particular form it has. What is at stake: whether morphology is a classification over a shared component set (an arrangement question) or a set of unrelated kinds.
- **R2, the quantitative residual (how often mergers set structure).** There is a gap between "we can recognise a disturbed or merger-driven morphology" and "we can state a frequency with which mergers, rather than other channels, set structure." What is at stake: whether a single number is even well defined, or whether the frequency is conditional on mass, gas fraction, environment and redshift.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a shape claim of the form "morphological types are different arrangements of a shared set of components, and the channels that arrange them are enumerated from the habitat," plus a separate, imported, conditional frequency claim of the form "the merger contribution to structure is a function of mass and epoch, not a universal constant." Binding the sought-for shape in advance makes it refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: two conditions, R1 and R2, so a union compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). Symmetry: morphology classes are arrangement-dependent, an S0 and an Sc are not interchangeable under exchange of components, so the bond carrying the difference is ordered or at least not a free symmetric join (Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance"). Scale: one object slot per galaxy, with population statistics at a second scale. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 morphology / classification | morphological types; bulge, disk, bar, arms, rings; classifiers and structural indices | descriptive class membership and description length | images, the sky, the measured light distribution | partition or family-resemblance classification |
| D2 mechanism / habitat | mergers (major, minor, dry, wet), bars and secular evolution, gas accretion, halo spin, environment | causal contribution to a galaxy's structure | simulations and observations | enumeration of a habitat (Fig 11) |
| D3 measurement / frequency | merger fraction, merger rate, ex situ stellar mass fraction | counts and rates per redshift and mass bin | imaging, kinematics, merger trees | ground reading, imported into the figures |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. All three have one: D1 the image, D2 the simulation or observation, D3 the counted or measured rate. D1 and D2 overlap where a classifier infers a mechanism, so that overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a merged domain. D3 draws on both D1 (structural merger indicators) and D2 (merger trees), so it is downstream of both.

**Partition or union.** The overall residual is a union (R1 and R2). Inside D1 the classes form a graded, overlapping family rather than a clean partition, which is the point F1 develops.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Classify morphology before attributing mechanism. Settle per-object readings before aggregating to a fraction (Fig 1 :19, Fig 3 :20). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Morphological types are arrangements over a shared component set (D1, triangulated, `general`)

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

A spiral, a lenticular and an elliptical are built from the same gross ingredients (stars, gas, a dark halo, angular momentum) arranged differently: a rotating disk plus spiral arms, a disk plus a large spheroid, a spheroid dominated system. Under Fig 12 that is an isomer or functional-group relation, not a list of unrelated kinds. A bar or a bulge acts as a functional group: its presence fixes the morphological family, and the qualifier picks the member.

**World corroboration, low overlap with the figures.** Rosch and Mervis (1975, Cognitive Psychology) found that category members share overlapping attributes rather than a single defining feature: "family resemblance offers an alternative to criterial features in defining categories." Conselice (2014) writes, "In many ways morphology is still a descriptive science." Both predict that the Hubble sequence behaves as a graded family of arrangements, exactly as Fig 12's functional-group reading predicts. The cognition source is from a different field and evidence pool, so this is genuine low-overlap corroboration.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural claim that types are arrangements. The specific roster of types is an import from astronomy, not a figure derivation.

**Refuter:** show that two morphological classes share no common component set (for example that an elliptical requires matter a pure disk cannot contain), or that the classification is a true partition with no graded family resemblance. Either would break the isomer or functional-group reading.

### F2 - The figures return a shape, not a name for the type (D1, single-source + canonical rule, `general`)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

The canonical figures README states the framework returns "a shape, not a name." So the figures can say that a structure sits in the slot and that arrangements differ, but they cannot select which component occupies which slot. Naming "the spiral arm is a gradient" or "the bulge is a spectrum" would be a binding from the physics frame onto a held atom, provenanced as such, not a derivation.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source for the figure limit, corroborated by the canonical README, which shares the author and the evidence pool and therefore carries little independent weight (Fig 2 :40).

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that derives a specific morphological component from the six-term set alone. None was found in the census.

### F3 - The habitat of morphology must be enumerated, and an actor's valence is relative to the residual (D2, figure rule single-source, world mass-dependence corroborated, `specified`)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

So we do not compose a story in which "merger causes spheroid" as a fixed rule. We enumerate what is in contact with a galaxy's morphology: the merger history, bars and secular evolution, gas accretion and star formation, halo spin, and the local density environment. Then we read the valence of each relative to the particular residual. A merger's morphological effect depends on gas fraction and mass ratio; the same actor is transformative in one context and negligible in another, which is exactly Fig 11 :49.

**World corroboration, from opened sources.** Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004): "we need a better understanding of the relative importance of mergers and secular evolution as a function of galaxy type and luminosity," and "Rapid processes that happen in discrete events are giving way to slow, ongoing processes." Dressler (NED essay on environment, based on Dressler 1980): "Over four orders of magnitude of increasing local density, the fraction of spirals decreases steadily as the fraction of SOs and ellipticals increases." Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2017): "mergers do not seem to play any significant role in determining the morphology" below about 10^11 solar masses, while "mergers play a dominant role in shaping the morphology of massive galaxies." These three predict exactly the mass- and environment-dependent valence Fig 11 requires.

**Mode:** `general` for the enumeration rule from the figure; `specified` for the world-sourced mass and environment dependence. The figure rule is single-source; the world dependence is corroborated across observational reviews, an environment study and a simulation study, though the two Illustris papers share one simulation (see F6).

**Refuter:** show a single mechanism that sets morphology uniformly at all masses and redshifts, or show that the channel list can be derived by composition from the figure set rather than read off the world.

### F4 - "How often" is a ground reading, a gap at the figures (D3, `gap` at figures, `specified` world-sourced)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

No figure line yields a frequency. The figures' closure is idempotent, so no amount of searching these thirteen figures will produce a merger fraction; the number is an import from the astronomy frame, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 and Fig 8). "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." The honest verdict at the figures is `gap` for the numeric answer. The world frame supplies it: Conselice (2014) defines the basic merger fraction and reports that "the galaxy merger fraction increases with redshift at all stellar mass and luminosity selections," and that "up to half of the stellar mass in modern massive galaxies were formed in mergers between 1 < z < 3." Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2016) report that accreted stars range "from about 10 per cent for Milky Way-sized galaxies to over 80 per cent" at the top of the mass range, while "the stellar mass growth of galaxies below a few times 10^11 solar masses is dominated by in situ star formation at all redshifts."

**Why SEARCHED first, then OUTWARD (Formula step 7).** SEARCHED: is a frequency hidden in the figure set and unfound? The census says no, and closure is idempotent. INWARD: sharpen our instruments? There is no figure instrument that returns a frequency, so INWARD has nothing to extend (Fig 6 :34). OUTWARD: import the astronomy measurement with provenance. That is the correct route, and it is where the number legitimately comes from.

**Mode:** `specified`, world-sourced, not `grounded` by the figures. **Refuter:** exhibit a figure line from which a population frequency follows, or show that the imported number violates its source gate (no traceable measurement or simulation behind it).

### F5 - The aggregate "how often" hides the per-object signal (cross-domain, triangulated, `general`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The second half of the question asks for an aggregate. The figures warn that aggregating across object slots discards the signal that says which galaxies are affected. The world sources agree that the frequency is conditional: Conselice defines "the basic merger fraction" as a count in a redshift and mass bin divided by the total in that bin, and Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2017) show the effect changes sign with mass. So "how often" does not have one answer; it has a function. Report the function, or state the mass and epoch of the number.

**Mode:** `general` for the warning, `specified` for the world's conditionality. **Refuter:** produce a mass- and epoch-independent frequency that survives the per-object check.

### F6 - Two Illustris papers are one evidence pool (verification, single-source caution, `general`)

Fig 2 :40 (leading clause included): "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2016) and Rodriguez-Gomez et al. (2017) are different questions and different papers, but both draw on the same Illustris simulation, the same merger trees and much the same author list. Their agreement is one pool talking, not two independent witnesses. Dressler (1980, cluster galaxy sample), Conselice (2014, Hubble Space Telescope structural surveys) and Kormendy and Kennicutt (2004, a literature review of nearby galaxies) are a different, mostly observational pool. Triangulation requires that the observational and simulation frames be kept separate and each ranged against the ground.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** show that the two Illustris analyses use independent simulations or independent merger-finding algorithms with no shared subgrid physics.

### F7 - Condensing morphology to a merger flag loses information (cross-domain, triangulated, `general`)

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

The round trip from a rich morphological description (bulge, disk, bar, arms, rings, kinematics, gas fraction, environment) down to a binary "merger set this galaxy, yes or no," and back up to a physical claim, is lossy. The gap R_T contains at least: gas fraction (wet versus dry), mass ratio (major versus minor), epoch, the secular channel, and environment. Conselice (2014) notes that structural indices such as asymmetry "correlate with ongoing merging activity" but that "galaxies involved in the merger process are only quantitatively asymmetric for about a third of the lifetime of the merger." That is a measured instance of the lost information. Report the gap qualitatively; do not set it to zero by treating the binary flag as equivalent to the morphology.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** show a condensation whose round trip preserves gas fraction, mass ratio, epoch and the secular channel.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device," the shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is found that directly governs population frequencies. The `gap` verdict in F4 is then withdrawn and the number re-derived from the figures.
2. Two morphological classes are shown to share no common component set, or the classification is shown to be a clean partition with no graded family resemblance. F1 fails.
3. A single mechanism is shown to set morphology uniformly across mass, environment and redshift. The habitat-enumeration and valence-relative claim in F3 fails.
4. An instrument or classifier is shown to resolve a galaxy's morphology and its causal history without contact with imaging, kinematics or simulation. The Fig 11 capability claim fails.
5. A mass- and epoch-independent merger fraction is produced that survives the per-object check in F5.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Route when no line closes the residual (SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD)

- **SEARCHED:** the figure set was censused for a frequency line. None found. Closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32), so more searching inside the figures adds nothing.
- **INWARD:** there is no figure instrument returning a frequency to sharpen (Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from"). INWARD has no purchase here.
- **OUTWARD:** import the astronomy measurement. Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." The description travels in effect terms (Fig 6 :30), with a source gate and a shadow test. This is how the number in F4 enters, and it enters with provenance, not as a figure result.

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and were never read against one another. Underivable in principle (no frequency in the figure closure) and unreachable at this budget (a frequency in the closure but unfound) are different facts; this is the former, so importing is the correct instruction, not an added atom.

## 9. Verdict of this audit

The figures give a clean shape for the first half and an honest gap for the second. Morphological diversity reads as different arrangements of a shared component set (isomers and functional groups), with the channels enumerated from a habitat whose actors have valence relative to the residual. The figures do not name which component occupies which slot, and they do not yield a frequency. "How often" is answered only by importing the astronomy ground, and the imported answer is conditional on mass, gas content, environment and epoch. Strains after re-census: one named and resolved (Fig 13 versus classification), one live and priced (the round trip gap R_T). Nothing is `grounded` by the figures.
