# The Tether's answer: crossed thresholds and restoration

**Mode: `specified` for the shape, `general` for the framework rules. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No ecosystem was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

An ecosystem has crossed a threshold. We ask whether removing the driver, or intervening, returns the target state and function. The gap R is per slot: the predicted return against the observed return, indexed by object, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"). A catchment average can be near zero while one reach has crossed. The question is whether that gap can be driven to zero or persists and does not trend down.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question is a union, not a partition.** "Restorable or irreversible" bundles overlapping conditions: driver still present, feedback loop holding the new state, absent actors or propagules, changed abiotic conditions, cascades three steps out. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54). A single lever clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing.

2. **The anchor is the measured return, not the habitat's appearance and not agreement.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." The habitat changes during restoration; the ground does not.

3. **Return is non-invertible and must be measured.** Fig 4 :28: "the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." A return to the same coarse reading does not name the source that produced it. The round-trip gap R_T is the honest report of what was given up.

4. **Restoration is an import, and the seat's power is asymmetric.** Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 6 :14 names import as "the only operator that moves the wall"; Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom." So searching harder inside the crossed state cannot recover what it lacks. What can change the reach is an actor, condition, or frame imported with provenance and a shadow test, and with its cascade enumerated (Fig 11 :65). Because exclusion is not available, Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

5. **A crossed state is held by a loop whose sign decides whether it closes.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A chain-shaped restoration plan cannot close a feedback-sustained state by construction.

6. **Same species list is not the same system.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 11 :49-50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Restoring the list is not restoring the substance. Where only the functional group matters, Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

7. **The framework names no specific restorable ecosystem.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The particular actor and threshold value are imports from ecology, settled by the ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular ecosystem is restorable or irretrievable. That is a ground question.
- Not that current restoration ecology is wrong, or that regime-shift theory is wrong. The shape is offered as a constraint on any answer, not as a rival answer.
- Not that "irreversible" is an absolute property of the world. It is a reachability verdict under what is held: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134), and "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that habitat recovery proves identity. Indiscernibility at an instrument is not identity.
- Not that adding an actor is harmless. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated (Fig 11 :65), and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts" (Fig 11 :69).
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an ecosystem measurement.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A measurement and a source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The checks are:

1. **Bundle check:** are the restoring conditions for the case disjoint, with empty intersections? If yes, the union finding fails for that case.
2. **Threshold / return check:** do the measured crossing point and return point coincide at the instrument's resolution, with no history dependence? If yes, the ordered-threshold reading fails for that case.
3. **Round-trip check:** does a return to the prior reading also return the sources that produced it, with a measured gap of zero at that resolution? If yes, the non-invertibility finding fails there.
4. **Import check:** can a restoration be shown that expands the reachable set without importing any actor, condition, or external frame, or an introduced actor be subtracted cleanly rather than superseded? If yes, the asymmetry finding fails.
5. **Loop check:** is the crossed state maintained by a chain with no re-entrant loop? If yes, the cycle finding does not govern that case.
6. **Anchor check:** can any measured outcome be named that settles "restored" and does not update on what a frame thinks? If not, every verdict on that case stays `candidate`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached a shape, not an answer.** The particular ecosystem is `gap` from the figures alone. The framework can say what must be unbundled, what the anchor is, what an import must carry, and what would reverse the verdict. It cannot say which actor is missing (Fig 13 :135), and "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). The record stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." (`FIGURES_README_canonical.md`).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether any particular ecosystem can be restored. It says that "restorable or irreversible" is a bundle of overlapping conditions, not a binary, so the first job is to unbundle it. It says that the anchor is the measured return of the target state and function, not the habitat's current appearance and not agreement among ecologists, and that a return to the same reading must have its round-trip gap measured because the trip is not reversible. It says restoration is an import: you can add an actor, you cannot subtract the habitat, so a managed system is permanent work rather than a solved state, and an introduced actor with an unenumerated cascade is a hazard, not a fix. It says two systems with the same species list can still be different substances because the difference lives in the relations. It refuses to name the missing actor, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The particular ecosystem stays open until the ground settles it.
