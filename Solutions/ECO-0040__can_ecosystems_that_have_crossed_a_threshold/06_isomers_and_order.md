# Isomers, order and the threshold: a supporting derivation for ECO-0040

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether a crossed ecosystem can be restored. Three sub-questions are owed and must be kept separate:

1. If two systems have the same species list, are they the same system? (structure)
2. Does the order of intervention change the result? (order)
3. Is the crossing point the same as the return point? (threshold)

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available: the habitat's actors, conditions and relations, enumerated from the world (Fig 11 :43-48). Bonds available: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: contact and its cascades. Anchor: the measured outcome, which does not update on what a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29, Fig 11 :73).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Threshold: the crossing is ordered, not symmetric

The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities", with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." It is one of the four ordered bonds ("swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes.").

So a threshold crossing is a comparison, not a symmetric join. A driver passing a value in one direction is not the reverse operation of the driver returning. This is exactly the world-source claim from the Regime shift page (2026-11-23): "the point at which the system flips from one regime to another is different from the point at which the system flips back."

- **Consequence:** "remove the driver" is not the inverse of "the driver crossed the threshold." The return path is owed its own composition and its own ground.
- **Figure:** Fig 9 :40-41 "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up / so keep every cut ranked and reversible." A cut across the threshold must be kept reversible in the record even when the system is not.
- **Falsifier Q1:** a system for which the measured crossing point and return point coincide at the instrument's resolution, with no history dependence. The ordered-bond reading would then govern a different quantity and must be re-censused for that case.

### Q2 — Structure: same species list, different substance

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 11 :49-50: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Mode `general` for the bond rule, `specified` for the ecological reading. A list of species is an ingredient list. Two lakes with the same fish, the same plants and the same nutrients can differ entirely in which relations hold: grazing, predation, shading, nutrient recycling. Under Fig 12 they are isomers, not variants of one thing. The difference is "invisible in the formula" (a species list) and "decisive in the behaviour" (clear versus turbid).

- **Consequence for restoration:** restoring the list is not restoring the substance. The bond must be re-established and settled against the ground, which is why an introduced actor must come with a cascade that was enumerated (Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering").
- **Falsifier Q2:** two systems with the same actors and demonstrably different bond types behave identically on the measured residual at the relevant resolution. Then the isomer claim fails for that system; the list was enough and the bond carried nothing.

### Q3 — Functional group: the qualifier is not the kind

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Mode `specified`. Restoration often does not require a particular species; it requires a functional group (a grazer, a pollinator, a nitrogen fixer, a top predator). The group says what kind, the qualifier says which member. This is the figure's own vocabulary, and it matches the ecology frame's functional-group concept, but the match is corroboration from a different pool, not a derivation of the ecology term from Fig 12.

- **Correction carried from `05_what_shape_means.md` §5b.** Because the needed actor is read off the habitat (Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world"), naming a member of the group is allowed as a provenanced import. What remains forbidden is claiming the framework derived the name or that the binding moves Fig 6's wall without adding an atom (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world").
- **Falsifier Q3:** a system in which replacing one member of the functional group with another changes the measured outcome, with the bond type held fixed. Then "the qualifier says which member" is false for that case and the member is load-bearing, not interchangeable.

### Q4 — Order: which comes first changes the term

The sequence bond → means "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Operators table :30-31). Fig 12 :112: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Fig 12 :112-113: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

Mode `general`. Remove-driver-then-reintroduce and reintroduce-then-remove-driver are different molecules over the same atoms. The comparison bond ⋛ is the goal; the sequence bond → is the route; subtraction − says how far along it is. A vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a crossed system contains, and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it.

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** A crossed state held by a positive feedback is a cycle, and a chain-shaped restoration plan cannot close it by construction. The plan must include a re-entrant step or it will not hold.

- **Falsifier Q4:** a case in which the order of the two interventions makes no difference to the ground reading, and no re-entrant step is required to keep the system in the target state. Then sequence and cycle do not govern that restoration and the claim is withdrawn for it.

### Q5 — Isolation is substitution, not return

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**

Mode `general`. A nursery, a test plot, or an off-site breeding programme is a substituted habitat. It proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."). When the organism or system is moved back, everything its goal required must have been reproduced, and the new habitat's unchosen actors come with it.

- **Falsifier Q5:** a substitution that reproduces everything the goal required with no unchosen actors and passes at the ground on return. Then the isolation law fails for that case.

## CONCLUSION

The threshold is an ordered comparison, so the return is a new composition and not an inverse. Two systems with the same species list can be isomers whose difference is carried entirely by the bond, so restoring the list is not restoring the substance. Functional groups let a member be named as a provenanced import without pretending the framework derived it. Order matters because a plan is a sequence and a goal is a comparison; a crossed state held by a feedback is a cycle that a chain cannot close. Isolation is substitution, not return.

None of this names a restorable ecosystem. It gives the structure, the order and the analogues that any such name must respect. The falsifiers are written before the ground reading.
