# Figure-grounded audit: can a crossed ecosystem be restored?

**Mode of this document: `specified`, not `grounded`.** It states the shape a restoration claim must have and what would have to be measured. `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."

**Status of this derivation: a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (essay, 2026-11-19): "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This document is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on. See `05_what_shape_means.md` §6.

**Sources.** `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (verbatim, local), `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`, the eight essays by Isaiah N, and the public ecology sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` (all opened 2026-11-23).

## 0. The question under audit, restated as an effect

> "Can ecosystems that have crossed a threshold be restored, or are some changes effectively irreversible?" (ecology.txt :49, section 4)

Option A: the crossed system can be returned to its prior state by relaxing the driver or by intervention. Option B: some changes are effectively irreversible, so the prior state cannot be reached again under the conditions that now hold.

Stated as a residual R: an action (remove the driver, reintroduce an actor, restore habitat) is followed by an observation (the target state and function return, or they do not). R is the gap `Γ(b,a) − o'` per slot s: the predicted return against the observed return. The question asks whether R can be driven to zero, or whether it persists and does not trend down no matter what is tried inside the available closure.

**What an answer would look like (Direction, bound as an ordinary term).** A `specified` shape with four parts: (i) which conditions must hold for the return to be reachable; (ii) which of them are union conditions that must be unbundled; (iii) what counts as the anchor that settles "restored"; (iv) what would refute the shape. This written shape is then allowed to accrue a residual like any other term (Formula "Direction": "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else").

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: the question presents one binary variable (restorable / irreversible), but the underlying residual is a transition between attractors with history dependence. Symmetry: the transition is ordered, not symmetric. Crossing from clear to turbid is not the reverse operation of crossing from turbid to clear; the reversal requires going past the point at which the change first occurred. That ordering is exactly what the comparison bond ⋛ carries: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :48-49), ordered ("swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Operators table :32-33). Scale: the driver is slow and large (climate, nutrient loading, land use); the state variables are fast and local (population densities, turbidity, canopy cover); the mechanism sits at an intermediate stratum. This is a membrane question (Fig 4) before it is a restoration question.

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The habitat, unmaintained | species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | contact and cascades | the ground: what the system actually does when nothing maintains it. Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." | partition of what composes inside the habitat; enumeration, never composition |
| D2 The intervention seat | the practitioner, restoration actions, introduced actors | introduction vs exclusion (asymmetric) | the ground: measured return of the target state and function. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." | union of conditions; "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57) |
| D3 The measurement and model frame | indicators, thresholds, early-warning statistics, models | prediction of the shift and of the return | the ground via an instrument, not via agreement. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" | dependency: perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning (Fig 3) |

**Partition or union.** The question is phrased as a partition (A or B). After the census it is a **union**: hysteresis, loss of propagules or mutualists, changed abiotic conditions, unenumerated cascades, and time lags overlap. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever (just remove the driver) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :55-56). This is finding F1.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** 1 perception: detect that a shift occurred and which slot crossed. 2 vocabulary: have terms for what the new state affords and what would count as progress. 3 objective: state what "restored" means in own terms. 4 planning: a route under incomplete information. 5 learn and carry: name what worked and carry it. "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19). A reading taken below the break (trying to plan a restoration before defining the objective) is "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :22-23).

Strain test applied before concluding: the word "threshold" in the question is an ecological comparison (a driver passing a value), and the word "wall" in Fig 6 is closure(Γ), the reach of a frame. Reading them as the same object strains both. They are different quantities. See the retraction log, R1.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — "Restorable or irreversible" is a union, not a partition (decisive for the shape)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Mode `specified`. The conditions that must be separated before asking "can it be restored": (a) the driver is still present; (b) the feedback loop that holds the new state is intact; (c) the actors or propagules needed for the old state are absent and must be imported; (d) the abiotic conditions have changed, so isolation would be a substitution rather than a return; (e) a third-order cascade is still acting (Fig 11 :52-53). A lever that only removes (a) fails wherever (b) to (e) also hold.

**Refuter:** show that for the system in question the conditions are disjoint, so that each condition alone accounts for a disjoint set of cases and the intersections are empty. Then the partition reading stands and this finding is withdrawn.

### F2 — Irreversibility has the shape of a non-invertible round trip, not an absolute property of the world

Fig 4 :28: **"the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."**

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 4 :31: **"It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**

Mode `general` (structural), instantiated as `specified` for any particular ecosystem. A system observed at a coarse level (clear water, coral cover, canopy percent) can return to a similar reading while the underlying arrangement differs. The round-trip gap R_T is "gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))" (Symbols table :75-77). Ecology's hysteresis is this shape: the system's trajectory A -> B differs from B -> A, and a reading of the endpoint does not name the path that produced it.

This is also a source-discrimination limit. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, 2026-08-28): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." A reading of "coral-dominated" or "algae-dominated" cannot by itself say which sources maintain it.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that discriminates the sources behind the same endpoint reading at the relevant scale, and show a return whose round-trip gap is zero at that instrument's resolution. Then "effectively irreversible" is refuted for that case at that resolution. Note the honest limit: indiscernibility at an instrument is not identity, and Fig 8 :39 says "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

### F3 — Restoration is an import, and the seat's power is asymmetric

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (import).

Fig 8 :32: **"no search, however long, adds an atom."**

Mode `general` for the asymmetry (a framework rule), `specified` for its ecological reading. In the figure's terms, the crossed state holds a closure the prior state lacks; searching harder inside that closure cannot recover the absent atom. What can change the reach is an import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8). Concretely: reintroduce a grazer, a mutualist, a seed source, a fire regime, a hydrological connection. The asymmetry matters: you can add the actor, but you cannot subtract the habitat you are in, and you cannot recall what you introduced if its cascade was unenumerated.

Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering"** (the figure cites Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994, and adds "Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated").

Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

**Refuter:** demonstrate an operator other than import that expands the reachable set for a crossed ecosystem (that is, a restoration accomplished purely by rearranging what is already held, with no added actor, condition, or external frame), and show it settles against the ground. Or show exclusion is available in a real restoration: that an introduced actor or changed condition can be subtracted cleanly rather than merely superseded.

### F4 — A crossed state is held by a feedback loop whose sign decides whether it closes

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

Mode `general` for the cycle structure, `specified` for its ecological role. Turbidity that shades out benthic plants, which otherwise stabilised sediment and slowed nutrient recycling, is a positive feedback that re-enters as input and closes the loop. Once the loop is settled it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it, which is why a later restoration works on a new substrate rather than on the old one. This is the mechanism behind the world-source reports of hysteresis (Wikipedia "Regime shift", 2026-11-23: "Regime shifts are hard to reverse and in some cases irreversible").

**Refuter:** show a persistent crossed state maintained by a chain with no re-entrant loop, or a loop whose sign cannot be identified even in principle at the measurement scale. Then the cycle finding does not govern that case.

### F5 — Describe the residual before choosing the restoration frame

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Mode `specified`. Restoring "the ecosystem" is not a well-formed objective until the slot that crossed is named. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A catchment-level average can look stable while one reach has crossed.

**Refuter:** provide a case in which the residual is genuinely aggregate and no per-slot description is available even in principle, and in which a frame chosen without a described residual still yields a restorer with a checkable ground. Until then, frame-first restoration is apophenia, not derivation.

### F6 — The response is discipline-shaped, and contact is the currency

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Mode `general`. A restoration that improves the practitioner in isolation (better models, better funding, more prestige) does not change contact and therefore "changes nothing" in the figure's sense. What changes contact is enumerating the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering. The DDT line is the figure's own example of a third-order residual that first-order enumeration misses.

**Refuter:** produce an agent-only improvement that demonstrably moves the ground (the figure itself says this would refute the capability claim, Fig 11 :71-72). Until one is shown, the currency of restoration is contact, not competence in the abstract.

### F7 — The anchor is the measured outcome; agreement is disqualified

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

Mode `general`. For this question the ground is not the habitat (which changes during restoration) and not the consensus of restoration ecologists. It is the measured return of the target state and function, an outcome that does not move when a frame changes its mind. This is a genuine strain risk and is handled by re-census, not by forcing a line: the habitat updates, the ground does not. See R2 in the retraction log.

**Refuter:** identify what in this question actually plays the anchor role if not a measured outcome, and show that it does not update on what any frame thinks. If no such thing can be named, the question is not yet ground-anchored and every verdict on it is `candidate` only.

### F8 — A link with no instrument is not a link that works

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Mode `specified`. The measurement frame (D3) sits at the perception and objective links. Early-warning indicators are instruments for "is a shift approaching" and for "has the return occurred"; they are not the objective itself. If the objective ("what counts as restored") has no instrument, planning effort lands above the break and arrives at a step that cannot use it.

**Refuter:** show, for a given system, an objective with a working instrument and a recorded verdict before any plan was made, and demonstrate that the plan then settled against the ground. That is a case where this link works; it does not refute the general rule.

### F9 — A restoration recipe is not a mint, and settlement is a separate condition

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 3: "gated by a product; any factor at zero forces inertness" with SUPPORT, REACHABILITY, NOVELTY. Step 5: "SETTLE — the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."

Fig 5 :41: **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."**

Mode `specified`. For a proposed restoration term: SUPPORT requires a live slot residual (something failed to resolve); REACHABILITY requires the actor or condition be composable from what the frame holds or be imported with gates; NOVELTY requires it not be a relabelling of something already in the library; SETTLEMENT requires the ground to pay. A plan that merely restates existing ecology with new words lands in Fig 5 :29-30: "nothing new here" (line 29) and "the machinery worked; the answer was already known" (line 30).

**Refuter:** produce a restoration term that passes all three guards and is settled by the ground, with provenance recorded. That would move the term from `candidate` to accepted and would not refute this finding; it would instantiate it.

### F10 — Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."

Mode `general`. When no restoration path is reachable under what is held, the honest report is `gap` or `unreachable at this closure`, with a characterisation of what would have to be imported. It is not a claim that restoration is impossible in the world. The framework "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it" (Fig 13 :135).

**Refuter:** a derivation that names the missing primitive from inside the closure, or a search that proves absence. The figures say both are unavailable to a frame; a genuine demonstration of either would break the framework, not just this finding.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** For the system in question the restoring conditions are disjoint and the intersections empty, so a single lever restores every case. Breaks F1.
2. **Round-trip refuter.** An instrument shows that a return to the prior reading also returns the sources that produced it, with a measured round-trip gap of zero at that resolution. Breaks F2.
3. **Asymmetry refuter.** A restoration is demonstrated that expands the reachable set without importing any actor, condition, or external frame, or an introduced actor is subtracted cleanly rather than superseded. Breaks F3.
4. **Cycle refuter.** A persistent crossed state is maintained by a chain with no re-entrant loop. Breaks F4.
5. **Anchor refuter.** No measured outcome can be named that settles "restored" and does not update on what a frame thinks. Breaks F7.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn for that case. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1. Ask what conditions are actually bundled before asking whether the system can be restored.
2. State the anchor explicitly in every exchange: the measured return, not agreement and not the habitat's current appearance.
3. Treat every proposed intervention as an import with a source gate, a shadow test, and a named cascade (F3, Fig 11 :65).
4. Measure the round-trip gap rather than trusting the endpoint reading (F2, Fig 4 :30).
5. Enumerate the habitat outward past first-order contact (F6, Fig 11 :52-53).
6. End in `gap` for the particular ecosystem. The figures return a shape and the conditions of reachability, not a name and not a promise.
