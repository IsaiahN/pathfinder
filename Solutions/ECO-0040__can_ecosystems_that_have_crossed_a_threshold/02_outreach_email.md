# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the four claims below must be presented with their mode and their refuter attached. If the recipient cannot see what would fail, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about threshold crossings and restoration

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. You can ignore this note if it is not your problem.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** "Can a crossed ecosystem be restored, or is the change irreversible?" is not one well-formed question. It is a bundle of overlapping conditions: the driver may still be present; a feedback loop may now hold the new state; the actors or propagules needed for the old state may be absent; the abiotic conditions may have changed; a cascade three steps out may still be acting. A lever that clears the first condition leaves the intersections standing because the second condition still binds. So the first deliverable is an unbundling, not a verdict.
>
> The second deliverable is a shape for "restored." In this framework the anchor is the measured return of the target state and function, not the current appearance of the habitat and not agreement among ecologists. Fig 2 :29 calls an anchor "whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A return to the same coarse reading is not enough: the framework requires measuring the round-trip gap, because "the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent" (Fig 4 :28).
>
> The third deliverable is an asymmetry. Adding an actor is available; subtracting the habitat is not, "because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you" (Fig 11 :61). So a restoration is an import with provenance and a named cascade, and a managed system is "permanent work rather than a solved state" (Fig 11 :63).
>
> **What this does not claim.** It does not claim that a particular ecosystem is restorable or not. It does not claim that current restoration ecology is wrong. It does not name a missing species or intervention from first principles. It is a shape, a set of conditions of reachability, and a refuter. Whether a given system returns is settled by measurement, not by this note.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. For the system in question the restoring conditions are disjoint and the intersections empty, so one lever restores every case.
> 2. An instrument shows that a return to the prior reading also returns the sources that produced it, with a measured round-trip gap of zero at that resolution.
> 3. A restoration is demonstrated that expands the reachable set without importing any actor, condition, or external frame, or an introduced actor is subtracted cleanly rather than superseded.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape for that case. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Read Fig 11 :61, :63, :66, :73; Fig 4 :28-31; Fig 9 :54-57; Fig 2 :29, :35.
> - The figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Public ecology background I checked: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_stable_state and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1692979/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
