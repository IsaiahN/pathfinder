# Expert verification packet — the Tether on crossed thresholds and restoration

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or restoration practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the shape, `general` for the framework rules. Not `grounded`.** No ecosystem measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can a crossed ecosystem be restored, or is the change irreversible?" is not one well-formed question. It is a union of overlapping conditions, so the first deliverable is an unbundling. The anchor for "restored" is the measured return of the target state and function, not the habitat's current appearance and not agreement. A return to the same coarse reading is not enough: the round trip is non-invertible and its gap must be measured. Restoration is an import, and the seat's power is asymmetric: adding an actor is available, subtracting the habitat is not, so a managed system is permanent work rather than a solved state. A crossed state held by a positive feedback is a cycle that a chain-shaped plan cannot close, and two systems with the same species list can be different substances because the difference lives in the relations. The framework names no specific restorable ecosystem. It returns the shape, the conditions of reachability, and refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and ecology-facing: the union and the anchor

- **Statement:** the binary as posed is a union of overlapping conditions, and the thing that settles "restored" must be a measured outcome that does not update on what any frame thinks. The habitat is not that anchor, because the habitat updates.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." And Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." And Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- **What to check:** for a system you know, list the conditions that would have to hold for its prior state to return. Are they disjoint, or do they overlap so that removing one driver leaves another binding? Name the measurement that would settle "restored" and say whether it could move just because an expert changed their mind.
- **Refuter:** the conditions are disjoint with empty intersections, so one lever restores every case; or no measurement can be named that settles "restored" without updating on a frame's opinion.

### C2 — measurement-facing: the return is a round trip, not an inverse

- **Statement:** a driver crossing a threshold is an ordered comparison, so the return is a new composition. A return to the prior reading may leave the sources that produced it different; the round-trip gap must be measured, not assumed zero.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :28 "the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent." And Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." And Operators table "a threshold relation between two quantities" for ⋛.
- **What to check:** is there an instrument that discriminates the sources behind the endpoint reading at the relevant scale? For a system you know, have the crossing point and the return point been measured separately? Is the return curve the same as the forward curve run backwards?
- **Refuter:** an instrument shows that a return to the prior reading also returns its sources, with a measured gap of zero at that resolution; or the crossing and return points coincide with no history dependence.

### C3 — intervention-facing: import, asymmetry, cascade

- **Statement:** restoration expands a reach by importing what the crossed state lacks. Adding is available, subtracting the habitat is not; an introduced actor with an unenumerated cascade is a hazard, and a managed system is permanent work.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :65 "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering". Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom."
- **What to check:** in a restoration you know, what was imported (grazer, propagule, hydrological connection, fire regime, mutualist)? Was its cascade enumerated before introduction, or discovered afterwards? Was the post-restoration state maintained by continuing work, or did it persist on its own?
- **Refuter:** a restoration expands the reachable set without importing any actor, condition or external frame; or an introduced actor or changed condition is subtracted cleanly rather than superseded; or a restored state persists with no continuing maintenance and no imported atom.

(Four further refuters, on the cycle, species-list isomer, order and isolation claims, are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4. They are listed there rather than here to keep this packet to twenty minutes.)

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular ecosystem is restorable or irretrievable. That is settled by measurement.
- Not that regime-shift theory or restoration ecology is wrong. The shape is a constraint on an answer, not a rival answer.
- Not that "irreversible" is an absolute property of the world. It is a reachability verdict under what is held. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that a recovered reading proves identity. Indiscernibility at an instrument is not identity.
- Not that introductions are harmless. Fig 11 :65 names cane toads and kudzu as the same move with the cascade unenumerated.
- Not that the framework can name the missing actor. Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A source-discriminating instrument at the relevant scale that measures the round-trip gap for a return.
2. Separate measurements of the crossing and return points, with a stated resolution.
3. A documented import with its cascade enumerated before introduction and a recorded maintenance burden afterwards.
4. A derivation or demonstration that one of the three refuters in §2 holds for a named system.

Prestige and agreement are not instruments. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 2 :29 and Fig 11 :73 (three short passages). List the conditions for one system you know and say whether they overlap.
2. Read Fig 4 :28 and :30. Say whether you have seen the crossing and return curves measured separately for that system, and what instrument would discriminate the sources.
3. Read Fig 11 :61, :63 and :65. Name what was imported in a restoration you know, whether its cascade was enumerated, and whether the result needed continuing work.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
