# Expert verification packet: the Tether on biology versus culture in intelligence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a behavioural geneticist, cognitive scientist, comparative psychologist or cultural evolution researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural refusal of the partition, not `grounded`.** No measurement at the level of a per-aspect source attribution is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which aspects of human intelligence come from biology, and which from culture, education and social learning?" requests a two-column attribution. That split is a union compound, not a partition. Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so an aspect does not survive removal of either term. Biological constraints set what a substrate can represent and compose at all (the reach). Culture and education are head starts and imports that move a frame's reach without adding any new primitive to the world. Their order is dependency: perceptual and conceptual prerequisites first, "learn and carry" second, capability at contact. The per-aspect split stays open until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the two-source split is a union compound; capability is agent-and-habitat

- **Statement:** no per-aspect assignment to a single source survives removal of the other term.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39, **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**. Also Fig 9 :55, **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- **What to check:** is there a named aspect of intelligence that remains fully intact in an isolated agent, or in a habitat with no agent? If yes, the capability-at-contact claim is false for that aspect.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an aspect that survives removal of one term, or an anchor that assigns it to exactly one condition and does not move when a frame pushes on it.

### C2: culture and education are head starts and imports, not new primitives

- **Statement:** handed-down knowledge makes the next search cheaper; it does not add an atom to the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :11-12, **"a head start, never a recording"**; Fig 4 :22, **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**; Fig 6 :25, **"An import adds no atom to the world."**
- **What to check:** does a documented case of cultural transmission (tool use in chimpanzees, vocabulary, schooling) add a new primitive to the world, or does it give a new learner access to something already reachable? Is the receiving learner still required to test it in their own world?
- **Refuter:** show transmission that adds a new world primitive, or a head start that needs no further contact and no testing by the receiver.

### C3: the two sources are a functional group in dependency order, not a flat mixture

- **Statement:** germline inheritance and cultural transmission are two carriers of the same function (head-start carrier), with different atoms; prerequisites come before "learn and carry".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :124, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Fig 3 :20, **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- **What to check:** can a learner acquire vocabulary and objectives (links 2 and 3) with no perceptual link (link 1)? The claim says no; Fig 3 :19 says *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- **Refuter:** show link 5 ("learn and carry") running successfully before links 1-4, or show the two carriers are Fig 12 isomers over one shared atom set (Fig 12 :117, **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."**).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. **Removal test:** hold one term fixed and remove the other; does the aspect survive intact?
2. **Contact test:** does a capability improvement that changes no contact still increase capability? Under Fig 11 :40 it must not.
3. **Discriminating instrument:** a design that returns different readings for germline-constrained and socially transmitted contributions to the same aspect, where current designs return one reading. This is the INWARD question.
4. **Partition test:** an anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29) and assigns the aspect to exactly one condition.
