- The README's *"shape, not a name"* governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. The sources here are already in the world.

### Q2 — Are the two sources isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Biological inheritance carries germline material; cultural transmission carries socially learned information. They do not share an atom set, so by Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**.
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Both carriers satisfy the same functional description: something that makes the next search cheaper (Fig 4 :24). The group is "head-start carrier"; the qualifiers are germline and culture. This is the reading that survives.
- **Falsifier:** if both can be written over the same atom set with only the bond differing, the isomer claim wins and the functional-group claim falls.

### Q3 — Catalyst and cycle

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** A teaching method survives its use. Fig 12 :107 (essay reading): *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish"*, so a settled method is a lookup for the next learner (Fig 12 :108-110).
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** World corroboration (secondary): Wikipedia *Dual inheritance theory* (read 2026-11-23) reports that genes and culture "continually interact in a feedback loop", with cultural practices altering selection pressures. Fig 12 governs; the article corroborates.
- **Falsifier:** show a handed-down method that is consumed by its first use, or a gene-culture loop whose output never re-enters as input.

### Q4 — What order do they run in?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :14-15 names link 5 as *"learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem."* That is the cultural/educational link. Links 1-4 (perception, vocabulary, objective, planning) are biological prerequisites.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** More schooling cannot repair a broken perceptual link; better nutrition cannot supply a vocabulary.
- Formula step 2: refuse the partition shape before minting any aspect assignment. Refusal precedes mint; ¬ is not a bond (`THE_FORMULA.md` composition section).
- **Order:** refuse the two-column shape → settle biological prerequisites → hand down head starts → evaluate capability at contact. Dependency, not rank. A failure above the break cannot be diagnosed from below it (Fig 3 :22-23).
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate link 5 running successfully before links 1-4, or an aspect assignment that survives without first refusing the partition.

## CONCLUSION

The two sources are **not isomers**; they are a **functional group** of head-start carriers with different atoms, plus a **catalyst/cycle** structure across generations. Their order is dependency order: biological prerequisites first, cultural head starts second, capability at contact, with refusal of the partition before either. This rescues what is defensible in the question without granting the two-column answer: both sources matter, but not as mixable ingredients in a flat formula.
