# What a monopole would be, and in what order the derivation runs

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves two supporting questions open. (1) In the composition vocabulary, is a magnetic monopole a new primitive, an isomer, or a new term over held atoms? (2) In what dependency order do the theoretical prediction, the cosmological consequence and the experimental search sit, and what would a detection do to that order? Both are answered without asserting that a monopole exists.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: space, time, energy (states) and spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. The physics frame adds terms (electric charge, gauge group, soliton, flux bound) as bindings from a frame whose closure differs. No seventh substrate primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is a monopole a new primitive?

- Fig 13 :116-117: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."
- Electric charge is not one of the six. Neither is magnetic charge. Both are charge assignments that qualify how a term couples to a gauge field, so both are derived terms, not substrate members.
- The 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole is described by the world source as a topological soliton in a Yang–Mills–Higgs theory, a smooth finite-energy configuration. That is a composition over held physics terms, not a new kind of space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient or network.
- **Result:** a monopole would be a new term in the physics library, built from held atoms. Claiming a seventh primitive would break the closure argument and is refused.
- **Falsifier:** derive magnetic charge as irreducible from the other five states and structures, or derive one of the six without it.

### Q2. Are electric and magnetic charge isomers? THE STRAINED READING, HELD NOT ASSERTED

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- World source (Wikipedia, "Magnetic monopole"): the generalized Maxwell equations possess a duality transformation rotating electric and magnetic charges and fields into one another; "one cannot uniquely decide whether a particle has an electric charge, a magnetic charge, or both, just by observing its behavior." The observed world fixes the ratio (all particles have the same magnetic-to-electric charge ratio, namely zero).
- Superficial fit: electric and magnetic charge share the field structure and differ in assignment, which resembles "same atoms, different bond, different substance."
- **Strain test fails.** Fig 12's objects are atoms joined by one of seven typed bonds. A duality rotation is an imported symmetry of the equations, not one of the seven bonds (conjunction, disjunction, identity, sequence, production, subtraction, comparison). The line only fits by treating a symmetry operation as a bond. By the strain test it governs another quantity, so the isomer claim is displaced, not asserted.
- **Better reading:** Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." "Charge" is the substructure that fixes how a particle couples; "electric" and "magnetic" are qualifiers selecting members. This fits without importing a new bond. It is `single-source` on the figure definition and unmeasured at the ground.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a duality assignment can be written as one of the seven bonds over the held atoms, the isomer reading is re-admitted and the functional-group reading is demoted. Until then the strain stays visible.

### Q3. What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order here is:

1. **Dirac (1931).** Sets the shape: if a magnetic charge exists, electric charge is quantized. Fixes the charge quantum, not existence.
2. **'t Hooft (1974) and Polyakov (1974).** Show that a smooth finite-energy monopole arises in a Yang–Mills theory broken to a smaller group. This is the atom inside the GUT closure.
3. **GUT prediction.** Compact U(1) gauge groups arising from GUTs imply monopoles; the world source "Grand Unified Theory" says some GUTs, such as Pati–Salam, predict them, but "there is no generally accepted GUT model."
4. **Monopole problem and inflation.** Stable relic monopoles would overclose the universe; inflation dilutes them. This is a cosmological consequence, not a detection.
5. **Experimental search.** MoEDAL and cosmic arrays return bounds and candidate events. This is the link that can settle existence.
6. **Inference back.** A resolution at step 5 would confirm the step-3 frame; a continued null result tightens bounds and leaves step 3 open.

A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can (Fig 3 :22-23). Step 5 has not yet returned a resolving reading, so effort at steps 1 to 4 cannot fix it. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

### Q4. What would a detection do to the order?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." The GUT prediction is settled inside the theory frame; it can serve as an atom at the next level.
- Fig 8 :26: "2: the atom crosses back", and Fig 8 :46 "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live." A detection is that crossing: an atom from the ground enters the theory frame and confirms the closure that predicted it.
- Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed" The extended Maxwell equations would acquire magnetic charge and current terms. The three guards still apply: support (a real reading), reachability (the term composes from held physics), novelty (it is not already in the library; the Dirac monopole is already a candidate form, so the novelty would lie in the measured realization).
- Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them," so the date, detector and charge quantum must be recorded with the result.

## CONCLUSION

A monopole would be a new term over held atoms, not a seventh primitive. Electric and magnetic charge are not Fig 12 isomers under the figure's own definition, because the relation between them is a duality symmetry, not one of the seven bonds; the functional-group reading fits better and is held as `single-source`. The dependency order runs from quantization to soliton to GUT prediction to cosmology to experiment, with experiment as the unresolved link. A detection would be an atom crossing back from the ground and would owe a new term, gated by the three guards.

This rescues what is checkable in the monopole question without asserting existence, a GUT model, or a new primitive. The strain (duality as isomer) is named, not smoothed.
