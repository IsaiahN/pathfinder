## Wider literature that bears on the question

- Dirac, 1931, the original quantization argument, reported in the Wikipedia "Magnetic monopole" article. It sets the shape of the charge but does not settle existence.
- 't Hooft (1974) and Polyakov (1974), the smooth finite-energy monopole, referenced on the 't Hooft–Polyakov page. This is the atom inside the GUT closure.
- Patrizii, Sahnoun and Togo, "Searches for cosmic magnetic monopoles: past, present and future," Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 377 (2019) 20180328, referenced from the MoEDAL page. Relevant to cosmic flux bounds.
- Milton (2006), cited above, for the history of candidate events and the state of magnetic-charge theory.
- Guth's inflation proposal, originally motivated by the monopole problem, reported in the cosmic inflation article. This is the cosmological consequence of taking the GUT prediction seriously.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full framework before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the bound and the refuter; offer links after.
- Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what resolving evidence would look like before giving them the article.
- Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A reply from a physicist who read the same reviews is a conversation, not corroboration.
- Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A wrong existence verdict is expensive, so independent checks are worth more than a large audience.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) ask what the reader would count as a resolving detection or a decisive bound; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three primary links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (with the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** the residual, the bound-not-refutation claim, the budget distinction, and the refuter. The reader checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, the figure text and the primary monopole sources. The stage-1 verdict is recorded before any framework material is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in:** the formula as a method to reuse, with each user writing their own refuter and stating their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The existence slot is deliberately left `gap`; if a checker reads that as a dodge rather than an honest stop, the offer has failed and should be withdrawn rather than defended.

**Hosting:** nothing in this folder is sent by this agent. The public links are already hosted by their owners. If you prepare a packet, prefer an opt-in download page over an attachment pushed to a list.
