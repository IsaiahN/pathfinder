## 0. The residual, in effect terms

`R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per developer-slot `s`, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

The question asks: for which slots (labs, firms, regions) does R stay small on advanced-AI capability tasks, and what do hardware, energy supply, data access and infrastructure do to that capacity?

- **Option A.** The constraints are chiefly a property of the agent. Who develops advanced AI is decided by individual talent. Leads to: no figure line governs talent as a free-floating property (no nameable anchor), and Fig 11 :40 refuses an agent-only improvement.
- **Option B.** The four constraints are habitat, instruments and budget filters. They decide contact and reach, not truth. Leads to: a checkable union claim, per-slot verdicts, and a declared gap on which named actor wins.

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, before the run: *the four constraints bound how much of the reachable space a developer can search and what the developer can represent at all; they do not settle whether a claim is correct*. Written down so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :65 on making the sought-for shape an ordinary term).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A box with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: four conditions that overlap, so a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*; Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). Symmetry: capability is a threshold relative to competitors, an ordered comparison bond rather than a symmetric join (Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). Scale: single developer loop, organization population, global supply chain, with a lossy membrane between (Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Developer loop | researchers, their Γ (library, models) | residual reduction, minted terms, settlement | the ground: benchmarks, real-world tasks | admitted, not sufficient alone |
| D2 Compute and energy substrate | chips, fabs, GPUs, memory, grid, cooling | energy (state) + gradient (structure) composing into flow; bits and actions as two prices | thermodynamics, the physical ground | admitted as budget filter |
| D3 Data and imports | datasets, corpora, sensors, curators | atoms imported into closure(Γ); independence between frames | the world's ground | admitted as the only wall-moving operator |
| D4 Infrastructure and channel | networks, cloud, review, distribution, instruments | contact, bracket fidelity | the ground maintained, which does not decay | admitted as channel |
| X1 Talent or genius as a free-floating property | the leader | talent | none nameable; absorbed into D1's agent-only reading, refused by Fig 11 :40 | not a domain yet |
| X2 Compute as the single all-purpose lever | the chip | spend | absorbed into D2 and D4; a filter, not a verdict (Fig 9 :60) | absorbed |

**Partition or union:** union, not partition. D2, D3 and D4 overlap (a chip needs power and cooling; data needs a channel to arrive; a channel needs hardware). Each overlap is a compound with its own loop, so no single lever clears the region where more than one condition binds (Fig 9 :55-57).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*):** perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry. D2 and D4 sit below the objective link; D3 supplies the atoms the vocabulary link needs. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 2. Findings, with verbatim figure lines and refuters

### F1 — Capability lives in agent-and-habitat, not in the agent alone (decisive shape claim)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :43-47 enumerates the habitat: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*; *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*; *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*; *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*. For this question the enumerated habitat contains hardware, energy supply, data access and infrastructure. They are conditions and relations in contact with R, not decorations on a talented agent.

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A GPU, a corpus or a grid connection is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another; there is no standing list of good resources.

**Mode:** `general` for the capability claim; `specified` for naming the four conditions as this question's habitat. **Triangulated** across Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 4 (kernel invariant across scale, only actors/currency/anchor change), low overlap between the two routes.
**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement, with hardware, energy, data and infrastructure contact held fixed, that demonstrably moves the ground on an advanced-AI capability task.

### F2 — The four constraints are budget filters, not verdict witnesses

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 12 :125-127:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
> **"and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."**

Hardware and energy price actions (what can be tried, how often, at what scale). Data prices the description (what can be stated and composed). They are in different units and do not add, so a system holding only one accounting will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. What the ground settles is a separate question. This is why "more compute" cannot be a verdict about who is right; it is a budget that decides how much of the space is actually visited.

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Concentrating the four constraints in few hands can lengthen the room without moving the truth; agreement among labs sharing one supply chain and one corpus is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

**Mode:** `general` for the filter/verdict distinction; `specified` for the four-constraint application. **Triangulated** across Fig 9 (filters vs witnesses) and Fig 12 (two currencies), distinct roles.
**Refuter:** a demonstration that doubling a budget filter, with ground and closure held fixed, flips the verdict of a claim rather than only how much of the space was searched.

### F3 — Energy is a finite substrate state and a spendable gradient

Fig 13 :14 (energy as a state): *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*. Symbols table :89-91: *"Conserved, finite, and directionless on its own."* Fig 13 :76:

> **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."**

Fig 13 :22 (gradient as a structure): *"a difference that can be spent"*. Fig 13 :68 (thermodynamics test): *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"*. Energy supply is not a metaphorical constraint; it is one of the six substrate terms, finite in a finite region, and the gradient over it is what can be spent to drive a computation. Landauer's principle, opened 2026-11-24 (Faist et al., Nature Communications 2015), corroborates from physics: *"Irreversible information processing cannot be carried out without some inevitable thermodynamical work cost."*

**Mode:** `general` for the substrate claim; `specified` for the data-centre application. **Triangulated** across Fig 13 (substrate) and the physics source (low overlap), with the physics source corroborating only, never deciding.
**Refuter:** a logically irreversible computation that returns no thermodynamic work cost, or a derivation of energy from the other five substrate terms.

### F4 — Data is the only operator that moves the wall, and more of the same adds no atom

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* (IMPORT). Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

Data access is the import channel: it supplies primitives that were not in Γ. A developer with a fixed corpus can search forever inside its closure and add no new atom (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). But data volume alone is not the whole story. Fig 8 :46: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :49: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Low-overlap data sources (different languages, sensoria, domains) give a union surplus that a single larger corpus of the same kind does not.

**Mode:** `general` for idempotence and import; `specified` for data-access application. **Triangulated** across Fig 6 (what moves the wall) and Fig 8 (where the surplus lives), distinct roles.
**Refuter:** reach expanded purely by internal composition with no imported atom, or a demonstration that a second corpus with high overlap adds the same surplus as a low-overlap one.

### F5 — Infrastructure is the channel; the ground does not decay, the channel does

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Infrastructure failure (dropped transients, stale percepts, pooled readings across labs) is a channel failure, not a change in what is true. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Better instruments are built from worse instruments already returning something; you cannot specify a sensor from an account of what it would find.

The CSA concentration note, opened 2026-11-24, corroborates the structural form: *"The modern AI development stack is not a loosely coupled collection of interchangeable tools. It is a tightly integrated, hierarchically dependent architecture"*. Dependence on a few chokepoints is a channel fragility, not a verdict about which model is correct.

**Mode:** `general` for channel decay; `specified` for infrastructure application. **Triangulated** across Fig 10 (channel) and Fig 6 (instrument improvement), low overlap.
**Refuter:** an increase in ground-settled capability traceable solely to a degraded or saturated channel, with agent and habitat fixed.

### F6 — Naming which developer or region wins is a gap, not a hidden answer

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The figures return a shape (the conditions any candidate must satisfy) and refuse to name the slot. The canonical rule is explicit: the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* about what a missing thing would look like. Naming a specific winner from a benchmark reading is the metamerism error of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* A scoreboard reading cannot comment on the source of the capability.

This is a `gap` after a genuine re-census, not a silence: unreachable is a verdict. Needed to close it is a source-discriminating instrument per slot, characterised in effect terms but not held.

**Mode:** `general` for the shape-not-a-name limit; `gap` for the named-winner question. **Single-source for the limit** inside the figure text (Fig 9 :32), triangulated with the canonical figures README.
**Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that names which of the four conditions binds in a given slot, with provenance and a shadow test.

### F7 — The middle link is where infrastructure usually breaks

Fig 3 :24-27 describe why chains break between perception and planning: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither [obvious failures nor obvious tests]"*, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Sambasivan et al. (CHI 2021), opened 2026-11-24, corroborate at the data layer: *"Data cascades are opaque and delayed, with poor indicators and metrics."* The under-measured link is data quality and channel contact, not chip count.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated** across Fig 3 (middle link) and the HCI source (low overlap).
**Refuter:** a measurement showing the middle link (data and channel contact) high and non-declining while capability stalls for a documented interval.

### F8 — Correlational self-reports are one evidence pool, not a witness

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The CSET survey *The Main Resource is the Human* (Musser et al. 2023), opened 2026-11-24, reports: *"Surveyed AI researchers are not primarily or exclusively constrained by compute access."* Read naively this conflicts with a compute-first claim. The conflict is returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict: the survey is a self-report channel reading from one pool, and its own conclusion that *"compute cannot be viewed as an all-purpose lever"* is exactly the union shape of Fig 9 :55-57. Handle it as one witness with a declared overlap to the other CSET source, not as corroboration from two independent frames.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Single-source** for the survey fact.
**Refuter:** a genuinely independent frame (different evidence pool) showing that a single one of the four conditions predicts who develops advanced AI across slots.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. An agent-only improvement, with hardware, energy, data and infrastructure contact held fixed, demonstrably moves the ground on an advanced-AI capability task (breaks F1 / Fig 11 :40).
2. A budget filter, with ground and closure held fixed, is shown to determine the verdict of a claim rather than only how much of the space was searched (breaks F2 / Fig 9 :60).
3. Reach is shown to expand by internal composition alone with no imported atom, or a high-overlap corpus is shown to give the same surplus as a low-overlap one (breaks F4 / Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union and the filter/verdict split, not with a ranking of resources. Optimising one link cannot rescue another (Fig 3 :19).
2. Per-slot verdicts, never an average over labs or regions (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. Treat energy and hardware as budget filters and substrate, data as import, infrastructure as channel. Do not let any filter hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
4. Ask what would refute the shape before offering any source. Bring a characterised residual, never the source (Fig 10 :52).
5. Keep the named-winner question open as a `gap` and state what instrument would close it (Fig 9 :32).
6. Send the generator (the derivation and the refuter), not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
