# Union, order and isomers: supporting derivation for REA-0089

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves three structural questions open: (1) are hardware, energy, data access and infrastructure four separate problems or one union compound; (2) in what order do they bind, so that an intervention on one can or cannot rescue a failure in another; (3) do two developers holding the same resources but arranging them differently count as the same thing under the figure's notion of substance? Each needs a derivation, not an assumption.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried in, not redone)

Atoms held: the four conditions as habitat terms, the six substrate terms, the seven bonds and negation. Currency: actions (hardware, energy) and bits (data, description), which do not add. Anchor: the ground for each slot. Union, not partition; order by dependency. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The four conditions overlap. Chips need power and cooling; data needs a channel to arrive; a channel needs hardware to run. A single lever (buy more chips) clears the region where only hardware binds and leaves the intersections standing, because energy or data still binds. The residue after such a lever is not a harder hardware problem; it is a specific compound.

**Result:** union (Fig 9 :55-57). Verdicts per compound, never averaged. **Refuter:** an intervention on one condition that clears every slot where any other condition also binds.

### Q2 — What order do they bind in?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Dependency order for this question: data access supplies what the vocabulary link needs; hardware and energy supply the actions the loop can afford; infrastructure is the channel by which percepts and methods arrive; only then can objective, planning and learn-and-carry run. A lab that optimises planning while the data and channel links are unmapped is spending effort above the break (Fig 3 :24-27). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**Result:** order by dependency, not by funding weight. **Refuter:** a documented case where improving a lower link, with upper links fixed and measured, fails to move the residual, or where an upper-link improvement moves it with lower links degraded.

### Q3 — Are two developers with the same resources the same substance?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Two labs holding the same atom set (same chips, same power contract, same corpus, same network) but arranging them differently (one imports a low-overlap data frame and keeps a channel to a paying ground; the other searches inside one closure with saturated metrics) are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their capability difference is invisible in a resource inventory and decisive in behaviour. If only some atoms are shared, the weaker claim is a shared **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): both are `habitat -> capability` members with different qualifiers.

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same chip is a benefactor to a compute-bound residual and idle capacity to a channel-bound one, so no standing list of good resources survives a change of residual.

**Result:** the isomer claim is checkable and carries a falsifier. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two developers with the same atom set and different arrangement show no category difference in ground-settled capability, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q4 — Why the two prices do not add

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :127: *"and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."*

Hardware and energy price actions; data prices the description. A system holding only one accounting (dollars, or token count) will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test and will not notice the shortfall. Fig 12 :128-129: *"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."* Summing them into one figure is the error named here, not the fix.

**Result:** read both currencies separately. **Refuter:** a single accounting demonstrated to predict both description cost and settlement cost across slots with no residual.

### Q5 — What crosses between a lab and the population

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A settled method (not a recording of one success) becomes an atom available to the next lab. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The round trip is lossy: Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

This is why a lab's success cannot simply be handed to another lab: what crosses is the method, and the gap between the method and the concrete habitat is the thing that must be measured, not assumed zero.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions form a union compound ordered by dependency, not a list of levers. Developers with the same resources arranged differently are isomers under Fig 12 :117, which makes the resource inventory insufficient to predict capability. Two currencies (bits and actions) are held apart. These claims need no new primitive and no import that moves a wall; they are the figure's composition rules applied to this residual. The standing falsifiers are in Q1 to Q4 above.
