# The Tether's answer: what constrains who can develop advanced AI

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured ranking. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks why the residual `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` stays small on advanced-AI capability tasks in some developer-slots and grows in others, given four named conditions. R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The four conditions form a **union compound**, not one lever (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Each overlap is a compound with its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Capability lives in developer-and-habitat, not in the developer alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Hardware, energy supply, data access and infrastructure are the enumerated habitat: actors, conditions and relations in contact with R (Fig 11 :43-47). Talent alone has no nameable anchor and is not a domain.

2. **They constrain by fixing the budget and the contact, not by settling the truth.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 12 :125-127: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*; *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*; *"and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."* Hardware and energy price actions; data prices the description. They bound how much of the reachable space is searched. They do not decide whether a claim is correct.

3. **Energy is a finite substrate state and a spendable gradient.** Fig 13 :14: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"*; Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."* Finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate (Fig 13 :68). This is a physical constraint, corroborated by Landauer's principle, not a budget analogy.

4. **Data is the import that moves the wall; volume alone does not.** Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* (IMPORT). Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A second corpus with high overlap is *"one frame wearing two names"* (Fig 8 :49); a low-overlap frame gives a union surplus (Fig 8 :46: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*).

5. **Infrastructure is the channel to a ground that does not decay.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Infrastructure failure is a failure to keep the anchor reachable, not a change in what is true. Instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something (Fig 6 :32).

6. **Which condition binds in which slot is a declared `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The framework returns the shape any answer must have; it does not name the winner. Closing the gap needs a source-discriminating instrument per slot.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that compute, energy, data or infrastructure is the single binding constraint. All four are filters or habitat conditions, and they overlap (Fig 9 :55-57).
- Not that a bigger model, a bigger cluster or a bigger corpus is thereby a better answer. Idempotent closure forbids same-kind volume from adding an atom (Fig 8 :32).
- Not that talent is irrelevant. Fig 11 :39 says capability is agent-and-habitat, never either alone. The agent is a necessary part of the compound.
- Not a ranking of labs, firms or regions, and not a prediction of who wins. That is a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any slot settles which condition binds.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the questions are:

- **Binding test:** for a given developer-slot, which of the four conditions, when varied with the others held fixed, actually moves R against an independent ground? A self-report survey is a channel reading, not this instrument.
- **Filter test:** does a budget change only how much of the space was searched, or does it flip the verdict of a claim? If the former, Fig 9 :60 holds.
- **Import test:** does a low-overlap data frame add a primitive the closure lacked, and does a high-overlap corpus fail to? This separates Fig 8 :46 from Fig 8 :49.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3:** an agent-only improvement with contact fixed that moves the ground; a budget filter shown to determine a verdict; reach expanding by internal composition alone with no imported atom.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions are enumerated from the habitat, not composed into a league table (Fig 11 :43). Declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where instruments can no longer distinguish sources and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A claimed winner from a scoreboard reading is indiscernibility mistaken for identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank the labs, firms or regions that develop advanced AI, and it does not claim that compute, energy, data or infrastructure is the single lever. It says that capability is a property of developer-and-habitat, and that these four conditions are the habitat, the budget filters and the channel that decide how much of the reachable space a developer can search and what the developer can represent at all. Hardware and energy price actions, data prices the description, and those two prices do not add; neither settles whether a claim is true. Energy is finite and spendable, data is the only import that moves the wall, and infrastructure is a channel to a ground that does not decay while the channel does. Which condition binds in which slot stays open, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, three falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the slot. The parity of resources is not the parity of capability: two developers with the same atoms arranged differently are isomers, and the difference is invisible in an inventory and decisive in behaviour.
