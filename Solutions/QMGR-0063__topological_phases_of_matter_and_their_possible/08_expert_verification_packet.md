# Expert verification packet: the Tether on topological phases and spacetime

**What this is:** a claim in a form a condensed-matter physicist, gravity theorist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No measurement at the quantum-gravity scale is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Topological" in matter names an arrangement robust against local perturbation, which is the figure's "The arrangement is the substance." The question about a connection to spacetime is a union of three conditions, not one question, and must be unbundled. Spacetime is not a primitive: a structure must sit between space and time, and the framework does not say which one. A laboratory topological phase is settled against the ground and can therefore become an atom for a second-order composition, but no bond is licensed for that step, the import needs a source gate, and the round-trip gap is unmeasured. The framework offers the shape any answer must have, not a name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

- **Statement:** topological order is an arrangement/stability claim. Two phases over the same local atom set but with a different global pattern are isomers under Fig 12; when only the motif is shared, they are a functional group. Topology is not a new primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. - **What to check:** is a topological phase distinguished by a global invariant that survives local perturbation, rather than by a local order parameter? (Ball 2018 quotes Wen: "Robustness against any local perturbations is what 'topological' implies here.") Can two phases be written over the same local atom set?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a topological phase that needs a local order parameter the other does not; or an invariant that cannot be expressed as a composition over the six; or show that no arrangement difference survives local perturbation. Any of these breaks C1.
