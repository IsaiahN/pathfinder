## 0. The residual

The question contains two uses of "structure" and one unexamined bridge between them.

1. In condensed matter, "topological" is an effect claim: the phase is characterized by a global invariant and is robust against local perturbation. In the National Science Review interview, Wen says "Robustness against any local perturbations is what 'topological' implies here" and that "topological order describes phenomena that cannot be characterized by symmetry and its breaking" (Ball 2018). The physics anchor is real: quantized Hall conductance, ground-state degeneracy that depends on the topology of the space, topological entanglement entropy (Chen, Gu and Wen 2010).
2. In gravity, "the structure of spacetime" is not a primitive under the six-term set. Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." So a structure must sit in the slot, and the framework does not say which one.
3. The bridge ("possible connection") is a sought-for shape, not a term. Fig 5 :62-63: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it." The remedy is the one the formula names in its "Direction" section: write down what an answer would look like, bind it as an ordinary term, and let it accrue a residual like anything else. That binding is done in the README and refused in §3 below.

**Effect statement.** What is being decided is whether the organizational principle that defines a topological phase can be composed into the structure slot of spacetime, and what would license that step. Option A: assert the connection. Option B: keep the shape, require a source gate, a shadow test and a ground settlement before any binding. A gives away the independence of the checkers. B gives them a refutable shape.

### Arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least three conditions are bundled: (i) topological order in matter, (ii) the structure of spacetime, (iii) a connection between them. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." This is a union, not one hard problem.
- **Symmetry:** a topological invariant is equivalence under local deformation or local unitary transformation; the "same" phase survives many different microscopic descriptions. In figure terms this is the symmetric bond's property that "two names, one referent" (Fig 12 :66-67) and the arrangement claim "The arrangement is the substance." (Fig 12 :94). It is not a ⋛ threshold comparison between two quantities.
- **Scale:** the condensed-matter description is a low-energy, coarse-grained one; the spacetime claim is a fundamental or, in emergent pictures, a higher-level one. Cross-scale composition is governed by Fig 4 and Fig 12 :104.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Topology is an arrangement claim, and the figures already name that structure (triangulated)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

A topological phase is defined by precisely this: the local ingredients can be the same while the global arrangement differs, and that difference is decisive in behaviour (Hall plateaus, degeneracy, edge modes). Wen's statement that topological order "describes phenomena that cannot be characterized by symmetry and its breaking" (Ball 2018) is the physics-facing version of the same claim.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 (framework-internal arrangement rule) and two independent physics sources (Ball 2018 interview; Chen, Gu and Wen 2010 PRB abstract). Low overlap between the code-style figure and the laboratory physics. **Refuter:** exhibit two phases with the same local ingredients and the same global invariant but different measurable behaviour, or show that the topological invariant is recoverable from the ingredient list alone. Either would break the mapping onto Fig 12.

### F2 — The connection is a union compound and must be unbundled (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :56: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The question bundles three things: topological order in matter, the structure of spacetime, and a connection. Jaksland (2026, Princeton, arXiv:2607.09823) reaches the same conclusion from outside the framework: behind "spacetime emerges from entanglement" hide "three distinct emergence claims about, respectively, metric, gravitational dynamics, and topological connectivity," and "these results are not about the same spatiotemporal aspects. They can therefore not just be grouped as evidence for one unanimous conclusion."

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Jaksland 2026 (philosophy of physics, low overlap with the Tether essays). **Refuter:** a single experiment or derivation that settles metric, dynamics and connectivity as one claim with one ground. Until then the "connection" is three questions wearing one name.

### F3 — "Spacetime" is not a primitive; the structure slot stays open (single-source for the figure, corroborated by the canonical README)

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

So "the structure of spacetime" is a structure in a state-structure-state molecule, not a thing given in advance. The figures return a shape. Naming that structure (network, or entanglement graph, or spectrum) is a binding from the physics frame, not a derivation. The canonical figures README states the limit as "a shape, not a name."

**Mode:** `general` for the cross-kind rule, `specified` for the open slot. Triangulated across Fig 13, Fig 9 and the local canonical README. **Refuter:** derive one of the six from the other five (breaks Fig 13 indivisibility), or exhibit a direct state-state composition the framework cannot refuse.

### F4 — The specific binding "entanglement = geometry" is an import with no source gate as stated (single-source for the figure rule, physics-facing check unmeasured)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure rule. **Refuter:** show that the six-term set alone forces which structure occupies the slot, with no provenanced import. If it does, the binding is a derivation and this finding is withdrawn.

### F5 — Topological phases are an isomer or functional-group claim, not a seventh primitive

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 13 :124: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."

Topological order is a property of arrangement, not a new member of the six. Under Fig 12's definition, two phases over the same local atom set but with different global pattern are a real isomer claim; when only the motif is shared, the honest weaker reading is a functional group. This is worked out in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure definition with an unmeasured physics-facing check. **Refuter:** if either phase can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### F6 — The cross-scale step needs a settled molecule and a measured round-trip gap

Topological phases are settled against the lab ground, so each can serve as an atom at a higher level. That makes the cross-scale composition legal in form. It does not make it true. ## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

1. One of the six terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh primitive is shown to be required and composable (breaks Fig 13 indivisibility and minimality).

A ground reading settles the connection without any import or with the three emergence claims collapsed into one: specifically, a measurement in which a topological invariant of a laboratory system fixes the spacetime metric with no additional boundary data and no residual, or a proof that metric, gravitational dynamics and topological connectivity emerge as one claim. (Breaks the open-slot verdict and F2.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
