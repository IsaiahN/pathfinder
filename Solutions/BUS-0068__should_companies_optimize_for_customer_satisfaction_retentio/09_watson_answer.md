## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about four candidate objectives for a firm: customer satisfaction, retention, profit per customer, and broader long-term trust. Option A: treat them as four values of one dial and return the best mix. Option B: treat them as a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors and return a separator with per-domain verdicts and a declared gap. What each leads to: A produces a composite score that a frame scores with quantities it also produces, and it promotes a convention into an outcome (Fig 1 :27, Fig 10 :47); B produces a per-slot comparison against a non-updating market, instruments with declared blind spots, a maintained channel, and refuters written before the run.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unchosen objective: which reading stays in contact with a market that does not move when the firm's own report of it moves. Arity: four conditions. Symmetry: "optimize for X" is ordered, and the candidates mix a judgment, a count, a money quantity and a channel relation, so a pooled correlation cannot choose the arrow. Scale: the individual customer slot; a population average hides the live signal (Fig 1 :19, :22). Question type: union with overlapping conditions, not partition (Fig 9 :55-56).
- **Domain 1, customer-measurement.** Actors: respondents, CSAT/NPS instruments, analysts, the incentive system paying for the score. Currency: reported judgments and correlations. Anchor: the customer's judgment is the ground for a question constitutively about a person, and only an instrument for the firm's payment question (Fig 2 :36-38). Verdict: instrument with a blind spot.
- **Domain 2, market-payment.** Actors: payers, competitors as alternatives, prices and costs, ledgers. Currency: money received less cost, per slot, over a horizon. Anchor: a counter or a market, which does not update on what the firm thinks (Fig 2 :29). Verdict: the ground reading for the transaction; the objective composed over it is a comparison, not an accumulation (Operators :51).
- **Domain 3, retention and habit.** Actors: repeat purchasers, switching costs, inertia, service recovery. Currency: repeat-purchase counts, churn, share of wallet. Anchor: none nameable; the same count is produced by delight, habit, inertia and barriers, so the reading cannot comment on its source. Verdict: recording, not generator (Fig 4 :25); proxy with residual-relative valence (Fig 11 :49).
- **Domain 4, trust and long horizon.** Actors: the firm as promise-maker, the customer who could defect, witnesses, a seat able to refuse. Currency: expectation of future behavior and the state of the channel after a shock. Anchor: a ground that does not update on the firm's own report (Fig 10 :24), plus a seat that cannot be reconstructed by the claim it checks (Fig 10 :42). Verdict: channel-and-seat property held by the pair, not a stock (Fig 11 :39-40).
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive distinguishable customer slots; acquire vocabulary for the bond between a reading and future payment; state the objective as a comparison against a non-updating ground; plan; verify and carry a method rather than a recording (Fig 3 :20, :27). Refuse a metric that has become an outcome-fixing convention before minting a composite replacement (Operators :58). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :36-38 distinguishes a question about a person from a question about a market, so there is no strain when satisfaction is an anchor in one and an instrument in the other. Fig 13's physical six-term set governs the substrate, not the marketing metrics, so mapping it onto the four candidates would be a category error, not a contradiction (Fig 4 :19-20). One live strain remains: Operators :51 makes the goal a comparison while "profit per customer" is often booked as a running total; re-census assigns the reading to quantity and the objective to comparison across a horizon, with its refuter in `01` F4.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The single-dial question is refused. The four candidates form a union with different anchors, so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Profit per customer is the ground reading for the transaction and becomes an objective only as a per-slot margin compared over a horizon that includes channel state. Satisfaction is the right anchor for a question about a person and only an instrument with a declared blind spot for the firm's payment question. Retention is a recording whose source the count cannot discriminate, so it stays a proxy with residual-relative valence until a discriminator is supplied. Trust is the state of a channel to a ground that does not update, plus a seat able to refuse, held by the firm-and-market pair rather than by the firm alone. No figure line returns a universal weight, horizon or allocation; that is a declared `gap`, reported as unreached rather than silence. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result, and a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, lists refuters before the conclusion, and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a composite score as if it were a ground, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." If the union verdict is wrong and one reading does predict all anchors, the firm loses the per-slot signal and optimizes a frame-internal average. Both costs are avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four per-domain verdicts are separable and none requires withdrawing a primitive. A composite already deployed can be superseded by the separator, and a rejected metric can be refused without deleting it from the record (Fig 6 :42). What cannot be undone cheaply is a channel severed by margin extraction, which is why the horizon condition is attached to the objective rather than offered as advice.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument for repeat purchases would move retention from recording to instrument and test the isomer claim in `06`; a longitudinal per-customer dataset showing that higher current margin leaves channel state and future margin unchanged at every slot would narrow F4; a satisfaction reading invariant to the firm's own attribute changes and predictive of payment across subgroups would reverse F3; a universal weight derived from the figure lines with no imported business quantity would close F9; a demonstration that one reading predicts all anchors at every slot with stable coefficients would withdraw the union verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
