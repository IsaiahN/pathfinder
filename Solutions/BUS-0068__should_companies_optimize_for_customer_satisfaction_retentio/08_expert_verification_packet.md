# Expert verification packet — the Tether on customer metrics and objectives

**What this is:** a claim in a form a customer-metrics or service-management researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal number. Not `grounded`.** No firm was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Satisfaction, retention, profit per customer, and long-term trust are not four values of one dial. They are four readings with different anchors, measured at different slots and on different timescales, and the four conditions overlap (a union, not a partition). The separator is: read the residual per customer; keep profit per customer as the objective only as a per-slot margin compared over a horizon that includes channel state, with the paying market as the non-updating anchor; keep satisfaction as an instrument with a declared blind spot, because human judgment is the right anchor only for a question about a person; keep retention as a recording whose source must be named before it can be promoted; and treat trust as the state of a channel to a ground that does not update plus a seat able to refuse, not as a stock to maximize. No universal weight, horizon, or allocation is returned; that is a declared gap. The framework gives a shape with refuters written in advance, not a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Framework-internal and empirical; checkable against the figure text and the meta-analyses

- **Statement:** the four candidates are a union with different anchors, and averaging them destroys the live signal. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** does a single customer-health score predict defection better than its per-slot components in a held-out segment after a service failure? Mittal et al. (2023, open access) report that satisfaction-outcome correlations change with the measurement of the outcome and the level of analysis, which is the shape this predicts; Keiningham et al. (2008) report that different metrics predict different loyalty behaviors.
- **Refuter:** one reading predicts the others' anchors at every slot with stable coefficients across goods, services, B2B and B2C, and one lever changes all four with no residue at the intersections. If that holds, the union verdict is withdrawn.

### C2 — The anchor test on satisfaction and the source test on retention

- **Statement:** satisfaction is the right anchor only for a question constitutively about a person; for a firm's payment question it is an instrument that can be wrong on the axis it does not measure. A repeat purchase cannot name its own source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- **What to check:** does the satisfaction reading move when the firm changes only the attributes being judged, with payment held constant? Can an instrument distinguish delight, habit, inertia and switching barriers as sources of a repeat purchase without changing the behavior being measured? The hard-problem essay states the general instrument requirement: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) find switching barriers retain even dissatisfied customers, so the same count covers different bonds.
- **Refuter:** a satisfaction reading that is invariant to the firm's own attribute changes and predicts payment and future contact across subgroups; or a source-discriminating instrument whose source label predicts future margin better than the repeat count. Either fires a narrowing, not a flip of the no-scalar verdict.

### C3 — The horizon and channel test on profit and trust

- **Statement:** profit per customer is the ground reading for the transaction, but the objective composed over it is a comparison over a horizon that must include channel state. Trust is a channel-and-seat property, not a stock.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **What to check:** does raising current per-customer margin, through price or service cuts, leave the channel state and the future payment distribution unchanged at every slot over a full horizon? Does a trust survey move when the firm defects where it could? Rust, Lemon and Zeithaml (2004) already compute CLV per customer before averaging and include brand switching, which is the right shape; they import a discount rate and margin from finance, which this framework does not supply.
- **Refuter:** a firm where higher current margin leaves channel state and future margin unchanged at every slot across a full horizon; or a trust instrument that is invariant to the firm's own report, moves on defection, and predicts future per-slot margin. The first narrows F4; the second narrows F6.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal weighting, percentage, horizon or discount rate. No figure line returns one; that is `gap`.
- Not that satisfaction is unimportant or should be abandoned. It is the anchor for a different question.
- Not that retention is worthless. It is a recording whose source must be named.
- Not that trust is the fourth quantity to balance against the other three. It is a channel-and-seat property.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a firm's data.
- Not a claim that these four are the only objectives a firm could hold. The census is of the question's candidates, not of all business objectives.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground is a longitudinal per-customer dataset with margin, channel state, defection after a defection opportunity, and a labeled source for each repeat purchase, read by independent frames that do not share the firm's own report as evidence. The three tests to run first:

1. **Source discriminator:** an instrument that separates barrier-retained from trust-retained customers and predicts their post-failure defection distributions.
2. **Horizon test:** current margin up, channel state and future margin unchanged at every slot, or not.
3. **Anchor test:** satisfaction invariant to attribute changes while payment is held constant, or not.

A consensus of executives, a prestigious author, or a customer-health dashboard is not one of these. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-56, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 2 :36-38, Fig 10 :24, Fig 11 :40. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against the Mittal et al. 2023 and Keiningham et al. 2008 results: do the effect sizes change with the measurement and the level of analysis as predicted?
3. Check C2 and C3 against your own field data or experience: have you seen a retention count that covered two different bonds, or a margin gain that severed the channel?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the sources of a repeat purchase, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
