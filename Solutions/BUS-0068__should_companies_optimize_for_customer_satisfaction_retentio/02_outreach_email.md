# Outreach draft — check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the separator in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has survived your own check of F1, F3 and F4. If F1 fires (one reading predicts the others at every slot), there is nothing to send.

---

**Subject:** One claim about customer metrics you can check in five minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from your own work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the four things your question names, satisfaction, retention, profit per customer, and long-term trust, are not four values of one dial. They are four different readings with four different anchors. You cannot average them into a customer-health score and expect the score to decide anything.
>
> The claim is a separator with four parts:
>
> 1. **Keep profit per customer as the objective only as a per-customer comparison over a horizon.** The paying market is the anchor. A population average hides the live account.
> 2. **Keep satisfaction as an instrument with a declared blind spot.** It is the right answer to "is this a good experience for this person?" It is not the right answer to "will this market keep paying?", and if the score moves when your own actions change the attributes being judged, it has stopped being a reference.
> 3. **Do not promote retention into an objective until you can name its source.** A repeat purchase can come from delight, habit, inertia, or a switching barrier. The repeat count cannot tell you which, and the same count can predict opposite futures.
> 4. **Treat trust as the state of a channel to a ground that does not update, plus a seat able to refuse a profitable-looking defection.** Not as a stock you can buy with a survey score. If your trust reading does not move when you defect where you could, it is not measuring trust.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a universal weighting, a right percentage, a three-year horizon, or that any one of these four is always best. No figure line I know of returns a number, so I am reporting that as a gap rather than filling it in. I am also not claiming that satisfaction is unimportant; it is the anchor for a different question, which is why it is kept as an instrument here.
>
> **What would refute this, written before I sent it:**
> 1. One of the four readings predicts the others' anchors at every customer slot with stable coefficients across goods, services, B2B and B2C, and one lever changes all four with no residue at the intersections.
> 2. A satisfaction reading neither moves when the firm changes the judged attributes nor fails to predict payment and future contact across subgroups.
> 3. Raising current profit per customer leaves the channel state and the future payment distribution unchanged at every slot over a full horizon.
> 4. An instrument discriminates among the sources of a repeat purchase and predicts future margin better than the repeat count.
> 5. A universal objective weight or horizon is derived without importing a business quantity.
>
> I wrote these down before the run. If any fires, I withdraw the relevant part and say where I was wrong. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually read. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Keiningham, Cooil, Andreassen and Aksoy, "Linking Customer Loyalty to Growth", MIT Sloan Management Review, 2008: https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/linking-customer-loyalty-to-growth/
> - Mittal, Han, Frennea, Blut, Shaik, Bosukonda and Sridhar, "Customer satisfaction, loyalty behaviors, and firm financial performance: what 40 years of research tells us", Marketing Letters, 2023: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11002-023-09671-w
> - Ranaweera and Prabhu, "The influence of satisfaction, trust and switching barriers on customer retention in a continuous purchasing setting", International Journal of Service Industry Management, 2003: https://doi.org/10.1108/09564230310489231
> - Rust, Lemon and Zeithaml, "Return on Marketing: Using Customer Equity to Focus Marketing Strategy", Journal of Marketing, 2004: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.68.1.109.24030
>
> The figures and the formula are public here: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why the order is this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient checks the separator without holding the framework interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A refuter is how the ground gets a vote. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| No universal number offered | Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" and Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands". |
| Satisfaction kept as instrument, not dismissed | Fig 2 :36-38: human judgment is the right anchor only when the question is constitutively about persons. |
| Retention refused as objective until its source is named | *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." |
| Trust re-grounded as channel plus refusal seat | Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` Direction: a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." |
