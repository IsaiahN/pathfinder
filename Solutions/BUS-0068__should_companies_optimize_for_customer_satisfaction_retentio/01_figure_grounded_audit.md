## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question offers four objects of optimization: customer satisfaction, retention, profit per customer, and broader long-term trust. It is phrased as a choice among alternatives, as if one were a scalar objective and the others were lesser versions of it.

Stated in effect terms, the residual R is: **which of four available readings stays in contact with a market that does not move when the firm's own report of it moves, and what is lost when a reading that is only an instrument is promoted into an objective?** Each candidate produces a different action when optimized:

| candidate | what it reads | what optimizing it produces | what it cannot see |
|---|---|---|---|
| satisfaction | a customer's evaluative judgment at a moment | effort on the judged attributes, with thresholds and nonlinearity | whether a paying market continues to pay |
| retention | repeat purchase over a window | switching barriers, lock-in, habit, reacquisition offers | why the repeat happened, and whether the channel is still open |
| profit per customer | money received less cost, per slot | price and cost discipline, segmentation | the horizon and the state of the channel after the extraction |
| trust | expectation that the firm will not defect when it could | restraint, service recovery, promises kept | it is not itself a reading; it is a property of a channel plus a refusal seat |

The sought-for shape is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (formula step, Direction): **a separator**, a four-condition test that says which candidate may be promoted into an objective, which must stay an instrument, and what ground would have to be present for the promotion to be legitimate. A single universal winner is *not* the bound shape; the census below shows why that shape would be a category error.

An answer is expected to look like: per-slot ground reading plus a non-updating anchor plus a maintained channel plus a seat able to refuse. If that shape cannot be derived, the honest output is `gap`, not a guessed number.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** Four candidates. They are not four spellings of one quantity. Each has a different instrument and a different anchor, and the empirical literature finds them decoupling in specific subgroups (Keiningham et al. 2007/2008; Mittal et al. 2023). R is therefore a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a single hard question and not a partition (Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 9 :55).
- **Symmetry.** "Optimize for X" is an ordered relation, not a symmetric one. The four readings are themselves a mix: satisfaction and profit are quantities (ordering; Fig 13 :20), retention is a count over a window (a recording of past behavior), trust is a relation between a firm and a ground (Fig 11 :24-25). A correlation among them cannot choose the arrow (operators table: `+` is symmetric, `→` and `⇒` are not).
- **Scale.** The residual lives at the individual customer slot, exactly where the firm's aggregation destroys it. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A firm-wide CSAT, retention rate, ARPU, or trust index is a population reading, and the live slot is where the ground actually pays.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1, customer-measurement frame.** Actors: respondents, survey instruments (CSAT, NPS, SERVQUAL-style dimensions), analysts, the incentive system that rewards the score. Currency: reported judgments, correlations, score movements. Anchor candidate: the customer's own judgment, which is legitimate only for a question constitutively about persons (Fig 2 :36-38). For the firm's question the judgment is an instrument, and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). Verdict: instrument, not ground.

**D2, market-payment frame.** Actors: customers as payers, competitors as alternatives, the firm's price and cost structure, the account ledgers. Currency: money received, contribution margin, discount rate, switching. Anchor: the paying market, a "counter, a market, a patient outcome" in the figure's own list (Fig 2 :29). This is the only candidate that is constitutively about the anchor for a business question. Verdict: ground for the transaction, read per slot and over a horizon.

**D3, retention and habit frame.** Actors: repeat purchasers, contractual lock-in, switching costs, inertia, habit, service recovery. Currency: repeat-purchase counts, churn rate, share of wallet. Anchor candidate: none that is nameable. Repeat purchase is produced by several sources at once, delight, inertia, and sunk costs (Keiningham et al. 2008), so the reading cannot comment on its source (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, metamerism). A domain with no nameable anchor is not a ground domain yet. Verdict: recording, not generator; proxy with residual-relative valence.

**D4, trust and long-horizon frame.** Actors: the firm as promise-maker, the customer as one who could defect, the community and regulators as witnesses, a seat able to refuse a beneficial-looking action. Currency: expectation of future behavior, willingness to forgive a failure, willingness to pay a premium, and the state of the channel after a shock. Anchor: a ground that does not update on the firm's own report of its trustworthiness (Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :29), plus a seat that can refuse (Fig 10 :42). Verdict: channel-and-seat property, not a stock and not the fourth quantity to maximize. Capability of the pair, not of the firm in isolation (Fig 11 :39-40).

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union.** The four conditions overlap. Satisfaction can be a leading indicator of retention (Mittal et al. 2023, r = 0.60 for retention), retention can be produced by switching barriers rather than satisfaction (Ranaweera and Prabhu 2003), profit per customer can be raised by extracting from a locked-in customer, and trust can be high while satisfaction is merely "satisfying" (Ranaweera and Prabhu find even a satisfying service-recovery process may be inadequate to prevent loss of trust). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing: Fig 9 :55-56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Unbundle before searching again (Fig 9 :54). Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged (Fig 1 :27; Fig 2 :40).

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the firm tell a satisfied customer from a paying one, and a repeat purchase from a locked-in one? Satisfaction is a reading; retention is a reading; profit is a reading of money; trust is not a reading at all.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the firm have terms for what a customer affords, including a term for the bond between payment and future contact? A vocabulary of ingredients can name a state and cannot name a route (Fig 12 :111).
3. **Objective:** can it say what would count as progress in its own terms, with a non-updating reference? This is the break. Fig 3 :25-26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
4. **Planning:** a route to the objective under incomplete information. Only reachable after 3.
5. **Learn and carry:** verify, name what worked, and carry a method rather than a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22-25).

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20-21). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The objective link here has a metric but no instrument for whether the channel is still open; that is the broken link, and most improvement effort in practice lands above it (Fig 3 :29-30).

Dependency order for the loop itself: refuse a wrong objective before minting a new one (formula step 2; operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"), describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53), then settle each per-slot reading before composing a firm-level verdict (Fig 12 :104).

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The four options are a union, not one dial (decisive on question form)

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

The empirical record is exactly this shape. Keiningham et al. (2007, 2008) found that no single metric was well correlated with all loyalty behaviors: "The best predictor of share of spending was past share of spending, not recommend intention. The best predictor of retention was repurchase intention, not recommend intention." Mittal et al. (2023) found the satisfaction-to-retention association large in intention measures (r = 0.65) and small in behavioral measures (r = 0.21), and smaller at the firm level (profit r = 0.10) than at the customer level. Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) found trust and switching barriers each add effects beyond satisfaction. These are different subsets on which different rules work, which is the figure's definition of a union.

**Refuter:** show that one of the four readings predicts the others' anchors at every slot with a single stable coefficient across subgroups, or that a single lever changes all four in the same direction with no residue at the intersections. If that holds, the union verdict is withdrawn and a single-objective reading is owed.

### F2 — The residual is per customer, and the firm's average throws it away (decisive on scale)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

Every one of the four candidates is normally reported as a population statistic. A CSAT average, a churn percentage, an ARPU, and a trust index are each a single number that hides which slots are live. Mittal et al. (2023) report substantial heterogeneity across studies and moderators, with firm-level associations systematically smaller than customer-level ones, which is what aggregation does to a live signal. The ground, by contrast, pays per customer: one account pays or does not, one account defects or does not.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a firm for which a population-level score has the same decision value as the per-slot residual under a service failure concentrated in one small segment. If the average tracks the live slots, per-slot indexing is not load-bearing here and this finding is narrowed.

### F3 — Satisfaction is the right anchor for a different question, not this one (decisive on D1)

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

"Is this a good experience for this person?" is constitutively about human judgment, so satisfaction is the anchor for that question and no audit can override it. But "should the firm optimize this?" is constitutively about a paying market. For that question satisfaction is an instrument: it can be wrong on the axis the firm actually needs, willingness to pay and future contact. Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." A high satisfaction-to-retention correlation is efficient for a shared meaning and is not automatically a verdict about the market. Keiningham et al. (2007) found none of the metrics, including satisfaction, to be a good predictor of revenue growth with the methodology they replicated, while Mittal et al. (2023) found a positive but modest satisfaction-to-profit association (r = 0.10). Both are consistent with instrument status.

A second mechanism: if the firm acts on the score and then re-measures the score, the reference updates on the firm's own action. Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

**Refuter:** show a controlled setting in which the satisfaction reading does not move when the firm changes the attributes being judged, and in which it predicts payment and future contact at the slot with stable coefficients across subgroups. Then satisfaction would pass the anchor test and F3 would be withdrawn for that setting.

### F4 — Profit per customer is the ground reading, but it is a comparison over a horizon, not a total to extract (decisive on D2)

Fig 2 :29 lists "a counter, a market" as anchors. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 12 :87: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Operators :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Profit per customer is the only candidate that is itself a reading of the anchor: money actually received less the cost of serving that customer. It is a quantity, and the objective built over it is a comparison (contribution margin against a threshold, now against later). It must be read per slot, because the aggregate hides the live account (F2). But maximizing the current reading can sever the channel: raise price, cut service, or lock in a customer, and the ground does not decay while the channel does. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 10 :28: "you have lost touch."

Rust, Lemon and Zeithaml (2004) are the closest world corroboration. Their customer-equity model computes lifetime value per individual customer before averaging, explicitly includes brand switching and competition, and treats the firm's question as return on marketing expenditure: the objective is a discounted comparison over a horizon, not current margin. Their airline example prices a 0.2-point quality improvement at a projected 44.7% ROI, and their grocery loyalty-program example returns minus 12.5%, which is the same quantity read with a different bond.

**Refuter:** produce a firm where raising current per-customer margin leaves the measured channel state and the future payment distribution unchanged at every slot, across a full horizon. If that holds, the horizon constraint on profit is empty and F4 narrows to "profit is the ground for the transaction."

### F5 — Retention is a recording of a behavior whose source the metric cannot comment on (decisive on D3)

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Repeat purchase is a recording, not a generator. It tells you what happened, not which mechanism produced it. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22) gives the instrument argument: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"; an instrument that reads the behavior "cannot comment on the source"; and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." Keiningham et al. (2008) name the sources explicitly, inertia, delight and sunk costs, and find that the best predictor of retention is repurchase intention rather than recommend intention, while the best predictor of share of spending is past share of spending. Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) find switching barriers have a significant positive effect on retention and moderate the satisfaction-to-retention relation, so a retained customer can be retained for a reason that has no relation to future willingness to pay.

D3 has no nameable anchor. By the census rule (a domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet), retention is not a separate ground; it is a reading whose valence depends on the residual. It is a proxy, not an objective.

**Refuter:** find an instrument that discriminates among the sources of a repeat purchase at the slot without changing the behavior being measured, and show that the resulting source label predicts future margin better than the repeat count. If that succeeds, retention is promoted from recording to instrument with a stated source, and F5 is narrowed, not erased.

### F6 — Trust is a channel-and-seat property, not the fourth stock (decisive on D4)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :27: "the output is not a verdict, it is". Fig 10 :28: "you have lost touch". Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Trust cannot be optimized as a stock, because nothing in the figure set makes it a quantity held by the firm alone. It is the state of the channel to a ground that does not update, plus a seat able to refuse a profitable-looking defection (Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"). A firm that improves its trust score without changing what happens when it could defect has improved an agent in isolation and changed nothing (Fig 11 :40). Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) supply the discriminating observation: "Even a 'satisfying' service recovery process might be inadequate to prevent loss of trust." That is a source-discriminating test, not a survey of trust.

This is a correction to my own first pass, kept visible in section 6: I initially treated trust as a fourth quantity to be balanced against the other three. Re-census assigns it to the channel and the refusal seat, which are properties of the pair (firm, market), not a term in the firm's objective function.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that returns a trust reading which (a) does not move when the firm changes its own reported trust, (b) moves when the firm defects where it could, and (c) predicts future per-slot margin across subgroups. If such an instrument exists, trust becomes a measurable state and F6 narrows to "trust is measurable but still not a scalar objective." If no instrument can be specified, F6 stands and the honest report is that trust is an unmeasured channel condition.

### F7 — Promoting an instrument into an objective is the named convention failure

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority." Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 2 :34: "the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

When a survey score decides bonuses, pricing, and investment, it stops being a convention for shared meaning and becomes an outcome-fixing authority. Fig 5 :26 asks "does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?", and Fig 5 :44 says a term that explains one occasion perfectly fails the bargain. The AI-safety literature gives the low-overlap corroboration from an entirely different field. Amodei et al. (2016) define reward hacking: "the objective function that the designer writes down admits of some clever 'easy' solution that formally maximizes it but perverts the spirit of the designer's intent", and quote Goodhart's law: "when a metric is used as a target, it ceases to be a good metric." They also describe the feedback-loop case in which "the original intent of the objective function ... gets drowned out by the positive feedback inherent in the deployment strategy", which is Fig 2 :34 in engineering clothing.

**Refuter:** show a metric whose correlation with the paying ground does not degrade under strong optimization of that metric, measured over several optimization cycles and across slots. Goodhart-type breakdown is well documented, so this refuter is deliberately hard to satisfy; if it is satisfied for a particular metric in a particular firm, that metric is promoted for that firm and the general finding is narrowed to the others.

### F8 — The firm typically breaks at the objective link, not at perception or planning

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :22: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :30: "Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."

The firm can measure satisfaction and retention readily (perception), can name them (vocabulary), but often cannot say what would count as progress in its own terms against a non-updating ground (objective), because it has no instrument for the state of the channel. Planning effort then arrives at a step that cannot use it. Keiningham et al. (2008) put the practical version plainly: metrics are "only guidelines", and "a metric can't reduce complex, multifaceted constructs to one or two dimensions". That is the middle-link failure: terms for what a situation affords without a grounded account of progress.

**Refuter:** show a firm whose satisfaction and retention instruments are demonstrably broken while its objective link is sound and measured. Then the break is not at the objective for that firm and F8 is re-censused to that firm's actual chain.

### F9 — No figure line returns a universal objective, weight, or horizon (declared gap)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

No figure line selects 60% satisfaction and 40% profit, or a three-year horizon, or a discount rate. Those are imports from a measurement frame with its own provenance. Under Fig 6 :24, "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." A universal allocation would be an import with no source gate and no shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52). This is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, and "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence": the separator is reported instead of a number, and the instrument that would close it is named in section 5.

**Refuter:** derive a universal weight from the figure lines without importing any business quantity. If that is possible, F9 is withdrawn and the answer gains a number. Until then, any number is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

### F10 — Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged

Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

An average of satisfaction, retention, profit and trust is a frame-internal composite scored by quantities the frame itself produces. A correlation among the four is one evidence pool talking when all four are read from the same customer at the same moment. The world literature confirms the structural point: Mittal et al. (2023) call for studies that examine multiple outcomes simultaneously and explicitly report that measurement characteristics, not just the construct, change the effect size. A single "customer health" score would erase exactly the moderators that carry the signal.

**Refuter:** show four independent instruments, each ranged separately against a non-updating ground with no shared source, whose verdicts agree. Agreement under independence would be corroboration, not a pool, and the four verdicts could then be composed. Until then they stay separate.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The whole separator is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Union refuter.** A single one of the four readings predicts the others' anchors at every slot with stable coefficients across exchange type, market type and measurement type, and a single lever changes all four with no residue at the intersections. (F1)
2. **Per-slot refuter.** A firm-level population score has the same decision value as its per-slot residual under a concentrated service failure. (F2)
3. **Satisfaction-anchor refuter.** A satisfaction reading neither moves when the firm changes the judged attributes nor fails to predict payment and future contact at the slot across subgroups. (F3)
4. **Ground refuter.** Raising current profit per customer leaves the channel state and the future payment distribution unchanged at every slot over a full horizon. (F4)
5. **Retention-source refuter.** An instrument discriminates among the sources of repeat purchase, and the source label predicts future margin better than the repeat count. (F5)
6. **Trust-instrument refuter.** A trust reading exists that does not move with the firm's own report, moves with defection where defection was possible, and predicts future per-slot margin. (F5/F6 boundary; if it fires, F6 narrows, it does not flip the no-scalar verdict.)
7. **Goodhart refuter.** A metric's correlation with the paying ground does not degrade under repeated strong optimization of that metric. (F7)
8. **Break-location refuter.** A firm's perception and vocabulary instruments are broken while its objective link is measured and sound. (F8)
9. **Weight derivation refuter.** A universal objective weight or horizon is derived from the figure lines with no imported business quantity. (F9)

If 1, 3, 4, or 9 fires, the core separator is withdrawn. If 2, 5, 6, 7, or 8 fires, the relevant finding is narrowed and re-censused, and the correction is made visible rather than erased. No part of this document may be labelled `grounded` until a firm's own data has been ranged against at least one of these conditions.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Promote profit per customer into the objective only as a per-slot comparison over a horizon, with the paying market as the non-updating anchor.** Fig 1 :19 for the slot, Fig 2 :29 for the anchor, Operators :51 for the comparison, Fig 10 :24 for the horizon. This is `specified`, not `grounded`.
2. **Keep satisfaction and retention as instruments with declared blind spots, never as outcome-fixing conventions.** Fig 10 :47, Fig 2 :38, Fig 4 :25. Satisfaction is the anchor for a different question and retention cannot comment on its source.
3. **Treat trust as a maintained channel plus a seat able to refuse, and give it an instrument or admit it is unmeasured.** A candidate discriminator comes from Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003): "Even a 'satisfying' service recovery process might be inadequate to prevent loss of trust." The figure grounds are Fig 10 :24, :42 and Fig 11 :40.
4. **Unbundle the union before choosing any lever.** Fig 9 :54-56. A lever that lifts satisfaction may leave the switching-barrier intersection standing; a lever that lifts retention may deepen a habit that does not pay.
5. **State the gap instead of filling it with a number.** Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135. What is owed is a longitudinal, per-customer dataset that records margin, channel state, and defection after a defection opportunity, read by frames that do not share its evidence pool.
6. **Send the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." `08_expert_verification_packet.md` carries the separator and its refuters in front of the links.
