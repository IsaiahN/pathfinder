## What the wider literature adds, and what it does not

1. **Decoupling is the empirical counterpart of the union verdict.** Keiningham et al. (2007/2008) show that different metrics predict different loyalty behaviors and that none is a good predictor of revenue growth under the methodology they replicated. Mittal et al. (2023) show the satisfaction-to-outcome coefficient changes with the measurement of the outcome and with the level of analysis. That is Fig 9 :55-56 in data: one lever leaves the intersections standing.
2. **Customer equity is the F4 shape, not a universal objective.** Rust et al. (2004) compute CLV per customer before averaging, include brand switching, and project return on expenditure. That is the per-slot, comparison-over-a-horizon reading. It imports a discount rate, margin and time horizon from the finance frame; those imports need provenance, and they are not supplied by the figures.
3. **Trust is a source-discrimination problem.** Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) show trust and switching barriers act beyond satisfaction, and that a merely satisfying service recovery may still lose trust. That is the discriminator the Hard Problem essay says an instrument must provide, not a survey score.
4. **Proxy promotion fails in a low-overlap field.** Amodei et al. (2016) reach the Goodhart failure from engineering, not marketing. Their "when a metric is used as a target, it ceases to be a good metric" is an independent corroborator of Fig 10 :47. It does not *decide* the question; the figures do.
5. **Satisfaction-productivity tradeoffs are conditional, not universal.** Anderson, Fornell and Rust (1997) find a positive association for goods and a negative one for services. Hogreve et al. (2018) challenge the "always maximize" rationale of the service-profit chain. Both corroborate per-domain verdicts rather than a single optimum.

Overlap declaration: the marketing sources (Keiningham, Mittal, Rust, Anderson et al., Hogreve, Ranaweera and Prabhu) are one broad evidence pool, not seven independent witnesses; Keiningham 2007 and 2008 are one research program. Amodei et al. is the genuinely low-overlap corroborator. The framework essays share the author's evidence pool with the figures completely, so their agreement is expected and carries no independent witness weight. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered; the two tensions noted in `01` section 6 were returned to the census and narrowed rather than used to flip a verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the separator and its refuter first. Sending the full framework before a recipient has described their own residual sends a recording of one occasion, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42** — "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework interior before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them any of the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** — "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not select recipients for receptiveness and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :35** — "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Seek a room that differs from the last and matches the residual it surfaced.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its refuter; (2) the question, what would they count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only if curious, the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method for their own problem, with the instruction to write a refuter and state a mode first.
