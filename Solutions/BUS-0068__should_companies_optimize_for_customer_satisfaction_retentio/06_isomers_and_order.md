# Isomers and order: structure among the four objectives

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the ordering rule, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` establishes that the four candidates form a union with different anchors and that no universal weight is available. A second derivation is owed: if the four are written over one fixed set of atoms, are they **isomers** (same atoms, different bond, different substance), members of one **functional group**, or unrelated compositions? And what is their **order** of dependency? An answer to either question must carry its own falsifier, because a structural analogy can be made to fit anything once the residual is described loosely (Fig 9 :27-28).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available from the business frame: **customer slot, purchase event, payment, cost, promise, response to a failure, competitor alternative, time window**. Bonds available from the figures: `+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction, `≡` identity, `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, and unary `¬` negation (Fig 12 :49; operators table).

Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

The four objectives written as bonded arrangements over those atoms:

| objective | arrangement | bond type | what the arrangement records |
|---|---|---|---|
| satisfaction | customer + response to an experience, read as a judgment | conjunction plus a reading comparison (`⋛`) | a state at a moment |
| retention | purchase(event t) → purchase(event t+1) over a window | sequence (`→`) on a recording | a past behavior repeated |
| profit per customer | payment − cost, then ⋛ against a threshold over a horizon | subtraction (`−`) plus comparison (`⋛`) | a quantity against a goal |
| trust | promise → (opportunity to defect) and ¬defection, ranged against a ground | sequence plus negation (`¬`), with a refusal seat | a relation, not a reading |

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the four objectives isomers over the fixed atom set? NO, as written

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Written as in the table, the four do **not** share one atom set. Satisfaction needs a response-to-experience atom that retention does not require; trust needs a promise-and-defection-opportunity atom that profit does not require. They are not isomers under the figure's definition. They are different molecules over overlapping but non-identical atom sets, which is why the question is a union and not a menu of arrangements of one thing (Fig 9 :54-56).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if every one of the four objectives can be written over exactly `{customer slot, payment, purchase event, competitor alternative, time window}` with no additional atom, differing only in which typed bond is used, then the isomer claim holds and the union verdict in `01` F1 must be re-censused toward a single arrangement question. Until such a writing is produced, the no-isomer finding stands.

### Q2 — Do they share a functional group? YES, weakly

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

All four are measurements taken at a customer slot over a time window. That shared substructure fixes the *kind* of thing they are: per-slot readings of a firm-customer relation. The qualifier selects which member: judgment, repetition, money, or expectation. So they are members of one functional group in the weak sense that they share a measurement scaffold. The group does not make them interchangeable; Fig 11 :49 says "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries," and the same holds for a reading.

**Falsifier:** show a case in which two of the four are measured at different slots or different windows and the shared scaffold no longer fixes any common outcome. Then the functional-group claim fails and the four are simply unrelated metrics.

### Q3 — The real isomer claim is inside retention, not across the four

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."

Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) give the clean case. Over the same atoms (a customer, a repeat purchase, a competing alternative, a switching barrier, a trust expectation, a service failure), two firms can produce the same retention count with different bonds:

- **Retention by barrier:** customer → repeat purchase, with competitor alternative − (removed or made costly). The bond is subtraction.
- **Retention by trust:** customer → repeat purchase, with promise → failure → repair, ranged against a ground that does not update. The bond is sequence plus a comparison, with a refusal seat.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The behavior in the count is the same; the downstream distribution after a service failure is not. The paper reports that trust has a significant effect on retention, that switching barriers also have a significant positive effect and moderate the satisfaction-to-retention relation, and that "Even a 'satisfying' service recovery process might be inadequate to prevent loss of trust." That last sentence is the discriminator.

This is the isomer claim worth testing, and it is `specified`, not `grounded`, because the discriminating instrument is a survey-based design, not a direct observation of the bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if barrier-retained and trust-retained customers show the same defection distribution after a service failure, at the same margin and the same satisfaction score, then the bond makes no behavioral difference and the isomer claim fails. The two retention modes collapse into one functional group with two labels.

### Q4 — Catalyst versus spent part

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A retained customer who continues to pay after a failure is a catalyst: the relationship survives its own use and regenerates the channel. A retained customer held by a barrier is a spent part: the retention is consumed once and does not regenerate willingness to pay. The same retention count covers both, which is exactly the Fig 12 :5 failure of an ingredient-only notation. Fig 11 :49 makes the classification residual-relative: the same customer is a benefactor to a margin residual and a malfactor to a channel residual.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate a retained customer whose repeated purchases neither regenerate nor deplete the channel, with measured channel state unchanged across uses. Then the catalyst/spent-part distinction has no purchase and is dropped for that case.

### Q5 — What order governs the four

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Operators :46: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."

The dependency order is:

1. **Perception** (customer slots are distinguishable and their payments are readable).
2. **Vocabulary** (the firm has a term for the bond between a reading and a future payment, not just the reading itself).
3. **Objective** (a comparison against a non-updating ground, not a score to be maximized).
4. **Planning** (a route under incomplete information).
5. **Learn and carry** (verify, name what worked, carry the method and not the recording).

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The missing instrument is at link 3 for the trust case and at link 2 for the retention case (a count without a source label). Effort spent on satisfaction surveys (link 1, where the measurements already exist) cannot rescue a break at link 3 (Fig 3 :19, :30).

A second order governs the loop itself: refuse before mint. Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." If a firm has found that a metric decides outcomes it should not decide (Fig 10 :47), the first move is a refusal of the metric's standing, not the minting of a composite replacement. Nothing is deleted by a refusal; the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :42-43).

### Q6 — Two currencies, and why a blended score hides the shortfall

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A customer-health score compresses four descriptions cheaply (few bits) but each additional reading costs actions to verify against a ground. Fig 12 :129 says "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind." A blended KPI reports the bits and ignores the actions, which is why it can look precise and fail to predict defection.

**Falsifier:** show that the verification cost of the four readings is zero or perfectly proportional to the description cost in a given firm. Then the two-currency caveat is idle for that firm and the blended score is permissible there.

## CONCLUSION

The four objectives are not isomers as written; they share only a weak functional group (per-slot measurement over a window). The genuine isomer claim is internal to retention: barrier-retained and trust-retained customers can hold the same retention count under different bonds, with different downstream behavior. A retained customer is a catalyst or a spent part depending on whether the relationship regenerates the channel, and that classification is residual-relative. The governing order is dependency, not importance, with the break at the objective link, and the loop requires a refusal before a composite mint. Every claim here is `specified`, with its falsifier written before the run, and none is a ground reading.
