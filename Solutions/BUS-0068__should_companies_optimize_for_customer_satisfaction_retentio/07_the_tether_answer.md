# The Tether's answer on customer metrics and objectives

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal number. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a company result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The four candidates are a **union of overlapping conditions**, not one dial. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Each candidate reads a different anchor: a judgment, a behavior, a payment, and a channel relation. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :27, Fig 9 :60).

## 2. The shape

1. **Read the residual per customer, not per population.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A firm-wide CSAT, churn rate, ARPU or trust index hides the live account. The ground pays per account.

2. **Profit per customer is the ground reading for the transaction, and the objective over it is a comparison over a horizon.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Operators :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." So promote profit per customer into the objective only as a per-slot margin compared across a horizon that includes the state of the channel. Raising current margin by cutting service or locking in a customer can leave the ground intact and sever the channel.

3. **Satisfaction is the right anchor for a different question and only an instrument for this one.** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." "Is this a good experience for this person?" is about people, so satisfaction settles that question. "Should the firm optimize this?" is about a market, so satisfaction is an instrument that can be blind to willingness to pay. If the score moves when the firm changes the judged attributes, it has stopped being a reference: Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."

4. **Retention is a recording, not a generator, and cannot name its own source.** Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." A repeat purchase can come from delight, habit, inertia or a switching barrier. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Keiningham et al. (2008) find different metrics predict different loyalty behaviors and none predicts all. Ranaweera and Prabhu (2003) find switching barriers retain even dissatisfied customers. Keep retention as a proxy with valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), never as the objective.

5. **Trust is re-grounded as a maintained channel plus a seat able to refuse, not a stock to maximize.** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A trust score that does not change when the firm defects where it could is not measuring trust. Capability belongs to the firm-and-market pair, and the refusal seat is part of the channel condition.

6. **Do not promote a metric into an outcome-fixing convention.** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." When a survey score decides bonuses and investment, it becomes an authority nobody can check. Amodei et al. (2016), from engineering, put the same failure as "when a metric is used as a target, it ceases to be a good metric." A composite customer-health score prices the description cheaply and hides the action cost of verifying it (Fig 12 :125-126).

7. **No universal weight, horizon or allocation is available; that is a `gap`, not a silence.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The separator is reported instead of a number.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that satisfaction is worthless. It is the anchor for a question about a person and a blind instrument for a question about payment.
- Not that retention is useless. It is a recording whose source must be named before it can be promoted.
- Not that trust is a fourth quantity to balance. It is a channel state and a refusal seat, held by the pair.
- Not that profit per customer may be extracted without a horizon. The channel can fall while the ground does not.
- Not a universal percentage, weighting, discount rate or time horizon. Those are imports with no source gate and no shadow test.
- Not `grounded`. No firm's data was read, and the discriminator for trust is still owed.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A longitudinal, per-customer dataset that records margin, channel state, defection after a defection opportunity, and the source of each repeat purchase, read by instruments that do not share the firm's own report as evidence. The specific tests are the pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4. The decisive ones:

- **Source discriminator:** does an instrument distinguish barrier-retained from trust-retained customers, and do their post-failure defection distributions differ? If not, the isomer claim in `06` fails.
- **Horizon test:** does raising current per-customer margin leave future margin and channel state unchanged at every slot? If yes, F4 narrows.
- **Anchor test:** does a satisfaction reading move when the firm changes only the judged attributes? If yes, it is not an anchor.
- **Goodhart test:** does a metric's correlation with payment survive repeated strong optimization of that metric?

Anything less, a survey, a consensus of executives, a famous practitioner's endorsement, is a frame, not a ground (Fig 2 :35; Fig 7 :35).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The figure set returns a shape and a route, then stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage. Indiscernibility is not identity. The census cannot say which primitive is missing (Fig 13 :135), but it can say what the missing instrument must do: discriminate sources, stay unmoved by the firm's own report, and read the channel rather than the score. That description travels to a frame that may hold it (Fig 8 :54).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner among satisfaction, retention, profit per customer and trust. It says the question is a union of four conditions with different anchors, so averaging them into a customer-health score throws away the live signal. Read the residual per customer. Profit per customer is the ground reading for the transaction, but it only becomes an objective when it is a comparison across a horizon that includes the state of the channel. Satisfaction is the right anchor for a question about a person and only an instrument for a question about payment. Retention is a recording of a behavior whose source the count cannot discriminate. Trust is not a stock; it is the state of a channel to a market that does not update on the firm's own report, plus a seat able to refuse a profitable-looking defection. No figure returns a universal weight, so the answer stops at a shape with falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close it is named rather than invented.
