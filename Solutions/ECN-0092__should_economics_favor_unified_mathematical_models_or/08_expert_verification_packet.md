# Expert verification packet: the Tether on unified versus tailored economic models

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economic methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the economic prediction, not `grounded`.** No measurement settles a disciplinary prescription. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "one unified model or many tailored models" is a bundle of three conditions that have been treated as one: a shared substrate, one model closing every slot, and whether added models add evidence or only vocabulary. Since the conditions overlap, the answer must split them and keep the three verdicts separate. What survives: unity of method and substrate across models, per-question residuals that are never averaged into a global score, no final all-purpose model, and a rule that many models buy surplus only when independently grounded at low overlap. Comparing settled models is a second-order composition needing no new primitive. The preference for unity is written down as a testable prediction, not asserted as a verdict. Four refuters were registered before the derivation; if any fires, the claim is withdrawn.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9, 7 and 13
- **Statement:** the question is a union compound; a final all-purpose model is refused; a shared substrate is not a terminal frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"*. Fig 7 :30-31: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"*. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a terminal economic frame from which no further question can be asked, derive a seventh primitive from the six, or show that the three conditions close on one residual so that the split is gratuitous.

### C2 : economics-facing, checkable against model comparison practice
- **Statement:** residuals must be reported per slot; model count is not evidence; low overlap with separate ground contact is what buys surplus.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 8 :48-49: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check:** take a proposed unified model and a tailored set. Are per-slot residuals published for each? Are the added models calibrated on different evidence? If overlap is held constant and count is varied, does surplus actually rise?
- **Refuter:** a unified model with no positive live residual on any slot; or a controlled comparison in which model count increases surplus while overlap is fixed; or two same-evidence models giving independent corroboration.

### C3 : framework-internal derivation with a named strain
- **Statement:** the kernel is unified while rooms are plural; comparing settled models is second order; the ⋛ operand type at second order remains open.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. Operators table :49: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.
- **Refuter:** two economic questions requiring different kernels with no common loop while both settle against the ground; or a demonstration that a settled model cannot serve as an atom; or that ⋛ must always take bare quantities, which would make the second-order comparison illegitimate.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that mathematical formalisation is bad for economics. The loop, substrate and operators are the shared method.
- Not that pluralism is automatically productive. High-overlap models add vocabulary, not evidence.
- Not that a bounded unified model cannot be useful. It can be settled as an atom at its own level; the refusal is of a terminal frame.
- Not that a famous economist's endorsement settles anything. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a whole discipline's method is claimed.
- Not a claim about which specific models economics should keep. That is a name; the framework returns a shape.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating comparison, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. Publish per-slot residuals for a unified model and a tailored set. Does either leave a live positive residual where the other closes one?
2. Hold calibration data fixed, vary model count, vary overlap separately. Which parameter actually buys surplus?
3. Find a comparison that discriminates convergent derivation from imported assumptions. Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.
4. Test the kernel claim directly: are there economic questions whose settled methods share no common loop?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 8 :48-49 and Fig 13 :115, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own field: when models are added, is the gain coming from independence or from count?

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
