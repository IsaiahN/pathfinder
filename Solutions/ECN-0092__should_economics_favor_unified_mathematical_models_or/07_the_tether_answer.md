# The Tether's answer: unified models or many tailored models in economics

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the economic prediction, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Economics has live per-question residuals. The same shock, given to twelve leading macro models under common assumptions, produces significantly different answers. Disagreement does not shrink with effort inside one model; each rule works well on a different subset. Option U (one unified model) proposes to close the whole set with one frame. Option P (many tailored models) proposes a frame per question. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The question is a union compound, not one choice, and the answer must keep the domain verdicts separate.

## 2. The shape

1. **There is no final unified economic model.** Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*; Fig 7 :30-31: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"*. Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."* A model after which nothing further can be asked is the terminal room the figures forbid. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Unity at the level of primitives is not unity at the level of models.

2. **The kernel and substrate are unified; models and rooms are plural.** Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* So the dichotomy is false at the level the figures govern. Share the loop, the substrate and the operators across models; do not share a single conclusion.

3. **Residuals must be reported per question, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A unified model's single global fit score hides exactly the signal that says where to look. The *Society of Agents* essay makes the same move for method choice: a method brilliant in one setting must not be drowned by an average across all of them.

4. **Many models earn their keep only at low overlap with separate ground contact.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* But Fig 8 :48-49: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40 says agreement about the world is worthless when agreeing frames share an evidence pool. Model count is a filter for the budget; independence is a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

5. **Comparing settled models is second order and needs no new primitive.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. Each model settles against its own ground first, then becomes an operand for comparison. The arrangement between models is priced information: Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

6. **The preference for unity must be bound as an ordinary term.** Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."* Ask what a unified model would predict that tailored models would not, write it down, and let it fail. Until then "unification" is a taste, not a result.

7. **Provenance decides whether a common assumption was converged upon or imported.** Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* A model that absorbs another school's assumptions must record the import; otherwise the contents alone cannot discriminate.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that mathematics is bad for economics. The kernel is shared and the substrate is the same; mathematics is part of the common method.
- Not that pluralism is automatically good. High-overlap plurality adds vocabulary, not evidence.
- Not that a unified model cannot be useful for a bounded domain. It can be settled as an atom at its own level; what is refused is the claim to be the final frame.
- Not that one school should dominate the checking arrangement. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- Not a derivation of any particular economic theory, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument settles a disciplinary prescription. World sources corroborate; they do not decide.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground and the pre-registered refuter

The refuter was written before the conclusion in `01` section 4. The relevant ground readings are:

1. **Global residual test.** Publish a per-slot residual table for a proposed unified model and for the tailored set. If the unified model leaves no positive live residual on any slot, the per-slot claim fails.
2. **Last room test.** Exhibit an economic frame from which no further question can be asked, or derive a seventh primitive from the six. Either breaks the no-final-model claim.
3. **Independence test.** Hold the calibration dataset fixed and add models with the same evidence. If surplus grows with model count while overlap is held constant, the independence claim fails. If two same-evidence models corroborate each other independently, Fig 2 :40 is wrong here.
4. **Kernel test.** Show two economic questions with different kernels and no common loop, both settled against the ground. The unity-of-method claim fails.
5. **Source-discriminating comparison.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*. Here, an instrument that shows whether two models with the same outputs arrived by convergent derivation or by imported assumptions would name what the outputs alone cannot.

A source-discriminating comparison is the general form of the ground. Agreement among methodologists is not one.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books* says: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Indiscernibility is not identity. One strain remains live, the operand type of ⋛ at second order (Operators table :49: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*); it is named, not smoothed.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side between one unified economic model and many tailored ones, because the question is a bundle of three questions that should be split. What survives the split: a shared kernel and substrate across models, per-question residuals that are never averaged into one global score, a firm refusal of any final all-purpose model (there is no last room), and a rule that many models buy evidence only when they are independently grounded and draw on different data. A pile of models calibrated on the same evidence is one witness stated many times. Comparing settled models is a second-order composition that needs no new primitive, and the preference for unity has to be written down as a testable prediction before it can count as anything but taste. What the answer offers is the shape any candidate regime must have, four refuters written in advance, and the comparisons that would close it. The choice of which models to keep stays open until the ground settles it.
