## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Union or partition?

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.

The three conditions are: shared substrate (C1), single-model closure of all slots (C2), and independence of added models (C3). C1 is a partition question about what the loop assumes. C2 is a reachability question about one closure. C3 is a union question about overlap between closures. They overlap because a school can share C1 and still differ on C2 and C3. Therefore the whole is a **union compound** and the three loops must be kept separate.

*Library of Babel* gives the same instruction in prose: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If a single residual closes all three conditions at once, so that the answer to C1 determines C2 and C3, the union reading fails and the question is a partition after all.

### Q2 : Are two economic models isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Two models tailored to different questions (say a labour-supply model and a monetary-transmission model) usually do **not** share an atom set in the sense Fig 12 requires; they share only the pattern *residual -> model -> ground*. By Fig 12 :123 that makes them members of the same **functional group**, not isomers. The group says what kind (a settled frame ranged against a ground); the qualifier says which member (labour, money, growth).

The stronger claim is available but harder. If both models are written over the full shared atom set of the methodology frame (same loop, same substrate, same operators), differing only in which bond mediates which residual, then they are genuine isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* That is the checkable isomer claim, and it is a claim about methodology, not about which model is empirically better.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If either model can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. This is the same test form the exemplar used for GR and QM; here it is applied to model families.

### Q3 : What bond does the "unified model" attempt use?

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

A proposal to unify economics by listing all the variables that all models use is a flat formula. It records ingredients and not arrangement. That is Fig 12's "collapse 6: the flat formula": *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*. Under a flat + (conjunction) the models are one list; under ≡ (identity) they are declared the same thing; neither bond carries the information that made them different. A regime of tailored models with a priced, typed bond between them keeps that information.

The comparison bond ⋛ is defined in the Operators table :49 as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**, and :51 adds *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. Comparing model A and model B on a residual is a comparison; declaring A ≡ B is an identity claim that needs its own ground.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If two models with identical atoms and different bonds make identical predictions on every slot, the bond carries nothing and Fig 12's pricing claim does not apply to model choice. Then the flat formula is not a collapse for this domain.

### Q4 : What is the order of the union?

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

Each model is settled against its own ground first. Only then can it serve as an atom for a second-order comparison. So the order is:

```
level 1:  residual -> model -> ground        (per question, one room each)
level 2:  (settled model A) ⋛ (settled model B)   (comparison, second order)
level 3:  methods distilled from level 2 cross up to the commons
```

This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import and not a new primitive. *How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the same order in institutional form: the room closes, then *"The commons, across problems. It holds only methods distilled from finished rooms."*

**Type caveat, still open.** The Operators table restricts ⋛ to two quantities. Whether two settled models-now-atoms count as quantities at that level is undecidable here. It needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain in `01` F7, not hidden.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** Show that a settled model cannot serve as an atom, or that comparison must always operate on bare quantities. That would make level 2 illegitimate and the union unordered.

### Q5 : Does adding a model add surplus or only spelling?

Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Fig 8 :48-49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** Fig 8 :14: *"their union adds nothing"*.

Hold overlap constant and vary count: no surplus follows from count alone. Hold count constant and vary overlap: surplus appears at low overlap. Therefore the operative variable is independence (Fig 2 :31-32), and model count is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

**Falsifier, written before the run.** A controlled comparison in which model count increases surplus while overlap is held constant falsifies this. That is a real empirical test: hold the calibration dataset fixed, add models with the same evidence, and see whether the union closes a new slot.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound, so it must be split before search (Q1). Two tailored models are normally members of a **functional group** rather than isomers, but become a real isomer claim when both are written over the full shared methodology atom set (Q2). "Unification" attempted as a flat ingredient list uses the wrong bond; the arrangement is the substance (Q3). Model comparison is second order and legal by Fig 12 :104, with a live operand-type strain (Q4). Adding models buys surplus only at low overlap, so count is a budget filter, never a verdict (Q5).

This rescues what is defensible in both options without taking either as stated: a shared kernel and shared method across models, and separate independently grounded rooms per question. It needs no new primitive, no terminal frame and no seventh term. What remains unproven is the operand type of ⋛ at second order and whether a given pair of models shares the full atom set.
