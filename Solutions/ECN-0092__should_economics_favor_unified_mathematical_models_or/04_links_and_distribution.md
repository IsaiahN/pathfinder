## How the wider literature bears on the verdict

The literature is secondary. It corroborates but does not decide, and no conflict triggered a reversal.

1. **The no-final-model claim.** The *Economic model* article states that current models *"do not pretend to be theories of everything economic"* and attributes this to computational infeasibility and missing theories for types of behaviour. This matches the figure verdict at F2 without sharing the framework's vocabulary. Effective field theory gives a second, lower-overlap instance from physics: scale-relative models are normal practice even in a field far more unified than economics.
2. **The per-slot claim.** The Brookings 12-model comparison and the *"profusion of separate models"* line are the empirical face of Fig 1 :19. Hayek's dispersed-knowledge argument, as reported in the same article, is a second route to the same shape from political economy.
3. **The independence claim.** The no-free-lunch result says no method dominates when averaged over all problems and that matching method to problem is what buys performance. Its own caveat about exploitable structure is recorded: this is an analogy for model choice, not a proof against all unification.
4. **A source that leans the other way.** The SEP entry records Friedman's 1974 unification account of explanation. It is included because a verdict written only from agreeing sources is worthless. It does not fire any refuter in `01` section 4, so it is recorded as a live tension in the methodology domain, not smoothed away.
5. **A venue already asking the question.** ICAPE's 2027 call asks whether pluralist economists are moving toward a unified approach and whether unity would reduce variation. The discipline is already running the experiment the figures describe; the contribution on offer is a split of the question and four written refuters.

**Overlap warning.** Wikipedia's *Pluralism in economics* repeats ICAPE's statement of purpose. Those two rows in `10_source_ledger.csv` are one witness wearing two names. The effective-sample-size logic is Fig 2 :31-32 applied to sources, not a rhetorical flourish.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source material.
- **Fig 2 :35:** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 7 :35:** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* Do not turn a list of recipients into evidence.

**Order in a reply:** (1) the split question, the shape claim and the four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the public links, with the note that the figures are the authority, not the folder summary; (4) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction to write a refuter before running it.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1, before any theory.** Send the residual in effect terms, the three-way split, the shape claim and the four pre-registered refuters. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement. Record the verdict before stage 2. This is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- **Stage 2, opt-in.** Only if the recipient is curious after stage 1, send the figures repository, the canonical README and the local verbatim extract. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says agreement from a shared evidence pool is the pool talking.
- **Stage 3, method reuse.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because the source article warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula"*.

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. If a recipient shows that a single model closes every slot, or that a terminal frame exists, or that high-overlap models corroborate each other, or that the kernel differs by question, the shape is withdrawn before any theory is offered.
