# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are copied verbatim into the message. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about phase transitions, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms.** A model predicts a collective state from local interaction
> rules. One rule is changed by a small amount. Sometimes the collective state barely moves;
> sometimes it jumps, a cascade crosses the system, or the attractor switches. I am asking what
> separates those two regimes. The candidate answer is not "how big the change is." It is "where
> the change lands."
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures say an interaction rule is a bond, a
> typed relation between two operands. Same atoms under a different bond is a different
> substance, not a small variant of the old one. They also say a cycle is a structure whose
> output re-enters as input, and that the sign of the loop decides whether it closes; a chain
> cannot close by construction. And they say a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome is
> a functional group. Put together, the shape is: a small change crosses into an abrupt
> collective transition when it lands on a load-bearing bond inside a re-entrant amplifying
> loop, the per-slot residual compounds through that loop, and a functional group fixes which
> new state wins. A pure feed-forward chain with no compounding remainder should respond
> smoothly to the same change.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming.** I am not giving you a critical exponent, a value of
> δ_c, a universality class, or a universal threshold. The figures return a shape, not a name
> and not a number. Those quantities have to be imported from your field with provenance, and
> they are not figure-derived. I am also not claiming that Fig 13's word "chaos" is the same
> thing as a phase transition; the shared structure is amplification of a remainder, not
> identity. And I am not claiming this settles any real system: this is a simulated derivation
> checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before sending this.**
> 1. A small rule change produces a discontinuous collective transition with no re-entrant
>    cycle and no compounding remainder, measured per slot.
> 2. In a re-entrant amplifying loop, flipping the sign of the perturbed bond leaves the
>    response smooth, with no jump and no growing susceptibility.
> 3. The jump size is fully explained by the magnitude of the change in a smooth but steep
>    linear response, with no bond identity, loop sign or functional group needed.
> 4. Every slot's residual stays flat while only the global mean jumps.
>
> If any of those holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract or narrow it. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage,
> no reply is needed.
>
> **One question for you, before I show you anything else:** what would you count as a failure
> of that shape in the system you know best? What instrument would you use to see it coming?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the source links. The figures, not my summary
> of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know and
> can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and
> state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> [your name]
