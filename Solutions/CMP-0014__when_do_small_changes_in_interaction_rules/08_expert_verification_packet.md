# Expert verification packet: the Tether on small rule changes and abrupt transitions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in phase transitions or complex systems can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rule, `gap` on numeric thresholds. Not `grounded`.** No experiment, measurement or simulation was run to produce this packet.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A small change to an interaction rule is a bond change: with the atoms held fixed, a different bond is a different substance, which explains how the change can be small in description length and large in consequence. The collective response becomes abrupt when that bond sits inside a re-entrant loop whose sign amplifies, so the residual compounds through the chain, and when a small substructure whose presence fixes the outcome, a functional group, is crossed. A feed-forward chain cannot close and should respond smoothly to the same change. The jump must be measured per slot against an anchor that does not move, and the step from micro rule to macro state loses information that must be enumerated as a round trip gap. The figures supply no critical exponent or threshold value; that part is declared `gap`, not filled with a borrowed name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** a rule change that holds actors and quantities fixed is a bond change, and a different bond yields a different substance, not a slightly shifted version of the old one. Where order matters, swapping operands is also a different substance; where the bond is symmetric, it is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :46, *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* Fig 12 :92, *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*
- **What to check:** does the changed rule in your system alter a typed relation while leaving the operand set unchanged? Which of the six bond tests applies? If the rule change introduced a new kind of actor or quantity, C1 does not govern.
- **Refuter:** show a bond change for which all seven bond tests return the same answers and the atoms are unchanged, yet the collective behaviour differs qualitatively; or show that the change added an atom, which moves the case to import (Fig 6 :23) and retires this clause here.

### C2: The structural condition; checkable by inspecting a model or experiment

- **Statement:** the discontinuous response occurs when the perturbed bond lies in a re-entrant amplifying loop, with a functional group crossing a threshold and a persisting part holding the new state. A pure feed-forward chain should respond smoothly to the same δ.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 13 :31, *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*.
- **What to check:** in your system, is there a re-entrant loop around the changed rule? What is the sign of that loop? Is there a substructure whose presence, not magnitude, fixes the outcome? Is there a part that survives its own use and keeps the loop primed?
- **Refuter:** a small rule change producing a discontinuous collective transition with no re-entrant cycle and no compounding remainder, measured per slot; or a sign flip inside a re-entrant loop that leaves the response smooth with no growing susceptibility. Either breaks this clause.

### C3: Measurement discipline; checkable against data

- **Statement:** the transition must be reported as a slot-indexed residual and a spread, not only as a global mean; the micro-to-macro claim carries a nonzero round trip gap; and the numeric part is not supplied by the figures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 4 :30, *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
- **What to check:** does the reported jump appear in individual slots before the mean moves? Which distinctions are lost in going from the rule description to the macro claim: slot identity, loop sign, group presence, anchor? Is any numeric threshold being attributed to the figures when it actually came from the literature?
- **Refuter:** a transition that is discontinuous in every slot simultaneously with no cascade order, or a micro-to-macro transform that preserves every distinction needed to predict the jump. The first narrows C3's slot clause; the second sets R_T to zero for that case.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a critical exponent, a value of δ_c, a universality class, or a prediction of when a named real system will tip. That part is `gap`.
- Not that chaos and phase transitions are the same thing. The shared structure is amplification of a remainder.
- Not that the magnitude of δ is irrelevant in all respects. It is not the shape-determining variable here; it can still matter within a closure.
- Not that the critical-phenomena literature is figure-derived. It is an import with source gate and shadow test, recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`, and it is secondary corroboration only.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was operated in producing this packet.
- Not a claim that people who agree with this packet count as evidence. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies agreement drawn from a shared evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground does. The decisive comparison is a controlled loop-versus-chain test in one domain, holding atoms and δ magnitude fixed:

1. Build or identify a case with a re-entrant amplifying loop around the changed rule and a matched case that is a pure chain.
2. Measure the slot-indexed residual and its spread in both, before and after the change.
3. Flip the sign of the perturbed bond in the loop case and repeat.
4. Check whether a functional group crossed a threshold and whether a persisting part held the new state.
5. Enumerate R_T between the micro rule description and the macro claim.

One clear result either pays for the shape or fires one of the refuters. Agreement with the framework is not a result.
