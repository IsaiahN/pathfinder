# The Tether's answer: when small rule changes trigger abrupt collective transitions

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rule, `gap` on numbers. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

## 1. The residual it answers

A library Γ predicts collective state from local interaction rules. A rule changes slightly. Sometimes the prediction holds and the state moves proportionally; sometimes the observation o' departs sharply and the residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o'| concentrates in a few slots (Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The question asks what separates the two regimes. It is a **union compound** at the mechanism level: Fig 9 :55, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds and leaves the intersections as their own compounds (Fig 9 :54). It is a partition at the anchor level: each empirical domain has its own instrument.

## 2. The shape

1. **The change is a bond, not a new atom.** Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** A one-rule change holds the atoms fixed and changes the typed relation between operands. That is why it can be small in description length and large in consequence. [`specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 4]

2. **The sign of a re-entrant loop decides whether the change closes.** Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Fig 13 :31: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"**. A feed-forward chain bounds the response; a re-entrant amplifying loop compounds the remainder until the state switches. [`specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13]

3. **A small substructure fixes the new outcome.** Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Abruptness is a threshold on the presence of a group, not on the magnitude of δ. A part that survives its own use (Fig 12 :119, *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*) keeps the loop primed so the new state holds. [`specified`, single-source for the definition]

4. **Measure it per slot, against an anchor that cannot move.** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** The instrument is the slot-indexed residual and its spread, plus a susceptibility or avalanche statistic in the relevant domain. Agreement among modellers is not the anchor. [`general` for the rule, `specified` for the instrument]

5. **Carry the micro rule up with a measured gap.** Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The kernel is unchanged across scales; what changes is actors, currency and anchor (Fig 4 :20, *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*). The macro claim is a lossy transform of the micro rule, with R_T enumerated. [`general`, triangulated with Fig 10]

6. **No number is available from the figures.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** There is no figure-derived δ_c, exponent or universality class. That part is `gap`, stated plainly: Fig 13 :134, **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. [`gap`, triangulated]

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a critical exponent, a δ_c, a universality class, or a prediction of when a named real system will tip.
- Not "chaos equals phase transition". The shared structure is amplification of a remainder; identity is not claimed.
- Not that the size of the perturbation is irrelevant in all frames. It is irrelevant to the *shape* of the criterion here; the magnitude can still matter inside a given closure.
- Not that the world literature is figure-derived. It is an import with provenance, secondary corroboration only.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment, dataset, simulation or measurement was operated for this solution.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

1. A controlled comparison in one domain: same atoms, same δ magnitude, one case with a re-entrant amplifying loop and one with a pure chain, measured per slot. A jump only in the loop case pays for F2.
2. A sign-flip test: in a re-entrant loop, flip the perturbed bond's sign and check whether the jump disappears while the susceptibility stops growing.
3. A discriminator instrument: baseline-referenced spatial variance or finite-size scaling, run before the event, that separates the bond-swap, sign-flip and group-crossing sources of the same reading (`05` section 4).
4. A round trip measurement: take the micro rule up to the macro claim and back, and enumerate R_T against a real dataset.
5. Standing refuters from `01` section 4: no-loop jump, smooth sign flip, magnitude-only explanation, flat slot residual. Any one fires against the shape.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four empirical domains have real anchors and real instruments, so the shape is testable. But no instrument was operated here and the numeric part is absent from the figure set. The answer stops at the limit of what these figures can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Indiscernibility is not identity. The world literature supplies the missing numeric frame; the figures do not.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not give a threshold. They give a location rule. An interaction rule is a bond, and changing one bond while holding the actors fixed makes a different substance, which is why a small rule change can have a large collective consequence. The change becomes abrupt when it lands inside a re-entrant loop whose sign amplifies, so the per-slot residual compounds through the chain, and when a small substructure whose presence fixes the outcome, a functional group, is crossed. A pure feed-forward chain cannot close and should respond smoothly to the same change. The jump must be measured per slot against a fixed anchor, not as a global average, and the micro-to-macro claim carries a round trip gap that has to be enumerated. What the figures cannot supply is the number: no critical exponent, no δ_c, no universality class. That is declared as a gap rather than filled with a borrowed name. The shape comes with five refuters written in advance, and a controlled loop-versus-chain comparison in any one domain would settle it.
