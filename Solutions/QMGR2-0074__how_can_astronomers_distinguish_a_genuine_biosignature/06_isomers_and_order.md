## DERIVATION

### Q1: Same molecules, different source: isomer or not?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."**

Consider two atmospheres, A and B, with the same bulk composition: O2, CH4, CO2, H2O, N2 at the same mixing ratios and temperature.

- As written as a bare composition they have the **same ingredient set**. The difference between "life made them" and "photochemistry made them" is carried entirely by the history, which is the bond in Fig 12's sense. By the figure's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour (one implies ongoing replenishment, the other does not).
- Fig 13 :36-37: **"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."** If O2 and CH4 were produced by one common abiotic process, detecting both adds no information over detecting one. The pair is informative only if the two channels do **not** draw on the same cause. That is the independence requirement again, at the level of molecules.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single abiotic mechanism is demonstrated to produce the observed O2 and CH4 mixture at the required rates in the observed context, the isomer reading fails and the two atmospheres are the same substance wearing different stories. The observed pair then collapses to one fact, not two.

**Status:** `triangulated` for the figure definition (Fig 12 isomer plus Fig 13 independence), `single-source` for the astrobiology application. The "isomer" word is being used as Fig 12 uses it, about arrangements carrying information the ingredients do not; a chemist would call this a source or history difference, not a molecular isomer. That difference in vocabulary is recorded, not hidden.

### Q2: The functional-group reading is the safer one

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A redox disequilibrium (an oxidant and a reductant coexisting far from equilibrium) is a **functional group** in Fig 12's sense: its presence fixes the kind of explanation (something is replenishing both), while the particular gases are the qualifiers. This is weaker than the isomer claim and does not require the two atmospheres to share every ingredient.

- The field literature agrees at the level of practice: the Wikipedia Biosignature article notes that *"the presence of oxygen and methane together could indicate the kind of extreme thermochemical disequilibrium generated by life"* while warning that *"not all atmospheric disequilibria has biotic causes."* The qualifier is still open.
- **Falsifier:** if a single context is found where the disequilibrium persists without any replenishment mechanism, biological or abiotic, the functional-group claim fails because the substructure no longer fixes the outcome.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 functional group plus the field literature).

### Q3: Dependency order is Fig 3, and it decides where the claim stops

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Applied to a biosignature claim:

| link | question | anchor | can it be skipped? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | Is the feature real, at this resolution, in this reduction? | independent re-reduction or instrument | no |
| 2 vocabulary | Do we hold a line that identifies it at the relevant temperature and pressure? | laboratory spectrum | no |
| 3 objective | What would count as source discrimination in this habitat? | enumerated habitat and modelled abiotic pathways | no |
| 4 planning | Which independent mechanism will we use to refuse the alternatives? | a second, low-overlap channel | no |
| 5 learn and carry | What was refused, what imported, what remains unreachable? | provenance record | no |

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Link 3 is the one that usually breaks: we have instruments for line identification and for significance, but the criterion for *source discrimination* is often assumed rather than measured. A "detection" announced at link 2 and interpreted at link 4 is a reading below the break; Fig 3 :23: **"It is a reading of nothing."**

**Falsifier:** a documented case where source discrimination was settled with no independent mechanism at link 4 and no habitat enumeration at link 3, reproducibly. That would break the dependency order and make the table wrong.

### Q4: Partition or union?

Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The three confusions (instrument, abiotic chemistry, biological source) are a **union**, not a partition. Each can hold alone and they can hold in pairs. A single lever clears the single-condition regions and leaves the intersections. The intersections are categorically different objects, not harder versions of the single-condition cases. This is why a one-number significance test cannot be the verdict and why `01` gives each domain its own loop plus a loop for the compound.

**Falsifier:** a controlled demonstration that the three conditions are mutually exclusive for a given dataset, so the union collapses to a partition and one lever can clear it.

## CONCLUSION

The same atmospheric composition with a different source is an **isomer in Fig 12's sense**: same ingredients, different bond (history), different substance, with the difference invisible in the spectrum. The safer and more usable statement is the **functional-group** reading: redox disequilibrium fixes the kind of explanation, while the particular gases are qualifiers. Both are `specified` and neither is `grounded`. The dependency order is Fig 3's five links, with link 3 (what would count as source discrimination) the likely break, and the confusion structure is a union (Fig 9 :57), not a partition. Four falsifiers are written above, before the conclusion.
