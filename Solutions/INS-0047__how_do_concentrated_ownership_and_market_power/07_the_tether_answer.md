# The Tether's answer on concentrated ownership and the rules of competition and labor

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

When a few owners control a large share of a market, rules that present themselves as impartial and open come to operate differently. The residual is the gap between the rule as it presents itself and the rule as it operates under concentration. It is a union compound, not one hard question: product-market competition and labor overlap inside the firm, which is a seller in one and a buyer in the other (Fig 9 :55-57). Each market and each labor segment carries its own `R`, so a national concentration ratio is a pointer to where to look, not the signal.

## 2. The shape

1. **A rule that decides an outcome has changed kind.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority". A convention makes no claim about the world. When it fixes outcomes it acquires a truth value about the world and becomes an authored constant. The change is categorical, not a matter of degree. This is the core of the answer.
2. **An anchor that moves is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when". Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor." A market or wage benchmark that moves when a concentrated actor pushes on it has become another frame, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices.
3. **Ownership reaches labor through a structure, not directly.** Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." A rule, contract, licensing regime, bargaining institution, or enforcement network must sit between the ownership condition and the labor outcome. The framework returns the shape of that chain, not the name of the institution. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
4. **The reshaping is second-order recursion with a sign.** Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom". A firm settled in the market becomes an actor at the rule level. Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A rule made once persists and shapes later occasions. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A rule that increases the concentration that produced it closes the loop; one that reduces it does not.
5. **Provenance decides, not wording.** Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures" differ. A captured rule and an independently derived rule read the same on the page. Searching the text and finding no capture is not evidence of no capture: Fig 8 :39, "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
6. **The signal is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The local labor market where one employer dominates is a live slot even when the national average is quiet.
7. **Concentration's valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The framework does not yield 'concentration is bad'. It yields a relation that depends on which residual is tracked.
8. **Agreement is a filter, not a witness.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Concentration scores and agreement among economists decide where to spend attention. The verdict needs independent instruments ranging against the ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a concentration threshold. No number is derivable from the figures.
- Not a named captured rule, agency, sector, or firm. That is a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not a verdict that concentration is always harmful. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that capture is proved for any real institution. That requires provenance and enforcement records.
- Not an averaging of competition rules and labor rules. They are separate loops plus their intersection (Fig 9 :55-57).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a wage series, an enforcement record, or a rule provenance chain.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

1. **Kind-change test.** Take a rule alleged to have become an authored constant. Is it checkable without consulting the seat that authored it, and does it still function as a two-names-one-referent convention? If yes, finding 1 fails.
2. **Anchor test.** With a before and after record, did the market benchmark or wage reference move when the concentrated actor pushed on it? If it did not move, finding 2 fails for that slot.
3. **Mediator test.** Exhibit an ownership-to-labor outcome with no intervening rule, contract, or institution, in a domain where cross-kind composition is claimed. If it exists, finding 3 fails.
4. **Provenance test.** Recover the authorship record of a rule. Does wording alone distinguish captured from independently derived rules? If it does, finding 5 is unnecessary.
5. **Per-slot test.** Find an aggregate statistic that preserves every live per-slot signal. If one exists, finding 6 fails.
6. **Loop sign test.** With an enforcement record, does the rule change increase or reduce the concentration that produced it? This is a measurement, not an argument.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set is a visible guess at compression axes, not a discovery of nature's own list. Declaring the binding of firms and rules onto state and structure roles makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. One strain remains, the operand type of the threshold comparison at second order, recorded in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Until a ground reading fires one of the six tests above, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :42, 'Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.').

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a captured rule or a concentration threshold. It says that a rule which decides an outcome has changed kind, from an impartial convention into a constant authored by whoever holds the seat; that a market or wage benchmark which moves when a concentrated actor pushes on it has stopped being an anchor; that ownership reaches labor only through a structure such as a contract, a licensing regime, or a bargaining institution, with the slot left unnamed; that the change is second-order recursion whose loop sign decides whether it closes; that provenance and not wording decides capture; and that the signal is per market and per labor segment, never an aggregate. It refuses to say that concentration is always bad, because the valence of an actor is relative to the residual. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific questions stay open until the ground settles them.
