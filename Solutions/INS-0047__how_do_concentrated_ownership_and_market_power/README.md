## Standing of this work

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading of any institution. The framework's own rule applies: LLM output cannot validate itself, and the truly testable output would have to be derived from a real ground. This package therefore reports the shape an answer must have, the per-slot residual a checker must look for, and what would refute it. It does not settle whether any particular owner captured any particular rule. Every substantive claim carries a mode.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a named firm, sector, or rule was captured. That would be a name where the framework returns a shape.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement gathered from people selected for agreement cannot settle a claim about the world.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Selecting recipients by receptiveness manufactures the very correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **A verifier cannot hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. All venues here are public submission or consultation channels.
