## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks how concentrated ownership and market power **reshape the rules** governing competition and labor. Stated as an effect, not a topic:

> When a few owners control a large share of a market, rules that present themselves as impartial and open come to operate differently. The residual is the gap between the rule as it presents itself and the rule as it operates under concentration. What is the shape of that change, and what would a checker have to observe to see it?

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, assert that concentration simply captures rules and yields one verdict about 'the powerful'.** This names an outcome before it describes the effect, frames the question as one hard problem, and hands the checker a claim built to be believed. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
- **Option B, describe the residual first and derive the shape of the rule change.** This yields a checkable set of constraints: the rule changes kind, the anchor moves, the link runs through a structure, the effect is second order, provenance decides, the signal is per slot, agreement is not a witness. This is the route taken here.

The residual is indexed per slot. Each market and each labor segment carries its own `R`. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single national concentration statistic is a filter for where to look, not the signal.

**Direction discipline (Formula step 'Direction').** The sought-for shape is bound here as an ordinary term before the run: *a rule under concentration changes from a convention into an authored constant, the anchor that should settle competition moves, and the change is a second-order composition through a structure*. Binding it lets it accrue a residual and be refused by the same tests as any other term. The pre-registered refuter is in section 4.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two conditions, product-market competition and labor, plus their intersection inside the firm. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Symmetry:** the power relation is asymmetric. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A comparison of concentration levels is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join of two operands. Operators table :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities".
- **Scale:** first order inside a market (firms compete, employers hire); second order at the rule level (the rules themselves change). Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Ownership and concentration (input condition) | owners, holding companies, boards, merger filers | control rights, ownership stakes, market share | yes: ownership registries, merger filings, concentration ratios |
| D2 Product-market competition (first order) | firms, consumers, entrants | prices, output, market share | yes: a market, prices, output (Fig 2 :29 names a market as an anchor) |

D1 is a condition rather than a full domain until it is anchored, and it is anchored by registries and filings, so it qualifies. D2 and D3 overlap inside the firm: the same concentrated owner is seller in D2 and buyer in D3. That overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either. D4 is the seat that is supposed to maintain conventions. Fig 10 :45: "It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the" (continued :56: "intersections standing, because the second condition still binds"). An antitrust lever clears the product-market region; it leaves the labor intersection standing. Three loops are owed: competition rules, labor rules, and the compound where the firm is a rule-maker inside itself and the public rule seat is captured.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Ownership condition (D1) is established.
2. First-order outcomes (D2, D3) are read against prices, output, and wages. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step" (continued :21: "that cannot use it.").
3. Second-order rule change (D4) is read only after the first-order outcomes it consumes have arrived. Fig 3 :17: "not reached, so not assessable". Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
4. Feedback closes back onto D1. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
5. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2), and describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

### Strain test before conclusion

An apparent strain: Fig 13 enumerates six primitives (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network), not firms and rules. Re-census rather than force it. Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The figures are domain agnostic by their own rule; here 'state-like condition' and 'structure' are roles, and the institutional actors are a binding from the institutions frame onto those roles. That binding is `specified` and ungrounded, not a derivation of a seventh primitive. Marked as such in F3. No strain remains after re-census, except the operand-type question noted in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 3. Findings

### F1. A rule that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"

Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

A convention makes no claim about the world; it is a shared meaning, two names for one referent. When it decides an outcome it acquires a truth value about the world and becomes a constant authored by whoever holds the seat. The effect-term reading for this question: a competition or labor rule that under concentration stops being checkable and instead fixes outcomes has changed **kind**, not merely degree. It is no longer an impartial convention; it is an authored constant.

This is the strongest figure-grounded point in the answer and should lead the packet. It is triangulated: Fig 10 states the operator on conventions, and Fig 2 :43 installs it as a guard on the anchor, with low overlap between the seat-maintainer frame and the ground frame.

**Refuter:** show a rule that decides an outcome and still functions as a two-names-one-referent convention with no truth value about the world, checkable by anyone without consulting the seat. If such a rule exists, F1 fails.

### F2. The anchor updates, and the drift is coherent (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when"

Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :34: "the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

The rules governing competition and labor are supposed to be fixed references: a market, a measurement, a wage benchmark. Under concentration they can begin to move when the concentrated frame pushes on them. The failure is exactly the panel-1 collapse in Fig 2: mutual update, coherent drift, nobody notices because every participant is locally correct. Fig 2 :36 limits the anchor test: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Here the question is constitutively about markets and wages, so those are legitimate anchors.

**Refuter:** show a rule or market benchmark that did not move when a concentrated actor pushed on it, with a before/after record. If it held, F2 is false for that slot.

### F3. Ownership reaches labor only through a structure (single-source on the figure, `general` shape)

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

A state-like condition (concentrated ownership) and a state-like outcome (wages, job access) cannot bond directly. A structure (a rule, a contract, a licensing regime, a bargaining institution, a network of enforcement) must sit between. The framework returns the **shape** of that chain; it does not name which structure. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

This is an instance of the domain-agnostic kernel (Fig 4 :20), not a derivation of a new primitive. The binding of institutional actors to state and structure roles is `specified` and ungrounded. The claim is structural and low-content by design: it says a mediator is required, not which mediator.

**Refuter:** exhibit a composition in which concentrated ownership produces a labor outcome with no intervening rule, contract, or institution, inside a domain where the framework claims cross-kind composition. That breaks Fig 13 :75 as applied and sends the audit back to census.

### F4. The reshaping is second-order recursion, not a first-order market effect (triangulated)

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A firm settled in the market becomes an atom (an actor) at the rule level. An institution survives its own use, so a rule made for one occasion persists and shapes later ones, and nothing in the rule text records which. The rule change then feeds back onto the ownership condition. Whether the loop closes depends on its sign: a rule that increases the concentration that produced it closes the loop; one that reduces it does not. Fig 12 :125-126 notes the two currencies: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." The cost in bits of a rule and the cost in actions of settling it are separate readings, so a cheap-looking rule can be expensive to check.

**Refuter:** show a rule change that does not reuse the settled firm as an atom at the rule level, or a feedback loop whose sign demonstrably does not close, with the enforcement record. Either breaks the recursion claim.

### F5. Provenance, not wording, decides whether a rule was captured (triangulated)

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures"

Fig 6 :12: "source gate: provenance"

A rule written by a captured seat and a rule independently derived to serve competition read the same on the page. The difference is entirely in the record of authorship. That is why the first check is provenance, not wording. Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." A search of the rule text that finds no capture is not evidence of no capture.

**Refuter:** exhibit a rule whose provenance is recoverable from its text alone, with no authorship record. If so, the provenance requirement is unnecessary for that rule.

### F6. The residual is per slot, never an aggregate (single-source)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

A national concentration ratio or an average wage gap can hide the live slots: the local labor market where one employer dominates, the sector where licensing blocks entry. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." A concentration index produced inside the market frame cannot be the metric that judges that frame.

**Refuter:** show an aggregate statistic that preserves every per-slot live signal, so no slot's residual is lost.

### F7. Filters are for the budget, witnesses are for the verdict (triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Concentration scores, tier labels, and agreement among economists are filters for where to spend attention. The verdict needs independent frames ranging separately against the ground: wage data, enforcement records, rule provenance, and at least one frame that does not share the same evidence pool. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." In this simulated run all seats share one pool, so agreement here is one witness, not corroboration.

**Refuter:** show agreement among genuinely low-overlap independent instruments that settles rule provenance with no ground reading.

### F8. Power is asymmetric, valence is relative, and the remedy must change contact (single-source)

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Concentrated ownership can introduce actors and rules, but cannot subtract the habitat: workers still need wages and markets still need exchange. Management is therefore permanent work, not a solved state. Crucially, the valence of concentration is relative to the residual. For a residual of scale and coordination, a large firm can be a benefactor; for a residual of open entry, it can be a malfactor. Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." This blocks the moralised reading of the question: 'concentrated ownership is bad' is not figure-derived.

**Refuter:** exhibit an actor whose valence is fixed across residuals, or a concentration that can remove the habitat rather than substitute it.

### F9. The verdict is a shape, not a name (triangulated)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The framework returns: a rule changes kind when it decides an outcome; an anchor that updates is not an anchor; ownership reaches labor through a structure; the rule change is second order; provenance decides; the residual is per slot. It does not return a concentration threshold, a named captured rule, or a list of guilty firms. Those require the ground.

**Refuter:** derive a specific numeric threshold or name a specific captured institution from the figures alone. If that succeeds, the shape-not-a-name limit is false here.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the discipline that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A rule that decides an outcome still counts as a two-names-one-referent convention, checkable without consulting the seat. (Breaks F1, Fig 10 :47.)
2. A direct ownership-to-labor composition is admitted with no intervening structure, in a domain where the framework claims cross-kind composition. (Breaks F3, Fig 13 :75.)
3. The rule change is demonstrably first order, with no settled firm becoming an atom at the rule level. (Breaks F4, Fig 12 :104.)
4. An aggregate statistic preserves every live per-slot signal. (Breaks F6, Fig 1 :22.)
5. Agreement among low-overlap independent instruments settles a rule's provenance with no ground reading. (Breaks F5 and F7.)
6. A specific concentration threshold or named captured rule is derivable from the figures alone. (Breaks F9 and the shape-not-a-name limit.)

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: a rule under concentration changes kind from convention to authored constant, the anchor that should settle competition moves, ownership reaches labor through an unnamed structure, the change is second-order recursion whose loop sign decides closure, provenance rather than wording decides capture, and the residual is per slot. All of it is `specified` or `general` and ungrounded. The specific empirical questions, which rule, which threshold, which market, remain open and require the ground. Strains after re-census: one operand-type question carried into `06_isomers_and_order.md` (threshold comparison at second order over settled molecules), named and not hidden.
