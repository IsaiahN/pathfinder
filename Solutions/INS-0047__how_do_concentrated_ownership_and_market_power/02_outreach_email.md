# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below are checked against the figures. Nothing here asks a recipient to trust a framework they have not seen.

---

**Subject:** Check one output about rules under concentration before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about how rules change under concentrated ownership, and I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** When a few owners control a large share of a market, rules that present themselves as impartial and open come to operate differently. The thing to explain is the gap between the rule as it presents itself and the rule as it operates under concentration. That gap is indexed per market and per labor segment, so a single national concentration number is a pointer to where to look, not the signal itself.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`), in three parts:**
>
> 1. A rule that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. The figures put it plainly: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" and "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority". An impartial convention acquires a truth value about the world and becomes an authored constant when it fixes outcomes. The rule changes kind, not just degree.
> 2. A rule that moves when a concentrated actor pushes on it is not an anchor. The figure rule is "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when" you push on it, "it is not an anchor." The failure is coherent drift that nobody notices because every participant is locally correct.
> 3. Ownership reaches labor through a structure, never directly: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." The framework returns the shape of that chain, not the name of the institution. Which structure occupies the slot stays open.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that any named firm or sector captured any named rule. I am not claiming a concentration threshold, a list of guilty actors, or that concentration is always harmful. The valence of concentration is relative to the residual: for a residual of scale it can help, for a residual of open entry it can hurt. I am also not claiming a verdict. This is a shape and a route, stated as `specified`, and simulated rather than grounded.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a rule that decides an outcome and still works as a two-names-one-referent convention, checkable without consulting the seat;
> 2. a direct ownership-to-labor composition with no intervening rule, contract, or institution, in a domain where cross-kind composition is claimed;
> 3. a rule change that is demonstrably first order, with no settled firm becoming an atom at the rule level;
> 4. an aggregate statistic that preserves every live per-slot signal;
> 5. a specific concentration threshold or named captured rule derived from the figures alone.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line 'this fails because...' is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Monopsony, evidence and limits: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopsony
> - Regulatory capture, Stigler 1971: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_capture
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures are in the local path above. Run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
