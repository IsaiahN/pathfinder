# Solution CMP-0081

**Question:** How can researchers choose the right level of detail when simpler models are easier to analyze but may omit decisive mechanisms?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 100

**Section:** 9. Modeling, measurement, and reproducibility

**Status:** done

## Restating the question as an effect

The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` indexed per slot `s`, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). A "level of detail" is a choice of library `Γ` at a scale: which atoms and bonds a model holds, and therefore which residuals it can register. A simpler model has a lower description cost and is cheaper to run, but its closure may not contain the mechanism that decides the residual on some slot. The question asks for a selection rule.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (per the formula's Direction rule): *a procedure that, for each slot, returns the coarsest model level at which the ground still returns a discriminating, non-updating reading and the round-trip gap still carries no live residual, together with a refuter and the instrument that would close the remaining gap.* That shape is written down here, made refusable, and tested in `01`.

## The verdict in one line

There is no single right level. The choice is a union of four conditions with different anchors, kept separate per domain and never averaged into a complexity dial: per-slot residual indexing, ground contact that does not update on the model's own report, a measured round-trip gap between the fine and coarse descriptions, and a low-overlap frame that would notice what this frame cannot. Simplicity is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. The specific level, the numerical threshold on the gap, and the name of the decisive mechanism are a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No universal detail level, no optimal parameter count, and no named decisive mechanism. Those are `gap` verdicts, not silences, and inventing one to make the answer feel complete would be the very error the figures refuse.

The reasons are figure-grounded. Per-slot indexing (Fig 1 :19) forbids a global dial; the anchor must not update on what a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29, Fig 2 :34); agreement among frames selected for receptiveness is a disqualifying correlation for a verdict (Fig 2 :40); and a verifier that has already absorbed the framework's interiors cannot check it (Fig 10 :42).
