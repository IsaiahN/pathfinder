## 5. Distribution rules (offer, do not push)

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the separator and its refuter as a method, not the conclusion as a recording of one occasion.
- **Fig 10 :52:** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first contact carries the residual and the refuter. The framework's interiors are withheld until a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking"* / *"any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not select recipients by likely agreement; do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :35:** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the four-condition separator with its refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then offer the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## 6. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Validate the output against the ground. No theory is shown. The recipient's verdict is recorded here.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, read the source. Once a recipient has absorbed the framework interior, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42).
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in.** Reuse the formula on another problem, writing down the sought-for shape and a refuter before the run.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The refuter in `01` §4 is what makes that check possible. This is a simulated derivation, and it is labelled as such in every file, so no recipient is asked to treat it as a ground reading.
