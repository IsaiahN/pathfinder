**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive the recipient's own check. The first contact carries the residual and the refuter, never the framework. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** What would you count as a failure of this model-detail separator?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question is how to choose a level of model detail when simpler models are easier to analyze but may omit decisive mechanisms. The residual must stay indexed per slot: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19). A single "model complexity" score is therefore the wrong object; aggregating across slots is how the live signal disappears (Fig 1 :22). What survives derivation is a separator with four conditions, each with its own anchor:
>
> 1. **Per-slot indexing.** Does the coarse model still register the residual on the slot that matters, or has averaging hidden it?
> 2. **Ground contact.** Is the coarsest level at which the reading still decides the question without updating on the model's own report? *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29).
> 3. **Round trip.** Carry the coarse description up, bring it back down, and measure the gap. *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"* (Fig 4 :31). Where the gap carries a live residual that does not trend down, the omitted mechanism was decisive and detail must be added.
> 4. **An independent frame.** A second model whose closure differs is needed to see what this one cannot. *"Residual first, frame second."* (Fig 8 :53).
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a universal level, an optimal parameter count, a threshold value, or that simplicity itself selects the right model. Ease of analysis is a budget filter. *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* (Fig 9 :60). I am not claiming that any particular mechanism is decisive in advance; the framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming this is settled: no measurement here decides it, so the mode is `specified`, not `grounded`. And I am not claiming this is a theory of everything or the only true method.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. A single universal detail level or complexity threshold is shown to give the correct answer on every slot (breaks per-slot indexing, Fig 1 :19, and the union reading, Fig 9 :57).
> 2. Description cost alone is shown to settle which mechanism is decisive with no contact with a ground that does not update (breaks Fig 1 :27 and Fig 9 :60).
> 3. A round trip with a negligible gap still leaves a model that misses a mechanism the ground later confirms (breaks the gap test, Fig 4 :30-31).
> 4. The reading used to settle the choice is shown to update on the model's own report while still settling the question (breaks Fig 2 :25 and :34).
> 5. Two models that reproduce the same pattern by different mechanisms are shown to be interchangeable without ground contact (breaks the isomer claim, Fig 12 :117).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures, verbatim text, and the formula: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md` in that repo
> - Worked derivation method: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what an answer would look like before you run it, state your own mode, and write your own refuter. The source says a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* and that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
