## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :31: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*.

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's four-condition separator to one sentence. Result: *choose the coarsest model that still measures what matters.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that sentence into a specific lab. Result: *use the smallest grid that still fits the data.*

**`R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.** The gap is what the abstraction cannot hold:

1. The one-sentence form collapses the union into one lever. It loses Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Coming back down, it tells a team to shrink the grid even when the failing condition is an updating anchor or a missing second frame.
2. It loses per-slot indexing. "What matters" has no slot, so it permits the average to swallow the live signal (Fig 1 :22).
3. It loses the round trip itself. "Smallest that fits" is a fit statistic, not a measurement of what the coarse description dropped (Fig 4 :30-31).
4. It loses the isomer and frame conditions. Two models fitting the same data are interchangeable in the compressed version, so the ground never votes between them (Fig 12 :117, Fig 7 :35).
5. It loses the gap verdict. The compressed sentence implies a level exists; it cannot say *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* (Fig 13 :134).

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such. It is suitable only as an invitation to check the full packet, never as a substitute for it.
