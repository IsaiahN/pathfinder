**Question:** How can researchers distinguish a genuinely evolving chemical system from a reaction that merely repeats?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 11

**Section:** 1. What counts as "life," and what counts as its origin?

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No experiment in this folder settles the discrimination, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question restated as an effect

Given a chemical system observed over time, what observable would let a researcher decide between two competing accounts of the same coarse reading? Option A: the system is undergoing cumulative, heritable, selectable change, so its set of reachable molecular arrangements grows. Option B: the system is cycling or oscillating over a fixed repertoire, so its trajectory returns to states it has already visited. Both can show growth, oscillation, self-reproduction and even chaotic patterns. What an answer would look like is a **source-discriminating observable**, one that yields different values for a closed repeating loop and an open evolutionary lineage. That observable is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused, rather than remaining an unbound preference (Formula, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. A room cannot add an anchor (Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). Agreement that shares an evidence pool is not two witnesses (Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). And a verifier who has already absorbed the claim cannot check it (Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).
