## Wider literature that bears on the residual

1. **Procedural justice and legitimacy.** The Tyler tradition predicts that fair procedures build standing, and that standing then produces compliance even with unpopular decisions. This corroborates the *shape* (standing is not recomputed from decision content) but does not derive it. It is an import with a source gate, not a figure result.
2. **The Gibson/Tyler/Rasinski dispute.** Gibson found little direct procedural effect and some legitimacy effect; Tyler and Rasinski reanalysed the same survey and found an indirect path through legitimacy; Mondak later found no experimental support for the causal direction. Because these studies draw on overlapping survey programs, their agreement is **one witness**, not three (Fig 2 :40, Fig 13 :38-39). This is why F9 stays a `gap`: the literature conflict cannot be settled by pooling it.
3. **Descriptive versus normative legitimacy.** SEP distinguishes belief-based accounts (Weber) from justification-based accounts (Rawls, Raz, Pettit). The figures govern the shape but do not settle which concept is in play, so this distinction was imported, recorded, and not used to decide the verdict.
4. **Content-independent reasons.** SEP *Authority* gives the clearest world corroboration of F8: the subject's reason comes from the source, not the directive's content. This is a low-overlap field (political philosophy) relative to the procedural-justice psychology literature, so it is the stronger of the two witnesses, though still secondary.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Validate the output against the ground. The recipient sees only the effect statement, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuters. Their verdict is recorded before any theory is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, the recipient may read the essays and the figure text. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40), because once they hold the interiors they can no longer be an outside frame (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a reusable method. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* warns that *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 2 and 3 only work if stage 1 survives. The stage-1 output here is a shape with an open mechanism gap. That is honest, but it means no claim is made that the question has been answered.
