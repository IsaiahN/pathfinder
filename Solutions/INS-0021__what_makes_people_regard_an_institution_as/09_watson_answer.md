## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the standing of an institution when a person opposes one of its decisions. Option A: legitimacy is recomputed from decision satisfaction, so a disliked decision withdraws standing. Option B: standing is an anchor that does not update on decision content, legitimacy is a convention rather than a verdict, and the decision disagreement is a separate per-slot residual. What each leads to: A makes legitimacy a running average, collapses the anchor into a negotiating frame, and hides live minority residuals in an aggregate; B gives a checkable shape with four written refuters and an honestly open mechanism gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between "this decision is wrong for me" and "I still accept the institution's standing to decide." Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-55: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either). Symmetry: asymmetric, because disagreement and standing occupy different slots and neither cancels the other. Scale: first order at the decision, second order at the standing.
- **Domain 1, the person's decision frame.** Actors: a person with belief `b`, weighting `w`, action `a`, observation `o′`. Currency: per-slot prediction residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`. Anchor: the person's own experience of outcomes. Question type: partition of which slot is live.
- **Domain 2, the institution's standing.** Actors: the institution, its procedure, its record. Currency: standing and provenance. Anchor: whatever settles "who has standing" and does not move when a frame pushes. Question type: anchor and convention. This is the governing domain.
- **Domain 3, the community agreement frame.** Actors: other people, reviewers, publics. Currency: agreement versus independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: valence check only, not a source of standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Disagreement registers per slot, then standing is anchored, then agreement is read with its valence (Fig 3 :19). Refusal before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test: Fig 2 :36 (an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it) was initially read as making the institution its own ground, which strains against Fig 10 :93. Re-census resolves it: the institution is a candidate anchor, while the ground for a question about people is human judgment (Fig 2 :37). Different objects, so no strain. The ⋛ operand type at the comparison of a residual with a standing slot remains a named, live strain.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives. Standing is an anchor that does not update on decision content, legitimacy is a convention rather than a verdict, standing survives the outcome it was earned on, provenance is not correctness, the institution cannot ground its own legitimacy, and the ground for this question is human judgment. Disagreement is a live per-slot residual, not a refutation of standing. The empirical mechanism does not survive as a figure derivation: it is a `gap` with four written refuters and an instrument still to be built. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, simulated result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished theory instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices as *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive, institution or literature claim has to be withdrawn. The four refuters are reversible tests, and the open mechanism can be filled by a provenanced import with a discriminating instrument. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled case in which an institution that responds to push on a single decision gains standing; a case where regard tracks outcomes with no lag; a case where a convention that decides an outcome remains checkable as a convention; a self-scored credibility measure that tracks an independent non-pooled ground; or a figure re-census that derives one of the competing mechanisms.
