# Expert verification packet - institutional legitimacy despite disagreement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of institutions, law or political legitimacy can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structure, `gap` on the mechanism. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An institution is regarded as legitimate when it occupies the anchor slot for the question of who has standing to decide, so its standing does not change because of what any frame thinks about a particular decision. Disagreement registers at the decision slot, per slot and not as an aggregate. Legitimacy is a convention, so agreement about its meaning is the product; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on, and provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately, never that it was right. The institution cannot score its own legitimacy, and a public agreeing from one evidence pool adds no anchor. The empirical mechanism is left open, not guessed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus political philosophy; checkable by reading Figure 2 and SEP Authority

- **Statement:** standing is an anchor that does not update on decision content; hence a subject's reason to comply comes from the source, not the directive's content.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."* World corroboration (secondary): SEP *Authority*, *"content-independent reasons"* grounded *"not in the content of the directive itself but in the nature of the source issuing it."*
- **Refuter:** an institution that becomes more responsive to push on a single decision gains regarded standing, with procedure and provenance held constant; or a figure re-census that makes the anchor a tie-breaker in a vote.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 10 and 1

- **Statement:** legitimacy is a convention, not a verdict; standing survives the outcome; legitimacy cannot be self-scored or read from an aggregate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 - *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :43 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :61 - *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*; Fig 1 :27 - *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **What to check:** does the empirical literature actually separate procedure from outcome, and does it measure standing per group rather than as a single approval mean? The Gibson/Tyler/Rasinski studies do partly, but they share survey programs, so their agreement is one witness.
- **Refuter:** regard for standing tracks each decision's outcome with no lag and no procedure signal; or a convention that decides an outcome remains checkable as a convention; or a self-scored credibility measure tracks an independent ground with no shared evidence pool.

### C3 - the open item, stated as a gap rather than padded

- **Statement:** no figure line selects among fair procedure, diffuse support, normative obligation and content-independent reasons as the mechanism. Over the observed effect they are isomers: same reading, different internal arrangement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Refuter:** a figure re-census that derives one of those mechanisms without an import; or a discriminating instrument that separates them on one reading. Either would close this gap and change the verdict.
- **Note:** the operand type of ⋛ when comparing a decision residual with a standing slot is also open. The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and a standing slot is not obviously a quantity. Named, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular institution is legitimate. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that legitimacy is public approval, trust, or satisfaction. Aggregate approval hides a live per-slot residual (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- Not that a legitimate ruling is a correct ruling. Provenance is not a verdict (Fig 10 :65-66).
- Not that fair procedure is *the* mechanism. It is a world import with a source gate, and the studies share evidence pools.
- Not that the question is settled. It is `specified`, ungrounded, and the mechanism is a `gap`.
- No claim is made about any named person, country or institution.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Specifically:

1. Hold procedure and provenance constant, vary push on one decision, and measure whether standing rises (refuter 1).
2. Track standing against outcomes over time for the presence or absence of lag and procedure signal (refuter 2).
3. Build an instrument that discriminates the candidate mechanisms on one reading (closes C3).
4. A figure re-census that refuses the anchor or convention claims outright.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :33, :36-37, :40-43 and Fig 10 :61, :65, :93. These are short passages.
2. Check C1 against what you know about authority and source-based reasons. Is the source/content distinction real in your field?
3. Check C2 against the legitimacy literature you know. Does separating procedure from outcome actually explain acceptance of unpopular decisions in your domain?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the candidate mechanisms, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
