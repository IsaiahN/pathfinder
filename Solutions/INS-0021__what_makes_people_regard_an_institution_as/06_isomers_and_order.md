# Union, unbundling and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the dependency order.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

"Why do people regard an institution as legitimate even when they disagree with a decision?" reads like one question. Fig 9 :54-55 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. The two conditions are (1) a decision the person opposes and (2) continued standing for the institution. The derivation must decide whether these form a partition or a union, and what order the loops run in.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for the census: the standing slot, the decision slot, the procedure, the record, the person's residual, and the community. No new primitive is available without import. The only operators needed are the comparison ⋛ (a threshold relation between two quantities) and negation ¬ for the refusal in the pre-registered refuter.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is this one question or two? CORRECTION TO A FIRST READING

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching again."* Seat Map :211: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- The two conditions overlap: the case of interest is precisely the intersection where disagreement and standing coexist. A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds.
- **Verdict: union compound.** The union has its own loop and its own ground (human judgment, Fig 2 :37). It is not the sum of "why people comply" and "why people approve."
- **I initially treated it as a partition** (two separate questions to be answered independently). That was wrong: it would have deleted the very intersection the question asks about. Retracted and recorded here. Triangulated (Fig 9 :54-55 and Seat Map :211).

### Q2 - What is unbundled, and what governs?

| unbundled part | role | governing figure |
|---|---|---|
| the decision slot | where disagreement registers, per slot | Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* |
| the standing slot | where legitimacy lives; pinned | Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."* |
| the procedure and record | provenance for how a ruling was reached | Fig 10 :62 - *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."* |
| the community agreement | valence check only, never a witness | Fig 2 :41 - *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* |

The standing slot governs. The decision slot is the residual that tests it. The community slot is the valence check. No part is averaged into another.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."*
- Order: (1) disagreement registers per slot, (2) standing is anchored, (3) agreement is read with its valence. Steps 2 and 3 cannot be reversed: if agreement is read first, it will be mistaken for the source of standing, which is the common-cause collapse (Fig 2 :18-21).
- Refusal comes before minting (Formula step 2): the claim that legitimacy is self-scored credibility must be refused before any mechanism is minted.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case is found where standing is genuinely created by agreement among independent frames with no anchor preceding it, the dependency order is reversed and this derivation fails. Fig 2 :46 says the anchor is *"not the tie-breaker in a vote"*; a case that makes it one would break that line.

### Q4 - Are the competing mechanisms isomers, and does it matter?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The observed phenomenon (compliance despite disagreement) is the same reading under several candidate mechanisms: fair procedure, diffuse support, normative obligation, content-independent reasons. By the source-discrimination argument in *The Hard Problem is About Source*, the reading does not determine the source. So the mechanisms are **isomers at the level of the observed effect**: same observable, different internal arrangement, different explanatory substance. This is why a single approval reading cannot choose among them.
- The stronger claim, that the mechanisms share a full atom set and differ only in arrangement, is **not** established here. The figure definition requires the same atoms; the literature has not been shown to use the same primitives. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Falsifier:** if an instrument exists that discriminates the mechanisms on a single reading, the isomer claim fails and the mechanisms are just different members of a functional group with distinguishable outputs.

### Q5 - What is the analogue that does hold?

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A settled standing relation at one level (a court, a regulator) becomes an atom for the next level (a constitutional order, a regulatory regime). This is recursion, not import, and it explains why institutions can nest without a new primitive at each tier.

The comparison ⋛ is a threshold relation between two quantities. At the level of this question it compares a person's decision residual with the standing slot: the standing holds while the comparison stays below the threshold that would withdraw it. The operand type is strained here, as it was in the QMGR exemplar: the Operators table restricts ⋛ to *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and a standing slot is not obviously a quantity. Named, not hidden. It would take an instrument, not an argument, to clear it.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union compound**, unbundled into a decision slot, a standing slot, a provenance record and a valence check. The standing slot governs, and the dependency order is disagreement, then anchoring, then agreement. The competing empirical mechanisms are isomers at the level of the observed effect, which is exactly why the reading cannot name the source. The recursion across institutional levels needs no new primitive. What remains open is the operand type of ⋛ and, more importantly, the mechanism itself (F9 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
