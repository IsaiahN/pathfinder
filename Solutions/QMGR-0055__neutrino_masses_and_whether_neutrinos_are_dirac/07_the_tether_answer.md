# The Tether's answer on neutrino mass and Dirac versus Majorana

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the routing, `general` on framework-level statements, `gap` on the direct Dirac-versus-Majorana verdict. Nothing here is `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question arrives as one phrase, "neutrino masses and whether neutrinos are Dirac or Majorana". R is actually three slots with different grounds:

- **s1, absolute mass scale.** Oscillations give Δm² only; β decay and cosmology give upper bounds.
- **s2, lepton-number character.** ΔL=0 (Dirac) versus ΔL=2 (Majorana). This is not a magnitude; it is a symmetry property of the mass term.
- **s3, mass-generation mechanism.** Yukawa, Weinberg operator, see-saw, or other.

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The union structure is named in Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

## 2. The shape

1. **A structure must discriminate, not a preference.** The shape is an instrument that separates a ΔL=0 mass term from a ΔL=2 mass term at the relevant mass scale. It is statable now; it is not yet implemented at the required sensitivity. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
2. **0νββ is the anchor for s2.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A positive signal violates total lepton number; the black box theorem connects that to a Majorana mass term regardless of the mediating mechanism. The anchor is the experiment, not the theory's preference.
3. **A positive result is a refusal; a null result is an absence.** Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Operators table :73: "− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim." A null is the former; a positive signal is the latter. They are not ends of one scale.
4. **A null result is not a Dirac verdict.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." A null excludes part of the space (inverted ordering under the standard mechanism) and leaves the rest.
5. **Dirac and Majorana are narrowed isomers.** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Same observable neutrino, different mass-term bond; the strong field-content version is retracted because ν_R is not shared (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).
6. **The ΔL=2 substructure is a functional group.** Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." It fixes s2; it does not fix s3.
7. **Dependency order.** Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A better absolute-mass limit cannot close s2. Describe the ΔL=2 effect first, then aim the instrument, then read the mechanism.

- Not that the absolute mass scale would settle the nature question. The slots have different grounds.

## 5. What would settle it : the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it by arrival, not by debate. Here:

1. **0νββ half-life:** a positive signal in any isotope at a significance that survives background discrimination; multi-isotope confirmation would make a mimicking peak less likely (Rodejohann). That closes s2 to Majorana and refuses exact lepton-number conservation.
2. **Inverted-ordering exclusion:** a null result at a sensitivity excluding the standard inverted-ordering band would close a defined sub-region of s2. It would not close all of s2.
3. **Non-relativistic capture:** a cosmic neutrino background capture measurement with sufficient statistics; the Springer review notes the Majorana rate can be "up to a factor of two larger". This is an OUTWARD import, run separately from 0νββ (Fig 6 :31).
4. **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** a figure line selecting Dirac or Majorana; a single reading fixing s1 and s2 at once; a 0νββ signal compatible with a strictly Dirac neutrino; an oscillation observable depending on Dirac versus Majorana.
