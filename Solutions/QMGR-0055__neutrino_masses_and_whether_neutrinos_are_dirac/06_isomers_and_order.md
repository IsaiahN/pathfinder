# Dirac versus Majorana: isomers, order, and the refusal operator

**Mode: `specified` on the structural relations, `general` on the framework rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

A fourth question follows: does the missing instrument come from INWARD refinement or OUTWARD import?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Are Dirac and Majorana neutrinos isomers? STRAINED, NARROWED

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Springer review section 2.2: a Dirac mass term is added "in direct analogy to the Dirac masses of quarks and charged leptons, by adding the positive chirality component ν_R"; a Majorana term is obtained by imposing "the charge-conjugated field ... also describes the same particle: ν ≡ ν^c". The same neutrino, two different mass-term bonds, two different substances (four degrees of freedom with a distinct antiparticle, versus two with a self-conjugate field).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two require different *observable* atoms at low energy, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.
- **Result:** the falsifier fires in its strong form. A minimal Majorana field needs only ν_L; a Dirac field needs ν_L and ν_R. The atom sets are not literally identical. Re-census: the claim holds only at the level of the shared low-energy observable neutrino, after the sterile sector is included on both sides. Narrowed verdict: **isomers of the observable neutrino under a shared field content, not isomers of the minimal field contents.** The strain is recorded here and in F6 of `01`; it is not smoothed over. Verdict `single-source` for the figure definition, corroborated by one review.

### Q2 : Functional group

- Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The ΔL=2 substructure is the functional group: wherever it is present, 0νββ is allowed in principle and the neutrino has a Majorana mass term (black box theorem, via Gomez-Cadenas et al.). The qualifier is the specific mechanism (light neutrino exchange, heavy neutrino, SUSY, triplet), which decides the rate but not the lepton-number character.
- This is why a positive signal settles s2 and not s3. Rodejohann: the black box mass is "too small to explain the neutrino mass scale (or its splitting) as observed in oscillation experiments", so "other, unknown, Majorana and/or Dirac mass contributions must exist". The group fixes the kind; it does not name the member.
- **Falsifier:** a mechanism that produces 0νββ with ΔL=0. None is known; the process is defined by two electrons and no missing energy. Triangulated for the framework rule; single-source for the physics statement.

### Q3 : Dependency order

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- The mass-scale link (s1) already works: oscillations, β decay and cosmology all return readings. The lepton-character link (s2) has an instrument returning only limits. A better absolute-mass limit therefore cannot rescue s2. Spending the budget there is effort above the break.
- Dependency: describe ΔL=2 in effect terms (Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"), then aim the discriminating instrument at s2, then read the mechanism (s3).
- **Falsifier:** an oscillation observable whose value changes with the Dirac/Majorana character. PDG says Majorana phases "play no role in neutrino oscillations", so none is expected. If one were found, the order would invert and this finding would be withdrawn. Single-source for the figure rule; corroborated by two reviews.

### Q4 : Positive result and null result apply different operators

This is the sharpest structural distinction in the derivation.

- Operators table :73: "Subtraction is the nearest bond and is not it. − is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim." Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- A **null** 0νββ result is a subtraction: no signal above background, the absence of an operand. It does not refuse the Majorana claim. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The reach is unchanged; only the budget spent is a fact about the budget.
- A **positive** 0νββ result is a negation: it refuses the held term "total lepton number is exactly conserved". Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". The record grows (the refusal is added); the reach falls because something was added. Fig 6 :42: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."
- Consequence: a ten-year null programme and a positive signal are not opposite ends of one scale. They are different operator families. Treating them as endpoints is the flat-formula error Fig 12 :14 names: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not."
- **Falsifier:** a null result at a sensitivity that excludes the ΔL=2 term for every ordering and mechanism would make the null a genuine negation. Then the operator distinction collapses for s2 and this finding is narrowed. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 5 amendment + Fig 6 amendment + Operators table).

### Q5 : INWARD, OUTWARD, and SEARCHED are three tracks

- Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Asked first, because it is cheapest (Formula step 7). Here: has the existing 0νββ pool been searched to its current sensitivity across all target isotopes? Multi-isotope work is precisely this track; Rodejohann notes "a multi-isotope determination of 0νββ would be preferable" to avoid a background peak mimicking a signal.
- **INWARD:** improve the instrument from a worse one already returning something. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The Ge-76 to Xe-136 to ton-scale progression is this track.
- **OUTWARD:** import a frame whose closure differs. Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." A non-relativistic cosmic neutrino background capture experiment is an OUTWARD candidate: same residual described in effect terms, a different closure, a source gate and a shadow test required. Springer review: "the capture rate for Majorana neutrinos can be up to a factor of two larger".
- Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." A null 0νββ result must not be used to argue against the CNB route, and a CNB design must not be used to relax 0νββ sensitivity.
- **Falsifier:** a result showing that the two tracks share all systematics (one evidence pool). Then independence is zero and the OUTWARD import buys nothing. Verdict `triangulated` for the figure rule; `single-source` for the CNB physics note.

## CONCLUSION

Dirac and Majorana are isomers only in the narrowed sense: same observable neutrino, different mass-term bond, different substance. The strong field-content version is retracted because ν_R is not shared. The ΔL=2 substructure is a functional group that fixes s2 and not s3. The dependency order is s1 then s2 then s3, with effort on s1 unable to rescue s2. A positive signal is a negation (refusal of exact lepton number), a null is a subtraction (absence, not refusal), and the two are not endpoints of one scale. SEARCHED is asked before INWARD and OUTWARD, which are run separately.

This framing keeps the parts of the question that are structurally clear and refuses the part that is not: no figure line selects Dirac or Majorana, and no instrument in this simulation has settled it.
