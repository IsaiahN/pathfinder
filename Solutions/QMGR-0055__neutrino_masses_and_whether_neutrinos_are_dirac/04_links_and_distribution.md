## Two points in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The black box theorem is one-way about the name.** "irrespective of the mechanism, ββ0ν necessarily implies Majorana neutrinos" (Gomez-Cadenas et al.). But Rodejohann immediately adds that the radiatively generated mass is "too small to explain the neutrino mass scale". So a positive signal settles the lepton-number character (a refusal of an exact symmetry) without settling the mass-generation mechanism. That is exactly the union split in F1 and the refusal in F4. Do not let a 0νββ discussion collapse s2 and s3 into one result.
2. **Scattering cannot settle it for relativistic neutrinos.** The Springer review's worked experiment ends "in practice, no": the wrong-helicity contributions are suppressed by m/E, and "Both interpretations are viable". This is the empirical content behind Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence": the null is a limit of the instrument, not a Dirac measurement. The same review notes that non-relativistic cosmic neutrino background capture could in principle discriminate, with a Majorana rate "up to a factor of two larger", which is the instrument-shaped thing to ask for.
