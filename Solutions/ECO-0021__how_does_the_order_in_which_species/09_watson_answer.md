## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether the map from an ordered sequence of species removals to ecosystem functioning and stability factors through the unordered removal set, or depends on the path. Option A: order is bookkeeping, only which species are lost matters. Option B: order is a live variable, the same set lost in different sequences can leave different functioning and stability. What each leads to: A discards a real variable if dependency and cascade coupling are present; B asserts a universal that fails where species are functionally interchangeable at fixed richness. The figures return a separator with per-domain verdicts, not a single winner.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The variance in functioning and stability attributable to the route rather than to the final removal set. Arity: an ordered k-tuple of removals, k at least 2, k! candidate orders. Symmetry: whether the outcome is invariant under permutation of the sequence is exactly the question. Scale: community and ecosystem scale, the habitat, not the single population. Fig 9 :22 "arity · symmetry · scale".
- **Domain 1, dependency structure.** Actors: species and relations, "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency" (Fig 11 :47). Currency: interaction strength, abundance, functional contribution. Anchor: a measured process rate that does not move with opinion. Question type: ordered-bond, union with D2 and D3.
- **Domain 2, functional redundancy.** Actors: functional groups, effect and response traits. Currency: substitutability at fixed richness. Anchor: the same measured function, indexed per function. Question type: symmetric-bond, union.
- **Domain 3, habitat and disturbance.** Actors: habitat conditions, disturbance timing, the maintaining seat. Currency: contact, introduction versus exclusion. Anchor: measured habitat state after the cascade stops. Question type: ordered-bond coupled to D1.
- **Domain 4, measurement.** Actors: instruments, sequence-resolved experiments, expert readers. Currency: residuals and ground contact. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: instrument gap.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The order effect is an overlap of dependency, redundancy and habitat coupling, so one lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate habitat contact outward including third-order effects (Fig 11 :43-48, :52-54); settle interchangeability before claiming an order effect; only then ask for an instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: the isomer claim (Fig 12 :117) strains against the symmetric-bond rule (Fig 12 :92) only if both are applied to the same bond. Re-census assigns them to ordered and symmetric bonds respectively. No strain remains after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Order dependence is a bond-type question. In dependency-structured communities the removals are joined by ordered bonds and order matters; in functionally redundant communities at fixed richness the bond is symmetric and order does not matter for the measured function. These verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The final species list is an artifact, while the dated sequence, intermediate states and secondary extinctions are the lineage; a survey that records final richness alone has discarded the variable. Over a fixed final species set, two ordered routes are isomers if behaviour differs, otherwise they are one term. No figure line returns a universal worst order, a threshold in k or a conservation priority, so those are a declared `gap`. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the ordered-bond verdict is wrong for a given function, a sequence-resolved swap test at fixed final set will show no effect and the finding is withdrawn for that function. The avoidable cost is spending a reader's independence by sending a conclusion about conservation priorities before the separator and its refuters; Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. The two-domain separator needs no withdrawal of any figure line and no new primitive; the symmetric case is a narrowed domain, not a retraction of the ordered case. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. What is not undoable is an experiment that was never run, which is why the `gap` is stated openly rather than filled.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled swap test at fixed final set showing identical trajectories across all orders would collapse the ordered bond to a symmetric one; a result showing final list plus interaction strengths predicts all outcomes with zero route residual would break the lineage claim; a figure line that ranks k! orders, or an instrument-independent derivation that does so, would close the `gap`. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
