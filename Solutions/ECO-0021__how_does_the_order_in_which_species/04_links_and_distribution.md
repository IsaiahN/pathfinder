## 3. Why the wider literature matters here

Three things in the fetched ecology literature bear directly on the separator:

1. **Order is empirically live in some systems.** Larsen et al. (2005) found order-dependent functional loss, and Ebenman (2011) reports that the literature suggests effects "will critically depend on the order". This is corroboration of the ordered-bond branch, not a derivation of it.
2. **Order is empirically inert in some systems.** Clare et al. (2024) found the same BEF relationships despite different loss and recovery sequences, attributing this to functionally similar species at comparable richness. This corroborates the symmetric-bond branch and is the conflict that keeps the verdict conditional.
3. **The route is not the list.** The extinction-debt and priority-effect sources both describe historical state that a current species list cannot reveal. This corroborates the lineage claim at F5.

These three are not a consensus. They are the reason the derivation returns a separator rather than a single answer.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and the four refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A reader who absorbs the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see under an ordered bond before giving them any framework material.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Do not rank recipients by receptiveness and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 8 :53:** *"Residual first, frame second."*

**Recommended reply order:** (1) the separator and the four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which function they would measure; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
