# The Tether's answer: does extinction order matter?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the separator, `gap` on a universal rule. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two communities lose the same species in different orders and end with different functioning and stability. The residual is not "which species were lost". It is the variance attributable to the **route**. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* So the answer is a separator with per-domain verdicts, not one verdict about ecosystems in general.

## 2. One loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### Loop D1, dependency structure of the community

**Verdict: order matters. `specified`, triangulated.**

Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* and :74: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Where a removal changes the conditions for a later removal, the removals are joined by an ordered bond and their order is part of the result. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"*. Fig 11 :52-54: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

World corroboration, not a derivation: Larsen, Williams and Kremen (2005) found that extinction order disrupted function more than random loss; Ebenman (2011) reports that effects of species loss "will critically depend on the order with which species go extinct". The two ecology sources share a pool and count as one witness.

### Loop D2, functionally redundant species at fixed richness

**Verdict: order does not matter for the measured function. `specified`, triangulated.**

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Where members of a group are interchangeable for the measured function, the bond is symmetric. Fig 12 :59: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Under a symmetric bond, k! orders are one term. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* rules out ranking those orders.

World corroboration, and a genuine conflict with an unconditional order-matters claim: Clare, Garcia and Bolam (2024) found BEF relationships *"largely the same despite species extirpation and recolonisation sequences being different"*, attributing this to species at comparable richness having similar functional effects. The conflict was returned to the census and resolved as a distinct domain; it did not flip any verdict.

### Loop D3, habitat and disturbance coupling

**Verdict: order matters through disturbance-selected sequences and cascades. `specified`, triangulated.**

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*. Fig 11 :61-62: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* A species can be subtracted; the contact structure it leaves is not rewritten by the subtraction, so later removals act on a changed but historically inherited network. Disturbance changes which species go first, which is why habitat loss and extinction order cannot be separated (Larsen et al. 2005). Wikipedia "Cascading failure" and "Extinction debt" corroborate the shape from systems engineering and ecology respectively, at low overlap with the BEF pool.

### Loop D4, the universal order rule

**Verdict: `gap`, unreached and needing an instrument.**

No figure line returns a worst order, a threshold in number of removals, or a universal law such as "lose generalists first". Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* What is owed is a sequence-resolved experiment at fixed final species set, with function measured per slot against a ground that does not move with expectation.

## 3. The shape in one block

1. Whether order matters is a **bond-type** question. Ordered bonds give order dependence; symmetric bonds give order independence.
2. The final species list is an **artifact**; the dated removal sequence and intermediate states are the **lineage**. Fig 13 :129: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available. The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*
3. Over a fixed final species set, two different ordered routes are **isomers** if their measured behaviour differs (Fig 12 :117). If their behaviour is identical they are one term, not two isomers (Fig 12 :92).
4. The residual must be indexed **per function and per removal slot**. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
5. The answer is a **shape, not a ranking**. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
6. Agreement among reviewers is not a witness. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that order matters in every ecosystem. The symmetric domain is a real domain with a real null verdict.
- Not any ranking of extinction orders, any critical number of removals, or any conservation priority.
- Not that the final species list is irrelevant. It is the artifact; it is necessary and insufficient.
- Not that priority-effect experiments are removal experiments. They are an analogue with a reversed sign (`06` Q4).
- Not that extinction order is a new primitive. Sequence and subtraction are already held bonds (Fig 12); binding them is provenance, not a mint.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a ground was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- Run the **swap test** directly: hold the final species set fixed, swap the positions of two removals, and measure each function at each intermediate state. That is Fig 12's own symmetry test applied to the ground.
- Measure **secondary extinctions and abundance changes** at each step, so the contact cascade is in the lineage rather than inferred from the endpoint.
- Use a **function-specific anchor** that does not update on the experimenter's expectation, and index it per slot (Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 1 :19).
- A pooled stability index alone is a lossy aggregation (Fig 4 :27). Two instruments with low overlap, each ranging separately against the same anchor, would begin to convert agreement into triangulation (Fig 2 :31-32, :40).

The stop is **unreached, not unreachable**. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The derivation records the lineage it took and the instrument it owes. That is what makes the stop checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a worst extinction order and does not claim order always matters. It says order dependence is a bond-type question: where removals are linked by dependency or contact cascades, swapping two of them changes the outcome, and the two routes are isomers over the same final species set; where the species are functionally interchangeable at fixed richness, the bond is symmetric and the order washes out. It also says the final species list is an artifact, not the result: the route is the lineage, and a survey that records only final richness has discarded the variable. These verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. The framework returns a separator, a set of refuters written in advance, and the instrument owed to close it. A universal ranking of orders stays open until a sequence-resolved experiment at fixed final set settles it against a ground.
