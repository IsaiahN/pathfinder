# Expert verification packet: the Tether on extinction order

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the separator, `gap` on a universal rule. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading and not an ecological result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Whether extinction order matters is a question about the type of bond joining the removals, not about a universal property of ecosystems. Where removals are linked by dependency or contact cascades, the bond is ordered: swapping two removals changes the thing, and two routes over the same final species set are isomers if their measured behaviour differs. Where the lost species are functionally interchangeable at fixed richness, the bond is symmetric and the orders collapse to one term. The final species list is an artifact; the dated sequence, the intermediate states and the secondary extinctions are the lineage, and the route is not recoverable from the artifact. The residual must be indexed per function. What is offered is that separator, four refuters written in advance, and the instrument owed to close the universal question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and operational; checkable by reading the bond table

- **Statement:** order dependence is decided by a swap test. Under a symmetric bond, swapping the operands does not change the term; under an ordered bond it does. Therefore "does order matter" is not one empirical yes or no but a bond-type question with two answers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :92 — *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Also Fig 12 :59 — *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* and Fig 12 :74 — *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* The Operators table states the consequence: *"Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms, and charging for the sequence is correct."*
- **Refuter:** show a community whose removals are dependency-linked in which every permutation of the same removal set gives the same measured function and stability at the same remaining set, within instrument resolution. That collapses the ordered bond to a symmetric one for that system.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against the two published results

- **Statement:** there are two domains with opposite verdicts. Dependency-structured, size-asymmetric systems (pollination by large bees, dung burial by large beetles) show order-dependent functional loss. Functionally redundant species at comparable richness (benthic bioturbation) show the same biodiversity-function relationship despite different sequences. This is a union of conditions, not a contradiction.
- **What to check:** is the size-asymmetry and functional-redundancy characterization faithful to Larsen, Williams and Kremen 2005 and Clare, Garcia and Bolam 2024? Would you measure a different function, and would the domain assignment change? The abstract of Larsen et al. states that *"Simulations confirmed that extinction order led to greater disruption of function than predicted by random species loss."* The abstract of Clare et al. states that *"BEF relationships for biodiversity loss and recovery were largely the same despite species extirpation and recolonisation sequences being different."*
- **Refuter:** either (a) a sequence effect on the redundant system's own function at fixed richness, larger than instrument noise, or (b) no order effect in the asymmetric system across all permutations. Either result collapses the two domains into one and the separator fails.

### C3 — measurement-facing; checkable in the design of any extinction-order study

- **Statement:** a final species list cannot carry the order. Two communities with the same final set can differ because the route is part of the result. Recording only final richness discards the variable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :129 — *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available. The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."* Also Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** show that final species identities plus final interaction strengths predict every function and stability outcome with zero residual variance attributable to route, across multiple orders at the same final set. Then the lineage carries no information the artifact lacks.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal worst order, a threshold number of removals, or a conservation priority ranking.
- Not that order matters in every ecosystem. The symmetric domain's null verdict is part of the claim, not an exception to it.
- Not that a species list is irrelevant. It is necessary and insufficient.
- Not a derivation of any ecological result from the figures. The ecology sources are secondary corroboration only.
- Not that priority-effect experiments are the same experiment as removal sequences. They are an analogue with the sign reversed (`06` Q4).
- Not that extinction order is a new primitive. Sequence and subtraction are already in the bond set (Fig 12); naming them is a binding, not a mint.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A swap test at fixed final species set, with function measured per slot against a ground that does not update on expectation. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the spectrometer resolved it. Concretely:

1. Hold the final species set constant. Swap two removal positions. Measure each function at every intermediate state, not only at the endpoint.
2. Record secondary extinctions and abundance changes so the cascade is observed rather than inferred.
3. Use separate instruments with low overlap where possible; agreement from one evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
4. If every permutation gives the same trajectory, C1's ordered bond is symmetric in that system and the claim is refuted there.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :92, :59, :74 and Fig 13 :129, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the two abstracts named above: is the two-domain assignment faithful, or is there a third condition that better explains both results?
3. Name one function for which you would expect a swap effect at fixed final set, and say why you would expect it.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
