## Independent domain census (from the residual line alone)

I redid the census from the residual line before aligning it with the primary's conclusions. WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The variance in ecosystem functioning and stability attributable to the removal route rather than to the final removal set. Arity: an ordered k-tuple of removals, k at least 2. Symmetry: invariance under permutation of the sequence is the question itself. Scale: community and ecosystem scale. I reached this by reading the question as "what varies when the list is held fixed and only the order changes", without using the primary's wording.
- **Domain 1, dependency / contact structure.** Actors: species and their relations. Currency: interaction strength and functional contribution. Anchor: a measured process rate. Question type: ordered-bond, union.
- **Domain 2, functional equivalence.** Actors: functional groups and effect traits. Currency: substitutability at fixed richness. Anchor: the same function, per slot. Question type: symmetric-bond.
- **Domain 3, habitat and disturbance.** Actors: conditions, disturbance timing, the maintaining seat. Currency: contact and asymmetry of introduction versus exclusion. Anchor: the post-cascade state. Question type: ordered-bond coupled to D1.
- **Domain 4, instrument.** Actors: experiments and instruments. Currency: residual and contact. Anchor: the ground. Question type: gap.

Order by dependency: describe R first (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate contact outward, including third-order effects (Fig 11 :43-48, :52-54); determine interchangeability before claiming an order effect; then ask for an instrument (Fig 3 :27).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 12's swap tests and isomer definition, Fig 13 :129 on route versus artifact, Fig 11 :52-54 on contact cascades, Fig 1 :19 on per-slot residuals.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: order matters under ordered bonds, does not matter under symmetric bonds, the list is the artifact and the route is the lineage, the universal ranking is a `gap`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete ecological prediction: losing large-bodied, functionally efficient species first causes faster functional loss than random or reverse order, in a size-asymmetric pollination or dung-burial system.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the function**; the shorthand says "functioning and stability" but the ground must be a specific measured rate, indexed per slot, or the order signal may be averaged away;
- the **symmetry condition**; a reader of the shorthand alone can easily turn "order matters under ordered bonds" into "order always matters", which the Clare result refutes in the redundant domain;
- the **fixed-final-set condition**; without holding the removed set constant, an observed order effect can be a different set of species lost by cascade, not a route effect at all;
- the **non-updating anchor**; the shorthand omits that the function measurement must not move with the experimenter's expectation, which is Fig 2's whole anchor rule.

One thing survives cleanly: order dependence is a bond-type question and the final list does not carry the route. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ecosystem reading exists in this derivation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
