**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why cascades die, stay local, or come back

> Hi [name],
>
> I am sending one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the fate of a cascade is decided by five structural features, not by a single universal number.
>
> 1. Per-slot support. A global average near zero can sit beside one live slot, and that live slot is where the next step comes from.
> 2. The sign of the feedback loop. An open chain cannot close, so it dies. A loop with a damping sign settles. A loop with an amplifying sign returns.
> 3. A spendable difference. When there is no direction left to drive the flow, propagation stops even if the network is still connected.
> 4. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses. A reservoir, a seed bank or an endemic pool makes recurrence possible; a one-shot part does not.
> 5. How far contact is enumerated. A cascade that looks local at first order can have its decisive residual three steps out.
>
> The three outcomes are three regimes of this one loop, not three separate questions. "Repeatedly reignites" is itself two conditions that can overlap: a closed amplifying loop, and a part that survives its own use. I keep their verdicts separate rather than averaging them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework gives you R0, a cascade condition, or a percolation threshold. Those are names your field gives the comparison, settled by your ground. The framework returns the shape any answer must have. I am not claiming that every local cascade is contained; isolation substitutes a habitat, it does not delete contact.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before sending:**
> 1. Repeated reignition in an acyclic propagation structure with no surviving part.
> 2. Die-out while a spendable difference is measured to remain and a closed amplifying loop is present.
> 3. Global spread with zero live slots at every slot and only a global average reported.
> 4. One number, defined without reference to the actors, currency or anchor, that predicts all three regimes in every domain.
> 5. A cascade that stays bounded after contact is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
