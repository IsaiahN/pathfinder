# Solution ORI-0037 - figure-grounded package

**Question:** Could a small RNA system sustain multiple generations of replication under natural conditions?

**Source:** `Questions/origins of life.txt` line 50 (question 37 in the origins block).

**Section:** 4. RNA-first and genetic information. The section-close line 55 names the same gap: the distance between showing that particular RNA reactions occur in the lab and establishing that a complete, sustained RNA-based evolutionary system could arise under plausible environmental conditions.

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. The empirical yes/no is not `grounded`: no measurement under natural conditions was taken in this derivation. Any specific numeric error threshold is declared a `gap`.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry process, not by this author).

**Status of the whole folder:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the question it reports on. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6. (An earlier draft attributed the word "simulacra" to this essay; that attribution is retracted at `01` section 8 because the word is not in the text read on 2026-11-23.)

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

Formula step "Direction" requires the sought-for shape to be written down and bound before the run so it can accrue a residual. The answer term here is:

> `SUSTAIN(R, n, H)` = the residual R for a replicating RNA system stays inside the band the ground pays for, across n handoffs, in habitat H where no maintainer authors H.

Option A: assert `SUSTAIN` holds for small RNA under natural conditions. Option B: the figures fix the conditions `SUSTAIN` must satisfy, refuse the assertion that they are met, and hand the measurement to the ground. Each option leads to a different next instrument, which is what makes this a checkable question rather than a preference.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that RNA replication could be sustained.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Adding agreeable readers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
3. Asking a checker to trust the framework before they have judged the residual spends the only independence they contribute. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. The public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` avoid that failure as well as the structural one.

## Suggested next step

Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure of the shape claim, record their verdict on stage 1 before showing any framework material, and only then offer the links in `04`. A reported failure is more useful than an endorsement.
