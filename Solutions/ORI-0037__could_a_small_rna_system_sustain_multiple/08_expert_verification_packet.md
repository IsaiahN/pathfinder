# Expert verification packet: the Tether on sustained small RNA replication

**What this is:** a claim in a form an RNA origins or RNA evolution specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the numeric threshold and the empirical yes/no. Not `grounded`.** No measurement under natural conditions is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and it comes from a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question of whether a small RNA system could sustain multiple generations of replication under natural conditions is a union of a channel condition and a habitat condition, not one hard problem. The figures return the conditions any yes would have to satisfy. Capability is a property of the replicator and its habitat, never of the molecule in isolation; the natural-conditions clause removes the maintainer and leaves pure contact; a laboratory serial-transfer series is a substituted habitat that proves wiring and never natural capability; multiple generations is a channel problem in which bracket fidelity and ground pressure multiply; and novelty requires a differing closure and a part that survives its own use. The figures do not return a numeric error threshold or an empirical yes or no. Four falsifiers were written before the conclusion. What is offered is the shape, the refuters and the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Capability is a property of replicator and habitat, and natural means unmaintained

- **Statement:** the sustained replication cannot be located in the RNA molecule alone, and the phrase "under natural conditions" is a habitat clause. It removes the seat, leaving pure contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."
- **Checkable by:** any reader against the verbatim figure lines, and any experimentalist against their own definition of a natural condition. The question to ask is whether the setup has a seat that supplies monomers, chooses a temperature or excludes a competitor.
- **Refuter:** an RNA system whose sustained replication is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes feedstock, surfaces and thermal cycling, with no compensating introduced actor. Or a "natural" setup in which a maintainer authors the habitat. That is a maintained garden and would break the term.

### C2: A lab transfer series is a substituted habitat, and proves wiring rather than natural capability

- **Statement:** a serial-transfer experiment with a supplied replicase, a defined buffer and activated monomers shows that copying, variation and selection work together. It does not show that the system would sustain itself where no seat supplies those things. Introduction is available to the seat; exclusion is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **What to check:** is this distinction faithful to how serial-transfer and in vitro evolution experiments are actually run? What was brought with the setup, and what was failed to be reproduced? Fig 11 :68-69 name the two silent failure modes: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced.
- **Refuter:** a measurement of sustained multi-generation replication in an unmaintained natural habitat with no introduced catalyst, no scheduled feedstock and no artificial compartment, run with the same per-slot residual accounting. If that lands, the substitution objection is discharged.

### C3: Multiple generations is a two-factor channel problem, and the threshold is not supplied by the figures

- **Statement:** across n handoffs the channel must carry both bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Zero either and the chain runs in place. The numeric fidelity threshold under natural conditions is a `gap`, not a hidden figure answer.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" Fig 7 :38 "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff", Fig 7 :39 "the ground's pressure on the room." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **What to check:** do these two factors map onto the quantities an RNA evolution experimentalist actually measures (fidelity across passages, and the selective regime that pays for the carried information)? Do published estimates support a single threshold, or do models disagree?
- **Refuter:** persistence across many generations measured while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure are at or near zero and non-declining. That would break the two-factor claim. Alternatively, a figure line yielding q_crit numerically would move the `gap`, though it would not refute the shape.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that small RNA replication is impossible, and not that RNA self-replication has been shown unreachable in principle. The empirical yes/no is a `gap`.
- Not that the RNA world hypothesis is wrong, or that any published experiment has failed. The Salk 2024 and Spiegelman-type results are genuine wiring results.
- Not that a lab result is worthless. It proves wiring; it does not prove natural capability.
- Not a numeric error threshold. Robertson and Joyce (2012) derive a relation and estimate a bound for a short replicator; Szostak (2012) gives a different per-position estimate; Summers and Litwin (2006) dispute the sharp threshold under realistic lethal-mutation spectra. These are world sources from overlapping and non-overlapping fields, and no figure arbitrates them.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in an unmaintained habitat was taken in this derivation.
- Not field consensus as evidence. Agreement among origins-of-life researchers is a filter for reading time, not a witness for the verdict.

## 5. What would settle it

A measurement, not a further argument. The needed instrument is a serial-transfer protocol in an unmaintained habitat: no introduced catalyst, no scheduled activated monomers, no artificial compartment, no excluded competitor, per-slot residual accounting, and separate readings of bracket fidelity and ground pressure. It must also record what came with the setup. A result that lands while the four refuters remain unfired would move the question from `gap` toward `grounded`. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap R_T is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.
