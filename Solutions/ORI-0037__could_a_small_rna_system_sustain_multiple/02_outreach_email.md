**Subject:** Check one shape claim about RNA replication before you decide what to make of it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is whether a small RNA system could sustain multiple generations of replication under natural conditions. The figures do not return a yes or no. They return the conditions any yes would have to satisfy, and they separate three things that the phrase "small RNA system sustaining itself" tends to bundle together:
>
> 1. Capability is a property of replicator and habitat, not of the molecule in isolation. Under "natural conditions" there is no maintainer: the habitat is pure contact, and what survives is what the ground kept.
> 2. A laboratory serial-transfer experiment is a *substituted habitat*. It shows that the wiring of copying, variation and selection works. It does not by itself show that the system would sustain itself where no one supplies the replicase, the activated monomers or the boundary conditions.
> 3. "Multiple generations" is a channel problem with two factors: the fidelity with which the bracket crosses each handoff, and the ground's continued pressure on what crosses. Zero either, and the chain runs in place without progress.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that small RNA replication is impossible, that the RNA world hypothesis is wrong, or that any published experiment has failed. I am claiming that the natural-conditions clause changes the habitat rather than the chemistry, and that a test-tube result proves wiring and never natural capability. The figures also return a shape, not a number: I have no figure-given error threshold, and I am not offering one from a model as if it were settled.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. sustained replication invariant across a habitat substitution that removes feedstock, surfaces and cycling, with no compensating introduced actor;
> 2. a lab demonstration that transfers to an unmaintained natural habitat with no substitution cost, no introduced enzyme and no scheduled activation;
> 3. many generations of persistence measured while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure are near zero and non-declining;
> 4. adaptation across many handoffs in a closed repertoire with no import and no catalyst that survives its own use.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - "The Origins of the RNA World", Robertson and Joyce, 2012: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3331698/
> - "The eightfold path to non-enzymatic RNA replication", Szostak, 2012: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1759-2208-3-2
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
