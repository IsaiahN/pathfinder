## DOMAIN CENSUS

R described by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and an anchor. A candidate with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

- **R, the residual.** Per molecule slot and per handoff, the gap between the library's predicted offspring and the observed offspring. Arity: three-place (replicator, habitat, generation index), with two indexed places. Symmetry: ordered and productive, so swapping parent and offspring does not preserve the relation; fidelity is a comparison, not a symmetric join. Scale: single-molecule loop, population in habitat, cross-generation channel.
- **Domain 1, the replication channel.** Actors: template, complement, activated monomers, any catalyst. Currency: copied bases, fidelity, residual per slot. Anchor: the actual offspring sequence. Admitted.
- **Domain 2, habitat and contact.** Actors: monomers, surfaces, thermal or wet-dry cycles, parasites, conditions. Currency: contact, introduction versus subtraction. Anchor: the ground, natural chemistry and differential survival. Admitted.
- **Domain 3, reach and variation.** Actors: atoms and bonds in the bounded library, imports. Currency: number of atoms, size of reach. Anchor: the closure test, what is derivable under what is held. Admitted.
- **Domain 4, observation and verification.** Actors: instruments, serial-transfer experiments, measurers. Currency: readings, bracket fidelity, ground pressure. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **Absorbed candidates.** Intrinsic vitality of a small RNA has no nameable anchor and is absorbed into domains 1 and 2. The RNA world as a single object is already inside domains 2 and 3.

Question type: union, not partition. C1 (channel maintained), C2 (habitat supplies atoms and pressure) and C3 (no seat authors the habitat) overlap, so clearing one leaves the next binding. Dependency order: enumerate contact, have terms for fidelity and separation, state the objective as keeping R small across n handoffs, design the transfer, name what worked and carry it (Fig 3). Strain test applied: lines that only fit with a strain were re-censused and govern different quantities, recorded in section 7 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## WORLD (secondary, corroboration only)

World sources are secondary. Each entry gives field, author, venue and year, a quote read from the source, the prediction it would confirm, and its standing. No source is used as a figure authority.

- **Origins of life, prebiotic chemistry.** Robertson and Joyce, Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology, 2012. Quote: "The myth of a small RNA molecule that arises de novo and can replicate efficiently and with high fidelity under plausible prebiotic conditions." Prediction: a small de novo replicator is not established under plausible conditions. Standing: corroborates the channel and threshold shape. Overlap: high with other origins sources, so this is one evidence pool, not an independent witness.
- **RNA evolution, origins.** Papastavrou, Horning and Joyce, PNAS, 2024, read through the Salk press release. Quote: "copying accuracy must exceed a critical threshold to maintain heritable information over multiple generations." Prediction: fidelity gates multi-generation heredity; the release describes the work as one step closer to RNA life in the laboratory, not an arrival. Standing: corroborates the two-factor channel reading. Overlap: high, Joyce appears in two sources.
- **Prebiotic chemistry, systems chemistry.** Szostak, Journal of Systems Chemistry, 2012. Quote: "high salt and divalent cations degrade templates on the same timescale as polymerization." Prediction: the failures are in repeated cycles, not the single copying step. Standing: corroborates the distinction between wiring and sustained capability. Overlap: high with the origins pool.
- **In vitro evolution, biophysics.** Furubayashi and Ichihashi, Biophysics and Physicobiology, 2022. Quote: "reached stasis after about 600 generations" in a constant environment, with diversification resuming when coevolving parasites change the environment. Prediction: the next generation must differ and the habitat must change for adaptation to continue. Standing: corroborates the differing-closure condition. Overlap: moderate, different method but adjacent field.
- **Virology, error catastrophe.** Summers and Litwin, Journal of Virology, 2006. Quote: the sharp threshold in standard models "depends on the assumption that all mutants keep replicating." Prediction: the threshold is assumption-dependent, not a figure-given constant. Standing: conflicts with a naive hard-barrier reading and was returned to the census under the model-assumptions domain, where it corroborates the `gap` on the numeric threshold. Overlap: low, a genuine second field.
- **RNA history, institutional newsletter.** Roland Fischer, NCCR RNA and Disease, reporting Spiegelman. Quote: "Working with simple chemical compounds, I take a primer of a living object and I generate many living objects from it." Prediction: a fast replicator produced with a supplied replicase and feedstock is not autonomous life. Standing: corroborates the substituted-habitat reading. Overlap: moderate, historical source in the same field.

The Summers and Litwin conflict is the only one triggered. It was not smoothed into agreement, and it did not flip a verdict. Most sources overlap heavily and are treated as one pool.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the empirical assertion is not made. A small RNA system's capacity to sustain multiple generations cannot be located in the RNA alone. The natural-conditions clause removes the maintainer and leaves pure contact. A laboratory transfer series is a substituted habitat and proves the wiring, not the natural capability. Multiple generations is a two-factor channel problem, and novelty needs a differing closure and a part that survives its own use. The numeric threshold and the empirical yes or no are `gap`, handed to the ground with four pre-registered refuters. The structural rules are `general`; the RNA application is `specified`; nothing is `grounded`. This is a candidate, not a settled result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, but real. The packet states mode, figure lines and refuters before the conclusion, so an error in the shape is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which figure 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and recording the reader's verdict before showing theory.
- **undoable:** yes. The empirical assertion was never made, so there is nothing ground-level to withdraw. If a refuter fires, the affected item is narrowed or dropped and the change is recorded visibly in the retraction table of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one; no primitive has to be removed.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A serial-transfer result in an unmaintained habitat with no introduced catalyst, no scheduled feedstock, no artificial compartment and no excluded competitor, under the same per-slot residual accounting, would move the empirical yes/no from `gap`. Sustained replication invariant across the habitat substitution, or multi-generation persistence with both fidelity and ground pressure near zero, would break the shape claim. A figure re-census that derives a numeric q_crit would fill the threshold slot. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
