## 0. The claim under audit

> Could a small RNA system sustain multiple generations of replication under natural conditions?

Restated in effect terms before any figure line is chosen:

- **R** is `|Γ(b,a) − o′|` per molecule slot and per generation: the gap between what the replicator's library predicts its offspring will be and the offspring actually observed (Fig 1 :19, Symbols table). It is indexed per slot and per handoff, never aggregated into one population number.
- **"sustain"** means R stays inside the band the ground pays for, across handoffs; the channel does not decay faster than the ground can correct.
- **"multiple generations"** means a chain of handoffs, each of which must differ from the last and still match the residual it surfaced.
- **"small RNA system"** means a bounded Γ with few atoms and a limited bond vocabulary.
- **"under natural conditions"** means a habitat no maintainer authored, with no introduced catalyst, feedstock supply or instrumented boundary.

The answer term was bound before the run in `README.md`: `SUSTAIN(R, n, H)`. Everything below tests what the figures allow us to say about that term, not whether the empirical world happens to instantiate it.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R described by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** three-place, (replicator Γ, habitat H, generation index n). Two of the places are indexed: the molecule slot and the handoff. The relation carries an ordered bond (generation → generation) and a comparison bond (fidelity ⋛ threshold), not a symmetric join.
- **Symmetry:** the replication bond is ordered and productive (Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before", :79 "is there a B before A fires? No"). Swapping parent and offspring does not preserve the relation. Fidelity is a comparison, not a conjunction.
- **Scale:** single-molecule loop, population in a habitat, and the cross-generation channel. The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point").

One line per candidate domain, giving actors, currency and anchor. A candidate with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| candidate | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| D2 Habitat and contact | monomers, surfaces, thermal/wet-dry cycles, parasites, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) | contact, introduction versus subtraction | the ground: natural chemistry and differential survival | admitted |
| D3 Reach and variation | atoms and bonds in Γ, imports | number of atoms, size of reach | closure test (what is derivable under what is held) | admitted |

**Union, not partition.** The question is a union of conditions C1 (channel maintained) and C2 (habitat supplies the atoms and the pressure) and C3 (no seat authors the habitat). Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A single lever cannot answer it; clearing C1 leaves C2 binding. The partition boundary inside the union is a different question: which compositions are inside closure(Γ).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. Mapped here: enumerate what is in contact with R (Fig 11 :43) → have terms for fidelity, strand separation and parasitism → state the objective as keeping R small across n handoffs → design the serial-transfer experiment → name what worked and carry it. The middle link is the one with no instrument in the general case (Fig 3 :27), which is why numeric thresholds are a gap below.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - A small RNA system is not a self-contained capable object (framework-internal, `general`)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."

Reading: "sustain" cannot be located in the RNA molecule alone. A claim of the form "this small RNA can sustain itself" asks the wrong question in effect terms. The answerable question is what contact between the replicator and its habitat keeps R small. Mode `general` for the rule, `specified` for the RNA application. **Status triangulated**: Fig 11 (capability) and Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change" (kernel invariant across scale) are low-overlap routes to the same boundary.

**Refuter:** an RNA system whose sustained replication is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes feedstock, surfaces and thermal cycling, with no compensating introduced actor. If that is measured, F1 fails.

### F2 - "Natural conditions" means unmaintained habitat, pure contact (framework-internal, `general`)

Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

Reading: the natural-conditions clause is a habitat clause, not a chemistry clause. It removes the seat that could introduce monomers, choose a temperature or exclude a parasite. The list of what matters is read off the world, not composed from the replicator's Γ. Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades"), so a second-order effect such as parasite invasion can be the thing that decides persistence.

**Strain, recorded rather than hidden:** Fig 11 :56 says "nothing selects" while Fig 11 :36 says "selection ran." Re-census resolves it: :56 denies an agentic selector (a seat), :36 asserts differential survival at the ground. Different objects, one word. No verdict changes, and the strain is logged in section 7.

**Status triangulated**: Fig 11 :56 (unmaintained mode) and Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (the ground settles). **Refuter:** a "natural" setup in which a maintainer authors the habitat, supplies activated monomers on a schedule, or excludes competitors. That is a maintained garden, not an unmaintained habitat; calling it natural would break the term.

### F3 - A test tube is a substituted habitat; a lab solve proves wiring, never natural capability (decisive for the clause, `general` rule / `specified` application)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat’s actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

Fig 11 :61: "The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Reading: this is the figure-grounded heart of the question. Serial transfer with a supplied Qβ replicase, a controlled buffer and a feedstock of activated monomers is a substituted habitat. It shows that the wiring of replication and selection works; it does not show that a small RNA system can *sustain* itself where no seat supplies the replicase, the activation chemistry or the boundary conditions. Introduction is available in the lab (you may add an enzyme), exclusion is not (you cannot subtract the natural habitat's lack of one, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you, Fig 11 :61). Two silent failure modes apply when moving the result outward: what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you (Fig 11 :68-69).

**Status triangulated**: Fig 11 :66-70 (isolation as substitution) and Fig 4 :28-30 (Going up throws away detail... take the round trip and measure the gap, a lossy cross-scale transform). **Refuter:** a measurement of sustained multi-generation replication in an unmaintained natural habitat with no introduced catalyst, no scheduled feedstock and no artificial compartment, run with the same per-slot residual accounting. If that lands, the substitution objection is discharged.

### F4 - Multiple generations is a channel-maintenance problem (`general` rule, `specified` application)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" Fig 7 :38: "the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"; Fig 7 :39: "the ground’s pressure on the room".

Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Reading: chemistry does not get "tired"; the transmission channel between generations does. Persistence across n handoffs requires bracket fidelity (the offspring still carries the parent's functional information) and ground pressure (the habitat still pays for that information). Zero either and the chain runs in place. "Multiple generations" is therefore a two-factor claim, and a one-factor answer (high fidelity alone, or strong selection alone) is a lever applied to a union (Fig 9 :55-57).

The RNA literature maps cleanly onto this figure distinction without being cited as authority: the Salk release (Papastavrou, Horning and Joyce 2024) reports that low-fidelity copies "drift away from their original RNA sequence and lose their function," while high-fidelity copies "retain function and evolve fitter sequences." Szostak (2012) lists strand separation, reactivation and copying rate as failures of the *cycles*, not of the single copying reaction. Both are world corroboration of the channel reading, figure-primary and world-secondary.

**Status triangulated**: Fig 10 :24 (channel decay) and Fig 7 :37-39 (two-factor handoff) are distinct figures with low overlap. **Refuter:** persistence across many generations measured while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure are at or near zero and non-declining. That would break the two-factor claim.

### F5 - The reach must be renewed from a differing closure (`general`)

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Reading: a small Γ has few atoms. Copying re-arranges them; it does not add one. Sustained evolution needs new atoms (monomers, a better catalyst, a compartment that changes the selection regime) from a closure that differs, plus a catalyst that survives its own use so the cycle can close. The figure distinction between catalyst and spent part is exactly the difference between a replicator lineage and a one-shot reaction. Furubayashi and Ichihashi (2022) report that a translation-coupled RNA system reaches evolutionary stasis after about 600 generations until coevolving parasites introduce a changing environment, which is their world-level version of "the next room must differ."

**Status triangulated**: Fig 8 :32 and Fig 12 :119/:121. **Refuter:** sustained multi-generation replication and adaptation in a closed Γ with no import and no catalyst that survives its own use.

### F6 - The numeric error threshold is a `gap`, not a hidden answer

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

Reading: the figures give the *shape* of the threshold: fidelity must exceed what the channel needs, and the need rises with the size of what is transmitted. No figure line supplies a numerical q_crit for a given RNA length under natural conditions. That number is owed from an instrument, not from a further reading of the figures. Declaring one here would be a filter handing us a verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."). Robertson and Joyce (2012) derive the relationship q^n > s and note that a 90%-fidelity replicator with tenfold superiority is bounded at about 12 nucleotides; Szostak (2012) estimates an error rate below about 2% per position for a ~50-nucleotide ribozyme. These are world estimates from different models and they disagree in the details, which is exactly why the figure-level slot stays open.

**Status: `gap` after a genuine re-census.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." This is unreached at this budget, not shown unreachable in principle. **Refuter:** a figure line that yields q_crit numerically, or a source-discriminating instrument that measures it in an unmaintained habitat. The first would move this finding; the second would settle the empirical question, not the figure reading.

### F7 - The figures return the conditions, not the empirical yes/no (`specified` answer, unreached empirical status)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Reading: the honest output of this run is not "yes" or "no." It is the set of conditions `SUSTAIN` must meet (F1 to F5), the location of the missing instrument (F6), and the refuters. The empirical question is handed to the ground. World sources currently say no demonstration of robust and continuous RNA self-replication from a realistic feedstock has been achieved (the Portland Press summary, read through its search-exposed abstract text; see the canonical-usage note on the failed full fetch), and Salk's 2024 result is explicitly a step toward, not an arrival at, autonomous RNA life in the laboratory. That is a report about the state of evidence, not a figure verdict, and it cannot be promoted to one.

**Status single-source for the figure limit (Fig 9), corroborated by the canonical README, which shares the author's evidence pool and therefore carries little independent weight.** **Refuter:** a figure derivation that returns a yes/no for a specific habitat would break the shape-not-an-answer rule and would have to be reconciled with Fig 9 :32 before being accepted.

### F8 - Field consensus is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim consisting of F1, F3 and F4 is refuted if any of the following is measured:

1. An RNA system's sustained replication is invariant across a habitat substitution that removes feedstock, surfaces and cycling, with no introduced compensating actor. (Breaks F1 and F3.)
2. A lab demonstration transfers to an unmaintained natural habitat with no substitution cost, no introduced enzyme and no scheduled activation, under the same per-slot residual accounting. (Breaks F3.)
3. Multi-generation persistence is measured while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure are at or near zero and non-declining. (Breaks F4.)
4. A closed Γ with no import and no surviving catalyst nevertheless sustains open-ended adaptation across many handoffs. (Breaks F5.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed, and the retraction is added to section 8 rather than silently edited away. Note that finding a numeric threshold (item in F6) is *not* a refuter of the shape; it fills an empty slot.

3. Label F6 and the empirical yes/no as `gap`. Do not let a literature consensus or a model estimate hand you the verdict.

## 9. Updated verdict

The shape survives; the empirical assertion does not get made. A small RNA system's capacity to sustain multiple generations cannot be located in the RNA alone (Fig 11 :39, :40). The natural-conditions clause removes the maintainer and leaves pure contact (Fig 11 :56). A laboratory transfer series is a substituted habitat and proves the wiring, not the natural capability (Fig 11 :66-70). Multiple generations is a two-factor channel problem (Fig 10 :24; Fig 7 :37-39), and novelty needs a differing closure and a surviving catalyst (Fig 8 :32; Fig 12 :119/:121). The numeric threshold and the empirical yes/no are `gap`, handed to the ground with four pre-registered refuters. Mode `specified` throughout, `general` for the metalaw statements, nothing `grounded`.
