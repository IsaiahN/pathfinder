# The Tether's answer: how long can ecosystems keep functioning?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain shape and on each separator, `general` on the reading of "functioning" as a threshold, `gap` on the universal number of years. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a duration after a key species falls below reliable functional abundance. Stated as an effect: the ecosystem still returns a "functioning" reading after the contact that produced that reading has changed. The gap is between what current abundances would predict and what the function assay still shows.

This is a **union compound**, not one hard question with one clock (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Its conditions are: life-stage persistence, a threshold crossing, network substitution, a stale channel, and optional management. A lever that clears one leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged into a half-life.

## 2. The shape

1. **The residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** There is no ecosystem-wide clock. There is a crossing time for pollination here, for decomposition there, for each functional group, at each site, under each perturbation. Read per slot or not at all.

2. **"Functioning" is a threshold, not an amount.** Fig 12 :87 gives the ⋛ test: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"** Operators table :49 calls ⋛ **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. The question's own phrase, "too rare to perform their roles reliably", is exactly that comparison. The ground is an independent function assay, not a count of named species. A system can read "functioning" while adults persist and recruitment has already collapsed.

3. **The debt is a channel delay, not a property of the ground.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 10 :25-26 names the ways it fails to update: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**. Fig 11 :73 separates the objects: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."** This is triangulated across Fig 10 (instruments) and Fig 11 (habitats), two figures with low overlap. The world corroborates with two different designs: whole-ecosystem fragmentation experiments (Haddad et al. 2015) and a pan-European comparison of plants and butterflies at the same sites (Krauss et al. 2010).

4. **Roles are residual-relative.** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** "Key species" is a binding relative to a named function and site, not a kind. Mutualism and parasitism are relations (Fig 11 :50).

5. **Arrangement carries the lag.** Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two sites with the same species list but different interactions are isomers; redundancy is ∥ (Operators :19, *"either suffices; not both required"*), a functional group fixes the outcome (Fig 12 :123), and a long-lived adult or seed bank is catalyst-like (Fig 12 :119) or a delayed cycle (Fig 12 :121). Order matters: → sequence and ⇒ production name routes, not just final states.

6. **Contact cascades outward.** Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43). The relevant end of the lag is the last function in the cascade, not the first species to disappear.

7. **Management is permanent, asymmetric work.** Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** Isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one (Fig 11 :66). A reintroduction can restore a function only if functional objectives are stated and monitored.

8. **The universal number is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** No figure line returns a duration. The framework returns the conditions under which a duration becomes measurable, then stops. This is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not a claim that no answer is possible anywhere.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal relaxation time, a typical decade, or a portable half-life.
- Not that species presence is a valid proxy for function. That is the metamerism error (*The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*).
- Not that "key species" is a standing list (Fig 11 :51), or that abundance alone determines the lag (Fig 12 :5).
- Not a guarantee that every debt can be paid by restoration. Introduction is available, exclusion is not, and isolation is substitution (Fig 11 :61-66).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles a duration for any named system.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another review. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the spectrometer resolved the green reading; the argument did not. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Threshold Test:** at what abundance does the function rate cross the required level for this function, at this site, and does an independent assay report the crossing at the time? This is the missing middle link of Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- **Slot Separation:** run the same assay on two functions and two habitats. If one duration does not fit both, the averaged answer is refuted (F1).
- **Cascade Endpoint:** enumerate outward until the last function crosses, rather than stopping at the first species to disappear (Fig 11 :52-53).
- **Bond Test:** compare two sites with the same species list and different interaction bonds. If the crossing times differ, the isomer claim is corroborated; if not, with bonds demonstrably equal, it is under-specified (F6).
- **Standing Falsifiers:** the seven conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The duration is reachable with a prospective, non-updating instrument on a named slot; it is unreachable from a figure set that returns shapes and from presence-only monitoring that cannot discriminate sources. Halley et al. 2016 report a timescale range *"from a few years to thousands of years"* and power-law decay spread across timescales, which is what a union looks like from the inside. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical figures README).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number of years, and it does not claim that ecosystems have a single clock. It says that the question is indexed per species and per function, so averaging sites and taxa throws away the only signal that says where to look; that "functioning" is a threshold, not an amount, so the relevant moment is when abundance falls below the level at which a role is performed; and that the debt is a delay in the channel between actors and a measured function, not a property of the ground, which does not wait. Roles are relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of key species; the arrangement of interactions matters as much as the species present, so two sites with the same list can behave as isomers; and contact cascades outward, so the lag ends at the last function to cross, not the first species to go. Management adds actors but cannot subtract the habitat. The universal duration is a declared gap, a verdict and not a silence. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, seven refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.
