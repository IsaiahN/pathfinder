# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Field: extinction debt research.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below are stated accurately and the reader has recorded a verdict on stage 1. The output must survive on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how long ecosystems keep functioning after a key species declines

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in extinction debt research and have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** there is no single duration that answers "how long can an ecosystem keep functioning after a key species becomes too rare to perform its role reliably?" What the figures and the field data support is a shape:
>
> 1. The residual is indexed per species-slot and per function-slot, not per ecosystem. Long-lived plant specialists and short-lived butterflies in the same grasslands can show different debts over the same period.
> 2. "Functioning" is a threshold, not an amount. The relevant comparison is whether an abundance is above the level at which the role is performed, not whether the species is present.
> 3. The debt sits in the channel, not in the ground. Long-lived adults, dormant stages, a redundant guild member and a saturated monitoring metric can keep the function reading on its old side of the threshold after the contact that produced it has changed. The ground, the measured function, does not wait.
> 4. The conditions form a union of overlapping cases, not a partition, so the answer is a set of per-slot verdicts and a cascade enumeration, not one half-life.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework returns a number of years, that a universal relaxation time exists, that "key species" is a standing list, or that any published half-life is the ecosystem's true clock. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the universal duration is recorded as a `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. A single duration predicts the crossing time for two different functions in two different habitats under two different perturbations, with a non-updating instrument on each, and no residual left in either slot.
> 2. Function declines linearly with abundance with no threshold in the observable range, and a presence proxy predicts the assay as well as a measured rate.
> 3. A function measurement demonstrably updates on a model's own prediction rather than on measurement, so the supposed anchor moves when pushed.
> 4. A figure line with a verbatim citation returns a duration, or a procedure returns one duration across habitats and taxa.
>
> If any of those holds, I will retract the shape claim for the domain in question. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like to know:** when you look at a case you know well, what would you count as the moment the function crossed, and what instrument would tell you that at the time? I am asking for your failure condition, not your endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract, ecology question): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Tilman, May, Lehman and Nowak 1994, *Habitat destruction and the extinction debt*, Nature 371:65-66: https://www.nature.com/articles/371065a0
> - Haddad et al. 2015, *Habitat fragmentation and its lasting impact on Earth's ecosystems*, Science Advances 1:e1500052 (open access): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4643828
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is local at `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
