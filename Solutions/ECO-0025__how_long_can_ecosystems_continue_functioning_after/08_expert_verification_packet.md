# Expert verification packet: the Tether on ecosystem function after key species decline

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. Field: extinction debt research.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the threshold reading, `gap` on the universal number. Not `grounded`.** No crossing time for any named ecosystem is asserted as measured. This is a shape and a separator, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

There is no single answer to "how long can ecosystems keep functioning after key species become too rare to perform their roles reliably?" The residual is indexed per species-slot and per function-slot, so averaging across sites and taxa discards the live signal. "Functioning" is a threshold comparison, not an amount: the relevant moment is when abundance falls below the level at which a role is performed. The observed lag is a delay in the channel between actors and a measured function, not a property of the ground, which does not wait. Roles are relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of key species; the typed interaction arrangement matters, so two sites with the same species list can stand as isomers with different lags; and contact cascades outward, so the lag ends at the last function to cross. Management can add actors but cannot subtract the habitat. The universal duration is a declared `gap`, with seven refuters written in advance and an instrument owed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Fig 1, Fig 10 and Fig 11

- **Statement:** the lag is per-slot and sits in the channel, not in the ground. A single ecosystem-wide duration is the forbidden average.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25-26, *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 11 :73, *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, can one duration predict the crossing for two different functions in two different habitats under two different perturbations, with an instrument that fires on both? Krauss et al. 2010 found a plant debt and no butterfly debt over roughly the same period at the same sites; Haddad et al. 2015 found function loss of 30% after a year and 80% after a decade in small isolated fragments, with size dependence. Is your case of the same kind?
- **Refuter:** one duration that fits all those slots with no residual left in any of them; or a function measurement that demonstrably updates on a model's own prediction rather than on measurement, so the anchor moves when pushed.

### C2 - threshold and role; checkable against a function assay

- **Statement:** "functioning" is a ⋛ threshold, not a stock, and "key species" is a binding relative to that threshold, not a standing kind.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87, the ⋛ test, *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"*; Operators table :49, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*; Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** in your system, does the function cross abruptly at an abundance level, or decline linearly all the way down? Anderson et al. 2011 found 84% lower seed production per flower and 55% fewer juveniles per adult where bird pollinators were functionally extinct, while adult densities stayed similar: a threshold crossed while the counted adults persisted. Does your assay resolve the same distinction?
- **Refuter:** a linear function-abundance relation with no threshold in the observable range plus a presence proxy that predicts the assay as well as a measured rate; or a single species list that keeps the same valence for every function, site and perturbation.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Fig 12 and Fig 11

- **Statement:** over a fixed species set, two systems with different typed interaction bonds are isomers with different possible lags; redundancy is a ∥ disjunction, and the cascade endpoint is three steps out, not first contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*; Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*; Operators table :19, the ∥ test, *"either suffices; not both required"*; Fig 11 :52-53, *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** take two sites with the same species list. Are the interaction bonds the same? If not, the isomer claim applies. Is the guild redundant under ∥, or are both members actually required (+)? Where does the cascade stop mattering for the function you care about?
- **Refuter:** two systems with identical bond structure, functional-group membership and life-stage structure but different crossing times under the same perturbation, with no unmeasured third variable; or a "redundant" guild whose stepwise removal reduces the function at the same rate as removal of the whole guild.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal duration, a typical decade, or a portable half-life.
- Not "the framework derives a relaxation time". It returns a shape and a gap.
- Not that species count or presence is a valid function proxy. The spectrometer example shows why a reading cannot name its source.
- Not a standing list of key species, and not a claim that any particular species is key for every function.
- Not that restoration can subtract an unwanted contact. Introduction is available; exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an ecosystem measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another review.

1. **Threshold Test:** at what abundance does the function rate cross the required level for this function at this site, and does the assay report the crossing at the time?
2. **Slot Separation:** run it on two functions and two habitats; one duration must not be made to fit both.
3. **Cascade Endpoint:** enumerate outward to the last function to cross, not the first species to disappear.
4. **Bond Test:** compare two sites with the same list and different typed interactions.
5. **Ground Test:** show that the function reference does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29-30).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 10 :24-28, Fig 11 :49-53 and :73, Fig 12 :87 and :117, Operators :19 and :49. Nine short passages.
2. Check C2 against a case you know: did the function cross while adults were still counted?
3. Name one instrument that would report the crossing at the time, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement (Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*).
