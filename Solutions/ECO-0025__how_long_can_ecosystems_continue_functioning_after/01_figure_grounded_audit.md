## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question asks for a duration: how long does an ecosystem keep functioning after a key species has fallen below the abundance at which it can perform its role reliably?

Restated in effect terms, without naming a mechanism:

- There is a **residual** `R`: a gap between what the current species abundances would predict for a function and what the function reading still shows. The system keeps returning a functioning reading after the contact that produced it has degraded.
- The question offers two candidate objects for that gap. **Option A:** it is a property of the ground, a single relaxation time that belongs to the ecosystem and can be measured once and reported as a number. **Option B:** it is a property of a channel and its habitat, indexed per species-slot and per function-slot, gated by a threshold, carried by the interaction bonds, and dependent on which conditions are in contact. What each leads to: A gives a portable number but hides the live slot signal and invites a policy deadline that is not grounded in any instrument; B gives a separable, checkable shape with explicit falsifiers and no universal duration.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** An answer to "how long" would look like: a duration attached to a named function, in a named habitat, under a named perturbation, with an instrument that reports when the function crosses its threshold, plus a refuter that would break the estimate. Writing this down before the run makes the preference refusable like any other term. The audit then asks whether any figure line returns such a duration. It does not.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** the residual is a comparison between an abundance reading and a required function level. At least two conditions are always in play, the species' own demography and the function that another party measures. This is Fig 9's union case, not one hard question: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55).
- **Symmetry:** "functioning" is not an amount that adds. It is a threshold relation, the ⋛ comparison whose test is *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"* (Fig 12 :87, Operators table :49). Decline and recovery are ordered, not interchangeable: the `→` and `−` bonds apply where a symmetric `+` would not (Fig 12 :55-83).
- **Scale:** three scales are chained. Individuals and populations change; the interaction network rearranges; the function reading changes. The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27), so the lag measured at one scale is not the lag at another. *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* (Fig 13 :76) is the structural reason: the same kind of quantity bonded to a different slot is a different term.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none can be named, it is not yet a domain.

| # | Domain | Actors | Currency | Anchor (nameable, non-updating) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Population and life-stage frame | species, populations, individuals, dormant stages, propagules | abundance, birth and death rates, generation time | a field count of individuals that does not change because a model predicted it |
| D2 | Function and threshold frame | pollination, decomposition, nutrient retention, primary production, the ⋛ comparison | function rate against a required level | a direct measurement of the function, not a species-presence proxy |
| D3 | Network and bond frame | species as atoms, interactions as bonds, guilds, modules | arrangement, redundancy, cascade length | an observed interaction record (who interacts with whom, and when) |
| D4 | Channel and observation frame | indicators, monitors, stale readings, pooled samples | lag between a change in contact and a change in the reading | the ground itself, which does not move: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24) |
| D5 | Maintainer and management frame | conservation managers, restorers, reintroducers | introductions, restoration, monitoring | the same function ground; the seat may add actors, not subtract the habitat |

D1 through D5 all have nameable anchors. No domain is admitted merely because it sounds relevant.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** The conditions overlap: a long-lived stage (D1) can keep a function reading alive after its own reproduction has failed; a redundant guild member (D3) can substitute for a lost pollinator; a saturated monitoring metric (D4) can hide the decline; a manager (D5) can introduce a substitute without removing the cause. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56). The right response is to unbundle before searching again (Fig 9 :54), not to force one global half-life.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

*"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* (Fig 3 :20). The order is:

1. **Perception:** obtain an abundance reading and a function reading that are not the same reading (Fig 3 :4-5). If the presence of the species *is* the function proxy, the anchor has been moved into the frame (Fig 2 :33-34).
2. **Vocabulary:** have terms for what a species affords a function (pollinates, disperses, fixes, shades) (Fig 3 :6-8).
3. **Objective:** say what counts as enough function, that is, the threshold (Fig 3 :9-11). This is where the chain usually breaks: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27).
4. **Planning:** choose an intervention under incomplete information (Fig 3 :12-13). Only meaningful after 1-3.
5. **Learn and carry:** verify what worked and carry the method, not the recording (Fig 3 :14-16; Fig 4 :22).

*"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19). Effort spent on better global half-life estimates cannot rescue a missing threshold instrument.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A single "how long" aggregates across slots and discards the live signal (decisive against Option A)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :13: **"A global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."**
Symbols table :43-44: **"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."**

The question is phrased as if the ecosystem were one slot with one clock. The world gives many slots: each species, each function, each site, each life stage. In the pan-European grassland study the same perturbation produced a detectable debt for long-lived vascular plant specialists and **no** detectable debt for short-lived butterfly specialists over roughly 40 years (Krauss et al. 2010, read in full). Averaging those two into one relaxation time would have erased the only signal that says where to look. Haddad et al. 2015 found on average more than 20% species loss after one year and more than 50% after ten, but the rate depended on fragment size: 50% decline in birds after 5 years in 1-ha fragments versus 12 years in 100-ha fragments (read in full). One number cannot carry that dependence.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, the Symbols table, and two field studies with different designs.
**Refuter:** show a single duration that predicts the crossing time for two different functions, in two different habitats, under two different perturbations, with an instrument that fires on both, and no residual left in either slot. If it works per slot across that set, the aggregation finding is refuted.

### F2 - "Functioning" is a threshold, and "too rare to perform reliably" is a comparison

Fig 12 :87 (the test for ⋛): **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"**
Operators table :49: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**
Operators table :52: **"A goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."**

The residual's content is not "how many individuals are left" but "is the abundance above the level at which the role is performed reliably?" That is a ⋛ comparison, an ordered threshold, not a sum. Anderson et al. 2011 (read via PubMed and two abstract mirrors) make the threshold concrete: functional extinction of bird pollinators in mainland New Zealand reduced seed production per flower by 84% and left 55% fewer juvenile plants per adult, while adult densities stayed similar. The adults persisted; the function had already crossed. Sekercioglu's accompanying Perspective (read) says hundreds of species *"now contribute negligibly to ecosystem function and are 'functionally extinct'"*. Functional extinction is a threshold crossed while the species is still present, exactly Fig 12 :87's comparison.

**Mode:** `general` on the comparison form, `specified` on the ecological threshold. Triangulated across Fig 12, the Operators table, and the two 2011 Science pieces.
**Refuter:** show that the function declines smoothly and linearly with abundance with no threshold anywhere in the observable range, and that a presence/absence proxy predicts the function reading as well as a measured rate. That would dissolve the comparison and leave an amount.

### F3 - The debt lives in the channel, not in the ground (the core verdict)

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**
Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

An extinction debt is the interval in which the channel is still returning a functioning reading after the contact has changed. The ground here is the measured function; it does not wait, and it does not change because a frame predicted a different date. The channel is the path from population to interaction to function reading, and it can be stale (long-lived adults that no longer reproduce), dropped (a transient pollinator visit no longer sampled), saturated (a metric that still reads "functioning" at the top of its range), or pooled (a guild average that hides one functional group collapsing). Each is named in Fig 10 :25-26.

This is **triangulated**, not merely repeated: Fig 10 governs instruments and channels, Fig 11 governs habitats and actors. Low overlap between the two routes; the same conclusion arrives from a different object. Fig 4 :27 adds the scale reason: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*

Wikipedia's *Extinction debt* entry (read in full, secondary aggregator, reduced weight) states the New Zealand case directly: the 1870 loss of pollinating birds caused a long-term reduction in reproduction of *Rhabdothamnus solandri*, *"as the plant is slow-growing and long-lived, its populations persist."* Persistence is the channel still reading after the ground has moved.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated.
**Refuter:** show a case where the measured function stays exactly at its prior value while both the actor that performed it and the channel that reported it have been independently shown to be gone, with a source-discriminating instrument. If the reading cannot be distinguished from intact contact, the channel claim is not yet refuted, only unmeasured; if it can, F3 stands. A cleaner refuter: show that a function measurement *does* update on the model's prediction, which would move the anchor into the frame and require a fresh anchor.

### F4 - The habitat is enumerated, and the residual is often three steps out

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**
Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
Fig 11 :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**
Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The relevant time is not the time until the counted species disappears. It is the time until the last function in the cascade crosses. Blanchard and Munoz 2023 (opened; full text paywalled, abstract and indexing read) state that *"the structure of interaction networks and the contrasting tempo of extinction in species, function and interaction can determine co-extinction debts scenarios"* and that altered organisms and processes can trigger *"cascading delayed co-extinction dynamics spreading across ecological networks."* Fig 11 :52-53 is the same shape with a different example. Krauss et al. 2010 conclude that future loss of vascular plants *"will inevitably lead to co-extinctions of specialized herbivores"*, a second-order effect. The debt must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, not scoped to first contact.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus two ecology sources that share a citation pool with each other, so one outside witness, not two).
**Refuter:** find a perturbation whose second-order and third-order contacts have been enumerated and whose function readings remain on their prior side of the threshold for the whole observed window. That would show the cascade is not load-bearing in that system, which is a legitimate per-domain verdict, not a global one.

### F5 - There is no standing list of key species

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

"Key species" in the question is a binding relative to a chosen function and a chosen site, not a kind. The same predator that maintains primary production by suppressing herbivores can reduce a different function (say, ground-nesting bird recruitment) at the same site. Fig 11 :50 says mutualism and parasitism are relations, not kinds. Anderson et al. 2011 and the 2026 Proceedings B systematic review (opened; direct fetch 403, abstract and body read through the public search index at reduced weight) both move toward function and traitroles: the review notes that persistence depends on *"interactions among traits rather than isolated characteristics, motivating a shift towards community-level analyses focused on species' functional roles"* and that *"richness within functional groups may be more important than overall species richness because ecosystem functions are maintained despite the loss of individual species."* That is Fig 11 :49-51 in a different vocabulary.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and the two literature routes, with the review's single-source status recorded honestly.
**Refuter:** produce a list of species that remains "key" for every function, at every site, under every perturbation, with a measured residual on each slot. Since valence is relative, such a list would require a fixed residual across all cases, which is a testable claim and almost certainly false.

### F6 - Arrangement, not ingredient list: redundancy, guilds and surviving parts

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**
Operators table :19: **"either suffices; not both required"** (the test for ∥, disjunction)

Two ecosystems can hold the same species list and lose function at very different rates, because the bonds differ. A pollinator guild with ∥ redundancy, *"either suffices; not both required"*, keeps the function after one member declines; a single specialized mutualist does not. A long-lived adult is a catalyst-like part that survives its own use: it is still present and still counted, but its contribution has stopped being regenerated. This is why the 2026 review's functional-redundancy sentence matters: richness in a functional group is the ∥ structure, not the atom count.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the bond definitions (Fig 12 and the Operators table are one framework pool), corroborated at the world level by the review. `06_isomers_and_order.md` works this out with a falsifier.
**Refuter:** show two systems with identical interaction bonds, identical functional-group membership and identical life-stage structures, but different lag times. If the arrangement is fixed and the time still differs, the bond claim is under-specified and a third variable is owed.

### F7 - Management is permanent work; introduction is available and exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A manager can add a substitute pollinator, restore habitat, or reintroduce a lost bird. The manager cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult anyone. Moving a residual into a reserve substitutes a habitat and inherits actors that were not chosen. The 2022 *Conservation Biology* restoration paper (opened via PMC, read) puts the operational version: *"Conservation translocations can help restore ecosystem function, but functional objectives must be stated and monitored to maximize success."* That is the seat doing its own office, with the ground, not prestige or species count, as the check.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and the restoration result.
**Refuter:** show a management action that permanently removes an unwanted contact from a habitat without substituting another, and that keeps the function reading on the prior side of the threshold without further work. If such a subtraction exists, the asymmetry claim is wrong for that domain.

### F8 - The chain breaks at the threshold instrument, not at the abundance survey

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :25-26: having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what counts as progress *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Abundance surveys work; function assays often work; the missing instrument is one that says, at the time, "this species has crossed below the level at which it performs role X for function Y here". Haddad et al. 2015 synthesize 35 years of experiments and still report that *"the effects of current fragmentation will continue to emerge for decades"*, noting the challenge of relating transient to long-term dynamics. Kuussaari et al. 2009 (abstract read) say local extinction *"can occur with a substantial delay"*. Hylander and Ehrlén 2013 (abstract read) say failure to distinguish mechanisms *"and to simultaneously consider both the size of the extinction debt and the relaxation time hampers our understanding of how extinction debts arise."* The middle link, an instrument for the threshold, is the least measured link in the chain.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure claim (Fig 3), corroborated by three literature routes that share the Kuussaari/Tilman pool.
**Refuter:** exhibit a deployed instrument that reports functional crossing per species-slot per function, with published error rates and a prospective test. If it exists and is widely used, the broken-link finding is retracted for those systems.

### F9 - Monitoring a proxy records an occasion; it is not a generator

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Detecting a debt by comparing present species richness with past habitat pattern is a recording of one occasion. Ridding et al. 2021 (opened via the NERC eprints PDF excerpt; direct journal page 403) found that *"the detection and magnitude of this debt however, differed among habitats, species with different levels of habitat specialisation, and method used"*, and that the most data-intensive method identified debts in all habitats while two cheaper methods missed them. Figueiredo et al. 2019 (abstract and Box 1 read; direct full text 403) define the extinction threshold and note that levels of organization respond hierarchically. The method is part of the answer: a proxy that does not measure function cannot tell you when function crosses, and a frame that defines "functioning" as the presence of the species has moved its reference into itself.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 2 and two methods papers.
**Refuter:** show that a presence-based proxy and an independent function assay cross their thresholds at the same time, within published error, across several habitats. Then the proxy is a valid compressed method and the recording objection is weakened for those cases.

### F10 - The conditions form a union, so verdicts stay separate

Fig 9 :4-5: **"First: is there a fact here?"** and **"Split it rather than search."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Life-stage persistence (D1), threshold crossing (D2), network substitution (D3), channel lag (D4) and management (D5) overlap. A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The reported range of relaxation times, "from a few years to thousands of years" (Halley et al. 2016, read in full), is not a single uncertain constant; it is a symptom of a union being read as one question. Halley et al. also find power-law rather than exponential decay, with *"extinctions are distributed more evenly across timescales rather than dominated by a single timescale."* That is the world's own version of "do not average the slots".

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and the Halley result.
**Refuter:** show one mechanism whose parameter set predicts the crossing time in all five domains with no interaction terms and no residuals. Then the union collapses to a partition and the per-domain verdicts may be combined.

### F11 - The universal number is a `gap`, and unreachable is a verdict

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

No figure line returns a number of years. Grep of the verbatim text across all 13 figures, the Operators table and the Symbols table found threshold comparisons, cascades, channels, habitats and bonds, but no duration constant. The honest report is `gap` on the universal number, of the **shape-not-a-name** kind rather than the underivable-in-principle kind: the framework can characterize what the answer would have to look like (per-slot, threshold-gated, cascade-indexed) but cannot name the number without a ground reading from a specific system. This is not silence, and it is not an invitation to invent a decade.

**Mode:** `general` on the gap, `specified` on the shape that replaces it. Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and Fig 8, three figures with different objects.
**Refuter:** produce a figure line (with its `Fig N :line` citation) that returns a duration, or a measurement procedure whose ground reading returns the same duration across habitats and taxa. Until one of those fires, the gap stands.

### F12 - Fame and agreement inside the literature cannot settle this

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"**

The extinction-debt literature is unusually consolidated: Tilman 1994, Kuussaari 2009 and Diamond 1972 appear as the reference point in nearly every review opened. That is one evidence pool. Citing more papers from that pool does not buy independence (Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). The source ledger records overlap for every row so that a shared pool is not mistaken for corroboration. The author's own interest in a clean result is declared here and handled by the pre-registered refuter, not by an assertion of neutrality.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 2.
**Refuter:** show two genuinely independent instruments, drawing on different evidence, returning the same crossing time for the same slot, with the anchor's non-updating status demonstrated. That would be triangulation rather than a room, and F12 would not apply to that pair.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written here before the conclusion, per Fig 10's discipline and the Formula's "Direction": committing a prediction before a run makes the sought-for shape an ordinary, refusable term. The whole shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Aggregation holds.** A single duration predicts the function-crossing time for two different functions in two different habitats under two different perturbations, with a non-updating instrument on each, and no residual left in any slot (breaks F1).
2. **No threshold.** Function declines linearly with abundance with no threshold in the observable range, and a presence proxy predicts the assay as well as a measured rate (breaks F2).
3. **The debt is in the ground.** A function reading demonstrably updates on the frame's own prediction rather than on measurement, so that the "anchor" moves when pushed (breaks F3 and Fig 2 :29-30).
4. **The list is standing.** One fixed list of key species keeps the same valence for every function, site and perturbation (breaks F5).
5. **Arrangement is inert.** Two systems with identical bond structure but different lag times, with no unmeasured third variable (breaks F6).
6. **Subtraction works.** A manager permanently removes a habitat contact without substituting another and keeps the function above threshold with no further work (breaks F7).
7. **A universal number is returned.** A figure line with a `Fig N :line` citation returns a duration, or a procedure returns one duration across habitats and taxa (breaks F11).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn for the domain in question, with the earlier reading kept visible in §6. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F3, F1 and F2: the debt is a channel delay, indexed per slot, gated by a threshold. This is the part that survives the figures and the field data.
2. Refuse the universal number explicitly (F11). Report `gap`, not a range presented as an estimate. Ranges such as "a few years to thousands of years" are a symptom of a union, not an answer.
3. Require an instrument for the threshold before anyone quotes a duration (F8). Until it exists, the middle link is unmeasured, not known to work.
4. Keep verdicts per domain and per slot, never averaged (F1, F10).
5. Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering (F4), and treat "key species" as a binding relative to a named function (F5).
6. State management as permanent work with asymmetric power (F7), and treat translocation as a substitute habitat, not a restoration of the old one.
7. Send the generator, the residual and the refuter first; offer the source links only after the reader has recorded a verdict (Fig 10 :52; Fig 4 :22).
8. Do not rank potential readers by likely agreement (Fig 2 :40). Use the institutional venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
