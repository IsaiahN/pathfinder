## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not *which of the three sources wins*, but *what kind of residual is live and what that residual owes*. In effect terms: an AI system holds a library Γ formed by training, receives an observation o′, and can admit atoms from an external source. Its prediction Γ(b,a) misses o′ by a residual R that is indexed per object slot (Fig 1 :19), not averaged into one global score (Fig 1 :22). A "conflict among three sources" is the name we give to that live residual before it has been sorted.

- Option A, "resolve by combining the three": leads to mutual update or averaging. Fig 2 :13 names the failure: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*; Fig 2 :14-15 says they converge on *"whoever had the most influence."* Where training and an external source share a corpus, Fig 2 :18-21 names the second failure: *"one evidence pool... they never talk, and still agree."*
- Option B, "range each frame separately against an immovable ground and sort the residual by kind before answering": leads to a refusal where a held term is false, an import where a peer frame holds the missing atom, and an honest `unreachable` where no ground reading exists. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** naming, for each residual kind, which operation is owed (refuse, re-fit, extend perception, mint, or import), plus an instrument that would discriminate a false held training regularity from an observation-channel failure from a shared-pool echo. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the AI conflict-resolution question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of belief revision.

Four figure-grounded reasons:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. An undeclared stake is modelled anyway. *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* (Fig 10 :60). The packet declares the author's stake in the framework and records that the second seat shares the same evidence pool.

Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
