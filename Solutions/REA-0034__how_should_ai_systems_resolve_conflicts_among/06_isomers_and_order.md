# Structure, order and analogues in a three-source conflict

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The plain-language question treats training, observation and external sources as three items to be combined. Two supporting derivations are owed: (1) are they the same kind of thing under the figure's bond rules, or are they bonded differently, and (2) in what order must the operations run? A third, the analogue check, tests whether the formal belief-revision literature is a genuine low-overlap corroboration or a borrowed name.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the three sources isomers, or different bond types?

- Operators table :70-73: *"Seven of the eight are bonds: they join two operands"*; negation *"joins nothing, so its symmetry entry is empty rather than no."*
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."**
- Training and an external source can hold the *same atom* under different bonds. Fig 8 :50-51: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* The difference is carried by the bond and the record, not by the atom.
- An observation is not a frame at all: it is the term against which R is measured (Symbols table, o′ and R). Bonding it symmetrically with a belief is a type error, the same shape as bonding two states directly in the six-term substrate.
- ⇒ The three are **not isomers**: they do not share one atom set under different arrangements. Training and external source are two frames that may hold the same atom by convergence or adoption; the observation is contact; the anchor is neither. The only honest comparison is a **functional-group** reading (Fig 12 :123-124: *"a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome"*): each has the role "ranges against the ground", but the role does not make them interchangeable.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if training, observation and external source can be written as the same atom set under three different bonds with no remainder, the isomer reading holds and this finding fails.

### Q2 — Contraction versus revision: ¬ is not a smaller weight

- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- Operators table :73-74: *"− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim. A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*
- Lowering w is a **subtraction-like** move: it removes influence from an operand while leaving the operand in place. Refusing a held term is a **negation**: it removes the term's standing to be composed over, and the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :42-43).
- The belief-revision analogue is the Levi identity K∗P = (K−¬P)+P, which defines revision through contraction, and the eight AGM postulates (Wikipedia, *Belief revision*, read this session). The analogy is real but partial: the AGM recovery postulate K = (K−P)+P can reintroduce a contracted belief, while Fig 6 :45-46 says a refused term *"becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth."*

**Conclusion:** contraction and re-weighting are different operations from refusal. The world literature corroborates the need for a distinct operator; it does not decide it. The recovery-postulate difference is a live strain, recorded, not smoothed (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6).

**Falsifier:** if a contracted term in a real system can re-enter the reach with no new mint and the residual still closes, the figure's monotonic-record claim is wrong for that domain.

### Q3 — Dependency order: refuse before mint, describe before frame

- Formula step 2: the fifth kind (REFUTED) is sorted by what is wrong, and *"no arrangement of the other four reaches it."* So a refusal cannot be reached by more searching, more weights or more imports.
- Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** Applied here: you do not design a source-discriminating instrument from a description of "independence"; you sharpen an instrument already returning a per-slot residual.
- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** The residual (persistent flat R, shared-pool echo, channel failure) is described before the external source is chosen.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** A first-order ground-settled belief becomes an atom for the second-order comparison with an external source.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** If the anchor is missing, better weights at D1 cannot rescue D3.

**Order:** (1) name the anchor and confirm it does not move; (2) run the boundary diff per slot; (3) if REFUTED, refuse before minting; (4) if a mechanism is owed, mint inside the closure, with support, reachability and novelty as a product (Fig 5 :33); (5) if the atom is outside the closure, import with provenance and a shadow test; (6) settle at the ground before composing over the result.

**Falsifier:** show a deployment where importing an atom before refusing a false held term closes the residual and the false term is thereby also corrected. Then the refusal can be reached through import and the order is wrong.

No analogue conflicts with the figure verdict. The two moderate-overlap cases (AGM, Popper) are in philosophy and are expected to rhyme; they are one evidence pool with the figures' own provenance, not independent witnesses. The four low-overlap cases are genuine corroboration but secondary.

## CONCLUSION

The three sources are not isomers. They are two frames plus contact plus an immovable anchor, and the differences are carried by bonds and records, not by atoms. Re-weighting is subtraction-like; refusing a false term is negation, and a vocabulary without ¬ can only accumulate. The operations are ordered by dependency: anchor first, refuse before mint, describe before import, settle before composing. The AGM literature corroborates the need for a distinct contraction operator but differs on reversibility; that difference is recorded as a strain.
