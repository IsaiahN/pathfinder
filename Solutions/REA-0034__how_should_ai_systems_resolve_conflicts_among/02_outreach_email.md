# Outreach draft — check the sorting rule before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape below survives the recipient's own check. If it does not, there is nothing else to show.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how an AI should sort a source conflict

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about belief revision. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an AI system's training, current observations and external sources are not three peers to be combined. They are two frames plus one point of contact, and none of them is the anchor. A conflict arrives as a per-slot residual, and before anything is changed that residual has to be sorted by kind. Four kinds say something is missing (extend perception, re-fit, mint a mechanism, import an atom). A fifth says something held is wrong, and what is owed there is a refusal of that term, not a smaller weight for it.
>
> The reason is not a preference for one design. If the three sources update on one another, they converge on whoever had the most influence, and the drift is coherent. If the training set and the "external" source came from the same corpus, they never talk and still agree, so the agreement is the pool talking. An anchor that is allowed to move when the frames disagree has become a fourth frame.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that averaging is always inefficient in practice; that the belief-revision literature is wrong; that I can name which stored term is false in any particular system; or that an external source is useless unless it passes a gate I have not described. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The claim is about the operations the residual owes, not about which source wins.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a three-source conflict closed by mutual update where the anchor demonstrably did not move and the result later pays at the ground;
> 2. a held term with a residual that persists and does not trend down, closed by re-weighting alone while still composing over downstream terms;
> 3. an external atom from outside the receiving closure that moves the reach with no provenance record and no pre-stated gap;
> 4. self-agreement, with no independent ground, that reliably predicts later ground readings on new slots.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a belief-revision problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
