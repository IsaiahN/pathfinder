# Expert verification packet — the Tether on three-source conflict resolution

**What this is:** a claim in a form a belief-revision researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No deployment measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Training, current observations and external sources are not three peers to be combined. They are two frames plus a point of contact, and none of them is the anchor that settles the conflict. A conflict arrives as a per-slot residual and must be sorted by kind before any operation is applied. Four kinds say something is missing and owe perception, re-fitting, minting or import; a fifth says a held term is false and owes a refusal, which is not the same as lowering a weight on it. External sources are imports, admitted only with provenance and a shadow test and debited against their independence. The ground decides. When no source-discriminating instrument exists, the honest output is `unreachable`, not a synthesised winner.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 9
- **Statement:** the three sources are not symmetric peers, and a conflict is a union of residual kinds that must be split rather than averaged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :46 — *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."* Fig 2 :13 — *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*; Fig 2 :15 — *"is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 9 :5 — *"Split it rather than search."*
- **Refuter:** a three-source conflict closed by mutual update where the anchor demonstrably did not move and the result later pays at the ground; or a single operation that closes a conflict the boundary diff assigned to two residual kinds.

### C2 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and the Operators table
- **Statement:** a persistent, flat residual on a held term owes a refusal (¬), not a re-weighting; nothing is deleted and the reach falls because a refusal was added.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 — *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 5 :59 — *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"*. Operators table :57 — *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Fig 6 :42 — *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*
- **Refuter:** a flat-residual term closed by changing w alone while remaining composable over downstream terms; or a contracted term that re-enters the reach with no new mint and the residual still closes (the AGM recovery-postulate strain).

### C3 — both framework-internal and world-facing; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 8 plus the cited sources
- **Statement:** an external source is an import, not a second witness by default. It needs provenance, a shadow test, and a debit against its independence. Training and a corpus-scraped "external" source sharing an evidence pool are one frame wearing two names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 — *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :51 — *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- **What to check against the world literature:** Shumailov et al. (2024, *Nature*) report that models trained on recursively generated data *"mis-perceive reality"*; the crowd literature reports that averaging removes random but not *"systematic errors that affect the opinions of the entire crowd in the same way"*; source criticism says *"If a number of independent sources contain the same message, the credibility of the message is strongly increased."* These corroborate; they do not decide.
- **Refuter:** an external atom outside the receiving closure that moves the reach with no provenance record and no pre-stated gap; or self-agreement with no independent ground that reliably predicts later ground readings on new slots.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that averaging is always inefficient, only that it is not a verdict under shared influence or a shared pool.
- Not that training is wrong and observations are right, or the reverse. Neither is an anchor.
- Not that the AGM tradition, the recovery postulate, or the world sources are mistaken. The recovery difference is recorded as a strain.
- Not that a particular stored term is false in any deployment, and not a name for a winning source. The framework returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and this simulated derivation cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument:

1. **Flat-residual test:** does a held training term's per-slot residual persist and fail to trend down under re-weighting?
2. **Independence test:** can a provenance record show the two sources draw on different evidence?
3. **Anchor test:** does the settling item change its answer when a frame pushes on it?
4. **Standing falsifiers:** the five listed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 5.
