# Isomers, union and order in the centralization question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two companies can publish the same org chart and behave differently, and two different charts can behave identically. The figures say the chart is only the ingredient list. What is missing is the typed relation between a decision and the evidence that settles it. Two questions are owed: (1) is the centralize/decentralize choice a union or a partition, and in what dependency order should it be made; (2) are two decision-rights systems with the same boxes **isomers** under Fig 12's definition?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the decision classes of a company (slot judgments, methods, shared meanings, missing atoms, standards). Bonds held: the seven, especially → sequence, ⋛ comparison, − subtraction, plus negation. No new decision right is available without an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- Fig 9 :4-5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

The decision to set a price in a region can be both a slot-local judgment (condition A) and a case where the atom is absent from the local closure (condition B). Those conditions overlap. A lever that clears only A (give pricing to regions) leaves the intersection standing, because B still binds. So the portfolio is a union and must be unbundled before any level is assigned. This is not a claim that centralization and decentralization are both half right. It is a structural claim that one scalar cannot carry two conditions.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a company whose decision classes are pairwise disjoint on measured residuals, so a partition is licensed and one lever per class clears the portfolio. If the intersections are empty and measured, the union claim fails for that company.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The order is: perceive the slot's residual, then have terms for what the slot affords, then state what counts as progress in that slot's own terms, then plan and delegate. A headquarters that delegates before the local objective is statable is planning above the break. Fig 3 :24-27 note that the middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what counts as progress, has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious instrument, so it attracts the least attention. In organization design that is the link between "the team has a dashboard" and "the team can say what a good decision would look like". A dashboard is not an objective.

- **Falsifier:** a company where delegation measurably improves ground outcomes while the local objective remains unstatable by the local team. That would mean the middle link can be skipped.

### Q3 - Are two decision-rights systems isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :43: **"one bond type costs nothing; seven cost 2.8 bits each"**

Write company A and company B over the same atoms: the same roles, the same data, the same decision classes, the same ground. Let them differ only in the bonds: in A a regional decision is `evidence → decision` (sequence) with the center reading the ground separately; in B the same decision is `center target ⋛ regional reading` (comparison against a fixed threshold). Same ingredients, different bond, different system. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not variants of one design. The org chart cannot tell them apart, because the chart records the atoms and not the bond.

The weaker reading is the **functional group**: both systems share the substructure `local contact → decision → ground reading`; the qualifier (which bond joins the center) says which member of the group each is. This is what a benchmark that only counts "how many decisions are made locally" can see. It sees the group and misses the isomer.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two companies with the same atoms but different decision-rights bonds show the same ground readings across the same slots and the same period, the bond carries nothing and the isomer claim fails; the functional-group reading stands. A tie on an internal satisfaction score does not count, because that is a frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :24-27).

### Q4 - Does a settled decision become an atom for the next level?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

A local decision that has been settled against the ground can be treated as an atom by the next level: a regional outcome becomes an input to a portfolio decision. This is legal only *because* it settled. A decision that has not been settled cannot be composed over, no matter how many layers ratify it. That is the figure-grounded difference between a decision that has paid and a decision that has merely been approved.

The same rule licenses the second-order claim in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: the center's portfolio choice is a composition over settled slot outcomes, not a re-decision of them. If the slot outcome was never settled (no ground reading), the portfolio composition has no atoms to use and the escalation is a replay, not a promotion.

- **Caveat, named not hidden:** settling is necessary and not sufficient. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "Settling is necessary for a term to be composed over and it is NOT SUFFICIENT." A settled outcome may still fail to compose because of its bond type. This needs a company-specific instrument, not an argument.

### Q5 - Analogue: how do collective systems actually decide?

This is a supporting analogue, not a governing figure line. List, Elsholtz and Seeley (2009), *Independence and interdependence in collective decision making: an agent-based model of nest-site choice by honeybee swarms*, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 364(1518), 755-762, read 2026-11-22 at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2689716. Quote: "The bees' independence in assessing the various sites' quality and their interdependence through communication are both necessary and sufficient for the reliability of the bees' decision process." Their model predicts a reliable choice only with the right mix; pure mimicry produces cascades onto a bad option, pure independence loses the concentration of attention.

Mapped to a company in effect terms: local teams must inspect their own ground (independence) while sharing methods (interdependence), and the center must not override the independence with a replicated narrative. This corroborates Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 7 :33, and it is a genuinely low-overlap field. **It does not return an optimal independence coefficient for a firm.** The analogue licenses the shape (both are needed) and no number.

- **Falsifier for the analogue:** a collective system in which reliability is unchanged when independent assessment is replaced entirely by mimicry. Under the cited model it should collapse into an informational cascade.

### Q6 - Price of the two readings

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A central standard is cheap to describe (a sentence) and may take a whole budget to settle at the ground. A local experiment may be expensive to describe and cheap to refute. Fig 12 :127: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind." So the decision-rights rule must carry two readings: the description cost of the rule and the action cost of checking it. Summing them into one "efficiency" score is the error the figure names, not the fix.

## CONCLUSION

The centralize/decentralize question is a **union**, not a partition, and must be unbundled before a level is assigned (Q1, falsified by measured empty intersections). The dependency order is perception, vocabulary, objective, plan (Q2, falsified if delegation works with no local objective). Two systems with identical boxes but different decision-rights bonds are **isomers** under Fig 12, not variants of one design (Q3, falsified by identical ground readings across both). A settled slot outcome becomes an atom for the next level; an approved but unsettled one does not (Q4). A low-overlap biological analogue supports the independence-plus-interdependence shape and returns no coefficient (Q5). Both the description cost and the action cost must be read, never summed (Q6).

What survives without a new primitive: the union, the order, the isomer distinction, the settlement gate, and the two-currency price. What does not survive: any universal percentage, any single "best" degree, and any claim that a chart records the arrangement.
