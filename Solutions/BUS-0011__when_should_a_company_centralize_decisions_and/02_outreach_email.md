# Outreach draft - check the decision rule before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can see the refuter without seeing the framework. If the stage-1 output cannot be checked on its own, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about centralizing versus local decision rights

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in organization design and I would like you to check one output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a company has many local slots (accounts, regions, products, plants) and the error signal from a decision lives on a particular slot, not on a company-wide average. From that alone it follows that decision rights should sit at the smallest frame that can predict the slot's residual and whose channel to that slot's ground is reachable and does not move when other frames change their minds. I am not claiming a single percentage of decisions should be centralized. The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so the honest answer is a rule per decision class, not one lever.
>
> The rule predicts a split. Centralize methods, shared meanings, instruments, and atoms that no local frame can represent. Do not centralize a local judgment whose ground the local team can read independently. A standard that fixes an outcome is not a standard, it is a decision, and it needs a different check.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a theory of everything, that it names the right org chart, that it says which structure is best for all firms, or that it is settled by experiment. It returns a shape that any answer must have, not the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a decision with a demonstrably live per-slot residual that is decided better centrally, repeatedly, on a measure that reads the slot's ground rather than internal compliance;
> 2. a pooled company-wide reading that retains a slot-specific failure the per-slot reading missed;
> 3. a ground that demonstrably moved because two internal frames updated on each other, with no independent reading catching it;
> 4. an improvement to the central team in isolation, with no change in contact with any local habitat, that measurably improves local outcomes;
> 5. one lever that clears the entire decision portfolio including the intersections, measured rather than assumed.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the rule for that decision class. A one-line "this fails because..." is worth more to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (local canonical copies): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - Public submission and contact venues in organization design: Journal of Organization Design, https://link.springer.com/journal/41469/submission-guidelines
> - Academy of Management, Organization and Management Theory division: https://www.aom.org/divisions-and-interest-groups/organization-and-management-theory
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what an answer would look like before you run it, and write down what would refute it before you run it. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the prediction has to be an ordinary term before it can be refused. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a reader who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states a mode and writes a refuter before running it.

One-to-one contact only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message. Nothing in this folder is sent by this agent.
