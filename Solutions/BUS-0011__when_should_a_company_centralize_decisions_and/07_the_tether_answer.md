# The Tether's answer: when to centralize and when to delegate

**Mode: `specified` for the decision-rights rule, `general` for the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a company-specific result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No company was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A decision in a company is an action on a slot. The residual `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` is the gap between what the deciding frame expected and what the slot returned. It is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.") and never by a company-wide average (Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."). The question is where authority should sit so that R_s can be seen and the ground reached. It is a **union** of overlapping decision conditions, not one lever (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The answer

1. **Authority follows the slot structure of the residual and the location of reachable, non-updating ground contact.** A decision whose signal lives in one slot and whose ground the local team can read independently belongs at that slot. Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
2. **Centralize methods, not recordings.** What moves up is a verified, reapplicable method; what moves down is a head start; a recording of one success never crosses. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A central pricing *review method* is a method. A central *price* is a recording pushed down as a replay.
3. **Centralize only genuinely absent atoms, with provenance.** A local closure cannot derive what it cannot represent. When a decision needs an atom no local frame holds (specialist legal judgment, safety physics, capital model, cross-region negotiation), import it from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23) and pay the shadow test and source gate. Default centralization is an unpriced import; a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).
4. **Keep the anchor non-updating.** The ground is the customer outcome, the market result, the safety incident, the realized margin, and it does not move because headquarters and the region agreed (Fig 2 :29, Fig 2 :35). Local teams are independent frames ranging against that ground separately. A center that revises its reading to match what locals report has become a mirror (Fig 2 :33-34).
5. **Do not escalate through agreeing layers.** Handing a decision up a chain of rooms that share a closure adds agreement and not evidence (Fig 7 :12-13). Select between differing local rooms against the ground. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
6. **The center's office is the channel, not the verdict.** Keep instruments reachable, route methods up and head starts down, rule with provenance, allocate, rotate stale checking frames (Seat Map :93). A standard that fixes an outcome is a verdict wearing a convention's clothes and has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a percentage.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No figure line returns an optimal centralization ratio or an org chart.

## 3. A decision-rights table (the operational form of the shape)

Each row is a separate verdict. They are never averaged into one score.

| Customer-specific concessions, account priorities, local scheduling | local | Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :40 | Is there a live per-slot residual and an independent ground reading at the slot? If yes, delegate. |

A question the table does not answer: what fraction of a particular company's decisions fall in each row. That is the declared `gap` of F10. It needs that company's ground readings, not a better argument.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that centralization is wrong. Several rows license it, and they are the ones where the ground must be the same everywhere or the atom is genuinely absent locally.
- Not that local teams are always right. A local team whose closure lacks the atom, whose ground is not independently reachable, or whose dashboard is the same as headquarters' has none of the properties that license delegation.
- Not a single percentage, a maturity model, or an org chart. Those are names where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that a famous framework or a successful competitor's structure settles anything. Prestige can fund a trial; it cannot settle it (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly.").
- Not `grounded`. No company measurement settled `left(R,φ)` for any row.
- Not that this derivation settles the question. It is simulated and internally consistent, not a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating reading per decision class, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument "cannot comment on the source", and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here the questions are:

- **Slot test:** does the decision have a live per-slot residual that a company-wide aggregate misses? (Fig 1 :22)
- **Anchor test:** does the proposed ground move when two internal frames update on each other? If it moves, it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Contact test:** does an improvement to the central team with no change in contact measurably improve local outcomes? If yes, Fig 11 :40 breaks.
- **Membrane test:** does the material crossing between levels survive re-application in a different slot, or is it a recording of one success (Fig 4 :23)?
- **Isomer test:** do two systems with the same boxes but different bonds produce different ground readings (Fig 12 :117)?
- **Convention test:** does the "standard" fix what value the ground should return? If yes, it is a verdict (Fig 10 :47).

## 6. The honest stop

Unreached, not unreachable, for any specific company. The figures fix the shape of the rule and refuse to name the allocation. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Here it is not even unreachable: the company's own ground readings would settle each row. What stops this derivation is that no such readings were taken. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. Standing falsifiers

Written in advance in `01` §4. In short: a slot-live decision decided better centrally on a ground measure; a pooled reading retaining a signal the per-slot reading missed; a ground moved by internal updating; an isolated central improvement that helps locally; one lever clearing the whole portfolio including intersections. Any one retracts the rule for that class.

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say centralize or decentralize. It says authority should follow the slot structure of the residual and the location of ground contact. A decision whose signal lives in one slot and whose ground the local team can read belongs locally. What headquarters should centralize is the method, the shared meaning, the instrument and the atom no local frame can represent, never a recording of one success pushed down as a replay. The ground, a customer outcome or a measured safety result, must not move because two internal frames agreed; a chain of agreeing approvals adds spelling, not evidence. A standard that fixes an outcome is a decision, not a convention. This returns the shape any answer must have and five refuters written in advance. It does not return a percentage or an org chart, and nothing in it is settled until a company's own ground readings say so.
