# Expert verification packet - the Tether on centralization and local authority

**What this is:** a claim in a form an organization-design researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the decision-rights rule, `general` for the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No company measurement is claimed. It returns a shape and a test, not an allocation.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A company's decision residual is indexed per slot, not per company. Authority should therefore sit at the smallest frame that can predict the slot's residual and whose channel to that slot's ground is reachable and does not move when other frames change their minds. Centralize methods, shared meanings, instruments and atoms genuinely absent from local closure; do not centralize a local judgment whose ground the local team can read, and do not send a recording of one success down as a replay. Escalating through rooms that share a dashboard adds agreement, not evidence. A standard that fixes an outcome has stopped being a convention and needs a verdict's checking arrangement. The framework returns this shape, not a percentage or an org chart.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** the error signal lives on a slot, and capability is a property of the team and its habitat, not of the team alone. Aggregating across slots deletes the signal that says where to look.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :40 - "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** is the per-slot claim actually a structural consequence of the figures, or is it being smuggled in from economics (Hayek's local knowledge) under a figure citation? The derivation in `01` F1 and F6 keeps the two sources separate.
- **Refuter:** a decision whose residual is genuinely single-slot at the needed resolution, where pooling retains the live signal and improves the ground reading; or an isolated improvement to the central team that measurably improves local outcomes with no change in contact.

### C2 - organization-facing, checkable by a practitioner against what their center actually sends

- **Statement:** what may cross between a local team and the center is a reapplicable method upward and a head start downward. A recording of one success never crosses. A standard that fixes an outcome is a decision wearing a convention's clothes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :23 - "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 10 :47 - "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** take one central rule you know. Does it state how to decide (a method) or what to decide (a recording)? Does it set a shared meaning or does it fix the value the ground should have returned? Under the rule the two cases go to different levels.
- **Refuter:** a recording of one success, carried up and replayed down with no re-application test, that improves ground readings across independent slots; or a standard that demonstrably fixes an outcome while leaving the ground non-updating and remaining checkable by a frame that did not author it.

### C3 - verification-facing, checkable by a researcher against their own data

- **Statement:** the ground must not move because two internal frames agree, and a chain of agreeing rooms adds no evidence. Selection between differing rooms is where the ground votes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 7 :35 - "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :12-13 - "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- **What to check:** in a decision review chain you know, do the rooms hold independent readings of the same non-updating ground, or do they read the same dashboard? If the latter, the chain is one long room regardless of how many approvals it has.
- **Refuter:** an escalation chain whose rooms share an evidence pool is shown to add ground-visible evidence at each handoff; or a ground that moved because two internal frames updated on each other with no independent reading detecting the move.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that centralization is wrong, and not that local teams are always right. Several rows in `07` license centralization; several kinds of local team have none of the properties that license delegation.
- Not a universal centralization percentage, a maturity model, or a universal org chart. Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that this applies to a firm exactly as it applies to a market. Hayek (1945) and the honeybee model are secondary corroboration from different domains; the binding to a company is `specified` and carries no source gate by itself.
- Not a theory of everything. There is no last room; each decision class keeps its own loop and its own ground.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by a company measurement.
- Not a claim that the framework names a level for every decision class in every company. That is the declared gap.
- Not a claim from prestige. "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read 2026-11-22.)

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading per decision class, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument "cannot comment on the source", and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

1. **Slot test:** does the decision have a live per-slot residual that a company-wide aggregate misses?
2. **Anchor test:** does the proposed ground move when two internal frames update on each other?
3. **Contact test:** does an isolated improvement to the central team improve local outcomes with no change in contact?
4. **Membrane test:** does the material crossing levels survive re-application in a different slot, or is it a replay of one success?
5. **Convention test:** does the "standard" fix what value the ground should have returned?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 11 :40, three short passages. Decide whether the per-slot claim is figure-derived or imported (C1).
2. Take one central rule you know and classify it as method, recording, convention or outcome-fixing decision (C2).
3. Take one approval chain you know and say whether its rooms hold independent readings of a non-updating ground, or read the same dashboard (C3).
4. Name the one measurement that would settle the classification for your organization. If none exists, say so. That is a legitimate answer and it is recorded as a gap.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. No reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
