## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **The local-knowledge argument (economics).** Hayek (1945) is the canonical statement that the relevant knowledge is "the knowledge of the particular circumstances of time and place" and cannot be conveyed to a central authority in statistical form. That is Fig 1 :22 written in a different vocabulary: aggregation deletes the local signal. It corroborates F1 but is a secondary witness, and it governs a market, not a firm, so the binding to a company is a `specified` mapping with no source gate.
2. **Collective decision making (animal behaviour).** List, Elsholtz and Seeley (2009) show both independence and interdependence are necessary for reliable collective choice. Pure mimicry produces informational cascades onto a bad option; pure independence loses the snowballing that concentrates attention. This corroborates F7 and Fig 2 :31-32, and it is a genuinely low-overlap field. It does not license the claim that a firm's optimal independence coefficient is 0.8; no figure or source returns that number.
3. **Contingency theory (public administration).** The JPART study reports that "moderation matters more than the polar ends" and that different change types respond to different degrees of centralization. This corroborates F5: one lever does not clear a union. The abstract was read; the full article was not, so no claim beyond the abstract is made.
4. **Systems decomposition (engineering).** Browning (2001) treats products, processes and organizations as complex systems whose behaviour arises from the integration of decomposed subsystems. This corroborates Fig 9 :4-5 ("Split it rather than search") and Fig 3 dependency order. It is a method analogue, not evidence that a particular org chart is correct.
5. **Practice framing (business textbook).** OpenStax 7.5 lists the contingencies practitioners actually use. These are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). They are recorded so the answer can be compared against what a reader will already expect, and so the figure-derived rule does not silently inherit their authority.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. They are secondary; the figures decide.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source", and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The reader checks the rule without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a reader has the framework, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown.
3. **Stage 3 sends a method**, which Fig 4 :22 permits. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user still states a mode and writes a refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The five refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are written before the run precisely so that a failure can be reported cleanly.

## Practical constraints

One-to-one contact only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message. Use public institutional submission portals and opt-in community channels. Do not use personal emails harvested from pages, purchased lists or private posts. GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules apply to unsolicited outreach in many jurisdictions.
