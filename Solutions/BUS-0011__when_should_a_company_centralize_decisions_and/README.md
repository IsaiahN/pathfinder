## The question restated as an effect

A company is a system with many local slots (regions, customer accounts, product lines, plants). A decision allocates authority between a central frame and a local frame. Option A, centralize: one frame at the top decides for all slots. Option B, local authority: each local frame decides for its own slot. What differs is not where an org chart puts a box, but where the residual is measured, whether the ground can be reached without passing through another frame, and whether the frames checking a decision are independent or are mirrors of one another. An answer would look like a rule that assigns decision classes to levels and states what would falsify each assignment. That rule is bound as an ordinary term in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## Verdict in one line

The figures do not return "centralize" or "decentralize". They return a shape: **authority follows the slot structure of the residual and the location of contact with the ground.** Centralize the things that must be the same for every slot and the things no local closure holds; decentralize the things whose signal lives in one slot and whose ground is reachable there. The split is a union of overlapping conditions, not one lever (Fig 9 :54-55). Every verdict is `specified`, candidate, and open to the five refuters in section 4 of `01`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that centralization or decentralization is good.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."
2. **Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A list ranked by receptiveness to a preferred structure is a list selected to agree.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends the only thing an outside frame contributes.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. Public institutional submission portals and opt-in community channels avoid both.
