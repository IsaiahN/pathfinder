## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question offers two prerequisites for the same origin:

- **A, metabolism first:** a self-maintaining reaction network, driven by gradients and mineral catalysts, synthesizes nucleotides and other monomers before any polymer exists. Polymers are downstream outputs.
- **B, polymers first:** an inheritable polymer with catalytic function (an RNA-world-like replicator) precedes network-level metabolism. Metabolism is downstream, because the polymer's enzymes or ribozymes perform it.

What is being decided is an **ordering/dependency** between two candidate terms. Effect: which term must be available before the other can be composed. The question as posed assumes a **chain** (one came first). The derivation asks: is the relation a chain at all, and what shape must any admissible answer have?

This is not one hard question. It bundles at least three conditions (supply of monomers, heritable ordering, catalytic closure). Fig 9 :4 warns: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

**Partition or union?** D1 to D3 are a **union**, not a partition: monomers feed both network formation and polymer formation; polymers (ribozymes) catalyze network reactions; networks concentrate and supply monomers. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The overlaps are compounds with their own loops, so each gets its own loop before any global verdict.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* Monomers precede polymer assembly; but a sustaining network may be required to maintain monomer supply; and a catalytic polymer can close the loop by catalyzing the network. The dependency graph is therefore cyclic, and the right order of inquiry is: split the union, then test closure, then (only if a genuine linear dependency survives) order the terms.

## 3. Findings F1 to F7

### F1 - The either/or is a bundled union, not a partition (decisive reframing)

Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The metabolism-first and polymers-first literatures each work well on a subset (monomer supply versus inheritance). That is the signature of several well-formed questions, not one hard question. Split into (a) can monomers arise prebiotically, (b) can a polymer inherit and catalyze, (c) does a network close a self-maintaining loop. A single lever ("which came first") clears regions where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Refuter for F1:** if one condition proves sufficient for a complete origin, so that the question is a genuine partition and a strict linear priority exists, the union reading is refuted.

### F2 - The dependency closes a cycle, which a chain vocabulary cannot express (decisive)

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

Fig 12 :48: *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."*

The network supplies monomers; monomers compose into polymers; a catalytic polymer (ribozyme) survives its use and catalyzes the network that supplies its own monomers. This is Fig 12's cycle: the output re-enters as input. Asking "which came first" imposes `->` (sequence, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :30) on a relation whose own figure says a chain cannot close by construction. The catalyst line explains why the loop can persist: a part that survives its own use is exactly what turns a linear chain into a self-maintaining cycle.

**Refuter for F2:** a demonstration that one subsystem can arise and evolve heritably with no contribution from the other, making the other strictly downstream, breaks the cycle and reinstates the chain.

### F3 - No rank among the kinds; the two structures join through a state (strain handled by re-census)

Fig 13 :4: *"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."*

Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*

**Strain test.** Reading metabolism as "the state kind" and polymers as "the structure kind" is a **strain**: both are assemblies over the six (metabolism is energy plus gradient plus network; polymers are ordering realized in space and time). A strained reading governs another quantity, so I re-census. The correct use of :4 is structural: the framework grants no priority to one kind over the other, so the question's demand to rank one above the other is already a category error. The correct direct mapping is :75: metabolism's gradient/network and the polymer's spectrum are two **structures**, and two structures join only through a **state** (energy flow or time). That is the cross-kind medium the cycle must pass through. Fig 13 :28: *"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."*

**Refuter for F3:** if a figure re-census shows the framework must rank one kind above the other, or that two structures may bond directly, the no-rank reading falls.

### F4 - The historical name is unreachable from the figures; it is a ground question

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

The figures return the **shape** of the dependency (a cycle crossing kinds through a state), not the historical name ("metabolism first" or "RNA first"). Which route actually ran on early Earth is decided by the ground: synthesis experiments, replication experiments, and the geological record. The figures cannot name it, and a search that does not find it is not proof it did not happen. This is why the verdict is `specified` on shape and `gap` on historical order, not an attempted answer to the either/or.

**Refuter for F4:** a ground reading that establishes a strict temporal order (one subsystem demonstrably absent while the other operated) closes the gap and overturns the co-emergence shape.

### F5 - Selection, not a designed first term

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*

The origin is a lineage that met its ground, not a term that authored the others. Asking which term "did the producing" as if it were a designer repeats the error the ground clause removes. Capability belongs to the network-and-habitat, and the habitat must be enumerated (conditions, gradients, surfaces, wet-dry cycles), not composed from a favored story.

**Refuter for F5:** a demonstrated mechanism in which one term designs or authors the other, rather than selection acting on a lineage, would break this reading.

### F6 - The ground is experiment, and agreement is not a witness

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

The chemistry frame (supply) and the information frame (inheritance) are low-overlap witnesses, so their agreement can mean something only if each is ranged separately against that anchor. Agreement among members of one camp is the pool talking.

**Refuter for F6:** if both frames draw on the same evidence pool (for example, both rest on the same single experiment), their apparent convergence is one witness and carries no triangulated weight.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single condition proves sufficient for a complete origin, so the union splits to a partition with a strict linear priority.
2. One subsystem is shown to arise and evolve heritably with no contribution from the other, making it strictly upstream.
3. A figure re-census shows a genuine `->` sequence with no cycle, or permits two structures to bond directly without a state.
4. A ground reading (synthesis or replication experiment, or geological evidence) establishes the historical order directly.

If any fires, the co-emergence shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.
