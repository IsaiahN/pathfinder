# The Tether's answer: did early metabolism produce the building blocks, or depend on genetic polymers?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural limit, `gap` on the historical order. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No experiment was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which prerequisite came first: a self-maintaining reaction network that supplies monomers, or an inheritable polymer that supplies catalytic function and sequence information. Answered as an either/or, it bundles three conditions (monomer supply, inheritance, catalytic closure) and treats a union as a partition. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The residual is an ordering question applied to a relation that is not one straight line.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split before answering.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Monomer supply, polymer inheritance and network closure each get their own loop and their own ground.
2. **The dependency is a cycle, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The network produces monomers; monomers compose into a polymer; a catalyst that survives its own use (Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*) returns catalytic function to the network.
3. **The two structures join through a state, with no rank.** Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* Fig 13 :4: *"no member of the pair is senior to the other."* Metabolism's gradient/network and the polymer's ordering meet through energy flow or time.
4. **Polymer and network are isomers over a shared monomer set, or at least functional-group kin.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* One is a chain (the degenerate case, Fig 12 :48), the other a network structure. Their union is second-order: each, settled, becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104).
5. **Selection, not a designed first term.** Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Capability belongs to network-and-habitat, not to a single privileged molecule.
6. **The historical order is not delivered.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures return the shape; the name ("metabolism first" or "RNA first") waits on the ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "metabolism came first", and not "RNA came first". Both are names for a route the figures cannot select.
- Not that chemistry has settled the origin. Patel et al. 2015 and Sutherland 2010 are secondary corroboration of the shape, not a ground reading and not a historical verdict.
- Not a new primitive, not a single lever, not a merge of the two literatures into one camp.
- Not `grounded`. No synthesis or replication experiment was run here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The anchors here are:

1. **Supply test:** can all monomer classes be synthesized from plausible feedstocks in one geochemical sequence (Patel 2015 is a step toward this, not the last word)?
2. **Inheritance test:** can a polymer copy itself heritably and catalyze reactions under the same conditions?
3. **Closure test:** does a network close a self-maintaining loop that returns catalytic function to its own production?
4. **Historical discriminator:** geological or experimental evidence that one subsystem was absent while the other operated. This, and only this, closes the `gap` on order.

Standing falsifiers from the audit: a single sufficient condition (union reduces to partition); one subsystem strictly upstream of the other; a figure re-census permitting a one-way sequence with no cycle; a ground reading establishing the order.

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* The six-term set is a visible guess at the compression axes, not nature's own list, and another instrument could change the shape. The derivation stops where the available instruments stop discriminating and records the lineage. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in the metabolism-first versus genetics-first argument. It says the question is a bundle that must be split before it is searched: monomer supply, polymer inheritance and network closure are three conditions with three grounds. It says the dependency between the network and the polymer is a cycle, not a chain, because a chain cannot close by construction; the network makes monomers, monomers make a polymer, and a catalyst that survives its own use returns catalytic function to the network. The two structures meet through a state such as energy or time, and neither kind is senior to the other. Over a shared monomer set the polymer and the network stand as isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, with their union one level up. What the framework refuses is the name. Which route actually ran on early Earth is left as `gap`, to be closed by synthesis experiments, replication experiments and geological evidence. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
