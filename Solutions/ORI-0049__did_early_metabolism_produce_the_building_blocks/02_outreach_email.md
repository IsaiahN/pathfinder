# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding which theory to believe

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive checking before any theory is shown. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on the metabolism / genetic-polymers question

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the "did metabolism come first, or did genetic polymers come first" question. I am not asking you to pick a side, and I am not asking you to read a theory first. I am asking whether the output below survives contact with what you already know. If it fails, that is the useful result.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question as posed is a single either/or, but it bundles at least three conditions: can building blocks (nucleotides, sugars, bases) arise prebiotically; can a polymer inherit sequence and catalyze reactions; and can a reaction network close a self-maintaining loop. Each condition has its own ground. Fig 9 :54 says that when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, you unbundle before you search again. Fig 9 :57 adds that a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
>
> The dependency between network and polymer does not look like a one-way chain. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The network supplies monomers; monomers compose into polymers; a catalytic polymer (a ribozyme) survives its own use (Fig 12 :119) and catalyzes the network that supplies its own monomers. The two structures meet through a state, Fig 13 :75: *"two structures join only through a state."* The framework grants no rank to either kind (Fig 13 :4).
>
> **What this does not say:** it does not say metabolism came first, and it does not say RNA came first. It does not name the historical route. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Which route actually ran is a ground question for synthesis experiments, replication experiments, and the geological record, and it is recorded here as `gap`, not silence. Patel et al. (Nature Chemistry, 2015) showed that precursors of ribonucleotides, amino acids and lipids can arise from common chemistry, and Sutherland (2010) called the isolation question open and addressable through chemistry. That is corroboration of the shape, not a ground reading and not a verdict on history.
>
> **What would refute the output (written before this message):**
> 1. one condition proves sufficient for a complete origin, so the union is really a partition with a strict priority;
> 2. one subsystem is shown to arise and evolve heritably with no contribution from the other, making it strictly upstream;
> 3. a figure re-census permits a genuine one-way sequence with no cycle, or a direct structure-to-structure bond;
> 4. a ground reading establishes the historical order directly.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures and formula (public repo, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Common origins of RNA, protein and lipid precursors (Patel et al., Nature Chemistry 2015): https://www.nature.com/articles/nchem.2202
> - Ribonucleotides (Sutherland, Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology 2010, open access): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2845210/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
