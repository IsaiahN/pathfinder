## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves two structural questions open: (1) are a metabolic network and a genetic polymer **isomers** over a shared atom set, or merely members of a common functional group; and (2) what **order** (bond type) actually holds between monomers, polymers and networks? Answering both fixes what "co-emergence" means structurally rather than rhetorically.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two cores isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :48: *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."*

Fig 13 :24: *"network. that entities relate, and influence travels."*

Write the polymer core as a **chain**, the degenerate case of one bond type (nucleotide monomers in `->` sequence). Write the metabolic core as a **network** structure, where entities relate and influence travels. Over a shared monomer set they are same atoms, different bond, different substance: Fig 12's isomer. The arrangement carries the information the ingredients do not (Fig 12 :5 *"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).

**Caveat, mode `specified`:** the prebiotic monomer sets may not be identical. HCN-derived network precursors and ribonucleotides overlap but are not the same inventory. So the isomer claim is a structural prediction to be checked against chemistry, not a settled fact.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two cores demonstrably require disjoint atom sets (a polymer with no atom the network can use, or a network with no atom the polymer can use), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading below stands.

### Q2 - If not isomers, what common structure do they share?

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Both cores require activated monomers and phosphate/carbonyl chemistry; both are cross-kind compositions `state -> structure -> state`. That shared substructure is a functional group: it fixes the *kind* (a cross-kind molecular assembly), while which atoms occupy the slots fixes the *member* (chain polymer versus catalytic network). This is the weaker claim that survives Q1's falsifier.

### Q3 - What order (bond type) holds between the terms?

Fig 12 :71: *"`->`: sequence. A then B; B needs A to have happened."*

Fig 12 :76: *"`=>`: production. A produces B; B did not exist before."*

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

- Monomers to polymer: `=>`, production. The polymer did not exist before the monomers were joined.
- Network to monomers: `=>`, production. The network synthesizes monomers.
- Polymer to network: **not** a one-way bond. It is the catalytic return: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use"* (Fig 12 :119). The ribozyme persists and catalyzes the network that produced its own monomers.
- The whole: a **cycle** of `=>` bonds closed by a catalyst. Applying `->` (sequence) to it is the category error named in F2: a chain cannot close by construction.

So the answer to "which came first" is not "A then B". It is a loop, and the only legitimate linear ordering is dependency order within one turn (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).

### Q4 - What is the union? The analogue from the exemplar

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*

Once a metabolic network and a genetic polymer are each settled against the ground, each becomes an atom for whatever composes over them: a self-maintaining, heritable protocell. Their union is therefore **second-order**, licensed by the settled-molecule rule rather than by a new primitive. This is exactly the move the exemplar used for GR and QM (`Solutions/QMGR-0037.../06_isomers_and_order.md`): two first-order cores, a second-order union, no imported seventh term. Here the two first-order cores are `state -> structure -> state` over the six-term set; their union is a cross-kind composition at the next level.

**Falsifier for Q4:** if a settled network cannot serve as an atom for a higher composition, or if the union requires a new primitive, the second-order claim fails.

## CONCLUSION

The polymer and the network are a real isomer claim **if** their monomer sets prove shared; otherwise they stand as a functional group (Fig 12 :124). The bond order between them is production (`=>`) closed by a catalyst, forming a **cycle**, not a sequence (`->`). Their union is second-order under Fig 12 :104, matching the exemplar's GR/QM analogue. All of this is `specified`, ungrounded; the historical route remains `gap`.
