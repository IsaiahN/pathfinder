# Expert verification packet: the Tether on metabolism and genetic polymers

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `gap` on the historical order. Not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The metabolism-first versus polymers-first question is a bundled union, not one well-formed question, so it must be split into monomer supply, polymer inheritance and network closure, each with its own ground. The dependency between the network and the polymer is a cycle, not a one-way chain: the network produces monomers, monomers produce a polymer, and a catalyst that survives its own use returns catalytic function to the network. The two structures join through a state (energy or time), and no kind is senior to the other. Over a shared monomer set the polymer (a chain) and the network stand as isomers; their union is a second-order composition of two settled cores. The framework does not name the historical route. Which actually came first is a ground question and is recorded as `gap`.

### C3 - ground-facing; checkable against the chemistry and the figures

- **Statement:** the historical order is not derivable from the figures; it is a ground question, and the chemistry literature treats it as open and addressable, with common-chemistry results that corroborate a co-emergence shape.

- **Refuter:** a ground reading (synthesis, replication or geological evidence) that establishes strict temporal priority, or a demonstration that the common-chemistry result cannot operate under one plausible geochemical sequence.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "metabolism first" and not "RNA first". The framework returns a shape, not a historical name.
- Not that Patel 2015 or Sutherland 2010 prove the origin happened co-emergently. They are secondary corroboration of the shape.
- Not a new primitive, not a unified theory of abiogenesis, not a merge of two camps into one.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

The anchors are experiments, not preferences. (1) Supply: can all monomer classes arise in one plausible geochemical sequence? (2) Inheritance: can a polymer copy itself heritably and catalyze under the same conditions? (3) Closure: does a network close a self-maintaining loop that returns function to its own production? (4) History: geological or experimental evidence that one subsystem was absent while the other operated. Only (4) closes the `gap` on order.
