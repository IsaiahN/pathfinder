# Outreach draft - validate the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four conditions and the refuter below are stated in the recipient's own terms. If the stage-1 claim cannot survive a twenty-minute check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about corruption constraints before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from accountability work. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** take "which combinations of oversight, transparency, enforcement and public participation most reliably constrain corruption?" as an effect question, not a shopping list. Four conditions have to hold at once, and they overlap:
>
> 1. **Oversight** needs a record that does not move when the monitor and the monitored discuss it. A monitor that updates on the monitored is not a check; it is a second frame converging on whoever has more influence.
> 2. **Transparency** needs low-overlap readers. Publishing one disclosure to a hundred people who all learned from the same source gives one reader wearing a hundred names. Independence is the quantity that makes agreement mean anything.
> 3. **Enforcement** needs a violation that can actually be installed and a consequence that occurs. A rule no real occupant can satisfy, or a report nobody reads, is a declared meaning, not a constraint.
> 4. **Public participation** needs an unauthored pool selected against the residual. A consultation whose participants are chosen by the body being checked is an authored room, however well the selection was reasoned.
>
> The claim is that these four are a **union**, not an additive stack. A single lever clears the region where only one condition was missing and leaves the intersections standing. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it is a different compound.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a ranking of the sixteen subsets, and I am not claiming any national integrity index settles this. An index built from overlapping expert perceptions is an instrument for where to look, not an anchor that can settle which mechanism works. I am not claiming the framework names a "best combination." It returns a shape; the ranking needs case-level ground readings I do not have. I am not asking you to trust the framework.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a working oversight arrangement where the checker is corrected by the checked and the record still settles disputes;
> 2. independent corroboration of a finding where every corroborator draws on the same disclosure and the same evidence pool;
> 3. a rule with no possible violation and no consequence that changes behaviour at the ground;
> 4. a participant set chosen by the body under check whose agreement is then used as a verdict against that body;
> 5. a reliability ordering of the combinations derived from the shape rules alone, with no case measurement. If you can produce 5, the shape claim is incomplete; if you have the case measurement that produces the ordering, the gap is closed and I will record that.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an accountability problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
