# Expert verification packet - the Tether on accountability combinations

**What this is:** a claim in a form an accountability researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No case-level corruption measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four mechanisms in the question form a union of overlapping conditions, not an additive stack. Oversight constrains only while the checking frame does not update on the checked frame and the record does not move when either is asked; transparency requires readers with low evidence overlap, since one disclosure read by people sharing one source is one reader wearing many names; enforcement requires a violation a real occupant can commit and a consequence that actually follows; participation requires a pool not authored by the body being checked. These conditions have a dependency order (reach the ground, name what would count as progress, keep frames independent, install the violation, then compose and carry). The figures return that shape, the instruments that would test each condition, and refuters written in advance. They do not rank the subsets; that ranking is a case-level ground question and is declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - the union and the anchor (framework-internal, checkable by reading figures 9 and 2)
- **Statement:** the four mechanisms are a union whose overlaps are categorically different compounds; oversight presumes an anchor that cannot be moved by a frame's opinion of it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57 - **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 2 :29 - **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
- **Refuter:** show a case family in which the four conditions are independent, the effects additive, the intersections behave as a harder instance of one condition, and the record moves with opinion while still settling disputes.

### C2 - independence and enforcement (field-facing, checkable against audit and FOI practice)
- **Statement:** more monitors and more published data do not by themselves increase independent corroboration or constraint. Independence is a quantity, not a headcount, and enforcement is a violable convention with a consequence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :48-49 - **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** Fig 2 :44 - **"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."** Fig 10 :18 - **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** and :53 - **"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."**
- **What to check:** in a procurement or audit setting you know, do the checking bodies draw on distinct primary evidence, and did a published finding ever fail to produce a consequence because the rule was unviolable or unread? Field corroboration already on record: the EU practitioner toolbox says *"the existence of open data itself does not automatically translate into lower corruption"* and that irregularities *"need to bear consequences."*
- **Refuter:** ten checking bodies sharing one source and one criterion producing ten independent corroborations at the ground, or a rule with no possible violation that changes behaviour at the ground.

### C3 - unauthored participation and the refused ranking (framework-internal plus measurement)
- **Statement:** participation contributes only when the outside pool is unauthored, and no figure line yields a ranking of the subsets by reliability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :73-74 - **"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."** Fig 7 :36 - **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 9 :32 - **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** and Fig 13 :134 - **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
- **Refuter:** an authored consultation whose agreement is used as a verdict against the body that selected it; or a reliability ordering of the sixteen subsets derived from figure lines alone without case measurement; or a pre-registered case-level comparison that settles the ordering and closes the `gap`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of the combinations, and not a claim that any one of the sixteen subsets is best.
- Not that a perception index is an anchor. Indices are instruments and *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not that more monitors, more disclosure, more rules or more participants is monotonically better.
- Not a theory of corruption's causes in the economics or sociology sense. No mechanism of motive or opportunity is asserted beyond the census.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a case-level measurement.
- Not a claim that this derivation is a ground reading. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A case-level instrument with four tests: an anchor that stays fixed when pushed; an effective number of independent checking frames computed with the correlation correction; a rule with a possible violation and an observed consequence; a participant pool assembled before the question existed. Plus, for the ranking only, a pre-registered comparison of subsets against a direct experience-based corruption measure. Until those exist, the empirical part stays `gap`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-57, Fig 2 :29 and :44, Fig 8 :48-49, Fig 10 :18 and :73-74. Six short passages.
2. Say which of the four conditions most often fails in the setting you know, and name the evidence that would prove it failed.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a real anchor from a perception index in that setting, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
