# WATSON answer: which mechanism combinations most reliably constrain corruption

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No case-level measurement of corruption was taken.

## QUESTION

Which combinations of oversight, transparency, enforcement, and public participation most reliably constrain corruption?

Restated as an effect: corruption is a union compound of opportunity, discretion, motive and weak constraint, and the question asks for the arrangement of four mechanisms that closes the contact failure. Option A treats them as additive ingredients and asks for a ranking of subsets. Option B treats them as overlapping conditions and asks for the shape a working arrangement must have. What each leads to: A delivers an ungrounded ranking from indices whose sources overlap; B delivers four testable conditions, instruments, and an honest `gap` where the ranking would be.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale: arity multiple (a union compound, not one hard question); symmetry asymmetric (principal/agent and monitor/monitored do not commute); scale three levels (transaction, organization, system) with a lossy transform between. Fig 9 :55-57 governs the union; Fig 4 governs the transform.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Oversight | overseer and overseen | readings, verdicts, record access | a behaviour record neither can move | admitted |
| D2 Transparency | multiple reader frames, a published record | observability, independence | the same record seen by low-overlap frames | admitted |
| D3 Enforcement | rule, enforcer, sanction | cost of violation | a violation that can be installed and a consequence that occurs | admitted |
| D4 Public participation | citizens, media, CSOs, outside experts | variation between rooms | a correction an insider could not produce about itself | admitted |
| X1 National integrity score | index producers and consumers | reputation, perception | not nameable; it moves when frames are pushed | instrument, not a domain |
| X2 More oversight as a stand-alone cause | monitors | headcount | absorbed into D1 | not separate |

**Union, not partition.** D1 to D4 overlap; a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds (Fig 9 :54-57). **Order by dependency:** reach the record, name the objective, secure independent frames, install the violation, compose and carry (Fig 3 :20, Fig 4 :30). Strain test applied: Fig 9's union and a four-item additive stack are different objects, so there is no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the ranking does not. Any working combination needs four conditions: a record neither monitor nor monitored can move; readers with low evidence overlap; a rule with an installable violation and a consequence; and an outside pool not authored by the body being checked. The four form a union whose overlaps are categorically different compounds, so no single lever clears them all, and headcount is not independence. The dependency order runs from a reachable anchor to a nameable objective to independent frames to the installed violation, then composition and carry. Which subset is most reliable in a given case is a ground question and is declared `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the union claim is wrong, a reformer spends effort keeping four conditions alive when one would do, which is an efficiency cost, not a corrupted verdict. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending an index-ranked conclusion instead of a shape with refuters, which Fig 2 :42 prices as *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the four conditions and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape, the retracted readings and the refuters are recorded with provenance, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor test showing the record moves when pushed; an effective-sample-size measurement showing independence stays near one as monitors are added; a rule with no possible violation that still changes behaviour; an authored consultation that refutes an insider claim at the ground; a case where two failed conditions clear additively with no categorically different residue; or a pre-registered case-level comparison that settles the ranking. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate` and the ranking stays `gap`.
