## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which of the four named mechanisms is strongest. It is what a working constraint on corruption must look like when several mechanisms are combined. In effect terms: an official holds discretion over a public good, the principal expects a public-serving outcome, and the residual is the gap between that expectation and what actually happens, measured per institutional slot and never averaged into one national score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- **Option A**, "add more mechanisms and the constraint grows monotonically": predicts that any stack of oversight, transparency, enforcement and participation is better than fewer, and that a single ranking of the sixteen subsets can be read off a cross-country index.
- **Option B**, "the four are overlapping conditions in a union, and the arrangement between them decides": predicts that a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds, leaves intersections standing, and that the reliability ranking is a ground question requiring case-level measurement (Fig 9 :55, Fig 9 :57).
- **What an answer would look like**, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape*, meaning the structural conditions any working combination must satisfy, plus an instrument that would tell which condition is failing in a given case, plus an explicit statement that the ordering of combinations by reliability is not derivable from the figures alone.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept separate per domain and never averaged. The empirical ranking is declared `gap`, not answered by silence and not smuggled in as a verdict.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles which of the sixteen combinations is best in a given country, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of accountability.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable reviewers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. **Correlation has valence.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Anti-corruption indices built from the same underlying expert surveys are one evidence pool, not many witnesses.
3. **A verifier must be outside the interior.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's internals.
4. **Legally**, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
