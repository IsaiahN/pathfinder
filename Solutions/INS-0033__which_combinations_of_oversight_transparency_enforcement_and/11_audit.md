## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran it on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :55-57 (union), Fig 2 :29 (anchor), Fig 8 :48-49 (independence), Fig 10 :18, :53, :73-74 (violation and unauthored pool), Fig 3 :20 (order).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `08`: four conditions, a union, a dependency order, a `gap` for the ranking.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a field-facing programme: establish an audit record, require independent verification, publish findings, attach sanctions, run a citizen panel.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- which condition is binding in a specific case. The shorthand says "four conditions"; the concrete programme cannot tell captured oversight from toothless enforcement, and a single package treats them alike;
- the correlation between readers. A citizen panel filled from one civil-society network is an authored room and its independence is near one, which the shorthand's word "participation" conceals;
- the ranking. The shorthand says the ranking is a `gap`; the concrete programme is routinely asked to name a best combination, and the gap disappears under that request.

One thing survives cleanly: a record neither monitor nor monitored can move, and a consequence that actually follows a finding. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no case-level ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the figures are the condensed version of the field findings" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
