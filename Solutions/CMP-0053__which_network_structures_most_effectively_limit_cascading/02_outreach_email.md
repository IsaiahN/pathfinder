**Subject:** One structural claim about cascades and diffusion that you could refute in a paragraph

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a network carries two flows at once. A local failure should die out; a beneficial signal, resource or practice should still travel. The same links carry both. Cutting links to contain the failure cuts the beneficial diffusion; adding links to help diffusion adds paths for the failure.
>
> **The output I want checked:** this is a union compound, not one hard question. It has to be unbundled before any topology is ranked. Two consequences follow. First, the valence of a link is relative to the flow, not a property of the link: a bridge that is bad for a cascade is good for a diffusion. Second, the residual has to be measured per node and per module, never averaged into one robustness score, and the habitat has to be enumerated outward to the step where the cascade stops mattering.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that modularity, decentralisation, scale-free structure or any named topology is the best. I am not claiming that this derivation settles which topology wins. It does not. The empirical ranking is a `gap` owed to a ground reading, and I am saying so plainly. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I ran this:**
> 1. On one system and one measurement, a single lever moves containment and diffusion in the same direction across every slot. Then it was a partition, not a union.
> 2. A link is exhibited whose effect on cascade size and whose effect on diffusion reach have the same sign under the same threshold rule. Then valence is carried by the link.
> 3. A figure line is quoted verbatim that ranks named topologies, or a measurement shows a structure violating cross-kind composition that still contains cascades and passes benefits.
> 4. The per-slot residuals turn out to be exchangeable, so the mean is a sufficient statistic.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim and record the correction. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
> - Architecture and formula: `https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the architecture link. Run it on a network problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
