# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cascades and beneficial diffusion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a network scientist, infrastructure engineer, ecologist or contagion researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the union and valence laws, `gap` on the ranking of named topologies. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Containing a local failure and passing a beneficial signal are two conditions on the same links, so this is a union compound, not one ranking problem, and it must be unbundled before any topology is named. The valence of a link is relative to the flow: a bridge that is bad for a cascade can be good for a complex diffusion, and a module boundary that contains a fault also blocks a practice. The habitat must be enumerated outward to the step where the cascade stops mattering, and failure probability and diffusion reach must be reported per node and per module rather than averaged into one robustness score. The missing piece is a link typed by its role under the flow (regenerating control versus one-shot redundancy), imported with provenance from another frame. The framework does not name a winning topology; that ranking is a `gap` owed to a ground. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 11 and Fig 9

- **Statement:** link valence is relative to the flow; there is no standing list of best structures. The question is a union compound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a link whose effect on cascade size and whose effect on diffusion reach have the same sign under the same threshold rule and measurement; or show that a single lever moves both monotonically in the same direction across every slot. Then valence is carried by the link and the union reading fails.

### C2 - framework-to-world; checkable against the cited studies

- **Statement:** the same structural feature receives opposite verdicts when the flow changes. Clustering slows a simple contagion but is required for a complex contagion; modularity stabilises antagonistic food webs while reducing persistence in mutualistic ones; coupling can increase or decrease cascade vulnerability depending on whether a beneficial control function is modelled.
- **Sources:** Centola and Macy 2007, AJS: *"For simple contagions, too much clustering means too few long ties, which slows down cascades. For complex contagions, too little clustering means too few wide bridges, which not only slows down cascades but can prevent them entirely."* Landi et al. 2018, Population Ecology, reporting Thébault and Fontaine 2010: modularity *"enhances food web stability, it has a negative effect on the persistence and resilience of mutualistic networks."* Korkali et al. 2017, Scientific Reports: *"robustness can be enhanced by interconnecting networks with complementary capabilities if modes of internetwork failure propagation are constrained."*
- **What to check:** are those characterisations faithful to the studies, and is the flow-dependence real rather than an artefact of different model families?
- **Refuter:** a controlled study on one system, with flow type as the only manipulated variable, that gets the same sign for both flows. Then the valence claim is an artefact of comparing different literatures.

### C3 - measurement rule; checkable against any cascade dataset

- **Statement:** a global robustness average is insufficient; the residual is per slot and the habitat is multi-step.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :52-54 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** does the mean conceal a live per-node or per-module residual in real outage or diffusion data? Kleinberg 2011 uses the maximum, not the mean, node failure probability (µ-risk) for exactly this reason. Bagrow et al. 2015 show module function fails *"well before the network disintegrates."*
- **Refuter:** show that per-slot residuals are exchangeable and the mean is a sufficient statistic for the system, or that no cascade reaches beyond direct neighbours at any resolution tried.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named topology is best, and not a ranking of modular, lattice, scale-free or multiplex networks. The ranking is `gap`.
- Not that modularity always helps or always hurts. Its valence is flow-relative.
- Not a derivation of a cascade threshold, not a new primitive, not a simulation result of our own.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the world sources independently corroborate one another. Most share an evidence pool; overlap is in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot comparative measurement, not a further argument. From The Hard Problem is About Source, an instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. **Union Test:** does one lever move containment and diffusion in the same direction across every slot?
2. **Valence Test:** is there a link whose cascade and diffusion effects share a sign under the same threshold rule?
3. **Catalyst Test:** are control and bridge links regenerating under the flow or spent after one use?
4. **Module Test:** does module-level function fail before node-level connectivity, and does the order hold across systems?
5. **Ranking Test:** with both flows defined on one system and per-slot outcomes recorded, which named topology dominates? This closes the `gap`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 9 :54, Fig 1 :19 (four short passages). Do the valence and per-slot rules say what the claim says?
2. Check C2 against your own field: is the flow-dependence of clustering, modularity or coupling real in the systems you know?
3. Name one per-slot instrument that would type a link as regenerating or spent under a flow, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
