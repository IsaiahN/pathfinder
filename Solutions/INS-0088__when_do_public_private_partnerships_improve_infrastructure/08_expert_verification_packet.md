# Expert verification packet - the Tether on PPP improvement and risk transfer

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public-finance researcher, procurement auditor or infrastructure economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No project-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a verdict on any procurement.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Improve" and "transfer risk" are two conditions, not two ends of one scale. A PPP improves infrastructure and services when (C1) the delivered service and the real public cost are read against an anchor that does not move when the contract's accounting or the appraisal's sponsor changes, and (C2) the risk that crosses to the private frame is one that frame can bear, at a price that changes when the event fires. Both are read per project and per risk slot. Aggregate value for money is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. Moving a liability off the balance sheet changes a reading, not the ground. Risk transfer is a typed bond, not a countable atom, so two PPPs with the same ingredients can be different substances. Capability lives in the arrangement and its habitat, not in the private identity of the operator. Which risk should be transferred in which sector is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - ground and anchor (checkable by an auditor or procurement researcher)

- **Statement:** the correct anchor is the delivered service and the real cost to the public, and it must not move with the accounting treatment or the appraisal's own VfM figure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Also Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." And Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** take one project where an off-balance-sheet treatment was part of the justification. Does the measured "cost" or "value for money" change when the liability is brought on balance sheet, while service and cash flows stay the same? If yes, the reading moved; no improvement has been shown. Note the UK Treasury Select Committee's 2011 recommendation "PFI should be brought on balance sheet" (read via the Wikipedia PFI article, indirect source).
- **Refuter:** bring the liability on balance sheet and show that VfM, real taxpayer cost and service outcome are all unchanged, with the contract otherwise identical. That would make the convention separable from the ground and narrow this sub-claim.

### C2 - risk transfer is a bond, and its valence is relative to the residual (checkable by a contracts or project-finance specialist)

- **Statement:** a risk matrix names operands, not the arrangement. Two PPPs with the same ingredients can differ in substance by which bond carries the risk.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "the bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** choose a demand-risk or refinancing-risk clause. When the event fired, did the loss actually land on the party nominally holding it, and did the payment change with it? Or was there a backstop, guarantee or refinancing that returned the loss to the public? The first is production; the second is subtraction. The same clause in a different habitat can have the opposite valence.
- **Refuter:** show identical loss-bearing for the same allocation across different receivers and habitats, or show a transfer that improves outcomes with no difference in who can bear the risk. That breaks both the bond claim and the valence claim.

### C3 - appraisal is an inward instrument unless sourced from outturns (checkable by a forecasting or project-appraisal researcher)

- **Statement:** an appraisal produced by the party that wants approval can drift from the ground; the outward instrument is a reference class of comparable completed projects.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** compare forecast cost, demand and benefits with outturns for a reference class. Flyvbjerg's 2006 paper (arXiv:1302.3642, read 2026-11-23) attributes inaccuracy to "optimism bias and strategic misrepresentation" and says reference class forecasting "achieves accuracy by basing forecasts on actual performance in a reference class of comparable projects and thereby bypassing both optimism bias and strategic misrepresentation." The Wikipedia reference-class page records the Edinburgh Tram business case at £320 million against an outturn of £776 million.
- **Refuter:** show PPP appraisals unbiased against outturn with no reference-class correction, and no different from public-procurement appraisals, in a prospective sample. That breaks the inward-instrument finding.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that PPPs are worse than public procurement, or better. The claim is about the conditions under which an improvement can be established.
- Not that any specific project has or has not delivered value for money. No ground reading was taken.
- Not "transfer all risks." Valence is relative to the residual; there is no standing list.
- Not that "private sector efficiency" is a property of private actors. Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- Not a name for the missing thing. Which risk, which sector is a `gap`. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not `grounded`. Mode is `specified` throughout.
- Not a claim that agreement with the shape is evidence. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not".

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): a color instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; the error "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

1. **Contact test:** per project and per risk slot, does the service and real public cost reading stay the same when the accounting treatment and appraisal sponsor change?
2. **Bond test:** when the risk fires, does the loss land on the nominal holder at a price that changed when the risk crossed, or does it return to the public?
3. **Outward-instrument test:** does the appraisal survive reference-class correction and independent outturn measurement?

A failure of any is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29 and :33, Fig 12 :5, Fig 11 :49, Fig 10 :24 (five short passages).
2. Pick one PPP you know well. Name the risk you think was genuinely transferred. State what happened when it fired. That is the bond test.
3. Name one instrument that would read the public cost without moving when the accounting changes, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A one-line failure is more useful than agreement.
