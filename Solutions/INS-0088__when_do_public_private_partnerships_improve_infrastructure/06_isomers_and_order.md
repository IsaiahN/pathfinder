# Why risk transfer is a bond, and why the question is a union

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The record (the question itself, plus the public commentary in `04_links_and_distribution.md`) treats "improve" and "transfer risk" as two properties a PPP can have. Two questions are owed: (1) is a risk transfer an atom that can be counted, or a relation that changes the substance, and (2) what is the dependency order of the two conditions, so that a lever applied to one does not get mistaken for a verdict on the whole.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (short form; full census in `01` §1)

Atoms already in the habitat: a public frame, a private frame, a contract, risk slots (construction, demand, refinancing, political, force majeure), a payment stream, an anchor (delivered service, real public cost), an appraisal instrument. Bonds available: sequence, production, subtraction, comparison, conjunction, disjunction, identity, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is "risk transferred" a count? NO

- The Operators table: "the bond carries information the operands do not, so a term that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Fig 12 :5 "the bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- A PPP risk-allocation matrix names operands (who, which risk, what payment). It does not name the bond (does the loss actually cross to a frame that can bear it, and does the payment change when the event fires?).
- ⇒ "Amount of risk transferred" is not an atom that can be summed. It is a typed relation, and the type is not recoverable from the operands. Mode `general` for the bond rule, `specified` for contracts.

### Q2 - Same ingredients, different PPPs: isomers or functional groups?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Take two PPPs over the same atom set: a public authority, an SPV, a 25-year concession, availability payments, construction risk, demand risk, a reference-class appraisal, an audit office. If they differ only in which bond carries demand risk (production: the SPV truly bears it and is paid for it; versus subtraction: the SPV nominally holds it while the public backstops it), they are **isomers** under Fig 12. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- Where two PPPs share only the general form (a public frame, a private frame, a risk crossing) but not the full atom set, Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The group says "a PPP with genuine risk crossing"; the qualifier says which risk and who bears it.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one of the two arrangements requires an atom the other does not (for example a sovereign guarantee present in one and absent in the other), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group claim stands. If a change of bond with the same atoms leaves loss-bearing identical, the bond-carries-meaning claim itself fails.

### Q3 - What is the order of the two conditions?

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- C1 (contact with an unmoved anchor) and C2 (a bond whose valence fits the receiving frame) are both required. Dependency order (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."):
  1. Can we read the service outcome and real public cost at all? (perception)
  2. Do we have terms for what each risk affords, that is, who can bear it? (vocabulary)
  3. What counts as progress, in the contract's own terms? (objective, the VfM metric)
  4. Allocate risk, set payments, write the output specification (planning)
  5. Audit, refinance, carry to the next procurement (learn and carry)
- Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break." The objective (step 3) has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious test, so effort lands in step 4 language (more risk transfer, more sophisticated finance) while step 3 stays unmeasured.
- **Falsifier:** if a single intervention clears both C1 and C2 and leaves no categorically different residue, the union reading fails and the problem was a partition after all.

### Q4 - The two currencies: bits and actions

- Fig 12's pricing rule: a composition is charged in bits (cost of describing it) and actions (cost of finding out whether it holds), in different units.
- A PPP contract can be cheap to state (a standard risk matrix) and take a whole programme budget to settle (an audit of whether the risk actually crossed and what the public paid when it fired). Conversely, a long, expensive contract can be cheap to refute (one discharged guarantee clause).
- The practical failure is treating the contract's own VfM figure as if it were both prices. It is a description price inside the frame; the action price is paid at the ground. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

### Q5 - The analogue, and its limit

The living-system analogue is ecosystem engineering under Fig 11 : "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A seat (the public authority) can add a private frame and a contract to the habitat. It cannot subtract the habitat: the taxpayer's exposure and the public's need for the service are facts about the world that do not consult the procurement. A managed PPP is therefore permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :61-64).

**Analogue limit.** This is a structural match (actors, currency, anchor), not a claim that a concession is a food web. The frame's own rule is to map relations, not looks, and to owe a reason where no analogue fits. Here the mapped relations are: seat acts on habitat; introduction available, exclusion not; capability is agent-and-habitat; valence relative to residual. Anything beyond those four is not carried over. No external analogue study was read for this derivation, so none is cited as corroboration.

## CONCLUSION

The two clauses of the question are not two properties of a PPP. "Improve" is a relation to an anchor. "Transfer risk" is a typed bond between frames. Over a shared atom set two PPPs can be isomers: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Over a partial shared set they are a functional group, and the qualifier (which risk, which receiver) is where the answer lives. The dependency order puts the objective (what counts as progress) before the allocation, and that is the link that lacks an instrument. Which specific risk to transfer in which sector remains a `gap`: the framework narrows the shape and stops.
