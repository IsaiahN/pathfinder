**Subject:** One checkable claim about PPP risk transfer, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know about public-private partnerships. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "when do PPPs improve infrastructure and when do they transfer public risk?" is not one question with two answers. It is two conditions that must both hold, read per project and per risk type:
>
> 1. **Contact.** The delivered service and the real cost to the public are read against an anchor that does not move when the contract's accounting or the appraisal's forecast changes. An off-balance-sheet liability is a changed reading, not a changed ground.
> 2. **Bond.** A risk that crosses to the private party actually lands on a party that can bear it, at a price that changes when the risk event fires. The same contract ingredients can be different substances depending on which bond holds.
>
> What the framework refuses: "the private sector is more efficient" as a free-floating explanation. Capability is a property of the arrangement and its habitat, not of the operator alone. Which specific risk to transfer in which sector is a declared gap, not a hidden answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. a single aggregate value-for-money number fully predicts service outcomes and later public bailouts, with no live per-risk signal that changes a decision;
> 2. the same risk allocation produces the same loss-bearing regardless of who holds the risk or what the project's habitat is;
> 3. PPP appraisals are unbiased against outturn with no reference-class correction, and no different from public-procurement appraisals.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Look at Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29 and :33, Fig 10 :47, Fig 11 :49, Fig 12 :5.
> - Canonical figures README and the eight-step formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The World Bank PPI database, for the ground-level record this shape would be checked against: https://ppi.worldbank.org/en/ppi
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a procurement you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
