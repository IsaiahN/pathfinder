## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a stress test can anticipate a rare cascade when the transmission mechanism and the network structure may change during the crisis. Option A (strong anticipation): a fixed-model stress test run in calm times can anticipate such a cascade, so a clean result is evidence of safety. Option B (shape only): a stress test returns the shape of a cascade under stated assumptions, and registers against the ground only while its closure holds terms for the moving mechanisms, its rooms are independent and its channel stays reachable. What each leads to: A licenses reliance on clean results and treats more scenarios as more evidence; B requires enumeration outward, independent frames, a measured round trip gap and an open slot until the ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the stress-test model's closure and what a crisis returns. Arity: two operands, instrument closure and moving habitat, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not one hard question; the two conditions, mechanism change and structure change, overlap. Symmetry: asymmetric, the ground does not update while the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Scale: first-order direct losses, second- and third-order cascade, cross-scale round trip R_T.
- **Domain 1, the stress-test frame.** Actors: institutions, exposures, behavioural rules, scenario, loss mapping. Currency: simulated loss and capital shortfall. Anchor: none of its own, because closure(Γ) is what the model can say, not what settles the question. Question type: partition (what is inside the model's closure).
- **Domain 2, the ground (the real crisis and habitat).** Actors: banks, funds, counterparties, conditions (liquidity, confidence, policy), relations (exposure, common assets, funding). Currency: actual losses, defaults, surviving contingency. Anchor: the ground. Question type: habitat enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed.").
- **Domain 3, verification and contact.** Actors: scenario designers, regulators, outside expert frames. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence here is zero; declared, not hidden.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perceive the change, then have terms for what the changed situation affords, then state what would count as progress, then plan and carry. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link is the break; scenario generation sits above it.

Strain test applied: the ecology early-warning literature (fixed bifurcation, stable mechanism) governs a different domain from mechanism-changing crises. Re-census narrowed it rather than forcing it onto the verdict; see §5.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Bounded NO, `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. A stress test cannot anticipate a rare cascade whose mechanism and network structure change during the crisis in the strong, out-of-sample sense. It is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70); more scenarios cannot add a missing mechanism (Fig 8 :32); the channel decays while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24); and a clean result proves nothing about absence (Fig 9 :44). What it can return is the shape of a cascade under stated assumptions, plus a route: enumerate outward, keep the anchor reachable, use independent rooms, measure R_T, bind the shape as an ordinary term, and import a frame that can hold the new mechanism. The BIS review keeps the narrow legitimate role (crisis management and disciplining thought) intact, so this is not a claim that stress tests are useless.

- **cost if wrong:** two-sided and locatable. If the NO is too strong, we undervalue a real discipline: scenario generation, crisis management and the identification of where models fail, which the BIS review endorses. If the NO is too weak and a clean result is treated as a warning, the cost is a false sense of security in exactly the conditions where decisions are largest. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong" prices the second error higher, so the packet asks for an out-of-sample refutation rather than an endorsement. The record, the mode labels and the pre-registered refuter make either error findable rather than invisible.
- **undoable:** yes. The reachable improvements are additive design changes (enumeration outward, reachable channel, independent rooms, measured R_T, pre-bound shape, imported frame). No substrate primitive must be withdrawn and no result has to be retracted to adopt them. A superseded packet can carry the correction in the retraction log.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An out-of-sample case in which a mechanism-changing stress test flags a cascade that the crisis confirms; a closure proof that the model's mechanism vocabulary is closed under crisis operations; a sealed-room argument giving a finite complete enumeration; or an instrument that stays in contact through a real crisis with no stale, dropped, saturated or pooled readings, verified against outcomes. A negative version also settles it: mechanism-changing cascades following clean results across crises in which the model's mechanisms held would confirm the channel-decay finding at the ground. Until one of these fires, the verdict stays `candidate`.
