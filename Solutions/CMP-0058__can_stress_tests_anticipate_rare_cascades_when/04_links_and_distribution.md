## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The question's premise is already a published concern.** The IMF working paper states that systemic risk amplification mechanisms "can change their structure and magnitude at different points in time," and that historical information is "an especially poor guide for the future" during a crisis. This is a world-source statement of exactly the residual; it corroborates the figure-derived finding but does not derive it.
2. **The failure has a name in the practice literature.** The BIS review says stress tests "lulled policymakers and market participants into a false sense of security" and that the then-current generation of models was "a long way from providing a realistic picture of the dynamics of financial distress" (PDF text located via search; the landing-page abstract is the quote verified above). This aligns with Fig 9 :44 and Fig 10 :26.
3. **Connectedness cuts both ways.** Gai and Kapadia's "robust-yet-fragile" result and Battiston et al.'s amplification of small network errors both match Fig 11 :52 and Fig 13 :31. They are corroboration from a different field, not a second witness to the same evidence (network science shares methods across these papers).
4. **A genuine tension, recorded.** The ecology early-warning literature holds that generic signals can sometimes indicate an approaching threshold. Re-census places it in a different domain (stable mechanism, fixed bifurcation) from this question (mechanism and structure change during the crisis). It narrows the claim to a shape; it does not flip the verdict. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.
5. **Aggregation hides the live signal.** The Banque de France / Bank of England 2026 system-wide stress test report notes that aggregated or representative parameters "tend to average out participant behaviour, thereby dampening non-linear feedback loops, overreactions, and other effects that drive real-world amplification" (PDF text located via search). This is the field's own version of Fig 1 :22.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22 and :25:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." and "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; send the architecture only after.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who absorbs the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27 to :28:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if a mechanism-changing cascade were imminent before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40:** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42:** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost of a false clean result is high, so buy independent frames, not a larger list of agreeable recipients.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual, the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and whether they know an out-of-sample case; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
