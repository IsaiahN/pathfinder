**Subject:** Check one output on stress tests before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** can a stress test anticipate a rare cascade when the transmission mechanism and the network structure may change during the crisis? A stress test substitutes a model for the system. The output asks whether a test that holds those things fixed can register against a crisis in which they move.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's rule for a test harness is that it is a substituted habitat and that "a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability" (Fig 11 :70). Two other rules point the same way: composing inside a closed model cannot add a mechanism the model does not hold (Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32), and the ground does not decay while the channel to it does (Fig 10 :24). So the shape claim is: a stress test returns the *shape* of a cascade under its stated assumptions, not the occurrence of a rare cascade, and a clean result is not proof of absence (Fig 9 :44). This is a prediction of a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that stress tests are useless. The BIS review of macro stress testing says they are ill-suited as early warning devices but can be effective as crisis management and resolution tools and can discipline thinking about financial stability. I am not claiming the framework names the mechanism that will change. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Nor am I claiming that more scenarios are more evidence: scenarios drawn from one model are one room, and "agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :13).
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. A stress test that explicitly lets mechanisms and links change issues a cascade alert that is then confirmed against the ground out of sample.
> 2. A formal demonstration that the model's mechanism vocabulary is closed under the crisis operations, so inward refinement suffices.
> 3. A sealed-room argument giving a finite, complete enumeration of cascade mechanisms and contacts for a real system.
> 4. An instrument that keeps the channel reachable throughout a real crisis, with no stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics or pooled readings.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually value from you:** what would you count as a failure of this output? In particular, is there an out-of-sample case where a mechanism-changing cascade was anticipated by a stress test? That single case would settle it faster than a discussion of the theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architectural narrative: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
