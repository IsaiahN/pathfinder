# The Tether's answer: Can stress tests anticipate rare cascades when the mechanisms and network structure may change during a crisis?

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

A stress test is a probe run against a model habitat. It anticipates a cascade only if its output would register against the ground: a flagged cascade is one the real system undergoes, and a cleared system is one that clears. The residual R is the gap between what the model's closure can represent and what a crisis returns. The question adds the strain that mechanisms and network structure move during the crisis, so the test holds fixed the very things that change.

R is arity two (instrument closure and moving habitat), asymmetric (the ground does not update), and multi-scale (first-order losses, second- and third-order cascade, cross-scale round trip). It is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not one hard question. Its two conditions overlap, so one lever clears only the region where a single condition holds.

## 2. The shape and the verdict

The verdict is **bounded NO**: a stress test cannot anticipate a rare cascade whose mechanism or network structure changes during the crisis in the strong, out-of-sample sense. What it can return is a shape under stated assumptions. Five figure lines carry the verdict, kept as separate findings and never averaged:

1. **Substitution.** Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A clean result is a statement about the model, not the system.
2. **Idempotent closure.** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"; Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom." Running more scenarios does not add a mechanism the model lacks. A "novel" scenario is novel relative to the model's atoms, not to the world.
3. **Channel decay.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." The changing mechanism is exactly what drops out of the instrument.
4. **No proof of absence.** Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." A clean test cannot distinguish safety from a missing mechanism.
5. **First-order scoping.** Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Direct-exposure tests miss the cascade that matters.

Structurally, the test and the cascade are **isomers**: same atoms (institutions, exposures, losses), different bond (test = sequence; cascade = production plus cycle). Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :121: "a chain cannot close by construction." That is why the test's chain and the cascade's cycle are different objects, not approximations of one another.

### What the figures say would help (the reachable part)

- **Enumerate outward:** "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering" (Fig 11 :44). Include common assets, funding links and cross-system feedback.
- **Keep the anchor reachable** (Fig 10 :29): update mechanisms and graphs as they move; treat the test as a probe, not a verdict.
- **Buy independence between rooms:** "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced" (Fig 7 :36); "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict" (Fig 7 :35). Many runs of one model are one long room.
- **Measure the round trip gap:** "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked" (Fig 4 :30). Record what the abstraction dropped when mechanisms changed.
- **Bind the shape as an ordinary term before the run** (Fig 5 :65-66, Formula "Direction"), so the sought-for cascade is refusable.
- **Import when the closure cannot hold the mechanism:** "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs" (Fig 6 :23). The network-science and ecology sources are candidate frames, not pooled witnesses.

These are design changes, all additive. None requires a new substrate primitive.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that stress tests are useless. The BIS review finds them ill-suited as early warning devices but effective as crisis management and resolution tools, and useful for disciplining thinking. The verdict is bounded to strong anticipation of a mechanism-changing rare cascade.
- Not that a specific mechanism will change, or when, or with what probability. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that a clean test is a warning, and not that a nil result is proof of safety.
- Not that more scenarios are more evidence (Fig 7 :13).
- Not `grounded`. No out-of-sample crisis reading settles left(R,φ) here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

The pre-registered refuter is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and was written before this conclusion. Restated as ground tests:

1. **Out-of-sample case:** a stress test with changing mechanisms and links flags a cascade, and a real crisis confirms it. This closes R directly.
2. **Closure proof:** a demonstration that the model's mechanism vocabulary is closed under crisis operations, so inward refinement suffices.
3. **Sealed room:** a finite, complete enumeration of cascade mechanisms and contacts for a real system, making a clean result a proof of absence.
4. **Reachable channel:** an instrument that stays in contact through a real crisis with no stale, dropped, saturated or pooled readings, verified against outcomes.

The BIS and Gai-Kapadia sources make the first of these hard to find: "the resilience of the system in withstanding fairly large shocks prior to 2007 should not have been taken as a reliable guide to its future robustness." The IMF paper makes the premise itself public: amplification mechanisms "can change their structure and magnitude at different points in time" and historical information becomes "an especially poor guide for the future" in a crisis.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures can say what a stress test cannot do with its closure as it stands, and what a missing thing would have to look like. They cannot name the mechanism that will move. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The line between depth (the mechanism is in the closure and was not found) and genuine absence (the mechanism is not in the closure) looks the same from inside the frame (Fig 6 :27), and only the ground separates them. Six terms and seven bonds make no claim to be nature's own list; declaring them makes a choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

This seat shares an evidence pool with the primary derivation and with the world sources. Its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). No measurement was taken.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say stress tests are useless. It says a stress test is a substituted habitat, so a clean result proves the model's wiring and not the system's capability. Because a test searches inside a fixed closure, more scenarios cannot supply a mechanism the model does not hold; and because the ground does not decay while the channel does, the mechanism that changes during a crisis is precisely what drops out of the readings. A clean test is therefore not proof of safety: from inside the model, "no cascade possible" and "the cascade mechanism is not in my model" look the same. It returns a shape under stated assumptions, not the occurrence of a rare cascade. Anticipation, if it is possible at all, requires enumerating contact beyond first-order exposures, keeping the instrument reachable as the habitat moves, using independent rooms instead of many runs of one model, measuring what the abstraction dropped, and importing a frame that can hold the new mechanism. Which mechanism will change is not named by the framework. That stays open until a ground reading closes it.
