# What "shape" means for this question

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :138-139: "it can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is:

> A rare cascade, if it is to be anticipated, must be anticipated by an instrument that stays in contact with a moving habitat. The model must hold terms for the mechanisms that can change, enumerate contact beyond the first order, and be corrected by the ground. The specific mechanism that will change is not named.

That is a shape. "Mechanism X will fail at time T" is a name, and the framework does not supply it.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." And: "Your ignorance, of not knowing what you don't know, is the actual obstacle between you and the answers, not the vastness of the library."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30 to :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." This is why the residual was stated in effect terms in `01` §0 (model closure versus moving habitat), not in the vocabulary of any one stress-test model.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." In this derivation the stop is not certainty; it is the point where further scenarios inside the same closure add no new atom (Fig 8 :32) and the remaining question has to be handed to a different frame or to the ground.
- Formula step 7 keeps SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD distinct. For this question: more scenarios (SEARCHED) cannot supply a changed mechanism; a higher-frequency funding or liquidity instrument (INWARD) extends contact only if an instrument already returns something; a different frame's model of time-varying networks or ecological early warning (OUTWARD) is a candidate import with a source gate and a shadow test.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For this question the name would be licensed by an out-of-sample case: a stress test flags a mechanism-changing cascade and the crisis confirms it.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50 to :52), with source gate and shadow test. Naming "liquidity spiral" or "fire sale" as *the* mechanism of the next crisis is a binding from another frame onto a shape slot, and must be recorded as such.

**Correction to an overstatement.** The phrase "shape, not a name" governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in Γ. It does not forbid using ordinary, already-held terms such as "network," "mechanism," "feedback" or "channel." What it forbids is presenting a selected term as the answer the framework derived. So saying "the channel decays" (Fig 10 :24) is a figure line, not a named mechanism, and saying "the next crisis will be a repo run" would be a name requiring a ground or a provenanced import. This correction keeps the rule from becoming a ban on vocabulary.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* gives the mechanism of why a reading cannot name its source: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality."

A stress-test result is a reading. Its cleanliness does not say whether the model or the world was right. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." The framework's own rule that a self-consistent frame can still be wrong is the reason a clean result cannot settle anticipation.

Same essay on the validation requirement: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This file and its siblings are therefore a simulation, not a test of the claim.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What is the shape of an answer to "can stress tests anticipate rare cascades when mechanisms and structure may change?"** It is a bounded shape with three constraints:

1. **Substitution constraint.** Any test result is a result about a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70). It proves wiring, not capability.
2. **Contact constraint.** Anticipation is possible only while the channel to the ground stays reachable (Fig 10 :24-29). Once mechanisms change and the readings are stale, dropped, saturated or pooled, the output is a loss-of-contact report, not a warning.
3. **Plurality constraint.** The frames that could supply a missing mechanism must have different closures and must be selected against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :35-36). Volume within one closure buys nothing.

The shape does not include a mechanism name, a timing, a probability or a particular institution. Those are ground readings or imports, owed separately.

## 5b. Correction to my own §1 to §4

My §1 could be read as saying the framework never names anything. That is too strong: the figures themselves name the six substrate terms and the seven bonds. The accurate statement is that the framework returns a shape for what is **missing**, and that names for held atoms are bindings with provenance. My §4 source point stands and is not retracted: a reading cannot name its own source without a discriminator, and the discriminator here is the ground, not an argument.

## 6. Consequences for contact

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A well-known stress-testing authority can get a claim a hearing, which is a budget. Their agreement cannot settle the claim. So `03_public_contact_points.csv` lists institutions and opt-in channels, not famous names, and `02_outreach_email.md` asks for a failure, not an endorsement.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are labelled as internal-consistency checks. They cannot settle the question they report on. The thing that would settle it is named in the pre-registered refuter: an out-of-sample case, a closure proof, a sealed-room argument, or a reachable channel through a real crisis.
