# Expert verification packet: stress tests and mechanism-changing cascades

**What this is:** a claim in a form a stress-testing or systemic-risk specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real crisis is claimed. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A stress test is a substituted habitat: a clean result proves the model's wiring, not the system's capability to survive a crisis in which mechanisms and network structure change. Because composition inside a fixed closure cannot add a mechanism, and because the channel to the ground decays while the ground does not, a stress test returns the *shape* of a cascade under stated assumptions, not the occurrence of a rare cascade. A clean result is not proof of absence. The reachable improvements are structural and additive: enumerate contact beyond first-order exposures, keep the instrument reachable as the habitat moves, use independent rooms rather than many runs of one model, measure the round trip gap, and import a frame that can hold the new mechanism. The framework does not name which mechanism will change.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 8

- **Statement:** a model run is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability; more runs in the same closure cannot supply a missing mechanism.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."
- **Refuter:** show a stress test whose habitat is the live system with the ground reachable throughout, so a synthetic solve is a contact reading; or demonstrate that a model's mechanism vocabulary is closed under the crisis operations. Either breaks the sub-claim.

### C2 - stress-testing practice; checkable against published reviews and the question's own premise

- **Statement:** the channel to the ground decays during a crisis, and the items that drop out (stale calibrations, behavioural adaptation, saturated or aggregated readings) are the mechanism-changing parts.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." World check: the IMF working paper states that amplification mechanisms "can change their structure and magnitude at different points in time" and that historical information becomes "an especially poor guide for the future" in a crisis; the Banque de France / Bank of England system-wide stress test report says aggregated parameters "tend to average out participant behaviour, thereby dampening non-linear feedback loops, overreactions, and other effects that drive real-world amplification."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a crisis in which the stress test's channel stayed reachable, all relevant mechanisms were represented, and the readings remained in contact with outcomes. Gai and Kapadia (2010) report the opposite for 2007: "the resilience of the system in withstanding fairly large shocks prior to 2007 should not have been taken as a reliable guide to its future robustness."

### C3 - framework-internal plus network science; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** a clean result is not proof of absence; a test and a cascade are isomers (same atoms, different bond) because the test's chain cannot close while the cascade's cycle does, and first-order contact scoping misses third-order effects.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :121 "a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **Refuter:** give a sealed-room argument with a finite, complete enumeration of cascade mechanisms and contacts for a real system; or show that all consequential cascade paths are contained in first-order direct exposures across multiple crises; or write the test and the cascade over the same bond set and show identical behaviour on mechanism-changing cases.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that stress tests are useless. The BIS review finds them ill-suited as early warning devices but effective as crisis management and resolution tools. The verdict is bounded to strong, out-of-sample anticipation of a mechanism-changing rare cascade.
- Not that a specific mechanism, timing, probability or institution is identified. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that a clean result is a warning, or that a nil result is proof of safety.
- Not that more scenarios are more evidence (Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not").
- Not `grounded`, and not corroborated by agreement among frames that share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In order of directness:

1. An out-of-sample case: a stress test with changing mechanisms and links flags a cascade that the crisis confirms.
2. A closure proof that the model's mechanism vocabulary is closed under crisis operations.
3. A sealed-room argument giving a complete, finite enumeration of cascade mechanisms and contacts.
4. An instrument that keeps the channel reachable through a real crisis, verified against outcomes.

A negative version settles it too: if mechanism-changing cascades repeatedly occur after clean stress-test results across crises in which the model's mechanisms held, the channel-decay finding is confirmed at the ground.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :70, Fig 8 :32, Fig 10 :24-26, Fig 9 :44, Fig 12 :117 and :121. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: is there a documented crisis in which the stress-test channel stayed reachable and the mechanism was represented? One case refutes it.
3. Check C3 against the network-contagion literature: are the consequential paths first order, or third order as the DDT line and the Battiston amplification result suggest?
4. Name the instrument you would trust to keep the anchor reachable during a crisis, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
