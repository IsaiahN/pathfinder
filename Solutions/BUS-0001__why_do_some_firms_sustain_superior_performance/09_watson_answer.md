## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** superior performance persists when both C1 (a maintained channel to a paying ground) and C2 (renewed reach through contact with a differing closure) hold, and is lost quickly when either fails; the capability that produced the advantage can change sign when the residual changes. The verdict is the union kept separate per domain, never averaged. Mode `specified`. The business evidence pool corroborates the two-condition shape only after re-mapping Barney to Γ; the conflict was returned to the census and did not flip the verdict. Low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not the firm-level fact. Which routines win is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A firm failing C2 would be sold channel instruments, or a firm failing C1 would be told to import more, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) sustained performance unchanged after the market stops paying, (2) advantage lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high, (3) two decades of same closure with new ground-verified readings and no import, (4) invariant capability valence across a change of residual, or (5) one intervention restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-firm persistence is fully predicted by averaged industry effects would also reverse it.
