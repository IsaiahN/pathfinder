## Q1 - Are two firms with the same resources isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two firms with an identical list of assets and capabilities have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, that is, if one composes those atoms as a repeated sequence and the other as a reconfigurable network, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the resource list. This is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that "it is not what you have, it is what you do with it", but it is stronger, because it says the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the firms share only a pattern (both use `state → routine → market`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A resource-list comparison that finds no difference between two firms cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two firms with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in persistence with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the business application. The world corroboration is Barney's premise that firms are heterogeneous in resources (abstract) plus Teece's *"distinctive processes (ways of coordinating and combining)"*, but both are the same strategy evidence pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a firm facing a changed market:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the market's return at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what the changed situation affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C1 (contact) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (reach) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the firm names what worked but never imports a new atom. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The market-facing link (1) has accounting and share data; link 5 has almost none. So budget will naturally pile up at link 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a firm whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Resource-based view** (Barney 1991: value, rareness, imitability, substitutability) | the firm's Γ, its library of hard-to-copy atoms | Γ is a real operand; Fig 6 :21 says minting cannot add an atom | Why the same Γ pays for decades in one habitat and not another. It has no contact variable, so it conflicts with Fig 11 :40 until re-mapped as a closure |
| **Dynamic capabilities** (Teece, Pisano, Shuen 1997: reconfigure internal and external competences) | the operator that renews reach | close to C2 and to Fig 6 :23 | It is a business-pool binding, not a derivation. It does not say what would refute it, and it does not separate C1 from C2 |
| **Core rigidities** (Leonard-Barton 1992) | the valence flip of a formerly good routine | matches Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119 | Bibliographic record only this session; used as a name, not as evidence |
| **Industry structure** (Porter, via Teece's review) | habitat conditions and entry barriers | part of D2; Fig 11 :46 names conditions as habitat elements | Rumelt 1991 reports *"small stable industry effects"* against *"very large stable business-unit effects"*, so it cannot be the whole answer, and averaging is barred by Fig 1 :22 |
| **Organizational ecology / resilience** (MathisonSlee et al. 2022) | rigidity trap, cross-scale interaction, response diversity | low-overlap corroboration of F5 and the membrane | No firm-level market measurement; it is an analogue, not this ground |
| **Cognitive entrenchment** (Dane 2010 via Trinh 2019) | individual expert schemas becoming stable | Fig 7 :36, Fig 10 :26 at a different scale | Individual stratum; Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at firm scale |
| **Luck / information asymmetry** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the two low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the firm-level fact.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the resource-based emphasis on things hard to copy, as the Γ operand under a closure that has not met a differing closure;
- the dynamic-capabilities emphasis on reconfiguration, as C2 and Fig 6 :23;
- the core-rigidity emphasis on the flip, as Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119;
- the industry-structure emphasis on habitat, as D2.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific resource or routine that will work next. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two firms with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two firms sharing only a `state → routine → market` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard business explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
