## Complexity Science and Network Theory: 100 Unresolved Questions

### 1. Foundations, definitions, and explanation
1. What distinguishes a genuinely complex system from a merely complicated one?
2. Can complexity be defined by a single measurable quantity, or does it require different measures for different systems?
3. Which features—nonlinearity, feedback, adaptation, heterogeneity, or network structure—are necessary for complex behavior to emerge?
4. When does describing a system as “emergent” explain its behavior, and when does it merely rename what is not yet understood?
5. Can macro-level patterns be derived from micro-level rules without losing essential information?
6. Which kinds of macroscopic properties are genuinely irreducible to the behavior of individual components?
7. How should researchers distinguish a system’s organizing principles from patterns produced by measurement or analysis choices?
8. Can two systems with very different components and mechanisms belong to the same universality class?
9. What makes a model of a complex system explanatory rather than simply capable of reproducing observed patterns?
10. Can a general theory of complex systems make testable predictions across domains without erasing important domain-specific differences?

### 2. Emergence, self-organization, and collective behavior
11. Under what conditions do local interactions produce stable large-scale order rather than continual fluctuations?
12. How can researchers distinguish self-organization from organization imposed by external constraints or hidden coordination?
13. Which types of local rules produce robust collective behavior across different initial conditions?
14. When do small changes in interaction rules trigger abrupt transitions in a system’s collective state?
15. Can emergent patterns be predicted from interaction rules when the components themselves are changing?
16. How do competing forms of positive and negative feedback determine whether a pattern grows, stabilizes, or collapses?
17. When does diversity among components promote collective resilience, and when does it impede coordination?
18. Can collective behavior arise without shared goals, central control, or common information?
19. How does the spatial scale of local interactions affect the size and persistence of emergent structures?
20. What evidence would show that a large-scale pattern has causal power over its constituent parts, rather than merely summarizing them?

### 3. Network structure and dynamics
21. Which network properties most strongly determine how fast or widely a process spreads: degree distribution, clustering, community structure, or something else?
22. When do highly connected hubs stabilize a network, and when do they make it more vulnerable to targeted failure?
23. How do overlapping, multiplex networks change the spread of information, disease, risk, or cooperation?
24. How should network models represent links that vary in strength, direction, duration, and meaning?
25. When can a network’s topology be treated as fixed, and when must link formation and removal be modeled as part of the dynamics?
26. How do hidden or unobserved connections bias conclusions about network structure and system behavior?
27. Can researchers infer a network’s causal influence from observed correlations when the network itself is only partially measured?
28. What makes a network robust to random failure but fragile to coordinated or strategic disruption?
29. How do spatial constraints and physical costs alter the behavior predicted by abstract network models?
30. When do community boundaries reduce contagion, and when do they help sustain it?

### 4. Prediction, uncertainty, and causal inference
31. Which complex systems are predictably governed by stable statistical regularities even when individual outcomes are unpredictable?
32. How can researchers distinguish fundamental unpredictability from uncertainty caused by incomplete data or imperfect models?
33. What determines the useful prediction horizon for a system with nonlinear feedback and changing structure?
34. Can forecasts remain reliable when predictions themselves alter people’s behavior and thereby change the system?
35. How should uncertainty be quantified when possible outcomes depend on poorly known mechanisms as well as random variation?
36. When do ensemble forecasts capture genuine uncertainty, and when do they simply repeat the same structural assumptions?
37. Can causal mechanisms be identified in systems where interventions change several connected parts at once?
38. How can researchers test whether a model’s predictions generalize to new conditions rather than merely fit historical data?
39. What kinds of data are sufficient to identify causal influence in a system with feedback, confounding, and hidden variables?
40. When is a system too sensitive to initial conditions for precise prediction, yet still predictable at the level of distributions or regimes?

### 5. Critical transitions, tipping points, and resilience
41. Can impending tipping points be detected reliably from early-warning signals in real-world systems?
42. How often do indicators such as rising variance or slower recovery falsely signal an approaching transition?
43. Do all abrupt changes result from gradual loss of resilience, or can some occur without detectable warning?
44. How do multiple interacting tipping points alter the likelihood and consequences of systemic change?
45. When can a system recover after crossing a threshold, and when does hysteresis make reversal difficult?
46. Which interventions can shift a system away from a tipping point without producing harmful side effects elsewhere?
47. Can resilience be measured in a way that distinguishes resistance to disturbance from the ability to recover or reorganize?
48. How do slow-moving variables, such as accumulated stress or resource depletion, create sudden system-wide change?
49. Under what conditions does modularity prevent local failures from becoming systemic cascades?
50. Can deliberate disturbance sometimes increase a system’s long-term resilience, and how could that be tested?

### 6. Cascades, contagion, and systemic risk
51. When does a local shock remain contained, and when does it trigger a cascade across a network?
52. How do threshold-based contagion and simple exposure-based contagion interact in the same system?
53. Which network structures most effectively limit cascading failures without blocking beneficial diffusion?
54. How do correlated risks change the usefulness of diversification in financial, ecological, or infrastructure networks?
55. Can systemic risk be measured before a crisis when connections and exposures are partly hidden?
56. When do interventions that protect individual components increase fragility at the system level?
57. How do feedback between physical networks and social or financial networks amplify crises?
58. Can stress tests anticipate rare cascades when the mechanisms and network structure may change during a crisis?
59. What determines whether a cascade dies out, becomes localized, or repeatedly reignites?
60. How should regulators or managers balance the efficiency of tightly connected systems against their exposure to cascading failure?

### 7. Epidemics, information, and social dynamics
61. How do mobility, household structure, institutions, and online connections jointly shape epidemic spread?
62. When does clustering in contact networks slow transmission, and when does it create persistent reservoirs of infection?
63. How do behavioral changes in response to an epidemic alter the epidemic’s own dynamics?
64. Which features of a social network make misinformation spread farther or persist longer than accurate information?
65. Can information cascades be distinguished from genuine changes in people’s beliefs or preferences?
66. Under what conditions do social movements grow through local recruitment, and when do they depend on broader structural shifts?
67. How do identity, trust, and group boundaries change the transmission of norms or political behavior across networks?
68. When does social influence promote consensus, and when does it intensify polarization or fragmentation?
69. Can a platform’s recommendation system be modeled as part of the social network rather than as an external influence?
70. How can researchers identify whether a movement’s apparent turning point reflects a real coordination threshold or changes in visibility and reporting?

### 8. Adaptation, learning, and evolution
71. How does learning by individual agents change the long-term behavior of the systems they collectively form?
72. When does adaptation make a system more resilient, and when does it cause agents to exploit the system in ways that undermine it?
73. How do strategic agents respond to network interventions, and when does that response defeat the intervention’s intended effect?
74. Can evolutionary dynamics explain the emergence of cooperation in networks when short-term incentives favor defection?
75. How do cultural transmission and biological evolution interact to shape collective behavior?
76. Under what conditions do adaptive networks converge to stable structures rather than continually reorganize?
77. How does competition among different strategies alter the system’s response to shocks?
78. When can learning produce collective intelligence, and when does it amplify shared errors?
79. How do institutions and informal norms constrain or redirect the adaptation of networked agents?
80. Can a system’s capacity to adapt be predicted from its structure before a crisis occurs?

### 9. Modeling, measurement, and reproducibility
81. How can researchers choose the right level of detail when simpler models are easier to analyze but may omit decisive mechanisms?
82. Which network measurements remain reliable when data are incomplete, sampled unevenly, or collected through biased platforms?
83. How should models represent agents whose rules, goals, or access to information differ across time and context?
84. What standards can establish that an agent-based model is empirically grounded rather than an illustrative story?
85. How can researchers compare models that reproduce the same observed pattern using different underlying mechanisms?
86. When should a model prioritize interpretability, predictive accuracy, mechanistic realism, or usefulness for intervention?
87. How can researchers test complex-system models when controlled experiments are impossible or ethically unacceptable?
88. What practices would make computational results in complexity science more reproducible across software, data, and parameter choices?
89. How can scale-dependent measurements be compared when a system looks organized at one scale and disordered at another?
90. Can machine-learning models reveal genuine system mechanisms, or do they mainly capture correlations that fail under changing conditions?

### 10. Intervention, control, and cross-domain theory
91. Is it possible to control a complex networked system without fully understanding its internal structure?
92. Which intervention strategies work when agents adapt strategically and the network changes in response?
93. When does targeting a small number of influential nodes outperform broad, distributed intervention?
94. How can intervention designers anticipate displacement effects, where reducing one risk shifts it elsewhere in the system?
95. What ethical principles should guide interventions in systems where benefits and harms are distributed unevenly?
96. Can systems be steered toward desirable outcomes while preserving their capacity for self-organization?
97. Which insights about epidemics, financial crises, traffic, ecosystems, and social movements genuinely transfer across domains?
98. How can researchers tell whether a shared mathematical pattern reflects a shared mechanism or only a superficial resemblance?
99. What kinds of cross-disciplinary collaboration are needed to build models that connect network structure to institutional, ecological, or behavioral processes?
100. Can complexity science develop reliable principles for deciding when to predict, when to prepare for multiple scenarios, and when to focus on resilience instead?
