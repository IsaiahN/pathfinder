## Institutions and Political Economy: 100 Unresolved Questions

### 1. Institutional origins and development
1. Why do some societies develop institutions that constrain powerful actors while others consolidate power in a few hands?
2. Under what conditions do inclusive political institutions emerge, and how much do they depend on prior economic or social arrangements?
3. Do institutions primarily reflect a society’s existing balance of power, or can they independently reshape that balance?
4. Why do similar institutional designs produce different outcomes in different societies?
5. How much do geography, disease environments, and resource endowments influence institutional development compared with political choices?
6. Can institutional change be explained by general principles, or is it largely shaped by historically specific events?
7. Why do some institutional reforms endure while others are reversed after political power changes hands?
8. How do crises create opportunities for institutional reform, and why do some crises instead strengthen entrenched power?
9. Can institutions be deliberately designed for conditions that do not yet exist, or do they mainly adapt through trial and error?
10. How should researchers distinguish institutions that cause prosperity from those that arise as a consequence of it?

### 2. State capacity and public administration
11. What forms of state capacity—tax collection, public services, enforcement, or information gathering—matter most for effective governance?
12. How can governments build administrative capacity without creating tools for repression or patronage?
13. Why are some states able to implement policies consistently while others struggle to carry out even basic decisions?
14. When does centralization improve coordination, and when does it undermine local knowledge and accountability?
15. How do governments acquire reliable information about citizens, firms, and local conditions?
16. What makes public agencies resistant to political interference without making them unresponsive to elected leaders?
17. How can states provide services impartially when public officials depend on political networks for employment or promotion?
18. When does privatizing or contracting public functions improve performance, and when does it weaken accountability?
19. How do weak states maintain legitimacy, and how do capable states lose it?
20. Can state capacity be measured separately from a government’s willingness to use that capacity fairly?

### 3. Trust, legitimacy, and compliance
21. What makes people regard an institution as legitimate, even when they disagree with its decisions?
22. How do fairness, effectiveness, identity, and participation combine to shape trust in public institutions?
23. When do people comply with rules because they believe the rules are legitimate rather than because they fear punishment?
24. How can institutions earn trust after histories of discrimination, violence, or corruption?
25. Does trust in institutions encourage cooperation, or do institutions work well mainly where trust already exists?
26. Why do citizens sometimes trust informal authorities more than the state?
27. How do unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement affect institutional legitimacy over time?
28. Can transparent decision-making increase trust when institutions produce unpopular outcomes?
29. How do misinformation and partisan identity alter public judgments about institutional performance?
30. What enables people to distinguish justified criticism of institutions from efforts to delegitimize them?

### 4. Corruption, patronage, and accountability
31. Under what conditions does corruption become a stable system of governance rather than isolated misconduct?
32. Why do anti-corruption campaigns sometimes reduce corruption and sometimes become tools for political purges?
33. Which combinations of oversight, transparency, enforcement, and public participation most reliably constrain corruption?
34. How do patronage networks provide social protection or political coordination while also weakening impartial governance?
35. When can informal payments help people navigate dysfunctional institutions, and when do they deepen exclusion and inefficiency?
36. How do officials decide whether to follow formal rules or the unwritten expectations of political and social networks?
37. Why do some accountability institutions constrain the ruling coalition while others mainly police its opponents?
38. How can whistleblowers and investigative journalism expose corruption without facing retaliation or capture?
39. Does increasing transparency reduce corruption if enforcement remains weak?
40. How do citizens coordinate against corruption when individuals face high personal costs for reporting it?

### 5. Power, inequality, and elite influence
41. How do economic elites shape political rules without directly controlling government?
42. When does wealth translate into political influence, and when can institutions prevent that conversion?
43. How do campaign finance, lobbying, media ownership, and professional networks interact to concentrate political power?
44. Why do some institutions constrain powerful groups while others reproduce their advantages across generations?
45. How do social class, race, caste, ethnicity, or gender affect access to public authority and legal protection?
46. Can institutions be formally impartial while producing systematically unequal outcomes?
47. How do concentrated ownership and market power reshape the rules governing competition and labor?
48. When do coalitions between business, labor, and government support broad development, and when do they exclude outsiders?
49. How does inequality weaken collective action for institutional reform?
50. What institutional arrangements can limit elite capture without making public decision-making unworkable?

### 6. Law, rights, and rule enforcement
51. What makes the rule of law credible when those who enforce the law are also subject to political pressure?
52. Why are some legal systems predictable in practice while others depend heavily on personal connections or discretion?
53. How do differences between written law and everyday enforcement shape people’s expectations and behavior?
54. When does judicial independence protect rights, and when can it insulate judges from democratic accountability?
55. How can legal institutions adapt to new social conditions without undermining stability and predictability?
56. Why do some states enforce contracts reliably but fail to protect people from coercion or discrimination?
57. How do customary, religious, and state legal systems coexist, compete, or influence one another?
58. What determines whether citizens can effectively use legal rights that formally exist?
59. Can institutions enforce rules consistently while allowing discretion for unusual cases?
60. How do legal institutions shape the distribution of power between the state, employers, workers, and citizens?

### 7. Collective action, conflict, and political order
61. Why can groups facing similar grievances organize collectively in some settings but remain fragmented in others?
62. When do political institutions channel conflict peacefully, and when do they intensify it?
63. How do ethnic, regional, religious, and class identities become politically mobilized?
64. What makes power-sharing agreements durable after civil conflict?
65. Can institutions prevent violence when political actors expect future exclusion or retaliation?
66. How do informal bargains between elites stabilize political order, and what makes them break down?
67. When does repression deter collective action, and when does it help unify opposition?
68. How do local institutions mediate disputes before they escalate into broader conflict?
69. Why do some transitions from authoritarian rule produce accountable institutions while others reproduce old power structures?
70. How can institutions make political competition credible when losing power may carry severe personal or economic risks?

### 8. Democracy, representation, and participation
71. What makes elected representatives responsive to constituents rather than donors, parties, or narrow interest groups?
72. How do electoral systems shape party organization, representation, polarization, and accountability?
73. When does direct public participation improve policy, and when does it privilege the best-organized groups?
74. How can democratic institutions represent future generations and people who lack political power?
75. Why do some democracies maintain stable public support despite severe disagreement, while others become vulnerable to breakdown?
76. How do parties balance internal discipline with representation of diverse views?
77. Can independent agencies protect long-term public interests without weakening democratic control?
78. What kinds of constitutional checks prevent the abuse of power without allowing governments to become paralyzed?
79. How do media systems and information inequality affect the quality of democratic accountability?
80. Under what conditions can democratic institutions correct their own failures before public trust collapses?

### 9. Economic institutions and distribution
81. How do property rights affect investment when ownership is secure for some groups but precarious for others?
82. Which labor-market institutions best balance worker security, economic adaptation, and employment opportunities?
83. How do welfare institutions change political coalitions, social trust, and willingness to pay taxes?
84. When do industrial policies build productive capacity, and when do they protect politically connected firms?
85. How do financial institutions distribute risk, credit, and economic power across society?
86. What institutional conditions help economies adapt to technological change without concentrating its gains?
87. How do tax systems shape compliance, public capacity, inequality, and perceptions of fairness?
88. When do public-private partnerships improve infrastructure and services, and when do they transfer public risks to private actors?
89. How do trade rules and global economic institutions constrain or enable domestic policy choices?
90. Which institutional arrangements help societies share the costs of economic shocks without undermining long-term investment?

### 10. Institutional change, adaptation, and research
91. How do ordinary people, officials, firms, and social movements reshape institutions through everyday practices?
92. Why do institutions often persist after the conditions that produced them have changed?
93. What mechanisms allow institutions to learn from policy failures rather than conceal or repeat them?
94. When does institutional flexibility help societies adapt, and when does it create uncertainty and opportunities for capture?
95. How can reformers change institutional incentives without triggering resistance that preserves the status quo?
96. Why do imported institutional models sometimes succeed and sometimes fail when applied in different political and social settings?
97. How should researchers separate institutional effects from the historical, economic, and cultural conditions that accompany them?
98. What evidence can establish whether an institution caused a change in trust, growth, corruption, or conflict?
99. How can comparative research account for informal rules and practices that official institutional measures overlook?
100. What would a general theory of institutional change need to explain about how rules shape people’s choices—and how people, in turn, reshape the rules?
