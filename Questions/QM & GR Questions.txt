Let’s do **one field at a time**. I’ll start with **astronomy and cosmology**, organized into modules. These are research questions, not 100 equally likely theories: some are major mainstream uncertainties; others are narrower questions researchers are actively measuring. The biggest debates often concern how to interpret evidence, not whether the evidence exists. For example, dark matter’s gravitational effects are supported by multiple kinds of observations, while its physical identity is unknown. <citation src="3,5"></citation>

## Astronomy and cosmology: 100 research questions

### A. Dark matter

1. What particle or object, if any, makes up most dark matter?
2. Is dark matter made of **WIMPs**, **axions**, another undiscovered particle, or several components?
3. Could some or all dark matter consist of **primordial black holes** formed in the early universe?
4. Does dark matter interact with ordinary matter through anything besides gravity?
5. Does dark matter interact with itself, and could that explain the structures seen in galaxies?
6. Is dark matter “cold,” “warm,” or a mixture—and what observations best distinguish these possibilities?
7. How much dark matter exists in the smallest galaxies and dark-matter clumps?
8. Do observations of galaxy centers match the dense dark-matter profiles predicted by simulations?
9. Why do some galaxies appear to have less dark matter than expected?
10. Could a single dark-matter model explain galaxies, clusters, gravitational lensing, and the early universe?

**Main approaches to compare:** particle searches; astronomical observations and simulations; alternatives that modify gravity. The strongest evidence supports some form of unseen gravitating mass, but no dark-matter particle has been confirmed. <citation src="3"></citation>

### B. Dark energy and cosmic expansion

11. Is dark energy a constant property of space, as in the standard cosmological model?
12. Does dark energy change in strength over time?
13. If it changes, is that change smooth, temporary, or connected to a new field?
14. Could cosmic acceleration instead mean that gravity behaves differently on very large scales?
15. Does dark energy have any connection to the vacuum energy predicted by quantum physics?
16. Why is the observed value associated with dark energy so small compared with some theoretical estimates?
17. Could apparent acceleration arise partly from how cosmic structure affects observations?
18. Which combination of supernovae, galaxy clustering, and gravitational lensing best measures the expansion history?
19. How much do current results depend on assumptions built into the analysis?
20. What future observation would most clearly distinguish a cosmological constant from changing dark energy?

**Main approaches to compare:** a constant cosmological term; dynamical dark energy; modified gravity; and possible observational or modeling effects. The standard model remains a central baseline, while new results are tested against it. <citation src="1,2"></citation>

### C. The Hubble tension and cosmological measurements

27. Do independent measurement methods converge when they use overlapping data?
28. Is the disagreement consistent across different teams and analysis pipelines?
29. Are other cosmological parameters also in tension, or is the problem confined to the expansion rate?
30. What combination of future measurements could tell a real physical discrepancy from an analysis problem?
