## Information Theory and the Foundations of Computation: 100 Unresolved Questions

### 1. What information is
1. Is information a physical property of a system, a relation between a system and an observer, or both?
2. Can semantic information—information that means something—be defined independently of an interpreting agent?
3. What distinguishes information from mere correlation?
4. Can a signal carry information if no system can detect or interpret it?
5. Is information fundamentally discrete, or can it be continuous at the physical level?
6. How should information be defined when the relevant possibilities or probability distributions are unknown?
7. Can information exist without uncertainty, or is uncertainty essential to the concept?
8. What separates information from knowledge, data, and evidence?
9. Do different physical systems instantiate the same information, or only structurally similar representations?
10. Is there a single notion of information that coherently applies across physics, biology, cognition, and society?

### 2. Information, physics, and thermodynamics
11. What physical resources are required to create, store, transmit, and erase information?
12. How universal is the relationship between information erasure and thermodynamic cost in real, noisy systems?
13. Can information be transferred or transformed without a corresponding physical change in the carrier?
14. How should information be defined in quantum systems when measurement outcomes depend on the measurement context?
15. What becomes of information in black holes, and how should its apparent preservation be reconciled with quantum theory?
16. Is the information content of a physical system observer-dependent, or can it be specified objectively?
17. How do information and causal structure constrain each other in physical theories?
18. Can information-theoretic principles explain why physical laws take their observed form?
19. What is the correct account of information in systems far from thermodynamic equilibrium?
20. Can information flow provide a more fundamental account of physical processes than energy flow alone?

### 3. Communication and coding
21. How close can real communication systems come to theoretical limits when channels change unpredictably?
22. Which coding strategies best protect messages when errors are correlated rather than independent?
23. How should a communication system allocate bandwidth among accuracy, speed, robustness, and energy use?
24. Can communication be made reliable when sender and receiver do not share a stable code or common context?
25. How do systems negotiate new meanings or protocols without an agreed-upon starting code?
26. What determines whether redundancy improves reliability or instead wastes scarce communication capacity?
27. How can a receiver distinguish noise from a rare but meaningful signal?
28. When does compression preserve the information that matters, and who determines what matters?
29. Can communication systems remain reliable when the channel is shaped by an adversary who adapts to the code?
30. How should information theory model communication that changes the beliefs or behavior of its participants?

### 4. Information, meaning, and interpretation
31. Can semantic content be measured without reducing meaning to statistical predictability or behavioral effect?
32. What makes a representation about something rather than merely correlated with it?
33. How do context and background knowledge change the information conveyed by the same signal?
34. Can two agents interpret the same data differently while both interpretations remain rational?
35. How should uncertainty about meaning be distinguished from uncertainty about whether a signal was received?
36. Can meaning be grounded in physical interaction without invoking a human-like interpreter?
37. What role do goals and needs play in determining which information is useful?
38. Can a message be objectively misleading, or does misleadingness always depend on an audience and context?
39. How can a system represent ambiguity without prematurely collapsing it into a single interpretation?
40. What would count as evidence that a machine understands information rather than merely processing its structure?

### 5. Biological information and the origin of life
41. In what sense does genetic material store information, and where does the biological meaning of that information reside?
42. How do cells distinguish functional signals from molecular noise in changing environments?
43. Can biological information be defined without reference to evolution, function, or an organism’s survival?
44. How do cells integrate signals from multiple pathways when those signals conflict or arrive at different times?
45. What mechanisms make cellular communication reliable despite molecular noise and component turnover?
46. How does information move between genes, proteins, cells, organisms, and environments?
47. Can epigenetic inheritance be described as information transmission, and what distinguishes it from inherited state or bias?
48. How do cells decide which environmental information to ignore, retain, or act on?
49. What is the minimum information-processing capacity needed for a system to adaptively respond to its environment?
50. How did early life acquire mechanisms for storing, copying, and interpreting information before modern cellular systems existed?

### 6. Brains, cognition, and perception
51. What kinds of information are represented in neural activity, and how can they be distinguished from mere statistical patterns?
52. Does the brain encode information in identifiable neural representations, or is information distributed across dynamics and connections?
53. How does the nervous system decide which sensory signals deserve attention?
54. What determines whether perception reflects incoming evidence, prior expectations, or their interaction?
55. How does the brain combine information from different senses when they conflict?
56. Can consciousness be explained in information-theoretic terms, and what observations would discriminate competing accounts?
57. How does memory preserve useful structure while discarding irrelevant detail?
58. When does compression in perception support efficient cognition, and when does it produce systematic distortion?
59. How can a brain estimate uncertainty about its own beliefs and decisions?
60. What information-processing features distinguish intelligence from sophisticated pattern recognition?

### 7. Learning, inference, and decision-making
61. How can a learner distinguish a stable pattern from a coincidence in a changing environment?
62. What is the best formal account of learning when data are sparse, noisy, and unevenly informative?
63. How should a system balance exploration for new information against acting on what it already knows?
64. When does prior knowledge improve inference, and when does it lock a learner into a mistaken model?
65. Can information gain alone guide rational inquiry, or must it be combined with relevance, cost, and consequences?
66. How should decision-makers compare information that reduces uncertainty with information that changes what actions are possible?
67. What makes a causal explanation more informative than a predictive correlation?
68. How can learners detect when the environment has changed enough to invalidate what they previously learned?
69. Is there a principled way to quantify the value of information before its content is known?
70. How should a decision system act when the information needed to choose well is unobtainable or fundamentally uncertain?

### 8. Computation and its foundations
71. What physical processes qualify as computation, rather than merely evolving according to rules?
72. Is computation a property of the system itself, or of how an observer maps its states to inputs and outputs?
73. What distinguishes a general-purpose computer from a system that can imitate a computation only under a chosen interpretation?
74. Are there physically meaningful limits to computation beyond familiar limits on time, memory, and energy?
75. How should computation be defined in analog, chemical, biological, and quantum systems?
76. Can computation occur without discrete symbols or a central processor?
77. What determines whether a computational model captures the causal structure of the system it describes?
78. Can any physical process be simulated efficiently, or do some systems resist useful simulation even when they are computable in principle?
79. How do randomness and nondeterminism affect what can be computed and what can be predicted?
80. What role does the observer play in deciding which physical states count as computational states?

### 9. Information in AI and machine learning
81. What information do large learning systems actually encode in their parameters?
82. How can researchers distinguish generalizable representations from patterns that only fit training data?
83. When does compression in a model preserve useful structure, and when does it erase rare but important cases?
84. Can a model reliably communicate uncertainty about unfamiliar inputs, or does it mainly reflect the limits of its training and evaluation?
85. How do training data, objectives, and feedback channels shape the information an AI system learns to prioritize?
86. Can a model identify when its sources are misleading, rather than merely reproducing their patterns?
87. What makes information generated by an AI system trustworthy when its internal reasoning is difficult to interpret?
88. How should systems combine information from tools, users, and learned parameters when those sources conflict?
89. Can AI systems develop useful internal representations of causal structure from observational data alone?
90. What evidence would show that an AI system can use information flexibly in new contexts rather than relying on familiar associations?

### 10. Social information, reliability, and misinformation
91. How do trust, reputation, and social identity affect whether information is believed or shared?
92. Why do some false claims spread more effectively than accurate information, even when corrective evidence is available?
93. How can a society distinguish reliable expertise from persuasive but poorly grounded claims?
94. When does repetition increase confidence without increasing the actual evidence for a claim?
95. How do media platforms’ ranking and recommendation systems alter the information people encounter and trust?
96. Can institutions make public information more reliable without suppressing legitimate disagreement or minority evidence?
97. How should information systems handle claims whose truth is uncertain, contested, or dependent on future events?
98. What kinds of transparency help people evaluate information, and when does disclosure overwhelm or mislead them?
99. How does unequal access to information shape power, coordination, and social inequality?
100. Can a shared information environment be maintained when communities disagree about sources, standards of evidence, and what counts as relevant context?
